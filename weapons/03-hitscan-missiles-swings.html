Hitscan, missiles and swings

A weapon hurts things in one of three ways. A hitscan is an instant ray. A missile is a thing that flies. A swing is a volume that sweeps through an arc over a few ticks. This lesson covers each one's numbers, then what your own game client shows at once and what only the server decides.

You'll learnthe instant shot's numbers and how spread works per ray; how a weapon launches a missile, and what the Missiles page decides about it; arcs, bounces, splits and homing; blasts, recoil and warnings; missile trails; the swing step and its numbers, the default swing, two swings at once, and a swing kept on a state the older way; and the line between predicted and server-decided Time35 minutes Needsa weapon with states (The phase machine); for missiles, the Missiles page, which the Enemies track covers in depth (Attacks)

Hitscan: an instant ray

The step fire an instant shot for … damage, range … m, spread …°, its ray …, where it lands … (hitscan) is in the Attack group when you add a step. On the tick it runs, the engine casts rays from the player and each ray stops at the first wall or body it meets. No travel time. The pistol, the shotgun, the chaingun and even DOOM's chainsaw are hitscans.

ArgumentIn the editorWhat it does
damage… damageWhat each ray that hits costs the target. A fixed number: there's no random roll.
rangerange … mHow far the ray reaches, in metres (the world's units). DOOM's guns reach 2048 of its map units; at 48 to a metre, that's about 42.67.
spreadspread …°Degrees of random turn, rolled for each ray: each one is turned somewhere between −spread and +spread, sideways.
countmore of itRays in one shot. Default 1. DOOM's shotgun is 7, its super shotgun 20. Up to 32 are predicted; more, and the weapons run on the server only.

"more of it": the rest of a step. A step's sentence sets the arguments it names: for an instant shot, its damage, range, spread, its ray and where it lands. Tap the step, and under the sentence more of it folds out every other argument the engine takes, by its own name and each with an ⓘ: a shotgun's count of rays, knockback, a slow, how wide the burst where it lands is; for a missile launch, its count, offset and angle; a push's vertical; a sound's predicted. Cinderfall's scattergun has its 8 rays there. Every weapon word lists them all.

Show where it went. A shot that leaves no trace is one the player can't tell landed. its ray and where it lands are two menus in the step: the first lists the tracer and particle kinds and not drawn, the second the same kinds and nothing. A new instant shot starts with its ray effect 1 (a tracer) and where it lands effect 12 (sparks), and so does the Pistol recipe's. Pick not drawn and nothing for a shot that reads as a punch.

The numbers in a sentence take a decimal: 42.67, not a fraction. (The swing's own fields, below, also take fractions such as -1/3.)

Why spread is per ray

Without a roll per ray, all seven of a shotgun's pellets would travel the same line, and a shotgun would be a pistol that hits seven times as hard at any distance. With it, the pellets fan out, and fewer of them land the further away the target is. That's the whole difference between DOOM's pistol (1 ray, spread 3) and its shotgun (7 rays, spread 14).

  • Set the target distance to 2 and fire the shotgun: about six of the seven rays land, 60 damage or so.
  • Move it to 20: about two land, most of them on the middle target.
  • Set count to 1 and spread to 0: a perfect rifle. Set count 20 and spread 26 for DOOM's super shotgun.

The spread is only sideways. Up and down is handled by aiming, below.

Aiming up and down

In third person, a player's input has a heading but no pitch, so the engine aims for you vertically. It finds the nearest visible solid body inside a narrow cone around your facing (5.625° each way, DOOM's number) and aims at its middle. In first person, your shot follows the view's own pitch instead. Corpses and bodies a script made non-solid are not aimed at.

Missiles: things that fly

The step launch the missile … (launch) fires one of the module's missiles. The missiles themselves are on their own page, Missiles in the rail, "shared by every monster and weapon of the module": a rocket launcher and a cyberdemon can fire the same rocket.

Tap a launch step, and edit missile opens that missile on Missiles. The weapon's Attacks tab lists the module's missiles too, with Open Missiles.

The missile, not the weapon, decides what a hit does. On Missiles, each missile has a side view of its flight as the engine flies it (Fire plays it), and says who fires it ("used by flare · fire"). Its parts:

  • Flight: Speed (m/s), Falls (m/s², empty flies straight), Homing, Lasts (then "when it runs out" happens), Launch height (empty: from the middle of the shooter, as the engine launches it), Hit size, Bounces and At most (missiles from one shot).
  • When it hits, When it runs out, When it bounces and On its way: what it does, as sentences: "hurt what it hit for 20", "a blast of 90 damage within 3.2 m, weaker at the edge", an effect, a sound, a split.
  • Looks: Drawn as (a prop, with its own sprite and scale), Trail (a particle kind that follows it from launch until it bursts, at that kind's rate, drawn along the flight when you press Fire; see Attacks, trails) and the states it plays flying and bursting. A new missile, and one a recipe makes (bolt), gets a prop of its own name, a glowing dot the studio paints, and is drawn as that while Drawn as says "(nothing: an unseen shot)". A missile left with nothing to be drawn as is named as a problem before you press Play (Attacks, looks).

The Enemies track walks through the page field by field: Attacks, missiles. What matters most for a weapon:

  • A falling missile flies a real arc. With Falls, a grenade rises and drops. A player's starts along their aim, up or down, and their own client flies the same arc. Thrown low or high makes Speed its launch speed.
  • It can bounce. Bounces off walls, floors and ceilings, keeping a share of its speed each time (Keeps of its speed), and doing When it bounces there.
  • It can launch missiles. "split into 3 … spread over 30°" in When it hits, When it runs out or On its way: a cluster bomb, bomblets dropped as it flies. At most caps a shot's missiles (16 unless you say).
  • Homing. A player's homing missile locks onto what their auto-aim picks. Homing starts after delays the turn, and Gives up beyond lets a target too far round go.

A Lasts stops a missile that never hits anything; without one, every shot into the sky would keep a slot for ever.

Plasma is a missile too: a fast one with a small hit and no blast, fired from an automatic fire state only a few ticks long. DOOM II's plasma rifle fires every 5 ticks.

Blasts, recoil and warnings

Three more steps shape how a weapon feels.

"a blast of …": damage from the weapon itself

a blast of … damage within … m (damage_area) hurts everything inside a circle, once. Walls are not checked: it's a shockwave, not a ray. In the engine's words it can also be moved in front of you (offset; 0 is centred on you: a ground slam), lifted off the floor (height), and made weaker at the edge (falloff). It hurts you too unless hurts_owner is off.

"push the player": recoil and rocket jumps

push the player away at … m/s (impulse) shoves the shooter. away is recoil; facing throws you forward, a dash. With the engine's vertical it lifts you. DOOM II's super shotgun shoves you back at 2.92 m/s; its rocket launcher at 2.08.

"warn with …": warn first, hit later

warn with effect … for … s (telegraph) draws a warning, saying the blow lands in that time. Put it at 0 s of a wind-up state and the blast later in the same state. The gap between them is the time others have to move.

Swings: melee you can dodge

A swing is a collider that sweeps through an arc over several ticks. A target that steps out of the way mid-swing is missed. It's the same thing a monster's claw uses, and it's edited the same way: a swing is an attack like the others, a step with its own numbers in it.

Press + add a step at 0 s on a state and pick swing for … damage, … m long, from … to … over … s (swing) in the Attack group. A new one reads swing for 15 damage, 1.5 m long, from -60° to 60° over 0.2 s. It happens at its moment of the state, like an instant shot, and your own client predicts it, so it appears on the tick it happens, not a round trip later.

In the sentenceThe engine's wordWhat it does
swing for … damagedamageWhat a connected swing costs.
… m longreachHow far it reaches, to the target's surface, not its centre.
from …° to …°sweep_from, sweep_toWhere the collider starts and ends, in degrees from your aim; positive is to your left, so −60° to 60° sweeps right to left. Equal values make a thrust that doesn't turn.
over … sticksHow long it is live. Longer is easier to see coming, and to dodge.

Under the sentence, more of it folds out the rest by the engine's own names, in its units (turns and ticks), each with an ⓘ: more of it (half_arc, min_reach, pitch_from, pitch_to, half_pitch, knockback, hit_vfx, hit_vfx_radius, lift, slow, slow_ticks, cooldown). A new step starts with a half_arc of 0.0833 (30° each side) and a cooldown of 10 ticks. Each box marked ? may be left empty.

Under "more of it"What it does
half_arcHow wide, each side of the aim. All these angles are turns: 1/8 (0.125) is 45°.
min_reachAn inner edge. Above 0 it makes a ring that misses anything pressed against you: a spear.
pitch_from, pitch_to, half_pitchThe vertical sweep (0 level, 0.25 straight up). Empty half_pitch means no height check at all, which is right for a fist. Set it, and an overhead chop really misses what is at your feet.
knockback, liftHow hard a hit throws the target, away and up. An uppercut is lift.
hit_vfx, hit_vfx_radiusParticles where it lands, picked by their look (12 · sparks).
slow, slow_ticksSlows what it hits. Hitting the same target again replaces the slow, it doesn't stack.
cooldownTicks after this swing ends before another may start. At most 255.

The rules a swing step keeps

  • A newer swing replaces one still sweeping. The first stops where it is and hits nothing more; the second starts from its own sweep_from with a fresh list of who it has hit. So two blows can hit the same monster twice. That is how the Blade's heavy slash follows its first.
  • A swing during the cooldown does not happen. The cooldown starts when a swing ends, from that swing's cooldown; a replaced swing never ends, so it starts none. A swing refused this way is not kept for later.
  • A weapon with a swing step doesn't swing on the button. If any of its states has one, the attack button only moves it from state to state, and the steps say when it swings.
  • What a hit costs is decided by the server. Your client predicts the collider; the damage arrives with the server's answer.
  • Other players see the step's own shape, not the weapon's default: the server names the step, and their client already holds your weapon's table.

The default swing

Whatever a step leaves out comes from the weapon's default swing, on Advanced under Its default swing: "Swung by the attack button while no state has a swing step, and what a swing step leaves out. A swing is a step like any other attack: its numbers in it." With none, it says "This weapon has no swing: …" and offers + swing, which starts a short jab: damage 10, reach? 1.5, half_arc? 1/8, sweep_from? −1/8 to sweep_to? 1/8, ticks? 12, cooldown? 20, with a preview that plays it on a loop from above and from the side. remove the swing takes it away. What neither gives, the engine's own default fills; a swing that ends up with no damage at all is refused when the weapon loads.

A weapon with no swing step at all swings its default swing on the attack button: the swing starts when the button goes down, as long as its cooldown allows, and a press while one is still sweeping is ignored. A weapon with neither a step nor a default swing doesn't swing: a shotgun's fire button makes no collider.

A swing on a state: the older way

Before swings were steps, a state could carry a swing of its own, swung by the attack button while the weapon was in that state. A state has no swing form any more, but one that carries a swing from before still shows it, at the end of its editor, under Its own swing (the older way): "A swing shape kept on the state, which the attack button swings. A swing step does the same with its numbers in it, at the moment it says." Its fields and preview are there to read and change, and turn it into a swing step makes it a step:

  • It asks first: "Make swing's swing a step at 0 s? A weapon with a swing step in any state swings only through its steps: the attack button then just moves it from state to state."
  • The step goes in at 0 s with the shape the state had, and the studio says so: "swing now swings through a step at 0 s, with the shape it had." Move its at for a wind-up.
  • Turn every one. Once one state has a swing step, the button swings no more, so a state still carrying the older swing stops swinging.

Gem Hollow's fists and Cinderfall's wrench still swing the older way (below), and work as they always have.

Behind the scenes, a player's swing is written through the world's effect stack, which has 256 slots shared by the whole world; a monster's swing goes into its own body instead, so a level of a hundred monsters doesn't use them up. You don't set anything for this. It is why switching weapons replaces the swing cleanly.

How a swing looks in the game

The game draws every swing as a slash streak: a band near the tip of the swept wedge, laid tick by tick between where the blade was and where it is, fading behind it. It is built from the swing's own numbers, so it shows exactly where the collider went, and it is drawn whether the debug overlay is on or not. Your own swing is drawn from what your client predicts, except in first person, where the weapon on screen is the swing. Other players' swings are drawn from what the server sends, in the shape of the step they swung; monsters' too.

On Advanced, under Its default swing, how it looks in the game sets the look of every swing the weapon makes: show (off draws nothing; the swing still hits), color, opacity (0 to 1), fade (seconds), width (how much of the wedge, from the tip inward: 1 is all of it), blend (add glows, normal paints over) and texture (a picture under sprites/, or (none: a plain band)). Each field shows the look the swing really has; one you set reads (own), and back to the default look clears them.

  • The look is the weapon's, not a step's: every blow of a combo uses it, while the streak's shape follows each blow's own numbers.
  • The default every swing starts from is on Player → slash, with back to the engine's look. Other players' swings always use it, because your client does not know what they are holding.
  • Monsters have the same form (Attacks).
  • The studio writes assets/sprites/swings.json only when something is set, and a deleted weapon takes its look with it.

What is instant, and what the server decides

Your own weapon runs twice: on the server, and on your own client, which predicts it from the same weapon table and the same rules. When the two agree, which is almost always, you see your shot the moment you press. When they don't, your client is corrected to the server's version. Other players' weapons are never predicted: you see them as the server reports them.

Shown at once on your screenDecided by the server, a moment later
  • the state changes, the fire rate, combos, refire
  • cooldowns: the next shot is allowed on the same tick on both sides
  • the ammunition count, and switching weapons with the number keys
  • where every pellet goes: the spread is rolled the same way on both sides
  • the tracers, the muzzle effects, and the gun's kick
  • sounds marked predicted (below)
  • recoil, and your rocket jump, even on a link where the server must sometimes repeat your last input
  • a swing step's collider, on its tick
  • your own rocket's flight, its trail and its burst, and the shove it gives the monsters near it (drawn only); a falling missile's arc
  • damage, pain and kills
  • what a missile really hit, and its blast's damage
  • a homing missile's flight: it locks onto what your auto-aim picks, and your client leaves it to the server rather than flying a straight copy
  • a blast's damage and knockback
  • pickups and the inventory a script changes
  • any weapon that uses a Lua step or condition: the whole weapon set then runs on the server only, and the server says so when it loads

Lag compensation. Your shot is judged against where you saw the others, up to 15 ticks (250 ms) back. If you saw the hit, it usually lands. Walls are not rewound: a door that has closed since still stops the shot.

Why sounds are marked predicted

A "play the sound" step (sound) plays a sound from the player, and everybody nearby hears it, sent by the server. But you, the shooter, can't wait a round trip to hear your own gun. So:

  • A sound step marked predicted is played by your own client the moment it predicts the step, and your client drops the server's copy when it arrives. Everyone else hears the server's copy. Gem Hollow's and Cinderfall's shot sounds are marked this way.
  • Unmarked, you hear your own shot a round trip late, while everyone else hears it normally.

Advanced's heard at once by the one firing list (+ predicted sound, with on primary, primary_held, secondary, secondary_held or swing, a sound, and every (ticks) for a held trigger) is the older way: a guess from the buttons alone. The client mutes it while it runs the weapon itself, which is whenever the weapon set doesn't use Lua. It is still worth filling in for a weapon that does.

Marking a sound step. The "play the sound" sentence names only the sound; tap the step and tick predicted under its more of it. A step you add without it plays a round trip late for the shooter.

A stale line in the vocabulary. The push's own description (impulse) still says it is NOT PREDICTED. That was true before the weapon machine moved into the shared core; recoil and rocket jumps are now predicted.

Exercise: three ways to hurt

  1. Make a weapon called rifle from the Pistol recipe. Tap fire, set Lasts to 0.6, and in its instant shot set 25 damage, range 42.67 and spread 0. Play: a slow, exact rifle.

  2. On fire, press + add a step at 0 s, then Moving and push the player away at 3 m/s. Set the speed to 2. Fire while standing still: you slide back.

  3. On Missiles, type fireball and press New: 15 m/s, gone after 3 s, "hurt what it hit for 10", drawn as a glowing dot (its own prop, fireball). Under Looks, give it a Trail: 11 · fireball. Back in Weapons, make a weapon caster from the Launcher recipe; on its fire, tap the launch step and pick fireball. Play: the shot flies trailing fire, and only does its 10 damage when it arrives.

  4. Make a weapon spear from the Blade recipe. Tap slash and its step, swing for 15 damage, 1.6 m long, from -60° to 60° over 0.2 s. Set both angles to 0, and under more of it, min_reach to 1: now it's a thrust that misses anything right against you. Do the same to heavy's step.

  5. Play with the spear and step back from a monster as it closes. Then thrust just as it reaches you. The thrust appears on the tick you press; the monster's health drops when the server's answer arrives.

A shotgun fires 7 rays with a spread of 0°. What does it do?

  • Fans its pellets evenly over 7°
  • Sends all seven rays down the same line: a pistol that hits seven times as hard at any range
  • Fires one ray for 70 damage that ignores walls

Spread is rolled per ray. With 0, every ray takes the same line, so distance makes no difference to how many land.

You want a rocket's explosion to do more damage. Where do you change it?

  • On Missiles, in the rocket's blast under When it hits
  • On the weapon's "launch the missile" step
  • On the weapon's Overview

The weapon only launches the missile. What a hit does belongs to the missile, which monsters and weapons share.

A Blade's slash swings at 0 s, over 0.2 s. You press again at 0.15 s, and heavy's swing starts. What happens to the slash?

  • It finishes its sweep, then the heavy one starts
  • Nothing: the heavy one is refused while a swing is still sweeping
  • It stops where it is, and the heavy one starts fresh: it can hit the same monster again

A newer swing replaces one still sweeping, with its own angles and a fresh list of who it has hit. Only a swing that comes during the last one's cooldown does not happen.

A weapon has a default swing on Advanced, and its fire state has a swing step that gives only its damage. You press fire. What swings?

  • The step, when fire reaches it, with its own damage and the default swing's reach, angles and length
  • The default swing on the press, then the step
  • The default swing only: the step has no shape of its own

With a swing step anywhere, the button swings nothing itself: it moves the weapon to fire, and the step swings. What the step leaves out comes from the default swing.
