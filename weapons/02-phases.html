The phase machine

Every weapon, from a fist to a rocket launcher, is the same machine: phases, a sequence of actions in each, and transitions between them. There is no "automatic" box to tick and no "cooldown" number. You get those by drawing the graph. This lesson explains how the machine runs, tick by tick, and then you build a weapon with it.

You'll learnthe six phase fields; the order the engine checks things in each tick; every condition, and which are events and which are states; how a shot flows from ready to fire and back; tapping versus holding; refire and cooldown; and the dry click Time35 minutes Needsthe pistol from Your first weapon

Try it first

The demo runs three weapons on the engine's rules, at the engine's 60 ticks a second. Hold the button (or click the demo and hold Space). Watch the phase light up in the diagram, and the timeline record each tick. Set the speed to ¼× to see single ticks.

Things to try:

  • pistol: hold the button down. It fires once. Tap fast: no more than one shot every 24 ticks, however fast you tap, because a press during fire has nowhere to go.
  • chaingun: hold. It spins for 24 ticks, then fires every 9 for as long as you hold. Let go during the spin and it goes back to ready without firing.
  • pump shotgun: seven rays per shot. Move the target further away and watch fewer rays land.
  • Press empty the pool and fire: the weapon goes to dry instead of fire.

The six phase fields

FieldWhat it does
anim?The animation state the player is put in for this phase, by number. In the default five, 0 is idle and 2 is attack. It is set on the tick the phase is entered, before its steps; empty leaves the state as it was. The same number picks the weapon's first-person animation (below). The client times the frames.
ticks?How long the phase lasts. Empty means it waits until a transition fires. When set, it is also a window: the phase's transitions can only fire while it is running.
next?Where to go when the ticks run out and no transition matched. Empty goes back to the weapon's initial phase.
sequence▶ at tick … do …: the steps. at is a deadline from entering the phase, not a delay after the step before it.
transitions❓ go to … when all of …: checked in order, every tick. The first that matches wins.
swingA melee swing for this phase only, in place of the weapon's. This is what makes a combo's last blow bigger (Swings).

The graph box sums up each phase: anim 2 · stop · 12t → ready and 0 out · 1 steps. An arrow labelled after 12 ticks is the next?; the others are transitions, labelled with their conditions. Tap an arrow's label to open its phase.

One tick, in order

Sixty times a second, for each player, the engine does this with the weapon in their hands:

  1. Run the sequence. Every step whose at has been reached, and that hasn't run yet in this visit to the phase, runs now, in list order. A goto step leaves at once, and the rest of the list is skipped.
  2. Try the transitions, top to bottom. The first whose conditions all hold wins.
  3. Check the clock. If no transition fired and the phase has run its ticks?, go to next? (or the initial phase).
  4. If the phase changed, go round again in the same tick: the new phase's tick-0 steps run, its transitions are tried, and so on.

Step 4 is why a chaingun can fire on the very tick its last shot ends: fire runs out, goes to ready, ready sees the button still held and goes to fire, and fire's tick-0 shot goes off. All in one tick.

At most five changes a tick. More than that is a loop, such as two phases that go to each other on always. The engine stops for that tick and complains: weapon 'x' changed phase 6 times in one tick … a transition loop. The weapon is stuck until you fix the graph.

Because at is a deadline, a step at 10 runs on the first tick the phase has been running for at least 10 ticks. If a tick is ever skipped, the step is late, never lost.

Switches that mean "default". An optional on/off argument, such as value? on grounded, shows off while it is unset, but unset means the default, which for grounded is on. To ask for "airborne", switch it on and then off again, so false is really written.

The primary button is attack: the left mouse button, F, or ATK on touch. The secondary is alt-attack: the right mouse button, or ALT on touch.

Events and states

This is the rule that matters most. An event (button_pressed, button_released) is true on one tick. A state (no_ammo, has_ammo, var_is …) is true for as long as it is true.

So a transition on a state needs an event beside it, or a reason to only be checked at the right moment. The engine's own example: send an empty weapon to a phase that switches weapons, on no_ammo alone. That transition fires on every tick the weapon is empty, so the moment a player chooses an empty shotgun, it is taken out of their hands. The weapon can't be carried at all, and it looks as if weapon switching is broken.

Two things the studio lets you write that the server refuses.

  • The not switch on a condition. Monsters' conditions take negate; weapons' do not, and the server rejects the arsenal with unknown field `negate`. The studio doesn't flag it. Use the opposite condition instead: no_ammo for "not has_ammo", grounded with value? off for "not grounded".
  • A transition with no conditions. + transition adds one with an empty list, and the server refuses it: `when` is an empty list, which would fire every tick. Always add at least one condition. If you mean "at once", add always.

How a shot flows

Most guns are four phases, and three of them are just waiting:

PhaseRoleShape
readyResting. Nothing happens until the trigger says so.no ticks?; transitions on the button
fireThe shot. Its sequence takes the ammunition and fires at tick 0. Its ticks? are the fire rate.ticks? and next?; usually no transitions, so presses during it are ignored
refire (optional)Decides, the moment a shot ends, whether to fire again at once.no ticks?; held ∧ has_ammo → fire, then always → ready
cooldown (optional)A pump, a reload, a recovery: time the weapon can't fire. With transitions, it is also a window, for a combo.ticks?, next? ready

Why a separate cooldown, when fire's ticks could just be longer? Because a phase has its own animation and its own window. DOOM II's super shotgun fires in a 24-tick fire, then plays a 62-tick reload with a different animation (anim? 3) and its own sound. As one number, that reload would be invisible.

Tapping and holding

The one word in ready's transition decides what kind of gun it is:

ready → fire onHolding the buttonTapping fast
button_pressedone shot. Semi-automatic, like the pistol.one shot per tap, but only taps that land while the weapon is in ready count
button_helda shot every time ready is reached: fully automatic, like the chaingunthe same as holding, at most one shot per fire

There is no "auto" flag anywhere. DOOM II's chaingun and pistol differ by that one word (and their ticks).

Taps are not remembered. A press that lands while the weapon is in fire is gone: fire has no transition, and on the next tick the button is no longer "pressed", only held. If a fast tapper should be rewarded, give fire or recover its own button_pressed transition. That is exactly how a combo window works (Recipes).

Refire, the explicit way

With button_held on ready, you don't need a refire phase: fire → ready → fire happens in one tick. A refire phase earns its place when the repeat should be different from the first shot. For example, a burst that only continues while there is ammunition, and does something else when it stops:

fire    ticks 6, next refire        sequence: consume, hitscan
refire  (no ticks)                   transitions:
          1. button_held ∧ has_ammo  → fire
          2. always                  → spindown
spindown ticks 30, next ready        sequence: a wind-down sound of your own

The dry click

A consume step only takes ammunition. It doesn't stop the shot: with an empty pool, a weapon whose fire starts with consume still fires, for free. Gate the shot in ready. DOOM II does it like this, for every gun:

ready  transitions:
         1. button_held ∧ no_ammo  → dry
         2. button_pressed         → fire      (button_held for the chaingun)
dry    ticks 1, next ready
         sequence: at 0  switch_if_empty
  • Why button_held and not button_pressed in the first transition: a chaingun can run dry in the middle of a burst. The press was long ago; the finger is still down. Asking for the press would leave the player holding an empty gun, pressing nothing.
  • Why the pair: with no_ammo alone, choosing an empty weapon would switch it away on the same tick. With the button beside it, the player can carry an empty weapon, and it only switches when they try to fire it.
  • Why first: transitions are tried in order. Put dry below fire and the press fires first.

Instead of switching, dry could play a click: a sound step and no switch_if_empty. Either way it's a phase you can see in the graph, not a branch hidden in code.

Phases in first person

A phase's anim? (and any set_anim step) puts the player in an animation state. The third-person body animates by that number, and so does the weapon in first person: on the weapon's first person tab, under per phase, each state the weapon's phases set gets an animation of its own.

  • Each card is titled state n · phases: the number, and every phase that sets it. Several phases can share one state; renaming a phase changes nothing here.
  • Drawn from pictures, a card has a frame list like at rest. Drawn from a sprite, it has an animation list: one of the sprite's animations, or (none: at rest / firing).
  • The switch under it says loops or plays once, holds its last frame. State 0 loops by default and the rest hold.
  • The game plays a state's animation while the player is in it, from the start each time the state is entered again, so a second swing of the same phase restarts it. It follows your own predicted state, so it changes on the tick you press, not a round trip later.
  • A state with nothing of its own falls back to at rest and firing, which is also what engines that do not play per phase use. If at rest and firing are empty, the studio writes them from state 0 and the next state.
  • With no phase setting a state, the section says so: give the phases an anim? in the graph first.

The preview's ▶ state n buttons play each state as the game would; a dimmed one has nothing of its own yet.

In Gem Hollow: two small machines

Both of Gem Hollow's weapons are two phases, the smallest useful machine:

fists     ready ★  anim 0, waits      1. button_pressed            → swing
          swing    anim 2, 18 ticks → ready    at 0: sound player.swing (predicted)
                                                its own swing: damage 10, 8 ticks

startoss  ready ★  anim 0, waits      1. button_pressed ∧ has_ammo (n 1) → throw
          throw    anim 2, 14 ticks → ready    at 0: consume 1, launch star_shot,
                                                     sound player.swing (predicted)
                                                at 10: switch_if_empty
  • Both are semi-automatic. button_pressed is an event, so holding the button gives one punch or one throw. A punch every 18 ticks is at most 3⅓ a second; a throw every 14, just over 4.
  • The dry check is a condition, not a phase. Instead of DOOM's dry phase, startoss puts has_ammo beside the press. With no stars, pressing does nothing at all: no click.
  • The switch happens after the last throw. switch_if_empty at 10 of throw swaps to the highest-order weapon with ammunition, the fists, right after the throw that used the last star. A throw with stars left does nothing at that step.
  • Each phase sets an animation state: 0 at rest, 2 (the hero's attack) while punching or throwing. That is what the third-person hero plays, and what per phase above offers in first person.

  • A combo in two phases. The wrench's recover listens for a second press. Press during those 10 ticks and you get swing2, which hits harder (35) and sweeps back the other way; wait, and the next press starts over at swing. Each phase brings its own swing (Swings).
  • A counter the weapon keeps. The revolver declares a weapon var, shots, starting at 0. Every fire runs add_var; after the sixth, cock sees var_at_least 6 and goes to reload, which set_vars it back to 0. That is a six-round cylinder over a pool of bullets. phase_elapsed 7 lets the cock play out first.
  • Two triggers on one gun. The scattergun's first card asks for the secondary button (right mouse) and two shells. It is first so that a right click never falls through to the single shot.
  • A phase that loops into itself. The riveter's fire has next? fire: every 4 ticks, a rivet, for as long as you hold. Letting go is a button_released card, in spinup as well, so a tap spins up and down without firing.
  • The dry click is a phase. All four guns have DOOM's dry phase: a click, then switch_if_empty. The riveter and the flare launcher also switch at the end of spindown and reload, so the last shot hands you the next weapon without a click.
  • A draw before ready. Every weapon's initial? is draw, not ready: 8 ticks with the wpn.draw sound at 0, then ready. A weapon starts in its initial phase each time it is brought up, so every switch, by key, wheel or swap, makes the sound and costs a beat before you can fire.

Every phase but draw, ready and dry sets its own anim? (10 to 23), so in first person each one plays an animation of its own. Each weapon is drawn from a ThreeSprite sheet (VM_WRENCH, VM_REVOLVER …) at scale 1.4, and its per phase cards name the sheet's animations: the riveter's state 20 plays fire with loops on, so the animation keeps going while fire repeats.

  2. In its transition card, remove button_pressed with its ✕, then add when button_held. Leave has_ammo where it is.

  3. Tap throw and set ticks? to 20, so a held button throws three stars a second. Check the bar says loads clean.

Exercise: a carbine, step by step

You'll turn the pistol from lesson 1 into a carbine: semi-automatic, then automatic, with a recovery, and a dry phase. Keep the demo above open in another tab to compare.

  1. In 🔫 Weapons, pick pistol. On ⬡ fire, set ticks? to 8 and leave next? on ready. Make sure the sequence has consume and hitscan, both at 0.

  2. Press + phase and call it recover. A new phase gets anim? 0, ticks? 20 and next? ready. Set its ticks? to 10.

  3. Tap fire and change its next? to recover. The graph now shows fire → recover → ready, two arrows labelled after … ticks. One shot now takes 8 + 10 = 18 ticks: just over three shots a second.

  4. Press + phase again, call it dry. Set ticks? to 1. Under add step, choose switch_if_empty.

  5. Tap ready. Press + transition. A new card appears at the bottom. Choose dry in its target list. With add when, add button_held, then no_ammo. The card reads: go to dry when all of button_held, no_ammo.

  6. The new transition is below the old one, so a press would fire before the dry check. Delete the old fire card with its ✕, then + transition again: target fire, condition button_pressed. Now dry is checked first.

  7. Check the bar says loads clean. Play, and empty the 50 bullets. Each press gives one shot; the empty pistol stays in your hands until you press fire, then switches if you have anything better.

  8. Make it automatic: on ready, in the fire card, delete button_pressed with its ✕ and add button_held. Play again and hold the button: a steady stream, one shot every 18 ticks.

  9. Last, give fast tappers a reward. Tap recover, press + transition, target fire, conditions button_pressed and has_ammo. A fresh press during the recovery now skips the rest of it. (A held button still waits: button_pressed is an event.)

Why has_ammo in the last step? The recover → fire transition skips ready, and with it the dry check. Without has_ammo, an empty carbine would fire free shots as fast as you tap. Any transition into fire needs the same guard.

A phase has ticks? empty and a single transition: button_released → fire. You press and hold the button. What happens?

  • It goes to the initial phase after one tick
  • It waits in that phase until you let go, then fires
  • It fires every tick while you hold

No ticks means the phase waits. button_released is an event, true only on the tick the button comes up. That is the shape of a charge.

ready has two transitions: first button_pressed → fire, then button_held ∧ no_ammo → dry. The pool is empty and you press fire. Where does it go?

  • dry, because the weapon is empty
  • Nowhere: two transitions match, so neither fires
  • fire: both match, and the first one in the list wins

Transitions are tried in order, and the first match wins. The dry check must come first. Since consume doesn't stop a shot, this weapon fires for free.

Why does DOOM II's dry transition ask for button_held rather than just no_ammo?

  • With no_ammo alone it fires on every tick the weapon is empty, so an empty weapon is switched away the moment it is chosen
  • Because no_ammo only works on the secondary button
  • So the click sound plays twice

no_ammo is a state: true for as long as the pool is empty. A state on its own fires every tick. The button beside it makes it happen only when the player tries to shoot.
