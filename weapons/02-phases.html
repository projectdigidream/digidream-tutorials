The phase machine

Every weapon, from a fist to a rocket launcher, is the same machine: states, the steps each one runs, and rules that move it between them. There is no "automatic" box to tick: you get automatic fire, a pump, a charge or a cooldown from the words you put in the rules. This lesson explains how the machine runs, tick by tick, and then you build a weapon with it.

You'll learnwhat a state holds; the order the engine checks things in each tick; every condition, and which are events and which are states; how a shot flows from ready to fire and back; tapping versus holding; refire and cooldowns; the dry click; and states in first person Time35 minutes Needsthe pistol from Your first weapon

States and phases. The editor calls each step of the machine a state. The engine's files and the vocabulary call a weapon's states phases (phases, phase_elapsed), which is where this page's title comes from. They are the same thing.

Try it first

The demo runs four weapons on the engine's rules, at the engine's 60 ticks a second. Hold the button (or click the demo and hold Space). Watch the state light up in the diagram, and the timeline record each tick. Set the speed to ¼× to see single ticks.

Things to try:

  • pistol: hold the button down. It fires once. Tap fast: no more than one shot every 24 ticks, however fast you tap, because a press during fire has nowhere to go.
  • chaingun: hold. It spins for 24 ticks, then fires every 9 for as long as you hold. Let go during the spin and it goes back to ready without firing.
  • pump shotgun: seven rays per shot. Move the target further away and watch fewer rays land.
  • bfg (cooldown): tap. One shot, then 96 ticks before the next, although fire itself lasts 30: the line under the button counts the cooldown down.
  • Press empty the pool and fire: the weapon goes to dry instead of fire.

The editor's own preview (on Behaviour) runs your weapon by the same rules, so everything here can be tried there too.

  • Seconds and ticks. The game runs at 60 ticks a second. Times are typed in seconds and kept in ticks, rounded to the nearest one, and the hint under Lasts says how many: "8 ticks; the game runs 60 a second". One tick is about 0.017 s, so 0.02 gives a state one tick.
  • A length needs somewhere to go. Give a waiting state a Lasts, and Then goes to starts as the state the weapon begins in (★). For most states you want ready: pick it.
  • A new state: + state asks for its name (New state, named), and Create places it after the state picked. It shows picture 0, lasts 0.3 s, then goes back to ready, the resting state. Nothing goes to it yet, and its card says "nothing goes here yet" until another state has a rule that does.
  • A state's card reads all of it as one sentence: "shows picture 2, for 0.23 s then goes on to ready", then its rules (→ throw when fire is pressed and it has at least 1 ammo), then its steps as chips (0 s · use 1 ammo). Tap any part to edit it.

The graph

Graph, at the top right, draws the same states as boxes, in the order the arrows read. Each box has its name, its picture and its length. A dashed arrow is Then goes to, labelled with the time; a solid one is a rule, labelled with its conditions in short (fire · ammo). Tap a box or a label to open that state. Drag to move around; the wheel or two fingers zoom, and fit brings every state into view.

One tick, in order

Sixty times a second, for each player, the engine does this with the weapon in their hands:

  1. Run the steps. Every step whose at has been reached, and that hasn't run yet in this visit to the state, runs now, in list order. A "leave now for" step (goto) leaves at once, and the rest of the list is skipped.
  2. Try the rules, top to bottom: the rules for any state first, then the state's own, then its branches as it ends. The first whose conditions all hold wins.
  3. Check the clock. If no rule fired and the state has run its Lasts, go to Then goes to.
  4. If the state changed, go round again in the same tick: the new state's 0 s steps run, its rules are tried, and so on.

Step 4 is why a chaingun can fire on the very tick its last shot ends: fire runs out, goes to ready, ready sees the button still held and goes to fire, and fire's shot at 0 s goes off. All in one tick.

At most five changes a tick. More than that is a loop, such as two states that go to each other on "always". The engine stops for that tick and complains: weapon 'x' changed phase 6 times in one tick … a transition loop. The weapon is stuck until you fix its rules.

Because at is a deadline, a step at 0.17 s runs on the first tick the state has been running for at least that long. If a tick is ever skipped, the step is late, never lost.

  • The Timeline lists the steps in the order the engine runs them, each with its time, and a mark per step on the line above them. A new step, or one whose time you change, goes in by its time; the others keep their order.
  • A step runs only once the steps above it have run. So a step at 0 s listed after one at 0.2 s waits for it, and the list says so: "(runs at 0.2 s, after the step before it)", with put them in time order to sort them.
  • + add a step at 0 s adds a step at the start. While the preview runs in this state, the button offers the moment the preview is at instead.

Rules and conditions

A state's rules are under Goes to, one card each: its number, go to and the state it goes to, then "when all of these hold:" and its conditions, each with a ✕. + add a condition adds one, + another rule adds a card, and the arrows beside the number (Check this rule earlier, later) change the order. Order matters: the first rule that holds wins.

+ add a condition opens a chooser, Add a condition: every condition must hold. Its groups are chips, one group shown at a time, and it opens on the group you used last. On a computer, a find among 19… box searches them all. A condition's numbers and names are fields in its sentence.

The fire button is the primary one: attack, the left mouse button, F, or ATK on touch.

A rule that does keeps working, as the first rule of Cinderfall's scattergun does, but the editor reads it as fire is pressed and can't set it.

Events and states

This is the rule that matters most. An event (fire is pressed, fire is let go) is true on one tick. A state (it is out of ammo, it has at least 1 ammo, the number … is …) is true for as long as it is true.

So a rule on a state needs an event beside it, or a reason to only be checked at the right moment. The engine's own example: send an empty weapon to a state that switches weapons, on it is out of ammo alone. That rule fires on every tick the weapon is empty, so the moment a player chooses an empty shotgun, it is taken out of their hands. The weapon can't be carried at all, and it looks as if weapon switching is broken.

Careful with rules for any state. A rule under Whatever it is doing is checked first in every state that follows it, so a state on its own there fires the moment it holds, mid-shot or mid-draw. The editor's own example, "when it is out of ammo, go to a reload state", needs fire is pressed beside it, or an empty weapon reloads for ever. A rule there can also be only once, which fires a single time.

How a shot flows

Most guns are four states, and three of them are just waiting:

StateRoleShape
readyResting. Nothing happens until the trigger says so.no Lasts; rules on the button
fireThe shot. Its steps take the ammunition and fire at 0 s. Its Lasts is the fire rate.Lasts and Then goes to; usually no rules, so presses during it are ignored
refire (optional)Decides, the moment a shot ends, whether to fire again at once.no Lasts; fire is held and it has at least 1 ammo → fire, then always → ready
recover (optional)A pump, a reload, a recovery: time the weapon can't fire. With rules, it is also a window, for a combo.Lasts, Then goes to ready

Why a separate recovery, when fire could just last longer? Because a state has its own animation and its own window. DOOM II's super shotgun fires in a 24-tick fire, then plays a 62-tick reload with a different picture (3) and its own sound. As one number, that reload would be invisible. (A wait that should outlast the weapon being put away is a cooldown instead.)

Tapping and holding

The one condition in ready's rule decides what kind of gun it is:

ready → fire whenHolding the buttonTapping fast
fire is pressedone shot. Semi-automatic, like the pistol.one shot per tap, but only taps that land while the weapon is in ready count
fire is helda shot every time ready is reached: fully automatic, like the chaingunthe same as holding, at most one shot per fire

There is no "auto" flag anywhere. DOOM II's chaingun and pistol differ by that one condition (and their length).

Taps are not remembered. A press that lands while the weapon is in fire is gone: fire has no rule, and on the next tick the button is no longer "pressed", only held. If a fast tapper should be rewarded, give fire or recover its own rule on fire is pressed. That is exactly how a combo window works (Recipes).

Refire, the explicit way

With fire is held on ready, you don't need a refire state: fire → ready → fire happens in one tick. A refire state earns its place when the repeat should be different from the first shot. For example, a burst that only continues while there is ammunition, and does something else when it stops:

fire      lasts 0.1 s, then goes on to refire     steps: use 1 ammo, an instant shot
refire    waits                                   rules:
            1. → fire       when fire is held and it has at least 1 ammo
            2. → spindown   when always
spindown  lasts 0.5 s, then goes on to ready      steps: a wind-down sound of your own

Cooldowns

A cooldown is a wait that belongs to the weapon rather than to a state. Two words make it:

  • the step start cooldown … for … s (set_cooldown), in Numbers and timers when you add a step. It notes the moment the wait is over, under a name of your choice.
  • the condition cooldown … is over (cooldown_ready), in Numbers and timers when you add a condition, beside the press that fires.

Both start with the name shot_cd, so they match as soon as you add them. A BFG that may fire once every 96 ticks (1.6 s) doesn't need a 1.6-second fire: fire can last 0.6 s, and the player can switch weapons right after the shot. That is the difference from a long state.

  • It outlives putting the weapon away. Switch away and back, and the cooldown is still running. A respawn clears it.
  • It is predicted on your client like the rest of the weapon, so the next shot is allowed on your screen at the same tick as on the server.
  • It is one of the weapon's numbers. Advanced lists it under Its numbers ("checked in ready; a cooldown started in fire"). A weapon may have 4 numbers, cooldowns included.
  • The preview counts it down: "cooldown shot_cd: 0.93 s left", then "over".

The demo at the top has a bfg (cooldown) preset to try it. A full recipe is in Recipes.

The dry click

A "use 1 ammo" step (consume) only takes ammunition. It doesn't stop the shot: with an empty pool, a weapon whose fire starts with "use 1 ammo" still fires, for free. Gate the shot in ready. DOOM II does it like this, for every gun:

ready  rules:
         1. → dry    when fire is held and it is out of ammo
         2. → fire   when fire is pressed            (fire is held for the chaingun)
dry    lasts one tick, then goes on to ready
         steps: 0 s · switch weapon if it is empty
  • Why fire is held and not fire is pressed in the first rule: a chaingun can run dry in the middle of a burst. The press was long ago; the finger is still down. Asking for the press would leave the player holding an empty gun, pressing nothing.
  • Why the pair: with it is out of ammo alone, choosing an empty weapon would switch it away on the same tick. With the button beside it, the player can carry an empty weapon, and it only switches when they try to fire it.
  • Why first: rules are tried in order. Put dry below fire and the press fires first.

The editor's recipes gate the shot another way: their rule asks for fire is pressed and it has at least 1 ammo, so an empty weapon does nothing at all. Instead of switching, dry could play a click: a "play the sound" step and no switch. Either way it's a state you can see, not a branch hidden in code.

States in first person

A state's Shows picture (and any "show the picture of state" step, set_anim) puts the player in an animation state. The third-person body animates by that number, and so does the weapon in first person: on the weapon's Looks tab, under per phase in Held in first person, each number the weapon's states show gets an animation of its own. Each state shows, beside it, lists every state's number.

  • Each card is titled state n · states: the number, and every state that shows it. Several states can share one number; renaming a state changes nothing here.
  • Drawn from pictures, a card has a frame list like at rest. Drawn from a sprite, it has an animation list: one of the sprite's animations, or (none: at rest / firing).
  • The switch under it says loops or plays once, holds its last frame. State 0 loops by default and the rest hold.
  • The game plays a number's animation while the player is in it, from the start each time a state shows it again, so a second swing of the same state restarts it. It follows your own predicted state, so it changes on the tick you press, not a round trip later.
  • A number with nothing of its own falls back to at rest and firing, which is also what engines that do not play per phase use. If at rest and firing are empty, the studio writes them from state 0 and the next number.
  • With no state showing a number, the section says so: give the states a Shows picture first.

The panel's ▶ state n buttons play each number as the game would; a dimmed one has nothing of its own yet. On Behaviour, the preview plays them as the weapon moves through its states.

Exercise: a carbine, step by step

You'll turn the pistol from lesson 1 into a carbine: semi-automatic, then automatic, with a recovery and a dry state. Keep the demo above open in another tab to compare.

  1. In 🔫 Weapons, pick pistol. Tap fire, set Lasts to 0.13 (8 ticks) and leave Then goes to on ready. Its Timeline has the instant shot, "use 1 ammo" and the sparks, all at 0 s.

  2. Press + state, type recover and press Create. It goes in after fire, lasts 0.3 s and goes back to ready. Set its Lasts to 0.17 (10 ticks).

  3. Tap fire and change its Then goes to to recover. In Graph, fire → recover → ready are now two dashed arrows. One shot takes 8 + 10 = 18 ticks: just over three shots a second.

  4. Press + state again, call it dry, and set its Lasts to 0.02 (one tick). Press + add a step at 0 s, then Ammo and switch weapon if it is empty.

  5. Tap ready. Its rule from the recipe goes to fire when fire is pressed and it has at least 1 ammo. Press + another rule, choose dry in its go to list, and add two conditions: fire is held (Fire button) and it is out of ammo (Ammo).

  6. The two rules can't both hold, since one needs ammo and the other none, so their order doesn't matter here. Move the new one up anyway with its arrow (Check this rule earlier), as DOOM does: with a rule on fire is pressed alone, the dry check must come first.

  7. Play, and empty the 50 bullets. Each press gives one shot; the empty pistol stays in your hands until you press fire, then switches if you have anything better.

  8. Make it automatic: in ready's fire rule, remove fire is pressed with its ✕ and add fire is held. In the preview, hold the button: a steady stream, one shot every 18 ticks.

  9. Last, give fast tappers a reward. Tap recover, press + another rule, choose fire, and add fire is pressed and it has at least 1 ammo. A fresh press during the recovery now skips the rest of it. (A held button still waits: fire is pressed is an event.)

Why it has at least 1 ammo in the last step? The recover → fire rule skips ready, and with it the ammo check. Without it, an empty carbine would fire free shots as fast as you tap. Any rule into fire needs the same guard.

A state has an empty Lasts and a single rule: → fire when fire is let go. You press and hold the button. What happens?

  • It goes where the weapon begins after one tick
  • It waits in that state until you let go, then fires
  • It fires every tick while you hold

No length means the state waits. fire is let go is an event, true only on the tick the button comes up. That is the shape of a charge.

ready has two rules: first → fire when fire is pressed, then → dry when fire is held and it is out of ammo. The pool is empty and you press fire. Where does it go?

  • dry, because the weapon is empty
  • Nowhere: two rules match, so neither fires
  • fire: both match, and the first one in the list wins

Rules are tried in order, and the first match wins. The dry check must come first. Since "use 1 ammo" doesn't stop a shot, this weapon fires for free.

A BFG's fire lasts 0.6 s and starts a 1.6 s cooldown, which ready's rule waits for. You fire, switch to another weapon at once, and switch back after half a second. Can you fire?

  • No: the cooldown outlives putting the weapon away, and has about a second left
  • Yes: switching weapons clears the cooldown
  • Yes, but only after the 0.3 s draw

A cooldown belongs to the weapon, not to the state, and keeps running while the weapon is away. Only a respawn clears it.
