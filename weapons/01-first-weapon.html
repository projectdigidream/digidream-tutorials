Your first weapon

A weapon is a small machine: a few named states, rules that move it from one to another, and steps each state runs. In this lesson you open the weapon editor, make a weapon from a recipe, try it in the preview, put it in the player's hands and fire it in a map. The next lessons take the machine apart.

You'll learnwhere weapons live, what the editor's tabs hold, what the preview shows, what each recipe gives you, how the player's kit decides what they start with, how a player picks a weapon, and how to play-test it Time25 minutes NeedsGem Hollow open in the studio (Home → ◆ examples → 📂 Gem Hollow); for your own weapon, a module with one map (Your first map), and the app for ▶ Play (Play, export, snapshots)

Where weapons live

Open 🔫 Weapons in the studio's rail. Weapons belong to the module, not to one map: "Weapons belong to the module; the order is the key that selects them." Every map of the module sees the same pistol.

They also run once for the whole game. The studio writes the weapons into a game script (maps/arsenal.lua), and the server runs it once, not once per level. So a player keeps what they hold, and the ammunition they carry, from map to map.

The section opens the weapon editor on the weapon you had last, or the module's first. If the module has no weapon yet, it opens on the list, ready for a new one.

The weapon editor

Along the top:

  • The weapon's name, with ⌄. Tap it to see every weapon of the module as a card, and to make a new one.
  • The tabs, below.
  • On Behaviour, List and Graph: the same states as cards of sentences, or as boxes and arrows.
  • ⋯: Rename, Duplicate and Delete. Delete asks first.

Rename can change the weapon's id. It sets the name players see, and the id becomes that name lower-cased, with _ for spaces: renaming startoss to Star Toss makes it star_toss. The pickups, the kit and the graphs that name the weapon by its id don't follow, so set them again afterwards. To change only what players see, edit Name on the Overview.

The preview

The preview on Behaviour runs the weapon by the engine's own rules, without starting the game. Press ▶ on its bar to run it (⏸ pauses), then press and hold Hold to fire: it is the fire button, and a press counts once, on the moment it goes down, as in the game.

  • The weapon held. A weapon with a first-person look (below) is drawn as the player sees it, playing each state's animation, with its shots and sparks over it. Without one, the preview says "fire up — this weapon has no first-person picture yet (Looks)" and draws its shots as dots.
  • In words. Under the buttons it says what the weapon would do now: "In ready: goes to fire (rule 1).", or "no rule holds; after 0.13 s, on to ready." The card of the state it is in is lit, and so is the rule that would fire.
  • Ammo and numbers. "ammo: 49 (Reset refills 50)", or "ammo: never runs out", then the weapon's numbers and cooldowns.
  • on the ground switches to in the air, for rules that ask. Reset starts again where the weapon begins, with 50 ammo.

What the preview doesn't do: hit anything, or play sounds. Damage and what a missile does are in the game.

When something is wrong

The editor marks a rule it can't use yet: "incomplete: choose where it goes (it never fires until then)", or "… add a condition". Such a rule is left out of the game, so it can do no harm while you finish it. Anything the server would refuse is counted on 🔫 Weapons in the rail and listed on Home under to fix, a tap away from the section. The check uses the same vocabulary the server loads.

A new weapon, from a recipe

Tap the weapon's name at the top left. The page lists every weapon as a card (slot 2 · 2 states), and above them, New weapon.

Type a name in its name, e.g. bfg. It is the weapon's id, which pickups, the kit and graphs use: it is lower-cased as you type, a space becomes _, anything other than letters, digits and _ is dropped, and it must start with a letter (24 letters at most). Then press a recipe:

RecipeIts noteWhat you get
Blankdrawn, then waits for a ruledraw ★ for 0.3 s, then ready, which waits. Nothing fires yet.
Pistolone instant shot each pressready → fire when fire is pressed and it has at least 1 ammo. fire lasts 0.3 s: an instant shot for 12 damage, range 32 m, spread 1°; use 1 ammo; show the effect 12 (sparks).
Launcherone heavy shot and a kickfire lasts 0.6 s: launch the missile, use 1 ammo, the sparks, and push the player away at 3 m/s.
Charge shothold fire 1 s to release; let go early and nothing happensready → charge when fire is pressed and it has at least 5 ammo. charge waits: on to release once fire has been held for 1 s, back to ready when fire is let go. release lasts 0.8 s: launch the missile, use 5 ammo, push the player away at 6 m/s.
Copy of weaponA copy of the weapon you were on.

Every recipe begins in draw (★, where it begins): 0.3 seconds of being brought up, then ready. A weapon starts again where it begins each time it is brought up, so every switch costs that beat. The new weapon's Name is its id with a capital (bfg → Bfg), and its Slot is one more than the highest so far. A message on the page says what you made: "pistol, from the Pistol recipe: one instant shot each press."

  • Ammunition. The Pistol, Launcher and Charge shot eat the module's first ammunition pool. The first weapon of a module also makes the arsenal: one pool, bullets (Name on the panel Bullets, Most a player holds 200, Starts with 50, Place on the panel 1), and the health ceilings 100 and 200.
  • Missiles. The Launcher and Charge shot launch the module's first missile. A module with none gets one called bolt: 16 m/s, gone after 2 seconds, and a blast of 15 damage within 2 m on a hit. A new bolt has no look yet: on ☄ Missiles, give it one under Drawn as, as for any missile (Missiles).

The game runs at 60 ticks a second. The editor shows times in seconds and keeps them in ticks: type 0.4 and the field's hint says "24 ticks; the game runs 60 a second". A time is rounded to the nearest tick, so 0.13 s is 8 ticks.

Exercise: start Gem Hollow with stars in hand

  1. Open Gem Hollow and choose 🧍 Player, then 🎒 kit. Under weapons in hand, switch startoss on beside fists.

  2. Under ammunition carried at the start, set stars to 10.

  3. Press ▶ Play. You start holding the Star Toss, because with several weapons you hold the one with the highest slot. Press 1 for the fists and 2 for the stars. Throw all ten and watch the weapon switch back to the fists by itself: that is the "switch weapon if it is empty" step in throw.

Exercise: make a pistol and fire it

Do this one in your own module, the one with a map from Your first map.

  1. Open 🔫 Weapons. With no weapon yet, the list is open; otherwise tap the weapon's name at the top left. Type pistol under New weapon and press Pistol. The editor opens on Behaviour with three cards: draw ★, ready and fire.

  2. Press ▶ on the preview's bar, then press and hold Hold to fire. The preview goes from ready to fire and back, and the ammo line counts down: "ammo: 49 (Reset refills 50)". Keep holding: one shot. Each new shot needs a new press.

  3. Tap the fire card's name. In the panel on the right, set Lasts to 0.4. The hint says "24 ticks; the game runs 60 a second": DOOM's pistol rate, one shot every 0.4 seconds.

  4. Under Timeline, tap "fire an instant shot for 12 damage, range 32 m, spread 1°" and set the spread to 3.

  5. Open Overview. Ammo is Bullets, the pool the first weapon made, and the sentence at the top ends "Ammo: bullets, 1 in fire.": the "use 1 ammo" step in fire.

  6. Open 🧍 Player, the 🎒 kit tab. Check that bullets under ammunition carried at the start is 50.

  7. Open your map and press ▶ Play. The pistol is in your hands (it is the only weapon), after its 0.3 s draw. Fire with the left mouse button or F.

Nothing happens? Look at 🔫 Weapons in the rail first. A count there means the server refuses the arsenal, and the player has no weapons at all. Home lists each problem under to fix, naming the weapon, the state and the field.

The player's kit

What a player starts with is set in 🧍 Player, on the 🎒 kit tab, not in the weapon. (The Player window's 🔫 weapons button comes back here.)

  • health: max and bonus max, the same two ceilings as the arsenal's.
  • ammunition carried at the start: one number per pool. It is the pool's Starts with.
  • weapons in hand: with choose them off, the player starts with the weapon of the lowest slot, the one that always works and can't be empty. Turn it on and a switch appears for each weapon. Tick the ones they start with.

When the player starts with several weapons, the one in their hands is the one with the highest slot. DOOM starts you with fists (1) and a pistol (3), and you start holding the pistol.

The kit is module-wide. Switch the tab to 🗺 only map and it only explains why: the weapons run once for the whole game, so a player arrives in a map holding what they held in the last one.

Choosing a weapon

  • Number keys. 1 to 9 pick the n-th weapon you hold, counted in slot order. So a slot is the key only while you hold every weapon below it: if you hold fists (slot 1), a pistol (3) and a chaingun (6), 3 is the chaingun. The switch is predicted, so it happens on your screen at once.
  • A pickup. Walking over a weapon you don't have yet gives it to you and puts it in your hands.
  • Running dry. A "switch weapon if it is empty" step (switch_if_empty) swaps to the highest-slot weapon that still has ammunition. A weapon only does this if you build it in, as the next lesson shows.
  • A graph. The graph nodes select_weapon, give_weapon (with select) and take_weapon. See Ammo and pickups.

No weapon panel or wheel from the studio yet. The weapons runtime has an opt-in panel listing what a player holds, but the studio's arsenal doesn't switch it on. In a studio-made module, players switch with the number keys, pickups and graphs. A graph and a panel of your own can make a wheel: Cinderfall does.

The wheel on Tab

The number keys work as in any module. Cinderfall adds two keys of its own in 🎮 Controls: arms on Tab (touch button ARMS) and swap on X (touch button SWAP). Neither does anything by itself: a game graph, arms, answers both. The wheel is a panel, also called arms, made in ▢ Panels: five buttons in a ring (wrench, revolver, scatter, riveter, flare), each with an icon beside it as decor. It is not modal, so you keep moving and aiming while it is up.

when_button_pressed (arms) → branch (panel_is_open arms)
    true  → close_panel arms
    false → open_panel arms → play_sound ui.open
            → branch (has_weapon wrench)   true: show_element wrench, icon_wrench   false: hide_element both
            → branch (has_weapon revolver) …  and so on for all five

when_panel_used (arms › revolver) → select_weapon revolver → close_panel arms → play_sound ui.select

So the wheel shows only what you carry, checked each time it opens with has_weapon. The panel was opened by a game graph, so it is answered by the game's graphs and can stay open as you cross into the next map.

Quick swap on X

The graph keeps two variables, current and previous, both starting at wrench.

when_weapon_changed → set_var previous (← get_var current) → set_var current (← when_weapon_changed.weapon)

when_button_pressed (swap) → branch (compare previous == wrench)   true: select_weapon wrench
                               false → branch (previous == revolver) true: select_weapon revolver
                                 false → … scatter, riveter, flare
  • The order of the two set_var nodes matters. previous is written first, from the old current; then current takes the new weapon.
  • One branch per weapon, because select_weapon takes its weapon as a typed name. A sixth weapon needs a sixth pair.
  • Swapping is a change too. The swap itself fires when_weapon_changed, so pressing X again goes back: two weapons, one key.
  • Per game, not per player. current and previous are the game graph's own, shared by everyone in it. In single player that is the same thing.

Seeing it: first person and the HUD

How the module is played is chosen in 🎮 Controls, under how it is played: platformer — third person or first person (fps) (see Controls). In first person, the gun is drawn over the view, and it kicks on the tick your client predicts the shot, not a round trip later. Each weapon's look in first person is set on its Looks tab (below).

The weapons runtime tells the HUD about the weapon in hand, so any module has these values to show without a script. Bind them in ▣ Screens on the HUD tab (Sky, screens and weather):

The weapon in first person

In first person, the game draws the weapon over the view the way DOOM drew its guns: a picture at rest that bobs as you walk, and frames that play when you fire. The Looks tab sets this for each weapon, under Held in first person. It is drawn only when the module is played first person; otherwise the panel says so at the top: "This is drawn only when the module is played first person: Settings → controls → first person (fps)." Gem Hollow is a platformer, so it shows that note, and its weapons have no first-person look.

The panel starts with a preview: a 320 × 200 screen (the dashed frame, with a line down the middle), played with the game client's own view-model code. ▶ fire plays a shot. Beside it is a ▶ state n button for each picture number the weapon's states show; The phase machine explains those. Then drawn from chooses one of two ways to draw the weapon.

From pictures, frame by frame

  • import a picture (PNG)… adds a picture to the module. With none yet, the panel says to import one for the weapon at rest, one per frame of its shot, and a flash if it has one.
  • at rest (any other state) is a list of frames that loops while the weapon is held. firing (any other state) plays once on every shot.
  • Each frame is a row: its number, a picture, how many tics it shows (35 a second, DOOM's clock, not the game's 60 ticks), and an optional flash picture drawn over it. The row's arrow moves it up and ✕ removes it; + frame adds one.
  • where each picture sits lists every picture in use, with its size and the x and y of its top-left corner on the 320 × 200 screen, with the weapon at rest. bottom centre puts it where a gun usually sits. From there the game bobs it as the player walks, and lowers and raises it on a switch.

From a sprite (ThreeSprite)

  • sprite picks one of the module's sheets. import a sprite (.tsprite)… imports a ThreeSprite project, and edit in ThreeSprite opens the picked one in ThreeSprite. Draw an animation for the weapon held and another for a shot.
  • at rest (fallback) and firing (fallback) pick those two animations.
  • scale is screen pixels per canvas pixel, and canvas N×N at x, y places the sprite's canvas on the screen; bottom centre puts it back in the middle at the bottom.
  • When the module is written, each frame's front view is cut from the baked sheet into a picture, placed where it sits on the canvas, and shown for as long as the animation's fps says.

The same look is what the preview on Behaviour draws, so once a weapon has one you see it held while you build its states. A weapon with nothing set shows no gun in first person.

Beside Held in first person, Each state shows lists the picture number of every state (the same number as a state's Shows picture), and Sounds the sound of every "play the sound" step, each with ▶.

Play-testing well

  • Try it in the preview first. It runs the same rules, at the same 60 ticks a second, and says in words what the weapon would do next. Hold, tap, let go mid-shot, and press Reset to start over.
  • Count ticks, not feelings. A Lasts of 0.4 s is 24 ticks. Write the rate you want as shots per second, then divide 60 by it.
  • Test the edges. Fire with the pool empty. Switch away mid-shot. Hold the button through a whole state and let go.
  • Read the Play log if the weapon misbehaves in the game but nothing is listed under to fix. The server prints load errors and runtime complaints there, such as a transition loop.
  • Try it on a busy map. Your own shots are predicted, so firing feels instant even on a slow link. Damage still comes from the server. What is predicted explains the split.

You hold fists (slot 1), a shotgun (slot 4) and a rocket launcher (slot 7). Which weapon does 2 select?

  • Nothing: no weapon has slot 2
  • The shotgun: the second weapon you hold, counted in slot order
  • The rocket launcher: the highest slot

Number keys count the weapons you hold, sorted by slot. They are not fixed places, so a gap in the slot numbers doesn't leave a key empty.

With choose them off in the kit, what does a player start holding?

  • Every weapon of the module
  • The weapon with the highest slot
  • The weapon with the lowest slot

The lowest-slot weapon is the default because it is meant to be the one that always works, like fists. To start with more, turn choose them on and tick them.

In the preview, you press and keep holding Hold to fire on a weapon made from the Pistol recipe. What happens?

  • One shot: "fire is pressed" counts the moment the button goes down, not while it stays down
  • A shot every 0.3 seconds for as long as you hold
  • Nothing until you let go

A press is an edge, in the preview as in the game. For a shot every time the weapon is ready again, the rule must ask for "fire is held" instead (The phase machine).
