Your first weapon

A weapon is a small machine you draw: a few named phases, and arrows between them. In this lesson you make one in the Weapons section, put it in the player's hands, and fire it in a map. The next lessons take the machine apart.

You'll learnwhere weapons live, what the workshop's panes and tabs hold, what "+ new weapon" gives you, how the player's kit decides what they start with, how a player picks a weapon, and how to play-test it Time25 minutes NeedsGem Hollow open in the studio (Home → Examples → 📂 Gem Hollow); for your own weapon, a module with one map (Your first map), and the app for ▶ Play (Play, export, snapshots)

Where weapons live

Open 🔫 Weapons in the studio's rail. Weapons belong to the module, not to one map, and the window says so in its bar: "weapons belong to the module, not to one map". Every map of the module sees the same pistol.

They also run once for the whole game. The studio writes the weapons into a game script (maps/arsenal.lua), and the server runs it once, not once per level. So a player keeps what they hold, and the ammunition they carry, from map to map.

The window is the same workshop the monsters use, because a weapon and a monster's brain are the same kind of graph. It has three panes. On a wide screen they sit side by side; on a phone, the tabs at the top right switch between them:

PaneWhat it shows
🎞 previewThe player's art, because the player holds the weapon. With no player art chosen yet, it says "no picture: give the body a sheet". The state chips 0 idle … 4 death play each animation state.
⬡ graphThe weapon's phases as boxes, and the ways between them as arrows.
☰ detailsSeven tabs of forms: the picked phase, the weapon, its swing, how the swing looks, how it is drawn in first person, its counters, and the arsenal. When the pane is narrow they wrap onto more rows.

The bar

  • ‹ back leaves the window.
  • There is no button across to the monsters: they are their own section in the rail. (The Player window keeps its 🔫 weapons button, to come here.)
  • A chip per weapon, then + new weapon.
  • On the right: loads clean, or 1 problem(s) in red. Hover over the count to read the problems. They are checked against the same vocabulary the server loads, so a problem here is one the server would refuse too. (Two mistakes slip past this check; The phase machine names them.)

The graph bar

+ phase asks for a name. auto layout tidies the boxes. delete weapon asks first, and also deletes any pickup that grants this weapon. When a phase is picked, three more buttons appear: ★ initial, → link and ✕ phase.

→ link does not work for weapons yet. Tapping the target phase throws an error in the studio, and no transition is added. (The code writes to states, which a monster has, but a weapon keeps its nodes in phases. The tap a target state button in the details pane runs into the same error.) Add transitions with + transition in the details pane and choose the target from its list. That always works.

A field name with a ? is optional: leave it empty and the engine uses its default. Every field has an ⓘ button with the engine's own description. The same words are listed in Every weapon word.

What "+ new weapon" gives you

+ new weapon asks for a weapon name (letters, digits, underscore), suggesting pistol. The name is the weapon's id: pickups, the kit and graphs use it. It is lower-cased, and anything other than letters, digits and underscores is dropped.

The weapon it makes already works. It has two phases:

PhaseFieldsDoes
ready ★anim? 0, no ticksWaits. One transition: button_pressed → fire.
fireanim? 2, ticks? 12, next? readyAt tick 0, a hitscan with damage 10 and range 32. After 12 ticks it goes back to ready.

The game runs at 60 ticks a second, so 12 ticks is a fifth of a second: at most five shots a second, one per press.

Its label is the name with a capital (Pistol), and its order is one more than the highest so far (the first weapon gets 1). It has no ammo?, so it never runs out.

The first weapon also creates the arsenal: one pool, bullets (label? Bullets, max 200, initial? 50, order? 1), and the health ceilings 100 and 200.

The weapon's own fields

FieldWhat it means
labelWhat the HUD and the weapon panel call it. The only string a player sees.
orderSelection priority, low to high. It sorts the weapons for the number keys, and it decides which weapon the player falls back to when one runs dry (the highest one that still has ammunition).
ammo?The pool it eats, chosen from the arsenal's pools. (never runs out) is right for a fist or a chainsaw.
per?What one shot costs. Default 1. It is only taken when a phase runs a consume action.
initial?The phase it rests in. A weapon with more than one phase must name it. A new weapon has ready, marked ★ in the graph.
icon?A picture for the HUD: a sprite frame key from the module's sheets, or a texture name.
aim_snap?How many directions aim snaps to while it is held. Empty or 0 is free aim; 8 is eight-way, for a side-on platformer.

The arsenal has one pool, stars (max 30, initial? 0), and a health max of 100. In 🧍 Player → 🎒 kit, choose them is on with only fists ticked, so every game starts with a bare fist and no stars. Because the fists have the lower order, they are also what you fall back to when the stars run out. The next lessons take both weapons apart.

Exercise: start Gem Hollow with stars in hand

  1. Open Gem Hollow and choose 🧍 Player, then 🎒 kit. Under weapons in hand, switch startoss on beside fists.

  2. Under ammunition carried at the start, set stars to 10.

  3. Press ▶ Play. You start holding the Star Toss, because with several weapons you hold the one with the highest order. Press 1 for the fists and 2 for the stars. Throw all ten and watch the weapon switch back to the fists by itself: that is the switch_if_empty step in throw.

Exercise: make a pistol and fire it

  1. Open 🔫 Weapons and press + new weapon. Keep the name pistol and press OK. Two boxes appear, ready and fire, and the bar says loads clean.

  2. Tap the fire box. The first details tab now reads ⬡ fire. Under ▶ at tick … do … you see the hitscan step. Set spread? to 3 and tracer? to a tracer style from the list, so you can see the shot.

  3. Still on fire, set ticks? to 24. That is DOOM's pistol rate: one shot every 0.4 seconds.

  4. Open 🔫 weapon. Choose bullets under ammo?. The pistol now has a pool, but nothing takes from it yet.

  5. Go back to ⬡ fire. Under add step, choose consume. A step appears with at 0. Now each shot costs one bullet. (You'll stop it firing when empty in the next lesson.)

  6. Open 🧍 Player, the 🎒 kit tab. Check that bullets under ammunition carried at the start is 50.

  7. Open your map and press ▶ Play. Press 1 if the pistol is not already in your hands, then fire with the left mouse button or F. Hold the button down: it fires once. Each new shot needs a new press.

Nothing happens? Look at the bar first. A problem count in red means the server refuses the arsenal, and the player has no weapons at all. Hover over the count: the message names the weapon, the phase and the field.

The player's kit

What a player starts with is set in 🧍 Player, on the 🎒 kit tab, not in the weapon:

  • ❤ health: max and bonus max, the same two ceilings as the arsenal.
  • 🔢 ammunition carried at the start: one number per pool. It is the pool's initial?.
  • 🔫 weapons in hand: with choose them off, the player starts with the weapon of the lowest order, "the one that always works, and which cannot be empty". Turn it on and a switch appears for each weapon. Tick the ones they start with.

When the player starts with several weapons, the one in their hands is the one with the highest order. DOOM starts you with fists (order 1) and a pistol (order 3), and you start holding the pistol.

The kit is module-wide. Switch the tab to 🗺 only map and it only explains why: the weapons run once for the whole game, so a player arrives in a map holding what they held in the last one.

Choosing a weapon

  • Number keys. 1 to 9 pick the n-th weapon you hold, counted in order. They are not fixed slots: if you hold fists (order 1), a pistol (3) and a chaingun (6), 3 is the chaingun. The switch is predicted, so it happens on your screen at once.
  • A pickup. Walking over a weapon you don't have yet gives it to you and puts it in your hands.
  • Running dry. A phase that runs switch_if_empty swaps to the highest-order weapon that still has ammunition. A weapon only does this if you build it in, as the next lesson shows.
  • A graph. The graph nodes select_weapon, give_weapon (with select) and take_weapon. See Ammo and pickups.

No weapon panel or wheel from the studio yet. The weapons runtime has an opt-in panel listing what a player holds (weapons.load{ panel = true }), but the studio's arsenal doesn't write that switch. In a studio-made module, players switch with the number keys, pickups and graphs. A graph and a panel of your own can make a wheel: Cinderfall does.

The wheel on Tab

The number keys work as in any module. Cinderfall adds two keys of its own in 🎮 Controls: arms on Tab (touch button ARMS) and swap on X (touch button SWAP). Neither does anything by itself: a game graph, arms, answers both. The wheel is a panel, also called arms, made in ▢ Panels: five buttons in a ring (wrench, revolver, scatter, riveter, flare), each with an icon beside it as decor. It is not modal, so you keep moving and aiming while it is up.

when_button_pressed (arms) → branch (panel_is_open arms)
    true  → close_panel arms
    false → open_panel arms → play_sound ui.open
            → branch (has_weapon wrench)   true: show_element wrench, icon_wrench   false: hide_element both
            → branch (has_weapon revolver) …  and so on for all five

when_panel_used (arms › revolver) → select_weapon revolver → close_panel arms → play_sound ui.select

So the wheel shows only what you carry, checked each time it opens with has_weapon. The panel was opened by a game graph, so it is answered by the game's graphs and can stay open as you cross into the next map.

Quick swap on X

The graph keeps two variables, current and previous, both starting at wrench.

when_weapon_changed → set_var previous (← get_var current) → set_var current (← when_weapon_changed.weapon)

when_button_pressed (swap) → branch (compare previous == wrench)   true: select_weapon wrench
                               false → branch (previous == revolver) true: select_weapon revolver
                                 false → … scatter, riveter, flare
  • The order of the two set_var nodes matters. previous is written first, from the old current; then current takes the new weapon.
  • One branch per weapon, because select_weapon takes its weapon as a typed name. A sixth weapon needs a sixth pair.
  • Swapping is a change too. The swap itself fires when_weapon_changed, so pressing X again goes back: two weapons, one key.
  • Per game, not per player. current and previous are the game graph's own, shared by everyone in it. In single player that is the same thing.

Seeing it: first person and the HUD

How the module is played is chosen in 🎮 Controls, under how it is played: platformer — third person or first person (fps) (see Controls). In first person, the gun is drawn over the view, and it kicks on the tick your client predicts the shot, not a round trip later.

Each weapon's look in first person is set on its first person tab (below).

The weapons runtime tells the HUD about the weapon in hand, so any module has these values to show without a script. Bind them in ▣ Screens on the HUD tab (Sky, screens and weather):

ValueMeaning

hud.ammo, hud.ammo_maxthe amount in that pool, and the pool's max
hud.ammo_poolevery pool, by name, such as hud.ammo_bullets

A text element with Ammo {hud.ammo} shows the count of whatever is in your hands.

The weapon in first person

In first person, the game draws the weapon over the view the way DOOM drew its guns: a picture at rest that bobs as you walk, and frames that play when you fire. The first person tab sets this for each weapon. It is drawn only when the module is played first person, and the tab says so at the top in that case: "This is drawn only when the module is played first person: Settings → controls → first person (fps)." Gem Hollow is a platformer, so it shows that note.

The tab starts with a preview: a 320 × 200 screen (the dashed frame, with a line down the middle), played with the game client's own view-model code. ▶ fire plays a shot. Beside it is a ▶ state n button for each animation state the weapon's phases set; The phase machine explains those. Then drawn from chooses one of two ways to draw the weapon.

From pictures, frame by frame

  • import a picture (PNG)… adds a picture to the module. With none yet, the tab says to import one for the weapon at rest, one per frame of its shot, and a flash if it has one.
  • at rest (any other state) is a list of frames that loops while the weapon is held. firing (any other state) plays once on every shot.
  • Each frame is a row: its number, a picture, how many tics it shows (35 a second, DOOM's clock, not the game's 60 ticks), and an optional flash picture drawn over it. The row's arrow moves it up and ✕ removes it; + frame adds one.
  • where each picture sits lists every picture in use, with its size and the x and y of its top-left corner on the 320 × 200 screen, with the weapon at rest. bottom centre puts it where a gun usually sits. From there the game bobs it as the player walks, and lowers and raises it on a switch.

From a sprite (ThreeSprite)

  • sprite picks one of the module's sheets. import a sprite (.tsprite)… imports a ThreeSprite project, and edit in ThreeSprite opens the picked one in ThreeSprite. Draw an animation for the weapon held and another for a shot.
  • at rest (fallback) and firing (fallback) pick those two animations.
  • scale is screen pixels per canvas pixel, and canvas N×N at x, y places the sprite's canvas on the screen; bottom centre puts it back in the middle at the bottom.
  • When the module is written, each frame's front view is cut from the baked sheet into a picture, placed where it sits on the canvas, and shown for as long as the animation's fps says.

The studio writes what the game reads, assets/sprites/weapons.json (each weapon's frames) and the pictures' places in assets/sprites/index.json, only when some weapon has a first-person look. A weapon with nothing set shows no gun in first person.

Play-testing well

  • Count ticks, not feelings. A ticks? of 24 is 0.4 s. Write the rate you want as shots per second, then divide 60 by it.
  • Test the edges. Fire with the pool empty. Switch away mid-shot. Hold the button through a whole phase and let go.
  • Read the Play log if the weapon misbehaves in the game but the studio says loads clean. The server prints load errors and runtime complaints there, such as a transition loop.
  • Try it on a busy map. Your own shots are predicted, so firing feels instant even on a slow link. Damage still comes from the server. What is predicted explains the split.

You hold fists (order 1), a shotgun (order 4) and a rocket launcher (order 7). Which weapon does 2 select?

  • Nothing: no weapon has order 2
  • The shotgun: the second weapon you hold, counted in order
  • The rocket launcher: the highest order

Number keys count the weapons you hold, sorted by order. They are not fixed slots, so a gap in the order numbers doesn't leave a key empty.

With choose them off in the kit, what does a player start holding?

  • Every weapon of the module
  • The weapon with the highest order
  • The weapon with the lowest order

The lowest-order weapon is the default because it is meant to be the one that always works, like fists. To start with more, turn choose them on and tick them.

You make a separate starting kit for map 3 by switching the kit tab to 🗺 only map3. What happens?

  • You can't: the kit is module-wide, because the weapons run once for the whole game
  • Map 3 starts the player with its own weapons
  • The player loses their weapons when they reach map 3

A player arrives in a map holding what they held in the last one. To hand out something on arrival, use a graph with give_weapon (see Ammo and pickups).
