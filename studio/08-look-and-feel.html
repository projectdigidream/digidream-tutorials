Sky, screens and weather

This page covers how the module looks around and over the play: the sky above the maps, the HUD and touch controls on screen, the panels a player opens, their fonts and colours, and weather and fog in single rooms.

Sky

Sky, beside Maps in the sidebar's World group, sets what the module and each map are drawn under. A new module names no sky, so it gets the game's dark background. + give the module a sky starts one.

A sky has a kind:

KindPicturesNotes
stripone picture round the horizon (DOOM's)any width × height; DOOM's are 256×128. Its left edge is at north, running clockwise. Also times round the horizon and height (how far up the sky).
panoramaone picture of the whole sphere, 2:1its middle faces north; 2048×1024 is kind to phones
cubesix square faceseast, west, north, south, up, down
nonenonethe game's dark background; sky ceilings show a dark gradient

Every kind but none can be turned, degrees. The pictures are textures, the module's own or the app's, picked with their thumbnails. A sky with a missing picture is said here and on Home. The form's last line shows the module.txt line it writes, such as sky default strip ….

The preview is the game's own sky. Drag it to look round; it says which way you are facing. It shows the picture as soon as the page opens (it stayed black until dragged).

In Gem Hollow the module's sky is a strip of rolling hills and clouds, the texture SKYHILLS (composed from Kenney's art in qPainter), set to times round the horizon 2 and height (how far up the sky) 0.7. The form's last line shows what it writes: sky default strip SKYHILLS repeat 2 height 0.7. Neither map has its own sky; every outdoor room has its ceiling open to it.

A map's own sky

The whose list at the top switches between the module (every map) and each map; a map with its own sky is marked (own). For a map, turn on map has a sky of its own. It starts as a copy of the module's. Turned off, the map is drawn under the module's sky again. You can also reach it from the map editor's More → Map settings….

Sky ceilings

A room shows the sky where its ceiling is open. In the room's inspector, turn on ceiling open to the sky. The ceiling becomes DOOM's sky flat, F_SKY1, lined up with the sky past the walls. Turn it off and the ceiling gets back the texture it had.

A graph can change the sky while the game runs with the set_sky node.

  1. Open Sky and press + give the module a sky. Keep kind at strip and pick a picture.

  2. Drag the preview round. Change times round the horizon to 2 and watch the picture repeat.

  3. In whose, choose one of your maps and turn on map has a sky of its own. Change its kind to none.

  4. Open a room of a map in the map editor and turn on ceiling open to the sky in its inspector.

Screens

Screens, in the sidebar's Interface group with Panels and Style, is what is drawn over the game. Its frame is drawn by the game's own HUD code, so it shows what that screen will show. The bar has:

  • every map and only map. A map can have a complete layout of its own, which starts as a copy of the module's.
  • The device: phone, landscape, phone, portrait, tablet or desktop, with a notch's safe area where it has one.
  • Four tabs: HUD, touch, templates and markers.

HUD

The list shows the HUD's elements. Tap one in the frame or the list to edit it. Drag it in the frame to move it: it re-anchors to the ninth of the screen it lands in, so it stays in that corner on a wider screen. + element… adds a text, a number, a bar, an image or a group.

  • An element binds to what the game knows, such as hp, or what the weapons runtime sends, such as hud.ammo or hud.weapon.
  • shown can be always, touch screens only or keyboard and mouse only.
  • An image takes its picture from a binding (picture from, such as hud.weapon_icon) or, in or always, names one picture: a sprite, or one of the module's textures. The name is looked up in sprites/ first, then in textures/, so an icon you painted in qPainter works as it is. The picture fills the element's w × h, whatever its own size, so a 128-pixel icon in a 48 × 48 box is drawn at 48 × 48.
  • sample data sets the values the frame shows, so you can see a low-health bar without playing.

The frame draws an image that names one of the module's own textures, as the game does: Gem Hollow's heart, coin and gem icons in the picture above are its textures HUDHEART, HUDCOIN and HUDGEM.

In Gem Hollow the module's HUD has nine elements along the top: heart (an image naming the texture HUDHEART, 48 × 48), hpbar (a bar bound to hp, red, redder below 30%), coin and coins ({hud.coins} / 152), gem and gems ({hud.gems} / 5), key and keys, shown only once a key is held, and stars at the top right, shown only with stars to throw. The graphs fill the hud.* values with set_hud. The menu map has screens of its own (switch to only menu): no HUD, and every touch button hidden, so the title screen is clean.

Touch

The touch tab moves, resizes, relabels and hides the engine's buttons for the module's control scheme. It cannot invent a button, because each one is an input. The module's own keys with a touch label appear here too, to place beside the engine's (see Controls). back to the engine's buttons undoes the module's layout for them.

Templates

Templates are for panels that a hand-written script opens: where such a panel sits and where each widget goes (+ template, + slot). The module's own panels are designed in Panels instead; Panels… goes there.

Markers

Markers are small UIs hung on things in the world: a bar over a monster, a sign over a door, a pointer over a goal. The markers tab designs their looks. Its frame is a close-up of a monster with the marker over its head, drawn by the game's own marker code at the chosen device's size.

  • The four built-in markers (health, label, marker, debug_health) are listed with the module's own. change it for this module changes a built-in one everywhere it is used, and make a new one from it copies it under a new name. + new marker starts one from scratch.
  • One of your own has a name (rename it, and the monsters, graphs and signs that hang it follow), a width and height, and elements you add with + element… and drag inside its box.
  • sample values set what the close-up shows, and used by lists who hangs it.

A monster wears one as A bar over it on its Overview, a sign placed on a map is one, and a graph hangs one with its mark node. See Markers: health bars, signs, pointers.

Nothing is written until something is changed, so a module that never opens Screens gets the game's default HUD.

Panels

Panels are the module's windows over the game: a shop, a dialogue, a choice. There is one library for every map and the game. A panel is one document, content and look together:

  • a title;
  • up to 32 widgets of five kinds: label, button, toggle, slider and select. Each has a name that graphs use;
  • decor behind them: text (which may show {hud.name}), a bar (a value, drawn as health is), and pictures.

The designer draws the panel with the game's own code, in a device frame over the module's HUD:

  • + new panel makes one, and + add… adds a widget or decor.
  • Drag the panel to move it (it re-anchors like a HUD element) and its edges to size it. Drag a widget or decor to place it, and a widget's corner to size it. Arrow keys nudge, and Delete removes.
  • Zoom with a pinch, the wheel, or the bar: −, +, the panel button (zoom onto the panel) and ⤢ (the whole screen).
  • The panel's details include its style (with a button beside it to go to Style), duplicate and delete panel.
  • Decor can be shown on touch screens only, or with a keyboard and mouse only. A phone frame draws the touch screen's, and a desktop frame the computer's, as the game does.

A new module's controls panel

A new module has one panel already, controls. Its welcome game graph opens it near the top of the screen for ten seconds as the game starts: the keyboard's keys on a computer, the on-screen controls on a touch screen. A player can close it with its ×. Its words and look are edited here, like any panel. To show nothing, delete the panel, or the welcome graph in Graphs. The subtitle line stays free for the game's own words.

In Gem Hollow there are two panels. title (titled GEM HOLLOW, centred, modal, not closeable) has the label tagline, "Three levers. Three keys. One star.", and the buttons play and quit. theend has again and quit. The menu map's graph opens title and answers its buttons; the ending graph does the same for theend (see Graphs).

Try

Switch from edit to try and the panel works as it does in the game. Each press says what a graph would hear, for example when_panel_used · shop · buy → value 1. open it again starts over.

Hooking it to a graph

Under graphs, the panel lists every graph that opens or answers it. open it from adds an opening node to a graph you choose, and answer adds the nodes that react to one widget. See Graphs.

  1. In Style, press + new style and give it colours.

  2. Open Panels, press + new panel, and add a button with + add….

  3. Choose your style in the panel's style.

  4. Switch to try and press the button. Read what a graph would hear.

In Cinderfall: fonts, panel skins and two skies

Cinderfall is under examples on Home. If yours doesn't show it, update the studio.

Gem Hollow uses the engine's own font and plain panels. Cinderfall dresses its screens:

  • fonts. Two, in assets/ui/fonts/: Special Elite (special_elite.ttf), a typewriter face for the UI, the objective and the subtitles, and Bebas Neue (bebas_neue.ttf) for the HUD numbers, panels and buttons.
  • panel styles. rust has a 9-slice picture for the box (skins/panel.png), the title bar (skins/title.png) and the buttons (skins/button.png, and skins/button_down.png when pressed). The pictures are cut from Freedoom textures. wheel is a round, see-through box with no picture, for the weapon wheel.
  • Panels. title (CINDERFALL, with NEW GAME and QUIT) and theend wear rust. arms, the weapon wheel, wears wheel. It is not modal, so the player keeps moving while it is up.
  • subtitles. Special Elite at 20, the speaker's name in amber, in a dark box.
  • Sky. A strip, SKYDUSK, from Freedoom, 4 times round the horizon at height 0.625. Both are DOOM's defaults, so the form's last line leaves them out: sky default strip SKYDUSK. The title screen's map, menu, is marked (own) in whose: it has a sky of its own, the strip SKYASH with the same numbers, written sky menu strip SKYASH.

Weather and fog

Rain, snow, dust, embers and fog belong to a room. In the map editor, select a room. Its inspector has a weather and fog section with a chip for each kind, each with its icon:

+ rain + snow + dust + embers + fog + fill

Weather can also have a volume of its own: an effect area, laid over any rooms and across their walls, such as a fog bank across a doorway. + fill fills a volume with a colour, for water, lava or coloured glass, and an effect area can be water the player swims in. All of it is in Effect areas, water and fills.

A room can have as many as it wants, such as rain and a low fog together. Each becomes a card with its settings and a ✕ to remove it.

  • An empty field is the engine's default, shown greyed as the placeholder. The studio reads the defaults from the engine itself, so they are always the game's.
  • A value set here is marked with • after the key's name, such as density •. ↺ clears it back to the default.
  • Distances are units above the floor. top defaults to the ceiling.
  • Tapping a color field first scrolls it up the form, so the colour picker has room to open below it.

The level view draws the effects with the game's own code, fog included. They are looks only: nothing in the game reads them, so a rainy floor is not slippery. A map holds up to 256 effects.

  1. Select a room with its ceiling open to the sky, and press + rain.

  2. Type 1.5 in windx. The key now reads windx •, and the rain slants in the view.

  3. Press + fog, and set top to 1 for a low mist.

  4. Press ↺ beside windx to go back to the default.

A graph can change a room's weather or fog while the game runs, with the set_sector_effect node in the node menu's move the map group. It takes a fill too, and an alpha.

Fog and snow can get in the way while you build. On a map with effects, the FX button beside PLAN / GAME, or X, hides them in the map view and shows them again. The game draws them all the same.

Animated textures

A wall, floor or ceiling texture can be animated: a sprite sheet of frames. When you import a PNG in Textures, the sheet dialog cuts it into frames and sets how many play per second. A picture exactly twice as wide as tall is taken as a still picture, because that is the shape of a sky strip or a panorama; frames side by side are guessed from three up. A texture's card opens it again: animate… on a still picture, cut & speed… on a sheet. Drawing frames and skies is covered in the Textures track: Animation and skies.

In a fog card, density shows 0.15 in grey and top shows ceiling in grey. What does that mean?

  • They are broken and need filling in
  • Nothing is set: the fog uses the engine's defaults
  • They were set by another map

An empty field is the engine's default, greyed. A value you set is marked with •.

You drag a HUD element into the top-right corner of a phone frame. What happens on a wide desktop screen?

  • It stays at the same pixel position, now near the middle
  • It is hidden, because it was placed for a phone
  • It stays in the top-right, because it re-anchored to that part of the screen

A dragged element re-anchors to the ninth of the screen it lands in, keeping its place there.

How do you give one map a different sky from the rest of the module?

  • Give that map a sky of its own in Sky (choose it in whose)
  • Change the module's sky every time you open that map
  • Add a fog effect to every room

A map's own sky is written as its own sky line; every other map keeps the module's.
