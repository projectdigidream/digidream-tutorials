How the player moves

The engine walks, runs, jumps, climbs and dashes by itself, and the player's settings tune it: how high the jump, how long you may jump after walking off an edge, whether a ledge is caught. A module can add movement of its own on top: swimming, sliding, gliding, a stun, with meters such as breath or stamina, and short pushes such as a dash or a jump pad.

You'll learnthe new jumping and climbing settings, how movement states and the ways between them work, how meters and layered moves work, how graphs listen to them and switch them, and how to make a pool the player swims in with a breath meter Time35 minutes Needsa module with a map (Your first map); for swimming, a pool (Effect areas, water and fills)

Two places: moves and movement

Open Player in the sidebar's Gameplay group. Two of its tabs are about moving, and they do different things:

TabWhat it isFor
movesThe player's settings: numbers and switches the engine's own movement reads.a higher jump, a double jump, a faster run, climbing, a dash, ledge grab
movementThe module's own movement states, the ways between them, meters and layered moves.swimming, sliding, gliding, a stun; breath, stamina; a dash, a jump pad

The moves tab is covered in Monsters, weapons, player: each setting is switched on to set it, for every map or one map. This page covers its new settings, then the movement tab.

The player's new settings

Jumping

Under jumping on the moves tab:

  • coyote_time (0.1 s): after walking off an edge, the ground jump is still there this long, so a jump pressed a moment late still works. Before, it lasted the whole fall. After it, a body that fell rather than jumped has only the air jumps that max_jumps leaves.
  • variable_jump (off): a jump is as high as JUMP is held. Letting go while the jump still rises cuts it short, so a tap is a short hop and a held press the full jump. Double jumps and wall jumps follow it too.
  • jump_release_keep (0.5): with variable_jump on, how much of the rising speed letting go keeps. 1 cuts nothing; 0 stops the jump dead.

Ledge grab

Under climbing:

  • ledge_grab (off): falling, the player catches the edge of a higher floor that a jump just missed, on any side, climbable or not. Rising past a ledge, a jump goes on by: it is caught on the way down.
  • ledge_reach (0.5 units): how far above the head a ledge's top may be and still be caught.

Hanging from a ledge, the body is head level with its top. Then:

  • JUMP with the stick towards the ledge pulls up onto it.
  • JUMP with the stick still is a small hop that falls back and catches the ledge again.
  • JUMP with the stick away leaps off.
  • Pushing away, or CROUCH, lets go.

Two animation conditions draw it, on the skin tab under which state draws which condition: hang, while hanging (it falls back to climb's state), and hop, for the small hop and the pull-up (it falls back to jump's).

Changing a setting from a graph

The graph node change player setting (change_player_setting, in the node menu's do to a thing) changes one of these settings while the game runs: a jump potion, a level where climbing is learnt.

  • setting: chosen from a list of exactly the player's settings, coyote_time, ledge_grab and variable_jump among them.
  • value, and how: set it (the default), add to it, or multiply it. An on/off setting takes 1 or 0.
  • seconds: for a while only, then it goes back by itself. Empty, it lasts until changed again.
  • everyone: every player in the level, not just the event's.

The player's own device predicts the change at once, so the stronger jump is there on the very next press. See Variables, numbers and saving.

Movement states

A player is always in exactly one movement state. The first is default: the engine's own walking, jumping and climbing, as it always was. A module can add up to 8 states of its own. Most of them are the same physics with other numbers: sliding is running with little friction, gliding is falling with little gravity, swimming is moving in 3D with a lift.

Ways in and out (the studio calls them ways) take a body from one state to another when their conditions hold: default → swim when the water reaches the waist and you are falling. Everything is predicted: the player's own device runs the same rules as the server, so a slide feels instant even on a slow connection.

The movement tab

The tab takes the whole width, with no preview beside it, so a phone shows a module's movement in a screen or two. From the top:

  1. A line saying what the tab is for.
  2. The engine's verdict: "The engine reads all of it." Every change you make goes through the engine's own parser. A line it refuses is outlined in red and opened, and the top says why: "The engine refuses the way swim → wade: … It is outlined below." It is listed among the module's problems, on Home too.
  3. Hints, in amber, about what a map is missing: "No map has water yet", or "No floor is tagged 7 yet" for a way that waits for a floor tag.
  4. start from (on an empty tab) or add from a recipe: seven starting points.
  5. states (N of 8): default first, then each of the module's states, each with its ways out under it, then from any state, and + a state.
  6. meters (N of 8), with + a meter.
  7. moves (N of 8), with + a move.
  8. as written in module.txt: the lines the tab writes, to read.

Each line says what it is in plain words: swim · swimming · top speed 5, speeds up 20, slows down 3, gravity × 0.3, floats up 8, jump 7 · no dash · draws "swim". Tap a line to open its editor under it; tap it again to close it. One editor is open at a time.

The recipes

A recipe adds its states, meters, moves and ways to what the module has. A name the module already has is left as it is. The status line says what the recipe needs.

RecipeWhat it adds
swimming (wade, swim, breath)wade and swim, and an air meter that drains with the head under water. Needs water on a map.
slide (crouch at a run)slide: crouch while running fast; it slows to a stop, or jump out of it.
sprint (stamina)sprint while DASH is held and there is stamina.
dash (costs stamina)a move, dash: USE dashes forward for a fifth of a second, for 20 stamina.
jump pad (floor tag 7)a move, pad: a floor tagged 7 throws the player up.
glide (hold jump while falling)glide: hold JUMP while falling to glide down slowly.
stunned (a script asks)stunned: a graph asks for it, and it lasts half a second.

The engine's own dash is on the DASH button too. If a module's sprint should be the only thing DASH does, turn the player's dash off: max_dashes 0 on the moves tab.

A state

A state's editor has:

  • name (up to 15 letters, digits and _). Renaming it renames every way and rate that names it.
  • animation: a name the player's picture draws while in this state (see Drawing a state).
  • moves: how the stick moves the body.
    • on foot: walking, with its own numbers (wading, sliding, ice, mud);
    • in the air: gliding, a slow fall;
    • swimming: in 3D, jump and crouch go up and down;
    • held still: the stick does nothing (stunned, frozen, a cutscene).
  • Its numbers: top speed, speeds up, slows down (in water, the drag), gravity × (a share of the world's gravity: 1 on foot, 0.3 in water), floats up (the lift in water, while it is at the waist or above) and jump (for a way that says jump). An empty box is as the player usually moves, from the moves tab. what the numbers mean says so in full.
  • Its flags, each a chip: no jumping, no dash, no attacking, no using, no crouching, weapon put away, counts as ground (it gives back the jumps and the coyote time, as landing does), can't be hurt, and starts switched off (see Switching them on and off).
  • delete this state. The ways and rates that used it go with it, and the status line says how many. Undo brings them back.

A way, and the order they are tried

A way's editor has where it goes from and to, then when all of these hold: up to four conditions, all of which must hold at once. + when… adds the first and + and… the next. The conditions:

ConditionHolds when
on the ground, in the air, going up, fallingthe body is so
a button pressed, a button held, a button let gothat button (jump, crouch, dash, use, attack and the others) was pressed this tick, is down, or came up this tick
its speedfaster than, or slower than, a number (along the ground)
after a time in the stateit has been in the state that many seconds
standing on a floor taggedthe room it stands on has that tag: ice, a jump pad
how deep in waterthe water reaches the feet, the waist or the eyes (or it is out of it), or is below one of them
a metera meter is empty, full, below or above a number
touching a wallit is against a wall
a script asked for ita graph or a script asked for this state (see Graphs)

For one condition or another, make two ways. Under the conditions, then says what happens as the way is taken: nothing, jump (the new state's jump), stop, a push up, forward or back, change a meter (add to it or set it), or start a move. tried sooner and later move it among the ways out of the same state, and delete removes it.

The order matters. Each tick, the engine tries the ways out of the state the body is in, top to bottom, then the ways from any state, top to bottom. The first whose conditions all hold is taken, and no other that tick. A way from any state never goes to the state the body is already in. The tab lists the ways in exactly this order, so what you read is what the engine does.

Meters

A meter is a number every body carries, from 0 to its most: breath, stamina, heat, a combo window. A meter's editor has:

  • name, most, starts at (empty: full) and a second: how fast it fills (plus) or drains (minus) by default.
  • its own rate in a state: a rule such as −2 a second in swim, when under water, with conditions of its own. The first rule that holds sets the rate: the state's own, then any state's, in order. With none, the meter's own rate.
  • delete this meter.

Ways read a meter (a meter: air empty) and change it (change a meter: a sprint that costs 10 to start). What happens when a meter runs out is a way, such as into a state that drowns, or a graph (below). A meter does no damage by itself.

On the HUD, each meter is two bindings, meter.name and meter.name_max, in the Screens lists: a bar with value of meter.air and out of meter.air_max is a breath bar. It moves without waiting for the server.

Layered moves

A move is a push that lasts a moment, on top of whatever state the body is in, and it keeps going through a change of state: a dash, a jump pad, a boost into a slide, a knockback. A move's editor has:

  • name, and it: holds a speed (keeps that speed for its time: a dash) or pushes once (adds it once, and keeps the brakes off that way for its time: a boost).
  • which way: facing or back (on the level, fixed as it starts), up or down. speed and seconds.
  • priority: one move runs on the level and one up or down at a time. A new one replaces a running one on its way only at a priority no lower.
  • costs a meter, and how much. Too dear, it does not start and nothing is paid.
  • at the start: switched on, or switched off (see Switching them on and off).
  • It starts by itself when its conditions hold. With none, it waits for a way's start a move or a graph.

Moves do not run while climbing or on a rope: those hold the body themselves.

Drawing a state

A state's animation is a name, such as swim. Once a state has one, the skin tab lists it under which state draws which condition, as swim (movement state): give it the number of a state of the player's picture, and the player is drawn with it while swimming. Left at by its motion, the player is drawn as the motion says (a swimmer would otherwise look as if falling).

Switching them on and off

A state or a move can start switched off, for a graph to switch on for one player: a glide found on a ledge, a dash learnt, swimming taken away in a cursed level.

  • A state: the chip starts switched off among its flags.
  • A move: at the start → switched off.

Switched off, a state is never entered (a body in it goes back to default), and a move does not start (a running one stops). The switches are each player's own, and predicted like the rest. If something starts switched off and no graph ever switches it on, the tab says so: "glide starts switched off, and no graph switches it on ("enable movement state" glide): it never runs, unless a script does."

The engine's own abilities (its dash, climbing, hovering, flight, the grapple) are the player's settings instead: switch those with change player setting.

Graphs

A graph listens to the movement, asks for states and changes meters. In the node menu:

NodeGroupWhat it does
when_movement_state_entered, when_movement_state_leftwhen …somebody began to swim, or stopped. state is optional: without it, any state. Gives entity and state.
when_meter_empty, when_meter_fullwhen …somebody's meter ran out, or is full again: once as it gets there, then not again until it has left.
ask_movement_statedo to a thingasks for a state. A way that says a script asked for it takes the body there when its other conditions hold: the module's rules decide.
set_meterdo to a thingsets a meter (how set) or adds to it (add; less than 0 takes): a bubble of air, a stamina potion.
start_movedo to a thingstarts a move, as a way's start a move would: a knockback, a boost.
enable_movement_state, enable_movedo to a thingswitch a state or a move on (on, the default) or off, for this body.
movement_state_of, meter_ofreadthe state an entity is in (default, or one of the module's), and a meter's value and max.

Their state, meter and move fields offer the module's own names. A name the module does not declare does nothing, and says so in the Play log.

Exercise: a pool with a breath meter

You need a pool first: an effect area made water, with a fill to see it. Effect areas, water and fills builds one on a new module's first map.

  1. Open Player → movement. Under start from, press swimming (wade, swim, breath).

  2. Read the lines. default goes to wade when the water is at the feet; wade back to default out of the water; from any state to swim when it reaches the waist and you are falling; swim to wade on the ground with the water below the waist, or with a jump while the head is above water.

  3. If your map has no water yet, the tab says "No map has water yet". Build the pool, then come back: the hint is gone.

  4. Tap the air meter's line. It holds 10, fills at 5 a second, and has its own rate in swim, −2 a second, when the water is at the eyes. Change most to 6, for a shorter breath.

  5. Open Screens. Add a bar with + element… → bar, and set value of to meter.air and out of to meter.air_max.

  6. Open your map's Graph. Add when meter empty with meter air and who players, then damage with amount 5, then set meter with meter air and value 2, and link them in a row. Out of breath, the player is hurt and gets two seconds' more air.

  7. If you are in the app, press Play. Walk into the pool: you wade, then swim. Hold CROUCH to dive, watch the bar drain, and come up with JUMP.

A glide found on a ledge

  1. On the movement tab, open add from a recipe and press glide (hold jump while falling).

  2. Tap the glide line, and tap the chip starts switched off. A hint says nothing switches it on.

  3. On a map, place a prop for the glider (a feather) and open the map's Graph. Add when prop touched with that prop, then enable movement state with state glide, then a subtitle: "Hold JUMP as you fall to glide."

  4. Back on the movement tab, the hint is gone.

The swim state has two ways out, both to wade, and there is a way from any state to swim. A swimmer stands on the bottom with the water below the waist. Which is tried first?

  • The way from any state, because it is the most general
  • Whichever was added last
  • swim's own first way, top of its list; any state's only after all of swim's own

A state's own ways are tried before any state's, each list top to bottom, and the first that holds wins. The tab lists them in that order.

You want a jump that is short when JUMP is tapped and full when it is held. Where?

  • Player → moves → jumping: switch on variable_jump
  • Player → movement: a new state with a smaller jump
  • A graph with when button released

variable_jump is one of the player's own settings: letting go while the jump rises cuts it, keeping jump_release_keep of the speed.

The air meter runs out under water. What happens, with only the swimming recipe?

  • The player takes damage until they surface
  • Nothing yet: running out does what a way or a graph says, and the recipe has neither
  • The player is sent back to the start

A meter is a number. A way (into a drowning state) or a graph (when meter empty, then damage) gives running out its meaning.

The glide state starts switched off. A graph runs enable movement state glide for one player. Who can glide?

  • Every player in the level
  • That player only: the switches are each player's own
  • Nobody until the level restarts

The node acts on its entity, the event's player unless another is linked. Each body has its own switches.
