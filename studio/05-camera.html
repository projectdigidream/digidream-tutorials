Camera and cinematics

Each map can say how the game's camera looks at it: from which angle, how far away, and whether the player may turn or zoom. It can also have camera paths — curves the camera rides — for a fixed-track shot or a flythrough.

You'll learnhow to set a map's camera angle and distance, lock them, tune how it moves, put it behind the player, draw and reorder a camera path, and choose how the camera follows Time20 minutes Needsa map with a few rooms (see Your first map)

The Camera sheet

Choose Camera (key 6), or More → Camera…. A sheet titled Camera opens. Its first line says how the game looks at this map when nothing is set: the engine's own camera, 45° around, 51° down, free to turn and zoom.

Angle and distance

  • set the angle: turn it on to fix the camera's angle. It starts at the engine's own, switches the view to the game view, and shows two sliders:
    • around: which way the camera faces, all the way round. The label shows the angle, for example around (45°).
    • down: how steeply it looks down, for example down (51°).
  • set the distance: turn it on to fix how far the camera stands back. The distance slider goes from 8 to 80 and starts at 38.
  • lock turning: the player cannot turn the camera.
  • lock zoom: the player cannot zoom.

The studio's game view follows these settings while they are set, so what you see is what the player will see.

Sprites stand upright in the world. So the steeper the camera looks down, the shorter they look — in the studio and in the game. Check your monsters after you change down.

How it moves

Under the angle is how it moves: how the camera chases the player while it follows them. Each slider shows the engine's own value until the map sets one; a value the map sets is marked (own). Every slider has a help button with its description, and its number can be typed as well as dragged.

SliderEngine's valueWhat it does
smoothing8How fast the camera catches up with the player, per second. Higher is tighter; lower floats behind.
weight (max accel)0The most the camera may speed up or slow down, so it takes time to get going and to stop. 0 is no limit.
look ahead0Units ahead of the player, along where they face, that the camera aims at. 0 aims at the player.
look ahead with speed0Further ahead the faster the player runs forward: their forward speed times this, about "this many seconds of running ahead". Forward only, never backwards or sideways.
look-ahead smoothing4How fast the aim point swings round when the player turns. Slower than the follow, so a turn does not lurch the view.
dead zone across, dead zone up and down2.5, 1.5How far the player may move sideways, or up and down, before the camera follows at all.

back to the engine's movement clears every one the map set. A graph's camera_follow node can change lead and lead_velocity later, for one scene.

For a fast runner, a little look ahead with speed (0.2 to 0.4) shows more of what is coming without moving the camera when the player stands still.

Behind the player

The engine's own camera stays where the player last turned it. For a game played over the shoulder, the section behind the player, under how it moves, makes it come round behind the player by itself.

  • turns by itself has three choices:
    • no: stays where it is turned, the engine's own camera.
    • swings round behind them: the camera turns to look along where the player faces, the short way round, unless the player turned it themselves in the last few seconds. Its distance and its angle down, above, are where it settles. Two sliders come with it:
      • after (s): how long after the player last turned the camera it starts swinging back. 2 until the map sets one.
      • how fast (turns/s): how quickly it swings round. 1.5 until the map sets one.
      Like the sliders above, each is marked (own) once the map sets it.
    • studio 0.4.3.1 on a lead: follows the way they go: the camera turns with the way the player moves, not the way they face, as if pulled along on a lead. Running sideways, it turns to keep them in view; running away, it follows; walking towards it, it backs off instead of swinging round behind their back. It has no sliders.
    In the stable studio 0.4.3 this is a switch, swing round behind the player, which is the second choice.
  • walls pull it in, with either choice: a wall or a ceiling between the camera and the player brings the camera closer at once, so it never looks through one, and it eases back out to its distance after. A platform's open edge lets it out over the platform, and its slab pulls it in like a wall. Best with a low camera behind the player.
  • set its height over the player: the camera sits a height above the player's feet, its distance behind them, and the angle down follows from the two, instead of the angle above. Two sliders come with it:
    • height (units, 3): how high above the player's feet it sits, 0 to 30.
    • looks at (units up, 1): the point above the feet it looks at and swings round: their middle, their head, or the floor (0).

Each switch and slider has a help button with its description. The camera never swings round in first person, nor while the map locks turning.

For a camera close behind the player, switch on all three, turn on set the distance and bring distance down to 8, then set the height to about 3 and looks at to about 1.5, the player's head.

From a graph

A graph's camera_follow node sends the camera back to following the player, from where it is told: distance, height (the angle down), angle, lock, lead and lead_velocity, and now the same as this section:

  • auto, auto_delay and auto_rate: swing round behind the player, after how many seconds, and how fast (the node has no lead: a script's auto_turn = 2 does that);
  • collide: walls pull it in;
  • camera_height and aim_height: its height over the player's feet, and the height it looks at. A negative camera_height goes back to the angle.

So a cutscene can end on a low camera behind the player, though the map itself starts with the engine's.

Camera paths

Further down the sheet is ⌁ camera paths. A path is a line the camera can ride. It bends outward at every corner, because the camera rides a smooth curve through the points, not the straight lines between them.

Each path has a row: its tag (and loop if it loops), how many points it has, and its length in units of curve. ✎ edits it; ✕ deletes it after asking. + new path makes a path and starts editing it.

Editing a path

While you edit a path, the view switches to the game view, and the sheet becomes Path tag and the path's tag. In the viewport:

  • Tap the floor to add a waypoint at the end.
  • Tap a waypoint, or the number drawn above it, to pick it. A tap takes the nearest waypoint within about 30 pixels on screen, so you need not hit its small handle.
  • Drag a waypoint to move it.
  • Hold a waypoint (long-press) to remove it.

A new waypoint arrives at the height of the one you last worked on. A camera path usually runs above head height, and a point dropped on the floor below would put the shot in the carpet. Every waypoint is numbered on the map, and the curve is drawn through them.

The sheet shows the picked waypoint as waypoint N of M, with:

  • height, x and y.
  • previous and next: pick the waypoint before or after.
  • remove: delete this waypoint.
  • earlier and later: move it one place along the path. The camera runs through the points in order, so the same points in another order are another path.
  • + after this: a new waypoint halfway to the next one. Use it to add a bend; adding at the end would change where the shot finishes instead.

Under the waypoint is the order: every waypoint in the order the camera runs through them, each with its position and height. Tap a row (or its number) to pick that waypoint; its ↑ and ↓ move it one place along the curve. reverse the order turns the whole path round, so the shot runs the other way.

Below the order:

  • height for every waypoint: sets all of them to one height at once.
  • tag: the number scripts and the camera use to name the path. Tags are not unique; the first path with a tag wins.
  • loop: the path joins its end to its start.
  • rides the sectors under it (on by default): each point moves with the room it sits over, so a path over a lift moves with the lift. Turn it off to pin every point to the world.
  • all cameras: back to the Camera sheet.

Do not let a path ride a room that a script moves in steps. Its position arrives over the network, and the camera judders.

Esc also stops editing the path.

How it follows

The mode menu under how it follows says what the camera does with a path:

ModeWhat the camera doesExtra fields
follow the player (the engine's own)The usual camera. No path.—
track: stay on the path, nearest the playerStands on the path at the point nearest the player, still aimed at the player. A fixed-track shot.path, blend rate
flythrough: ride the path at a speedRides along the path, taking both its position and its aim from the path.path, speed (units/s), blend rate
auto: take the path when the player comes nearTracks the path while the player is near it, and follows the player otherwise.path, take it within, blend rate
  • path: which path, by tag. It starts as (choose a path).
  • speed (units/s): −40 to 40, 6 to begin with. A negative speed rides the path backwards, looking backwards.
  • take it within: how near the player must come for auto to take the path, 1 to 40. It lets go again at 1.5 times that distance, so a player walking along the edge does not make it flicker.
  • blend rate: how fast the camera fades onto the path and off it, 0.5 to 20. There is no cut: the camera eases from one to the other.

For a flythrough, the sheet says how long the shot lasts: the path's length divided by the speed. Write that number down if a script times a cutscene: the engine has no event that says a flythrough has finished.

These settings are where the map starts, for everyone who joins it. A trigger's camera action can change any of them later (see Triggers), and a graph can run a whole cutscene (see Graphs).

In Gem Hollow: its cameras

  • world sets its own angle: 45° around, 36° down (flatter than the engine's 51°, so the platforms read as a side-on course) and a distance of 15 (closer than the engine's). Turning and zoom stay free, and it leaves how it moves at the engine's values. Its how it follows is the engine's: follow the player.
  • Six paths in world. Tag 1 is the intro's flight over the whole level, 16 waypoints, about 497 units of curve. Tags 2, 3 and 4 are short three-point shots of the red, blue and gold gates opening. Tag 5 is a loop round the vault for the ending, and tag 6 the vault door opening. None is used by the map's camera settings: the graphs fly them with camera_flythrough and camera_track.
  • menu, the title map, sets how it follows to flythrough on path 1, a looping ring of eight waypoints round the island, at 1.6 units a second. So the title screen circles slowly behind the menu, with no graph needed.

Exercise: a tracking shot

  1. In your copy of Gem Hollow, open menu, choose Camera and press ✎ on path tag 1. Tap a waypoint's number to pick it, move it one place down the order with ↓, and watch the ring kink. Undo with ⌘Z.

  2. Open your own map and choose Camera. Turn on set the angle and drag around until the main room faces you. Turn on lock turning.

  3. Press + new path. Tap the floor four times along one side of the room, at about head height and above.

  4. Pick the second waypoint and press + after this. Drag the new waypoint out to make a bend.

  5. Set height for every waypoint to 6. Then press reverse the order and see the numbers run the other way.

  6. Press all cameras. Set mode to track: stay on the path, nearest the player and choose your path's tag in path.

  7. Change mode to flythrough: ride the path at a speed and read how long the shot lasts. Set speed (units/s) to 3 and read it again.

  8. Set mode back to follow the player. Under how it moves, set look ahead with speed to 0.3. Its label now ends in (own).

  9. If you are in the app, press Play, run, and watch the camera lead you.

  10. Back in Camera, turn off lock turning. Under behind the player, set turns by itself to swings round behind them (in the stable studio, switch on swing round behind the player), switch on walls pull it in, and set after (s) to 1.

  11. Switch on set its height over the player. Set the height to 3 and looks at to 1.5.

  12. Press Play. Turn the camera with Q and let go: a second later it swings back behind you. Run along a wall and watch the camera come in rather than look through it.

You want the camera to stay on a rail beside a corridor while still looking at the player. Which mode?

  • flythrough
  • track
  • follow the player

Track takes its position from the path and still aims at the player. Flythrough takes its aim from the path too.

Your path has the right points but bends the wrong way. What do you try first?

  • Delete it and tap the points again
  • Turn on loop
  • Put the points in the right order: in the order list, move rows with ↑ and ↓ (or pick a waypoint and use ← earlier / → later)

The camera runs through the points in order, so the order is part of the shape. You can move a point along the path without deleting it.

A flythrough path is 30 units of curve and the speed is 6. How long is the shot?

  • 5 seconds
  • 180 seconds
  • Until the engine reports the flythrough has finished

Length divided by speed: 30 / 6 = 5 s. The engine has no completion event, so a cutscene is timed from this number.

You set turns by itself to swings round behind them, but in the game the camera never turns by itself. The map also has lock turning on. Why?

  • The camera never swings round while the map locks turning
  • It needs walls pull it in too
  • It only works on maps with a camera path

A locked camera keeps the angle the map gave it. The automatic camera, like the player, may not turn it. It is also never used in first person.

You want the camera to show more of the course ahead when the player sprints, but not to drift when they stand still. Which slider?

  • dead zone across
  • look ahead with speed
  • smoothing

Look ahead with speed adds the player's forward speed times its value to the aim point, so it is zero when they stop. A plain look ahead would lead even at rest.
