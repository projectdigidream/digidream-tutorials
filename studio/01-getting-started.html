Getting started

The studio is a module editor that ships inside the game. A module is a whole game: its maps, monsters, weapons, textures, music, sounds and screens. You edit it on a desktop or a phone, press Play to walk through it, and export it as a pack the engine loads unchanged.

You'll learnhow to launch the studio, how Home and the rail's groups are laid out, how to open the Gem Hollow example, how to start a module of your own, how to change the studio's style, and what undo applies to Time15 minutes NeedsDigidream Studio installed on a desktop or an Android device (Get the tools)

Launch it

  1. Open Digidream Studio the way you open any app. If you haven't installed it yet, see Get the tools; it also covers the warnings some systems show on the first launch.

  2. While it loads, a log under the progress bar says what it is doing. If it gets stuck, the log shows the stage and the error in red, and copy this copies the log for a bug report.

The desktop and Android apps are the same studio. Both edit, play-test with Play, and export. One difference: installing extra plugins works on the desktop only (see Plugins).

There is no iOS build yet.

Home

The studio opens on the module's Home page. At the top are the module's name, its about text and the studio's version (studio 0.4.2.11) with a check for updates button beside it. When a newer studio is out, a card under them says so, with what changed, update now where the studio can update itself, download and not now (see Get the tools). If you sync with GitHub, the module's sync state shows beside the version too (see Sync with GitHub). Below them are the actions that apply to the whole module:

  • play from map and export. Play on Home starts on the module's starting map and names it, as in play from first. You choose the starting map in Settings → starting map; left at the first by name, it is the first map in alphabetical order, which is where the game starts too. Play in the map editor and on the rail is different: it starts on the map you are editing. A module reopens on the map you edited last, so the rail's Play plays that one. Export says what it saved, such as "Exported gemhollow.zip (… KB): a pack to open in the studio on another device…".
  • import pack, which brings in a pack someone exported.
  • new module, which starts a module of your own, after asking what kind of game it is (see A module of your own).
  • snapshots, plugins (7) (the count of plugins), and play log (map) after a play-test.

Below them are:

  • maps (count): a thumbnail of each map, its rooms drawn from above in their floor textures, with a count of rooms and things.
  • What is wrong: every problem the studio can find in any map, trigger, graph, monster or weapon. Each problem links to where it is. When there are none, the list says "nothing wrong that the studio can see".
  • the module: the sections, one tile each, with a count.
  • examples: the example projects that ship with the studio (see below).
  • Style: how the studio itself looks on this device (see below).
  • other modules, when the device holds more than one: a chip per module, to switch. Under them, delete a module… puts a delete id button beside each other module (the open one cannot be deleted from under itself). Each asks first, and says a module's maps go with it: export it first to keep a copy. done hides the buttons again.

Gem Hollow, the example project

The studio ships with Gem Hollow, a small but complete platformer. It is the running example of these tutorials: most pages have an In Gem Hollow box that shows how it does the thing the page teaches.

  • A title map, menu, where the camera circles an island while the title panel offers Play and Quit.
  • One big level, world, in four zones: the Meadow, the Isles (water), the Cliffs and the Keep (lava). A lever opens the gate at the end of each of the first three zones, and three keys open the vault in the Keep, where the Star waits.
  • Three monsters (slime, bee, snail), two weapons (Bonk and the Star Toss), 27 props, 25 textures, 5 music tracks and 30 sounds, a HUD of its own, the sky SKYHILLS, and cutscenes at both ends, all drawn as graphs.

Its art is Kenney's CC0 "New Platformer Pack", its music CC0 chiptunes; its characters were made in ThreeSprite and its sounds in DigidreamSound.

  1. On Home, under examples, press Gem Hollow. The first time, the studio makes a module of your own from the example and opens it. Home now shows two maps, menu and world, and nothing wrong.

  2. Change anything you like: it is your copy. Pressing Gem Hollow again opens the same copy, with your changes.

  3. To start over, press a fresh copy. It makes another, untouched copy beside yours.

For a tour of all of it, see Gem Hollow, a tour.

Cinderfall, the second example

Cinderfall is the newer example: a first-person shooter, with five districts joined without a load, people to talk to and a boss. It sits beside Gem Hollow under examples.

  1. On Home, under examples, press Cinderfall. As with Gem Hollow, the studio makes a module of your own from it, with 12 maps, and opens it. a fresh copy makes another, untouched one.

  2. Scroll to the foot of Home. Under other modules, the chips switch between Cinderfall and your other modules.

If Home doesn't show Cinderfall, your studio is older than these tutorials. Update it.

See Cinderfall, a tour.

A module of your own

When you are ready to make your own game, start a new module from Home. The studio asks two questions:

  1. On Home, press new module. The first question is its id, "module id (letters, digits, dashes)". Type my-game and press OK. (If a module with that id is already on the device, the studio says so and makes nothing.)

  2. The second question is "What kind of game is it?". Press first person: a shooter or a platformer, seen from outside. cancel makes nothing.

  3. The studio opens the new module on its Home. Its name is the id you typed; change it in Settings.

studio 0.4.2.1 An id is plain letters, digits, _ and -. Accents come off by themselves: île becomes ile. A letter with no plain spelling is named, as in "An id is a-z, 0-9, _ and -: … cannot be in it. Type the id again", and the id is asked again instead of losing the letter unseen.

What a new module holds

A new module is a copy of the starter, which comes with the studio. Its one map, first, has a room, a door, a pit with a switch that raises its floor, a platform, an exit switch, a start spawn, two lights and a spirit to fight: a flying monster drawn twice as big as before, with a body to match, that bites gently (6 damage, at most every 1.5 seconds). The starter also has:

  • A door that works as the door preset does. E opens it from either side, pressing it again while it is open does nothing, and it shuts by itself 4 seconds after it has opened. Its three cards are on the map's Triggers sheet (see Triggers), with the pit's switch and the exit.
  • The player's death, shown on every map. When the player dies, the screen goes dark, "You died. Back to the start!" is shown, and the screen clears again. It is a game graph, death (under the game's graphs in Graphs), so it answers a death on any map, maps you add later included. Change its words, or delete it.
  • studio 0.4.2.1 The keys, shown once. As the game starts, the game graph welcome opens the panel controls at the top of the screen for ten seconds: a line for a keyboard (WASD walk | SPACE jump | SHIFT dash | E use | F punch | Q/R turn the camera, or for first person WASD walk | mouse look | click or F fire | SPACE jump | E use) and one for a touch screen. (next release The platformer's line says F punch, not F attack: it has no weapon, and F swings bare hands.) A player can close it with its ×. The subtitle line stays free for your own words. Change its words and look on the Panels page, or delete the welcome graph (or the panel) to show nothing.
  • Shots you can see. Three particle kinds of its own on the Particles page: 20 a muzzle flash, 21 a tracer and 22 sparks where a shot lands.

The studio then makes it yours:

  • No description. Its about is empty, for you to write in Settings.
  • A clean screen. The debug overlay, the engine's numbers over the game, is off. You can turn it on for your own play-tests in Settings → when I press Play (see Play and export).
  • The controls of the kind you chose, first person or platformer. You can change them later in Controls.
  • First person starts with a gun in hand: a weapon called gun, made from the Pistol recipe, drawn on the screen with the studio's placeholder gun until you give it pictures (see The placeholder gun). Its shot uses the starter's muzzle flash, tracer and sparks, and the flash in hand stays up long enough to be seen.

A module made from an older starter is mended as it opens: its starter.lua script, which made every swing that landed cost twice, is replaced by the new one, unless you changed it. The starter's other new parts (the death screen, the keys, the effects) are not added to it.

The studio's style

The studio wears a style: its colours, fonts, control shapes and icons. Five ship with it: Claude (the default), Paper, 90s, PS1 and Aero (Frutiger Aero: frosted glass and glossy buttons). You choose it in the Style card at the foot of Home, or in Settings under Look — this device, not the module:

  • style: which one.
  • mode: auto — follows the system, or one of the style's modes, such as light and dark (Paper and Aero call theirs day and night).
  • pointer: touch — finger-sized, mouse — compact, or auto — follows the device, which is where it starts.
  • import a style…: brings in a style made with uikit and baked into one file (a .skin.json, or the .skin.js a tool vendors). It is listed after the shipped ones, marked (imported), and forget this style removes it.

The style is this device's choice, never the module's: it is not exported, and it changes nothing in the game. The desktop and Android apps keep it in a file of their own too, so a forced quit does not lose it. (Do not confuse it with the rail's Style section, under Interface, which styles the game's screens; see Sky, screens and weather.) The plugins (ThreeSprite, qPainter, DigidreamSound, DigidreamSynth, Code and the Packager) wear the studio's style too.

Each style draws the studio's icons in its own look: lines, colour pixels, glass. These tutorials quote a label by its words, such as play from first or snapshots, and leave out the icon before them, because the words are the same in every style. A button that is only an icon is named by its usual sign, such as ✕.

The rail

On a wide or landscape screen, a rail down the left lists the sections. Each one shows a count of its problems. The rail's foot holds Undo, Redo, Play and Export. Tapping the section you are on again goes back to its list, for example from a prop back to all the props.

The sections are in groups, each under a small heading: World, Gameplay, Art, Sound, Interface, Logic and Module. Where the rail is too narrow for words and shows only icons, a line stands for each heading. The rail scrolls when it is taller than the window: Logic and Module are at the bottom. A plugin that brings a page of its own, such as Sync or Package, puts it under Module.

On Home, the module has a tile for the main sections too, each with a count.

On a phone held upright there is no rail. Home is the list of sections, and every page has a way back: module on a page, and ‹ beside undo in the map editor.

On Android, the phone's Back works as in other apps. It closes a dialog, or goes back to the page before. On Home, where there is nothing further back, it puts the studio in the background instead of quitting it, so you come back to the same place. While you type, the field is lifted above the keyboard. A quick tap on a button, such as the game's touch FIRE, is never lost.

Every place has an address

The address bar always says where you are, for example #/monsters, #/map/world or #/graph/world%3Alevers. Reloading the page brings you back to the same place. Coming back from Play returns you to the map you were testing, with the view as you left it.

What undo applies to

Undo has two scopes:

  • In the map editor, it undoes changes to that map.
  • On a page (Monsters, Textures, Settings…), it undoes changes to the module.

The keys are ⌘Z to undo, and ⇧⌘Z or ⌘Y to redo. On Windows and Linux, use Ctrl in place of ⌘.

studio 0.4.2.1 Undo survives Play. Coming back from a play-test, the map's undo and the module's are as you left them, so you can play, see what went wrong, and undo the change that did it. Each graph keeps its zoom and scroll over the trip too.

Undo is for the last few minutes. For anything older, the studio keeps snapshots of the module on the device. It takes one when the module is opened, every ten minutes while you edit, and before anything that replaces work. See Snapshots.

  1. With your copy of Gem Hollow open, open Settings (under Module, near the foot of the rail) and change its name to My Hollow.

  2. Press ⌘Z. The name goes back to Gem Hollow, because a page undoes changes to the module.

  3. Press ⇧⌘Z to bring the name back. Then reload the page and check you land on Settings again.

  4. In the Look card at the bottom of Settings, set style to Aero, then to 90s. Set it back to Claude when you have seen enough.

You want a copy of Gem Hollow you can safely wreck, while keeping the one you already changed. What do you press?

  • Gem Hollow again
  • import pack
  • a fresh copy

The Gem Hollow button opens the copy you already have, with your changes. a fresh copy makes another, untouched one beside it.

You rename a monster on the Monsters page, then open a map and press ⌘Z. What is undone?

  • The monster's rename
  • The last change to that map
  • Nothing, because undo is off in the map editor

Inside the map editor, undo applies to the map. Go back to a page to undo changes to the module.

You switch the studio to the Aero style and export your module. What does a player of the exported game see?

  • The game as before: the style is the studio's look on your device, not part of the module
  • The game's HUD and panels in Aero
  • A question asking which style to use

The style is kept on the device and never written into the module. The game's own look is set in Screens, Panels and Style (the rail's Interface group).
