Getting started

The studio is a module editor that ships inside the game. A module is a whole game: its maps, monsters, weapons, textures, music, sounds and screens. You edit it on a desktop or a phone, press Play to walk through it, and export it as a pack the engine loads unchanged.

Two ways to run it

The studio is a web app served at /studio/. The same app runs in two places, and they differ in one thing: whether you can press Play.

In a browser (dev server)In the app (desktop or Android)
Edit everything✓✓
Export a pack✓ (downloads a .zip)✓
▶ Play✗ — needs a game server✓
Install plugins✓desktop ✓, Android ✗

In a browser

  1. From the repository root, start the dev server:

    node engine/tools/devserver.js --module starter

    It listens on port 8080. Set DNP_HTTP_PORT to use another port.

  2. Open http://localhost:8080/studio/. While it loads, a log under the progress bar says what it is doing. If it gets stuck, the log shows the stage and the error in red, and copy this copies the log for a bug report.

  3. Press ▶ Play. In a browser it answers "Play needs the app. In a browser, export a pack and run it with the engine." This is expected. To play-test in a browser, export the pack and run it with the engine:

    unzip starter.zip -d modules/
./engine/scripts/run_stack.sh --module starter

As an app (Play works)

A build that opens straight into the studio:

./engine/scripts/make_app.sh --module starter --studio    # desktop
./engine/scripts/build_apk.sh --module starter --studio   # Android

Ready-made builds for every platform, plugins included, are written to builds/ by packager/scripts/release_studio.sh.

The macOS app is signed ad hoc and not notarized, so on another Mac the first launch needs right-click → Open.

There is no iOS build yet.

Home

The studio opens on the module's Home page. At the top are the module's name, its about text and the studio's version (studio 0.3.0). Below them are the actions that apply to the whole module:

  • ▶ play and ⇪ export.
  • ⇩ import pack, which brings in a pack someone exported.
  • ＋ new module, which starts a new module as a copy of the starter template.
  • 🕘 snapshots, 🧩 plugins (6) (the count of plugins), and 📋 play log after a play-test.

Below them are:

  • 🗺 maps: a thumbnail of each map, its rooms drawn from above in their floor textures, with a count of rooms and things.
  • What is wrong: every problem the studio can find in any map, trigger, graph, monster or weapon. Each problem links to where it is. When there are none, the list says "✔ nothing wrong that the studio can see".
  • ◆ the module: the sections, one tile each, with a count.
  • ◆ examples: the example projects that ship with the studio (see below).
  • Style: how the studio itself looks on this device (see below).
  • other modules, when the device holds more than one: a chip per module, to switch.

Gem Hollow, the example project

The studio ships with Gem Hollow, a small but complete platformer. It is the running example of these tutorials: most pages have an In Gem Hollow box that shows how it does the thing the page teaches.

  • A title map, menu, where the camera circles an island while the title panel offers Play and Quit.
  • One big level, world, in four zones: the Meadow, the Isles (water), the Cliffs and the Keep (lava). A lever opens the gate at the end of each of the first three zones, and three keys open the vault in the Keep, where the Star waits.
  • Three monsters (slime, bee, snail), two weapons (Bonk and the Star Toss), 30 props, 25 textures, 5 music tracks and 30 sounds, a HUD of its own, the sky SKYHILLS, and cutscenes at both ends, all drawn as graphs.

Its art is Kenney's CC0 "New Platformer Pack", its music CC0 chiptunes; its characters were made in ThreeSprite and its sounds in DigidreamSound.

  1. On Home, under ◆ examples, press 📂 Gem Hollow. The first time, the studio makes a module of your own from the example and opens it. Home now shows two maps, menu and world, and nothing wrong.

  2. Change anything you like: it is your copy. Pressing 📂 Gem Hollow again opens the same copy, with your changes.

  3. To start over, press a fresh copy. It makes another, untouched copy beside yours.

For a tour of all of it, see Gem Hollow, a tour.

In Cinderfall: an example you import

Cinderfall is new and still being tested. It is not under ◆ examples yet, so you bring it in as a pack.

Cinderfall is a second example: a first-person shooter, with five districts joined without a load, people to talk to and a boss. Until the studio offers it under ◆ examples, you get it the way you get any pack someone sends you:

  1. From the repository root, run node tutorials/tools/pack-example.mjs cinderfall. It writes cinderfall.zip.

  2. On Home, press ⇩ import pack and choose the zip. Cinderfall opens as a module of its own, with 12 maps.

  3. Scroll to the foot of Home. Under other modules, the chips switch between Cinderfall and your other modules.

Unlike 📂 Gem Hollow, an imported pack is not a copy: it is the module. Import it again and it replaces the module, after a snapshot. See Cinderfall, a tour.

The studio's style

The studio wears a style: its colours, fonts, control shapes and icons. Five ship with it: Claude (the default), Paper, 90s, PS1 and Aero (Frutiger Aero: frosted glass and glossy buttons). You choose it in the Style card at the foot of Home, or in ⚙ Settings under Look — this device, not the module:

  • style: which one.
  • mode: auto — follows the system, or one of the style's modes, such as light and dark (Paper and Aero call theirs day and night).
  • pointer: touch — finger-sized, mouse — compact, or auto — follows the device.
  • ⇩ import a style…: brings in a style made with uikit and baked into one file (a .skin.json, or the .skin.js a tool vendors). It is listed after the shipped ones, marked (imported), and 🗑 forget this style removes it.

The style is this device's choice, never the module's: it is not exported, and it changes nothing in the game. (Do not confuse it with the rail's 🎨 Style section, which styles the game's screens; see Sky, screens and weather.)The plugins (ThreeSprite, qPainter, DigidreamSound, DigidreamSynth, Code and the Packager) wear the studio's style too.

The labels in these tutorials use each icon's plain glyph, such as ▶ play. A style may draw the icon as a picture instead, but the words are the same in every style.

On a phone held upright there is no rail. Home is the list of sections, and every page has a way back: ‹ module on a page, and ‹ beside undo in the map editor.

Every place has an address

The address bar always says where you are, for example #/monsters, #/map/world or #/graph/world%3Alevers. Reloading the page brings you back to the same place. Coming back from Play returns you to the map you were testing, with the view as you left it.

What undo applies to

Undo has two scopes:

  • In the map editor, it undoes changes to that map.
  • On a page (Monsters, Textures, Settings…), it undoes changes to the module.

The keys are ⌘Z to undo, and ⇧⌘Z or ⌘Y to redo. On Windows and Linux, use Ctrl in place of ⌘.

Undo is for the last few minutes. For anything older, the studio keeps snapshots of the module on the device. It takes one when the module is opened, every ten minutes while you edit, and before anything that replaces work. See Snapshots.

  1. With your copy of Gem Hollow open, open ⚙ Settings and change its name to My Hollow.

  2. Press ⌘Z. The name goes back to Gem Hollow, because a page undoes changes to the module.

  3. Press ⇧⌘Z to bring the name back. Then reload the page and check you land on Settings again.

  4. In the Look card at the bottom of Settings, set style to Aero, then to 90s. Set it back to Claude when you have seen enough.

You're in a plain browser at localhost:8080/studio/ and want to play-test. What do you do?

  • Press ▶ Play — it starts a local server
  • Open /index.html?studio=1 in another tab
  • ⇪ Export the pack, unzip it into modules/, and run it with run_stack.sh — or use the app

Play needs a shell with a game server in it, and the desktop and Android apps have one. In a browser, the studio can only edit and export.

You rename a monster on the Monsters page, then open a map and press ⌘Z. What is undone?

  • The monster's rename
  • The last change to that map
  • Nothing, because undo is off in the map editor

Inside the map editor, undo applies to the map. Go back to a page to undo changes to the module.

You switch the studio to the Aero style and export your module. What does a player of the exported game see?

  • The game as before: the style is the studio's look on your device, not part of the module
  • The game's HUD and panels in Aero
  • A question asking which style to use

The style is kept on the device and never written into the module. The game's own look is set in Screens, Panels and Style (the rail's 🎨 section).
