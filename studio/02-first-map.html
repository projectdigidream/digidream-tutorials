Your first map

A map is a set of rooms drawn from above. You tap their corners, the studio joins rooms that touch, and every room and wall has a form for its heights, light, textures and flags. By the end of this page you will have two joined rooms and know your way round the map editor.

The Maps section

Open 🗺 Maps from the rail, or tap the map's name at the top of the map editor. Each map has a row with a thumbnail, its name, and a count of its sectors, things and triggers. The map you are editing is marked ▶.

  • Tap a row to open that map in the map editor.
  • + new map asks for a name (letters, digits, dashes) and makes a map with a single room.
  • ✕ deletes a map after asking. A module always keeps at least one map. Undo on the Maps page brings it back, and the studio takes a snapshot before it deletes.

Every map goes into the pack when you export. ▶ Play starts the map you are editing.

The metamap

Under the list is ▦ metamap. Turn on put the maps on a grid to say where each map sits. Tap a map, then a cell, to place it. Tapping an empty cell with nothing picked makes a new map there.

  • Without a pitch, the grid only says which maps are near which, so the next map is in memory before a player reaches it.
  • With one shared world (walk from map to map), the maps are one world. Each one is drawn in its own cell, and two maps join where both have an open side along the same line. You open a side in the wall form (see The wall form).

You do not need the metamap for a first map. Leave it off until you have two maps that should join.

The map editor has three parts: the viewport where you draw, the tool bar, and a sheet that shows the form of whatever you selected.

  • ⟲ and ⟳: rotate the view. Q and E rotate it by 45°.

On a keyboard, the arrow keys pan and + and - zoom.

Drawing a room

Choose ▭ Room. The status line says "tap corners, tap the first one again to close -- or drag a rectangle". There are two ways to draw:

  • Corner by corner. Tap each corner. Tap the first corner again to close the room, or press Enter. A room needs at least three corners.
  • A rectangle. Press and drag. When you let go, the rectangle is a room.

Press Esc to throw away the corners you have tapped so far. Corners snap to the grid, and to the corners and walls of rooms already there, so rooms meet cleanly. When a room closes, it is selected and its form opens.

How rooms fit together:

  • Rooms that touch share a wall. You can walk across it, unless you force it to a wall.
  • A room inside a room is a platform when its floor is higher, and a pit when its floor is lower.

Try it here. This is a stand-in for the studio's grid, not the studio itself.

  1. In the demo above, tap four or five corners, then tap the first one again. The room fills.

  2. Tap three corners and press Enter (click the grid first so it has the keys). A triangle closes.

  3. Start another room, then press Esc. The corners go away.

  4. Drag across an empty part of the grid. A rectangle room appears when you let go.

Reshaping

With ⌖ Select:

  • Drag a corner to move it.
  • Drag a selected room to move the whole room.
  • Long-press a wall to add a corner in its middle. The new corner is selected, ready to drag.
  • Long-press a corner to remove it. A room keeps at least three corners.
  • Delete or Backspace deletes what is selected.

A selected corner has its own small form: x, y, remove corner and select sector.

The room form

Tap a room to select it. Its form is titled Sector and its id. If something is wrong with the room, that is said first, at the top of the form.

FieldWhat it sets
floor, ceilingHeights, from −32 to 64 in steps of 0.25. The ceiling always stays above the floor: drag one through the other and the other moves.
lightThe room's brightness, 0 to 255.
thicknessHow thick a platform's slab is drawn, 0 to 16.

ceiling open to the skyReplaces the ceiling with a window onto the map's sky. Turning it off brings back the texture it replaced.
all middles, all uppers, all lowersSet one texture on every wall of the room at once.
edgesWhat the room's edges are (see below).
tagA number that triggers and scripts use to name rooms.
duplicateA copy of the room, shifted by 2, with the same heights, light, textures and walls.

Upper and lower are the bands a wall shows only where another room is behind it: above that room's ceiling, and below its floor.

Edges

The edges menu has three choices:

  • room: walls (by floor height inside another): the usual room. Inside another room it becomes a platform or a pit, by its floor height.
  • floating platform: open edges, fall off: no walls; you can walk off the edge.
  • cut into the floor of the room around it.

The floor and ceiling patterns

The form has a ▦ floor pattern and a ▦ ceiling pattern section, with the same fields each:

  • turn (°): turns the texture, 0 to 360 in steps of 5.
  • shift x, shift y: slide it, in world units.
  • size: how many times larger the tile is drawn.
  • painted in: the world (patterns line up across rooms) or the sector (the pattern rides the room when it moves, for a lift or a door).
  • see-through: makes the surface transparent. When it is on, opacity appears. 254 shows the picture's own holes, for a grate or a fence. Lower values are glass. A see-through surface is still solid to walk on.

Below the patterns is 🌦 weather and fog, where you add rain, snow, dust, embers or fog to the room. That is covered in Sky, screens and weather.

The wall form

Tap a wall to select it. The form says whose side it is. A wall between two rooms has two sides, one for each room. Tap the wall again, or press select the other side (sector …), to get the other room's side.

  • middle, upper, lower: the wall's three textures.
  • force wall (a shared wall only): makes it solid, even though a room is behind it.
  • open: the room ends here (to the next map) (an outer wall only): there is no wall. On the metamap, this is where a neighbouring map joins, if its side is open too. Elsewhere it is a drop off the edge of the world.
  • flags: chips you turn on and off (below).
  • see-through and opacity, as for floors.
  • x offset, y offset: slide the texture on the wall.
  • unpeg upper, unpeg lower: anchor the upper or lower texture from the other end, as DOOM's unpegged flags do.
  • split wall: adds a corner in the middle, so a switch or a door can sit on half a wall.
  • select sector: goes to the room this side belongs to.

Exercise: two joined rooms

  1. On 🗺 Maps, press + new map and call it first. The map editor opens with one room.

  2. Choose ▭ Room and drag a rectangle that touches one side of the first room. They now share a wall.

  3. In the new room's form, raise floor to 1 and set light to about 160.

  4. Choose ⌖ Select, then long-press the outer wall of the new room. A corner appears in its middle. Drag it outwards.

  5. Tap the shared wall. Read which room's side it is, then press select the other side.

  6. Turn on force wall, then undo with ⌘Z.

  7. Press V to see the game view, and Q / E to turn round it. Press V again to go back to the plan.

Practise the keys

You have tapped three corners with the Room tool. How do you close the room?

  • Double-tap the last corner
  • Tap the first corner again, or press Enter
  • Press Esc

Tapping the first corner again or pressing Enter closes it. Esc throws the corners away.

You want an extra corner in the middle of a wall. What do you do?

  • Long-press the corner next to it
  • Tap the wall twice
  • Long-press the wall with Select (or press split wall in its form)

Long-pressing a wall adds a corner; long-pressing a corner removes it. Tapping a wall twice selects its other side.

You draw a small room inside a big one and give it a lower floor. What is it?

  • A pit
  • A platform
  • An error: rooms cannot overlap

With edges set to the usual room, a room inside a room is a platform when its floor is higher and a pit when it is lower.
