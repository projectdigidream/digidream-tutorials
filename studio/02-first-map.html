Your first map

A map is a set of rooms drawn from above. You tap their corners, the studio joins rooms that touch, and every room and wall has a form for its heights, light, textures and flags. By the end of this page you will have two joined rooms and know your way round the map editor.

The Maps section

Open Maps from the rail (the first section, under World), or tap the map's name at the top of the map editor. (On Home, a map's thumbnail opens it straight away.) Each map has a row with a thumbnail, its name, and a count of its sectors, things and triggers. The map you are editing is marked with a pencil before its name, and its count ends · editing.

  • Tap a row to open that map in the map editor.
  • ▶ on a row plays the module from that map (play from map), whichever map you are editing.
  • + new map asks for a name (letters, digits, dashes) and makes a map with a single room.
  • ✕ deletes a map after asking. A module always keeps at least one map. Undo on the Maps page brings it back, and the studio takes a snapshot before it deletes.

Every map goes into the pack when you export. Play in the map editor and on the rail starts the map you are editing, the one with the pencil. When you open a module again, the studio opens the map you edited last, so the rail's Play plays it. Play on Home starts the module's starting map, which you set in Settings (see Play and export).

The metamap

Under the list is metamap. Turn on put the maps on a grid to say where each map sits. Tap a map, then a cell, to place it. Tapping an empty cell with nothing picked makes a new map there.

  • Without a pitch, the grid only says which maps are near which, so the next map is in memory before a player reaches it.
  • With one shared world (walk from map to map), the maps are one world. Each one is drawn in its own cell, and two maps join where both have an open side along the same line. You open a side in the wall form (see The wall form).

In one shared world, a map that has a cell shows that cell's limits in the map editor as a thick, bright band, as thick at every zoom. Where the band runs is where this map ends and its neighbour begins, so you can see at a glance how much room is left before a side.

You do not need the metamap for a first map. Leave it off until you have two maps that should join.

The map editor

The map editor has three parts: the viewport where you draw, the tool bar, and a sheet that shows the form of whatever you selected.

Note that 5 is Graph and 6 is Camera, the other way round from the bar's order. A key pressed with ⌘, Ctrl or Alt is never taken as a tool's key: ⌘V pastes, it does not switch the view.

At the top, beside the status line, are the view buttons:

  • ↶ and ↷: undo and redo for this map.
  • ⟲ and ⟳: rotate the view. Q and E rotate it by 45°.
  • PLAN: switches between the flat plan view and the game view. The button then reads GAME. The key is V.
  • studio 0.4.2.11 FX, on a map that has fog, rain, snow, dust or embers: hides them in the map view while you build, and shows them again. The key is X. It is struck through while they are hidden, and the game draws them all the same. See Effect areas, water and fills.
  • On a phone held upright, ‹ takes you back to the module.

The studio decides what a gesture is when it starts. A drag that started as a tool drag stays one if a second finger lands. A pan stays a pan if one finger lifts early. So a wall never ends up drawn where you meant to move the view.

On a keyboard, the arrow keys pan and + and - zoom.

Corners are drawn twice as big on a touch screen, and a tap within a finger's width of one selects it. The corners and labels grow and shrink with the zoom, within bounds: zoomed in, start no longer covers its room, and zoomed out, the corners stay in sight. studio 0.4.2.1 A small room is easy to tap too: a tap well inside a small platform or a thin door picks the room, and its walls and corners win only near its edge.

Drawing a room

Choose Room. The status line says "tap corners, tap the first one again to close -- or drag a rectangle". There are two ways to draw:

  • Corner by corner. Tap each corner. Tap the first corner again to close the room, or press Enter. A room needs at least three corners.
  • A rectangle. Press and drag. When you let go, the rectangle is a room.

From the moment you choose Room, every room's corners and walls are drawn, so you can see what a new corner will snap to. Undo (⌘Z) while drawing takes back the last corner you tapped, one at a time. Press Esc to throw away all the corners you have tapped so far.

Corners snap to the grid, and to the corners and walls of rooms already there, so rooms meet cleanly. Snapping reaches half a unit at most, however far out you zoom, so a corner meant to leave a gap is not pulled onto a wall metres away. To put corners off the grid, switch it off (see Snap to the grid, or not).

When a room closes, it is selected. Its form opens only if a form was already open, so drawing rectangle after rectangle is not covered up; otherwise the status line says what was drawn, such as "Room s12 drawn. Tap it for its heights and textures.". Tap it to open its form.

The textures a new room takes

A new room is drawn with the textures and heights you used last. A room drawn inside another room starts as that room is instead: the same floor, ceiling, light and textures, so a platform drawn in a courtyard is not born with the last pit's floor or a ceiling under no sky. The status line then says "… drawn inside s4, as high as it". When there are no textures yet, so that your first room is never an empty outline, the studio picks some:

  1. the module's own textures: one whose name has FLOOR (or FLAT, GROUND) for the floor and ceiling, and one whose name has WALL for the walls;
  2. if the module has none, the textures that come with the app;
  3. if there are none of those either, it adds two plain grey tiles with a faint grid to the module, PLAIN_FLOOR and PLAIN_WALL. The status line says so, and you can change them on Textures.

A texture you picked yourself is never replaced.

Name your textures with FLOOR and WALL in them, such as STONE_FLOOR and BRICK_WALL, and new rooms start in them.

How rooms fit together:

  • Rooms that touch share a wall. You can walk across it, unless you force it to a wall. A wall that becomes an opening this way shows no middle texture, on either side, so the doorway is not bricked up in the game.
  • studio 0.4.2.11 A room drawn over other rooms keeps their wall textures. The walls it turns into openings put their textures aside, and take them back once they are solid walls again: when the room is deleted, say, or made an effect area.
  • A room inside a room is a platform when its floor is higher, and a pit when its floor is lower.
  • studio 0.4.2.2 A platform can reach across rooms. A room drawn over two or more rooms, with its floor above all of them, is a platform standing on them, with open edges, not a walled box. A room across a border whose floor is level with, or below, a room it reaches over is still refused, and the studio says why.

Try it here. This is a stand-in for the studio's grid, not the studio itself.

  1. In the demo above, tap four or five corners, then tap the first one again. The room fills.

  2. Tap three corners and press Enter (click the grid first so it has the keys). A triangle closes.

  3. Start another room, then press Esc. The corners go away.

  4. Drag across an empty part of the grid. A rectangle room appears when you let go.

Reshaping

With Select:

  • Drag a corner to move it.
  • Drag a selected room to move the whole room.
  • Long-press a wall to add a corner in its middle. The new corner is selected, ready to drag.
  • Long-press a corner to remove it. A room keeps at least three corners.
  • Delete or Backspace deletes what is selected. Deleting a room also takes the triggers that worked it (a door's, say), and the status line names them; undo brings them all back (see Triggers).

A selected corner has its own small form: x, y, remove corner, select sector, and a button for each of the two walls that meet at it, wall to corner id. On a phone, a wall beside a corner is hard to land on; from the corner it is one tap away.

Several rooms at once studio 0.4.2.8

With Select, you can take several rooms and work on them together:

  • Shift+click a room to add it to the selection, or take it out. On a phone, hold a room instead, or switch on select more rooms in a room's panel (the map's More has Select several rooms (tap to add) too): every tap then adds a room or takes it out, until you switch it off or press Esc.
  • Shift+drag on the floor draws a box, and takes the rooms in it.
  • ⌘A (Ctrl+A) takes every room of the map.

Dragging one of them moves them all, with the rooms inside them and the monsters, props and lights standing in them. The panel, titled with how many rooms there are (5 rooms), has:

  • copy, duplicate and, once something is copied, paste. The keys are ⌘C, ⌘D and ⌘V (Ctrl on Windows and Linux). A paste lands in the middle of the view, ready to drag into place; a duplicate sits a little beside the rooms it copies.
  • turn left and turn right (a quarter turn), and mirror.
  • raise 0.25, lower 0.25, raise 1, lower 1, and raise all by for any amount you type (a negative one lowers them).
  • floor texture, ceiling texture and light: one for all of them. A texture button says (mixed) while the rooms differ.
  • add more by tapping, select none, and delete n rooms.

Each room is still edited on its own panel: tap it without Shift.

A copy is kept on the device, so it pastes on another map too: open that map, and its More menu has Paste n rooms. A copy carries no tag and no triggers, and the map's start spawn stays where it is.

  1. In your copy of Gem Hollow, open world and zoom in on the Isles' lake. With Select, Shift+click two islands. (On a phone, hold one, then the other.) The panel says 2 rooms.

  2. Press raise 1, then V to see both islands stand a unit higher in the game view. Press V again, then undo.

  3. Press ⌘C. The status line says what was copied. Open Maps, open menu, and choose More → Paste 2 rooms. Undo it again.

The room form

Tap a room to select it. Its form is titled Sector and its id. If something is wrong with the room, that is said first, at the top of the form.

FieldWhat it sets
floor, ceilingHeights, from −32 to 64 in steps of 0.25 (with the grid off, a 256th of a unit). The ceiling always stays above the floor: drag one through the other and the other moves.
lightThe room's brightness, 0 to 255.
thicknessHow thick a platform's slab is drawn, 0 to 16, in the same steps as the heights.
floor tex, ceiling texTap to pick a texture. (none) clears it.
ceiling open to the skyReplaces the ceiling with a window onto the map's sky. Turning it off brings back the texture it replaced.
all middles, all uppers, all lowersSet one texture on every wall of the room at once. Each button shows the texture its walls have, or (mixed) when they differ. all middles textures the room's solid walls only: an opening into another room keeps no middle, so it stays open.
edgesWhat the room's edges are (see below).
tagA number that triggers and scripts use to name rooms.
duplicateA copy of the room, shifted by 2, with the same heights, light, textures and walls.
copy, select more roomsCopy the room to paste elsewhere, or start adding rooms to the selection by tapping (see Several rooms at once).
deleteRemoves the room, with the triggers that worked it. The status line names them, and undo brings them all back.

Upper and lower are the bands a wall shows only where another room is behind it: above that room's ceiling, and below its floor.

Type a slider's number. The number at the end of every slider is a field too: tap it, type 2.5 and press Enter (or tap elsewhere). The value lands on the slider's own steps and range. A decimal comma counts as a point, so 2,5 works as well. On a phone, a swipe up or down over a slider scrolls the form instead of moving the slider.

Edges

The edges menu has these choices:

  • room: walls (by floor height inside another): the usual room. Inside another room it becomes a platform or a pit, by its floor height.
  • floating platform: open edges, fall off: no walls; you can walk off the edge.
  • cut into the floor of the room around it.
  • studio 0.4.2.9 effect area: only its effects (rain, snow, fog...), laid over anything, nothing drawn: not a room at all, but a volume for weather and fog that you can lay over other rooms. See Effect areas, water and fills.

The floor and ceiling patterns

The form has a floor pattern and a ceiling pattern section, with the same fields each:

  • turn (°): turns the texture, 0 to 360 in steps of 5.
  • shift x, shift y: slide it, in world units.
  • size: how many times larger the tile is drawn.
  • painted in: the world (patterns line up across rooms) or the sector (the pattern rides the room when it moves, for a lift or a door).
  • see-through: makes the surface transparent. When it is on, opacity appears. 254 shows the picture's own holes, for a grate or a fence. Lower values are glass. A see-through surface is still solid to walk on.

Below the patterns is weather and fog, where you add rain, snow, dust, embers or fog to the room. That is covered in Sky, screens and weather.

The wall form

Tap a wall to select it. The form says whose side it is. A wall between two rooms has two sides, one for each room. Tap the wall again, or press select the other side (sector …), to get the other room's side.

  • middle, upper, lower: the wall's three textures.
  • force wall (a shared wall only): makes it solid, even though a room is behind it.
  • open: the room ends here (to the next map) (an outer wall only): there is no wall. On the metamap, this is where a neighbouring map joins, if its side is open too. Elsewhere it is a drop off the edge of the world.
  • flags: chips you turn on and off (below).
  • see-through and opacity, as for floors.
  • x offset, y offset: slide the texture on the wall.
  • turn (°): turns the texture on the wall, 0 to 360 in steps of 5, as a floor's turns. Use it for diagonal planks, or a picture on its side.
  • size along and size up: how many times larger the texture is drawn along the wall and up it, each from 0.125 to 8. 2 draws bricks twice as big; 0.5 makes them half size. Set them apart to stretch a picture one way only, such as planks twice as long but no taller. size up follows size along until you set it. The turn and the sizes apply to all three of the side's textures (middle, upper and lower).
  • unpeg upper, unpeg lower: anchor the upper or lower texture from the other end, as DOOM's unpegged flags do.
  • split wall: adds a corner in the middle, so a switch or a door can sit on half a wall.
  • select sector: goes to the room this side belongs to.

Snap to the grid, or not studio 0.4.2.1

Corners, rooms, things and points snap to the map's grid as you place them. To put them exactly where you tap, switch the grid off: More → Snap to grid: on (turn off), or press G. The status line says "grid off: things go exactly where you put them (a corner still joins a corner or wall it lands on)". Press G again to snap once more.

  • With the grid off, a corner goes where you put it, to a 256th of a unit, and stays there when you edit the room again. It still joins another room's corner or wall that it lands on, so rooms meet cleanly.
  • studio 0.4.2.8 A room's floor, ceiling and thickness, and a thing's height, take a 256th of a unit too, so a typed 1.1 stays 1.1 (with the grid on, it is kept to a quarter).
  • The choice is this device's, remembered for every map. It changes nothing in a map you made before.

How big a map can be next release

A map can have up to 16384 rooms, 65535 corners and 65535 sides, and use up to 1024 textures: the engine's own limits. (The studio used to stop a map at 512 rooms, 4096 corners and sides, and 256 textures, before Play or Export.)

In Gem Hollow: the world map

Open Gem Hollow (Home → examples → Gem Hollow), then its world map. It has 81 rooms. The whole level does not fit on screen at first: use More → Fit view, then pan with the right button or two fingers.

  • Four zones, left to right: the grassy Meadow, the Isles in their lake, the stone Cliffs with their ring terraces, and the Keep in its lava. Short corridors join them, and each holds a gate.
  • The gates are rooms whose ceiling is down on their floor, like a closed door: GATERED with tag 11, GATEBLUE with tag 12, GATEGOLD with tag 13, and the vault door (VAULTDOOR) with tag 14. A graph opens each one by its tag (see Graphs).
  • Water and lava are rooms too, tagged 900 (water) and 901 (lava), with their floor below the ground around them. The Isles' lake is a room with edges set to a room; the small pools in the Meadow are cut into the floor of the room around it.
  • The rafts and spinning platforms are rooms with edges set to floating platform, tagged 31–33 and 41–43, so a graph can move them.
  • Every outdoor room has ceiling open to the sky on, so the hills of the module's sky show above.

  1. In world, choose Select and tap the Isles' lake. Its form says floor −3, WATER, tag 900.

  2. Tap the red gate between the Meadow and the Isles (zoom in on the corridor). Its floor and ceiling are both 0, and its tag is 11.

  3. Press V to see the zones in the game view, and Q / E to turn round them.

In Cinderfall: districts joined without a load

Cinderfall is under examples on Home. If yours doesn't show it, update the studio.

Gem Hollow puts its whole level in one map. Cinderfall splits its world into nine maps, the ones named w_…, and joins them with the metamap:

  • put the maps on a grid and one shared world (walk from map to map) are both on. The cells are 112 × 112, and kept around a player is 3 cells each way, so every district stays in memory.
  • The hub, w_hub, is in the middle cell. Old Town is west of it, the Railhead east, the Mine and the Foundry north (two maps each), and the Heart south (two maps).
  • Neighbouring maps join where both have an open side on the same line. The player walks across, with no load and no change_map.
  • Open w_hub in the map editor: its cell's limits are the thick, bright band round it, and the neighbours are drawn beyond the band, with their names on them, such as w_railhead to the east.

Its other three maps, menu, n_arrival and n_epilogue, are not on the grid. Graphs move the player into and out of them with change_map.

Exercise: two joined rooms

  1. In your copy of Gem Hollow (or any module), on Maps, press + new map and call it first. The map editor opens with one room.

  2. Choose Room and drag a rectangle that touches one side of the first room. They now share a wall.

  3. The status line says the room was drawn. Choose Select and tap the new room to open its form. Tap the number at the end of floor, type 1 and press Enter, then drag light to about 160.

  4. Choose Select, then long-press the outer wall of the new room. A corner appears in its middle. Drag it outwards.

  5. Tap the shared wall. Read which room's side it is, then press select the other side.

  6. Turn on force wall, then undo with ⌘Z.

  7. Press V to see the game view, and Q / E to turn round it. Press V again to go back to the plan.

Practise the keys

You have tapped three corners with the Room tool. How do you close the room?

  • Double-tap the last corner
  • Tap the first corner again, or press Enter
  • Press Esc

Tapping the first corner again or pressing Enter closes it. Esc throws the corners away.

You want an extra corner in the middle of a wall. What do you do?

  • Long-press the corner next to it
  • Tap the wall twice
  • Long-press the wall with Select (or press split wall in its form)

Long-pressing a wall adds a corner; long-pressing a corner removes it. Tapping a wall twice selects its other side.

You draw a small room inside a big one and give it a lower floor. What is it?

  • A pit
  • A platform
  • An error: rooms cannot overlap

With edges set to the usual room, a room inside a room is a platform when its floor is higher and a pit when it is lower.

You want a ledge's corner a little off the grid's lines, and its floor at 1.1. What do you do?

  • Zoom in further: the grid gets finer
  • Switch the grid off (More, or G), then place the corner and type 1.1
  • Nothing: corners and heights always snap to quarters

With the grid off, corners go where you put them and heights take a 256th of a unit. A corner still joins another room's corner or wall it lands on.

You copy three rooms on one map with ⌘C. How do you put them on another map?

  • You can't: a copy only pastes on the map it came from
  • Export the map and import it again
  • Open the other map and press ⌘V, or More → Paste 3 rooms

The copy is kept on the device, so it pastes on any map, in the middle of the view. It carries no tag and no triggers.
