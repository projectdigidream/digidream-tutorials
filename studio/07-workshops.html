Monsters, weapons, player

The workshops are where the things that move are made: monsters, the weapons the player holds, and the player. Monsters and weapons are graphs of states you draw. The player is a set of forms. Controls, at the end, gives the module keys of its own.

You'll learnhow a monster's brain, swing, slash and body fit together, how a weapon's phases work (and where its first-person look is set), how to set the player up for every map or one map, and how to add a key Time30 minutes Needsa module with a sprite sheet for the monster (Sprites track), and a map to play-test in

This page is an overview. To build monsters and weapons in depth, follow the Enemies and Weapons tracks. Every word the engine accepts is listed in Every monster word and Every weapon word.

They belong to the module

Monsters, weapons, props, particles and the player belong to the module, never to one map. Every map of the module sees the same imp and the same shotgun. The window says so in its bar: "monsters belong to the module, not to one map". A map holds only its geometry, what is placed in it, its triggers, its camera, its cues and its paths.

The three windows share one layout:

  • A bar with ‹ back and the names of the monsters or weapons with + new monster or + new weapon. Only the Player has a button across, to 🔫 weapons; Monsters and Weapons are reached from the rail.
  • A problem count in the runtime's own words, or "loads clean".
  • Three panes: 🎞 preview, ⬡ graph (not for the player) and ☰ details. On a wide screen you see them side by side; on a phone, these tabs switch between them.

Monsters

A monster's brain is a state machine. Each state says how the monster moves and faces, which animation it plays, when it goes to another state (its transitions), and what it does at given ticks while it is in it (its sequence). The game runs at 60 ticks a second.

+ new monster asks for a name and gives the monster a working brain to start from. It has five states:

StateWhat it does
look ★stands still; goes to chase when it has a target it can see
chaseruns at the target; goes to attack when the target is within 1.5 and its cooldown is ready
attackfaces, claws for 8 at tick 10, starts a 60-tick cooldown, then back to chase
painflinches for 10 ticks
deathstops being solid and stays dead

The ★ marks the initial state. A new monster also gets a body, drawn with the module's first sprite sheet.

The state graph

The graph pane draws each state as a box with its animation, its movement, its ticks and where it goes next, and how many transitions and steps it has. Arrows are transitions. Its bar has:

  • + state, which asks for a name.
  • With a state picked: ★ initial, → link (then tap the state to go to; it adds a transition), and ✕ state.
  • auto layout and delete monster.

Drag a state to move it. The board zooms like a graph's: pinch, the wheel, or −, + and ⤢ (every state in view).

The flinch and the drops

  • 💢 the flinch. With a state called pain, a bigger hit, a longer flinch adds a share of each hit's damage to the pain state's ticks, up to a cap. The tab spells out how long a 10-, 50- and 100-point hit makes it flinch.
  • 🎁 drops. What the monster leaves where it dies. + drop adds an item it always drops. + weighted choice turns a drop into one of several; a choice with no item means "nothing", which is how you make a drop a chance.

The swing

A swing is a collider that sweeps through an arc over several ticks, so a player who steps aside is missed. The preview plays it on a loop at the game's tick rate: from above for its reach and arc, from the side for its pitch, with the part already covered and the cooldown between swings. A monster's claw and the player's fist use the same swing, edited the same way.

The slash

In the game every melee swing is drawn as a slash: a streak near the tip of the swept arc that fades. Its look has its own 💨 slash tab in Monsters, Weapons and Player, under 🌀 how it looks in the game: show, colour, opacity, fade, width, blend and a texture (a picture under sprites/).

  • Player → 💨 slash sets the default every swing starts from (and other players' swings use as it is). ↺ back to the engine's look clears it.
  • A monster's or weapon's 💨 slash sets only what differs from the default. Each field shows the look the swing really has, and marks what is its own; ↺ back to the default look clears it.

The studio writes these into assets/sprites/swings.json, only when something is set. The Weapons track covers it in depth.

  1. Open ☠ Monsters and press + new monster. Call it grunt.

  2. On 👤 body, choose its sprite sheet and check each state 0–4 plays the right animation. The preview's state chips play each one.

  3. Tap the attack state. On ⬡ attack, find the melee step under ▶ at tick … do … and raise its damage to 12.

  4. On 🧠 brain, add a drop with + drop.

  5. Place a grunt on a map (Placing things) and press ▶ Play to meet it.

Weapons

A weapon is a phase machine: the same graph as a monster's brain, with phases in place of states. Automatic fire, a charge, a combo and an alt-fire are shapes you draw rather than flags you tick. + new weapon starts one with two phases: ready, which goes to fire when the button is pressed, and fire, which shoots a 10-damage hitscan and returns to ready after 12 ticks.

TabWhat it holds
⬡The picked phase: its fields, transitions and sequence, as for a state. A phase can carry its own swing, replacing the weapon's while it is in that phase. That is what makes a combo's third blow different from its first.
🔫 weaponThe weapon's own fields (label, order, which ammunition pool it uses …), 🔊 heard at once by the one firing (+ predicted sound: played by the player's own client at once, rather than a round trip later), and 🎒 what its own pickup grants.
🌀 swingThe weapon's swing, for every phase that has none of its own.
💨 slashHow the weapon's swing looks in the game, over the default (see The slash).
🎞 first personHow the weapon is drawn over the view when the module is played in first person: pictures, frame by frame (at rest and firing, each shown for some tics at 35 a second, with an optional muzzle flash, and where each picture sits on a 320 × 200 screen), or a sprite (a ThreeSprite project: an animation at rest and one firing, and one per animation state its phases set). A preview plays it, with ▶ fire. The Weapons track covers it in depth.
# varsThe weapon's own counters.
🎒 arsenalShared by every weapon: 🔢 ammunition pools (+ ammunition pool), ❤ health ceilings, and 📦 pickups (+ pickup).

The weapons run once for the whole game, not per level, so a player keeps what they hold from map to map.

Player

The player has no graph, because nothing about a player is a state machine. The window is a preview and six tabs: 🧍 skin, 📏 size, 🏃 moves, 🎒 kit, 🔊 sounds and 💨 slash (the default look of every swing; see The slash).

Long lists cope. A list of more than 12 chips (the props that can draw the player, the sheets for a body, the things to place) gets a find field, such as "find among 31…" on Gem Hollow's drawn by, and scrolls in a box of its own. The picked chip and the + … ones always stay in view.

Every map, or only this map

The player is set up once, for every map. A map that needs it different (a giant's level, a low-gravity one) overrides only the parts it names. The size, moves and kit tabs open with a switch:

  • 🌐 every map edits the module's player.
  • 🗺 only map edits the open map's override. Each part follows the module until you switch it to its own, and says what it follows.

An override is part of the map, so it undoes with the map.

TabWhat it setsPer map?
🧍 skinWhich of the module's props draws the player (drawn by), how big it is drawn (drawn at (scale)), a cross-fade (s) between animations, and which state each animation condition plays. The drawn scale changes nothing the game simulates.no
📏 sizeThe body the player collides as: radius and height. It is part of each map's content, so changing it re-bakes every map that does not have its own.yes: own size on map
🏃 movesEvery movement parameter, grouped: moving and turning, jumping, climbing, dashing, hovering and flight, the grapple, the bare-handed swing. Switch one on to set it; an unset one says what it is anyway (the engine's, or what first-person controls set).yes: name — own here
🎒 kit❤ health (max, bonus max), 🔢 ammunition carried at the start, and 🔫 weapons in hand. With choose them off, the player starts with the lowest-order weapon.no: a player arrives holding what they held in the last map
🔊 sounds🏃 moving (jumping, landing hard), 💥 hurt (hurt, dying) and 🎒 taking a pickup, each picked from the module's sound packs with ▶ to hear it. (none) is silence.no

The moves are written into module.txt as player lines, and the server builds each arriving player from them. A number is written as an exact decimal, so what you type is exactly what the server reads.

  1. Open 🧍 Player. On 🧍 skin, choose a prop under drawn by.

  2. On 🏃 moves, with 🌐 every map, switch on a jumping parameter and raise it a little.

  3. Switch to 🗺 only map. Turn on the same parameter — own here and give this map a different value. The other maps keep the module's.

  4. Press ▶ Play and jump.

In Gem Hollow

  • Monsters. slime (20 health), bee (10) and snail (40). Each brain starts in look, wandering, and has chase, attack, pain, gone and death. The slime adds squash: when the target is above it (a player landing on it), it goes flat and is gone, the classic platformer stomp.
  • Weapons. fists, labelled Bonk: ready → swing (18 ticks) on the button. startoss, the Star Toss: ready → throw when the button is pressed and there is ammunition, which consumes a star, launches the missile star_shot and switches away when empty. On 🎒 arsenal: one pool, stars (up to 30), and three pickups, heart (heals 25), star_pouch (gives the Star Toss and 12 stars) and star_ammo (5 stars).
  • Player. Drawn by the hero prop with a 0.08 s cross-fade, and with the jump, fall and run conditions mapped to the hero's own animations. Its rules are four player lines in module.txt, set on 🏃 moves with 🌐 every map: 
    player move_speed 8
player jump_impulse 9.5
player max_jumps 2
player max_dashes 1
    so the hero runs at 8, double-jumps and has one dash. On 🎒 kit it starts with fists only; the Star Toss is found in the Isles.
  • Controls. platformer — third person, with no keys of its own.

  1. In your copy of Gem Hollow, open ☠ Monsters and pick slime. Tap the squash state and read its transitions and sequence on ⬡ squash.

  2. Open 💨 slash and change the colour. Then open 🧍 Player → 💨 slash: the default is unchanged, and the slime's field is marked as its own.

  3. Open 🔫 Weapons, pick startoss, and read the throw phase's steps.

  4. On 🧍 Player → 🏃 moves, find max_jumps (2) and set it to 3. If you are in the app, press ▶ Play and triple-jump.

Controls

🎮 Controls is the control mapper. At the top, how it is played chooses platformer — third person or first person (fps). Below it are the module's own keys: actions such as reload, map or inventory, which graphs answer by name.

  • + key adds a key called action and waits for you to press one: the button says press a key… (Esc).
  • Each key is one line: its number, its name, ⌨ key (tap it and press a key), a list to choose the key from where there is no keyboard, and a 👆 touch label of up to 12 characters, with no spaces. An empty touch label means no touch button.
  • ↑ moves a key up, and ✕ deletes it. The order matters: key i is bit i of what scripts see.
  • At most 8 keys. The section head counts them: ⌨ the module's own keys (3 of 8).
  • When a key has a touch label, place the touch buttons… opens Screens, where you place it beside the engine's buttons.

Below the keys, 🎮 the engine's buttons lists the buttons every module has and their keys, for reference: jump on Space, dash on Shift, use on E, attack on F or the left mouse button, and so on.

Warnings and errors

Controls warns, under the key's line, about:

  • a key on one the engine already uses (E is use; Q and R turn the camera; W A S D move);
  • two keys on the same key;
  • a name one of the engine's buttons already has (a graph naming it would mean the engine's).

What the server would refuse is an error, here and on Home.

Renaming a key renames it in every graph that answers it. Graphs answer keys with when_button_pressed, when_button_released and button_held: see Graphs.

Players cannot rebind keys yet. The module's list is the key map.

In Cinderfall: a first-person player and two keys

Cinderfall is under ◆ examples on Home. If yours doesn't show it, update the studio.

Gem Hollow is a third-person platformer with no keys of its own. Cinderfall shows the other side:

  • Controls. how it is played is first person (fps). Two keys of its own: arms on Tab, with the touch button ARMS, and swap on X, with SWAP. They are written as key arms Tab touch ARMS and key swap KeyX touch SWAP in module.txt.
  • Who answers them. The game graph arms: when_button_pressed on arms opens the weapon wheel (the arms panel), or closes it; on swap it selects the weapon held before.
  • Player. Drawn by the warden prop. On 🏃 moves: move_speed 10, jump_impulse 7, max_jumps 1 and max_dashes 0, so one jump and no dash. On 🎒 kit it starts with the wrench only.
  • Weapons. Five: wrench, revolver, scatter, riveter and flare. In first person, each is drawn by its own sprite (VM_WRENCH, VM_REVOLVER…) with an animation per phase, such as the revolver's cock and reload (see Phases in first person).
  • Monsters. Six: husk, gunner, hound, spitter (it flies), brute, and the boss smelter (2400 health, with a brood state below half health).

You want a low-gravity moon level, with the player's jump higher only there. Where do you set it?

  • In 🧠 brain of a monster called player
  • In 🏃 moves with 🌐 every map
  • In 🏃 moves with 🗺 only moon, switched to its own

A map overrides only the parts it switches to its own, and every other map keeps the module's values.

Why is there no starting kit per map?

  • The weapons run once for the whole game, so a player arrives holding what they held in the last map
  • Kits are set in each map's Map settings instead
  • Only the first map of a module may have weapons

The arsenal is a game script, run once for the game. A map has nothing to hand out on arrival.

You add a ninth key in Controls. What happens?

  • The first key is dropped to make room
  • It is an error: a game has 8 keys of its own at most, and the server ignores the rest
  • It works on keyboard only, with no touch button

Each key is one bit of the input frame, and there are 8. Controls says so in red, and so does Home.
