Monsters, weapons, player

Monsters and weapons are made in their editors: what they do reads as sentences, or as a graph, beside a preview that runs the real rules. The missiles they fire have a page of their own. The player is a window of forms. Controls, at the end, gives the module keys of its own.

You'll learnhow the monster and weapon editors are laid out, how a state reads and is edited, what the preview shows, how phases make a boss, where a monster's looks and body are set, a weapon's aim assist, what the Missiles page is for, how to set the player up for every map or one map, and how to add a key Time30 minutes Needsa module with a sprite sheet for the monster (Sprites track), and a map to play-test in

This page is an overview. To build monsters and weapons in depth, follow the Enemies and Weapons tracks. Every word the engine accepts is listed in Every monster word and Every weapon word.

They belong to the module

Monsters, weapons, missiles, props, particles and the player belong to the module, never to one map. Every map of the module sees the same imp and the same shotgun. The editors say so: "Monsters belong to the module; every map can place them." A map holds only its geometry, what is placed in it, its triggers, its camera, its cues and its paths.

The monster and weapon editors

Open Monsters or Weapons, in the sidebar's Gameplay group. The editor opens on the first one, and its name, at the top left, opens the list of them all: each monster with its health and how many states it has, each weapon with its slot.

New, from a recipe

In the list of monsters (or weapons), New monster has a name field, its name, e.g. spitter, and a button per recipe. Type a name, then press a recipe: the new one is made from it and opened. Names are lower-cased as you type, with spaces turned into _. Copy of slime starts from the one you were editing instead.

RecipeWhat it starts as
Blankstands there; can be hurt and dies
Melee gruntwalks up and hits
Shooterkeeps its distance, warns, fires a missile
Flyerhovers, keeps away, fires from range
Chargerwinds up where you can see it, then rushes you
Boss (3 phases)three phases at 66% and 33%, summons and a leap

Weapons have Blank (drawn, then waits for a rule), Pistol (one instant shot each press), Blade (a slash each press, a heavier one if pressed again during it), Launcher (one heavy shot and a kick) and Charge shot (hold fire 1 s to release; let go early and nothing happens). A new monster is drawn with the module's first sprite sheet, its states matched to the sheet's animations by name. A recipe that fires a missile uses the module's first missile, or makes one called bolt, drawn as a glowing dot the studio paints. A weapon from a recipe is held in first person as a placeholder (a gun, or a blade for Blade), at rest and firing, until it has pictures of its own (see The placeholder gun).

The ⋯ button at the top right has Rename, Duplicate and Delete. A rename may have capitals and spaces: the name is kept for show, and the files use it lower-cased, as the editor tells you (the files call it …). A monster's body, and every brain and missile that names it, follow the rename.

Behaviour: states as sentences

A monster's brain, or a weapon, is a set of states. Each one says what it does while it is in it and when it goes to another. The game runs at 60 ticks a second; the editor talks in seconds. On Behaviour, with List, each state is a card that reads as a sentence:

  • The first line: what it plays, how it moves and where it faces, and how long it lasts: plays walk, runs at the target and faces the target, until a rule below sends it on, or …for 0.4 s then goes on to chase. A star marks where it begins.
  • Its rules, in the order they are checked: → attack when the target is within 1.4 m and cooldown ready is over. Every condition of a rule must hold. A rule with no state to go to, or no condition yet, says it is incomplete and never fires.
  • Its steps, as chips with their time: 0.17 s · melee hit for 10 damage within 1.2 m.

The cards are grouped: Flow, where it begins and what it does, and Reactions, the states entered from any state when something happens to it (pain, death, and any state that goes back to what it was doing). A weapon's are one group, States, beginning with being drawn. A state nothing goes to says nothing goes here yet. Tap any part of a card, or its edit, to open the state beside the list.

+ state asks for its name (New state, named) and places it after the state picked. Then give some other state a rule that goes to it.

Editing a state

The state picked opens in the side panel, under the preview, with its name (type to rename it: every rule that went to it follows), make it where it begins, duplicate and delete. The buttons does, goes to, at the end and steps jump to its four parts:

PartWhat it holds
What it doesPlays (an animation of its sprite), Moves, Faces, and Lasts in seconds (empty: it stays until a rule sends it on). With a length, Then goes to: a state, (stays), or for a monster back to what it was doing (resumed), which returns to the state it interrupted and carries on where it was, without running its steps again. Then Phase (when it has phases), Follows "whatever it is doing", and for a monster Pain can cut it short and Blocks. A weapon's state has Shows picture in place of Plays, Moves and Faces.
Goes toits rules. Each one has where it goes, its conditions, + add a condition, arrows to check it earlier or later, and remove. + another rule adds one.
When its time is up (optional branches)for a state with a length: where it goes as its time runs out if a condition holds, before its Then goes to. + a branch adds one.
Timelineits steps on a line from 0 s to its length, then listed. Pick one to edit it. + add a step at 0 s adds one (at the moment the preview is at, when it is running in this state). Steps run in the order listed, each at its time: one listed after a later one waits for it.

Adding a condition or a step opens a chooser of everything there is, in groups, one group at a time; it opens on the group you used last. On a computer, a box (find among N…) searches every group. The last group, More (the engine's own words, edited in its own form), holds whatever the editor has no sentence for yet, so nothing the engine can do is out of reach.

A swing is a step like any other attack (see The swing and the slash), so a combo's third blow differs from its first by the numbers in its own swing step. A weapon state no longer has a swing form of its own. A state from an older module that still carries one shows it under Its own swing (the older way), with turn it into a swing step, which moves that shape into a swing step at 0 s.

Whatever it is doing

Above the states, the Whatever it is doing panel holds the rules for any state. They are checked first, before a state's own rules, and the first that holds wins. A monster's two built-in ones are listed there too: when it is hurt (78% of hits) → pain and when its health reaches 0 → death, which the engine does for any state named pain or death. + a rule for any state adds one of yours, such as fleeing when its health is below 30%. Tick only once to make a rule fire a single time: a boss's phase change. A state with Follows "whatever it is doing" unticked ignores these rules.

The preview

The Preview runs the rules by the engine's own timing: a monster decides every 8 ticks, a hit's pain resumes what it interrupted, a press is a press. It names the state it is in, outlines the card or rule that would fire now, and says why in a line such as Now in chase: no rule holds, so it stays. Under it are the numbers it remembers and its cooldowns, such as cooldown ready: 0.77 s left.

  • A monster's preview: Run (or Pause), Hit, Flinch, Kill and Reset, and the target: in sight or hidden, nearer, further, and its health. What it summons stands where it landed and counts as the monsters near it; other monsters and props are only in a level.
  • A weapon's preview shows it held, in first person: Hold to fire, on the ground or in the air, and Reset.

On a phone, the preview stays closed until you ask for it, and a step opens in a sheet over the side panel.

The graph, and phases

Graph draws the same states as boxes with arrows, each arrow labelled with what it waits for (≤3 m · slam_cd, 0.67 s). It pans with one finger or the mouse, zooms with a pinch or the wheel, and has −, + and fit. Tap a box or a label to open that state.

A phase is a group of a monster's states, such as a boss's second, angrier half. In the graph each phase is a lane of its own. In the list each phase has a header: its name, how many states it has and what starts it, how fast it runs at, duplicate and remove. With two phases or more, only the phase of the state you are editing is unfolded; the others name their states in a row, and the arrow at a header folds or unfolds it.

+ phase, beside + state, makes the next phase in one go. It copies the last phase's states (chase becomes chase_2), makes it 30% faster, and adds a rule for any state that starts it once, when the monster's health drops below 50% (then half the last threshold, for the next). Then make it angrier: change its states, its speed or what it hits for. The Boss (3 phases) recipe starts with three.

Looks, and a monster's own body states

On a monster's Looks, pick its sprite, or Import a sprite…. A sprite with a ThreeSprite project has Edit in ThreeSprite; without one, Draw a sprite of its own… starts a new sprite for this monster, so the art other things are drawn with is never replaced. A sprite as it is first drawn has no animations yet, so the monster is drawn as one still picture until it has some, and Looks names the states that play it for want of their own. Play each animation from any side the sprite has (a sprite drawn from one view says side: one picture, the same from every side and offers none), then match them under Each state plays. A state whose animation the sprite does not have is named in a warning, with what the game plays instead: "death: the sprite has no animation called death, so the game plays idle instead. Pick one, or draw it." Sounds lists every sound its steps play; Bring in sounds of your own… goes to Sounds.

Under Its body, Its own light (not lit). The game lights monsters, props and players by the map, as it lights walls and floors: their room's light, and the lamps near them, so a monster in a dark room is dark and one beside a torch takes its colour. Ticked, the monster is drawn at full light instead, like a fireball or a ghost.

Behind the names are numbers: the engine draws a body by state number, 0 to 4 (idle, walk, attack, pain, death) and any above. Advanced → Its body states shows them, with the body's radius, height, sprite height, scale, speed, solid and sprite sheet. A monster's own states have their own numbers too: a leap or a charge that plays its own animation is listed as state 5 and up, named by the states that use it. + another state adds a number. You rarely need it: choosing an animation on Looks adds the number it needs. Its sprite sheet row ends in + import sheet…, which brings in a sheet; a single plain .png comes in as a one-picture sheet, the same from every side, and a file that is not a PNG is refused with a message.

The swing and the slash

A swing is a collider that sweeps through an arc over several ticks, so a player who steps aside is missed. It is an attack like any other: a step of a state, in the Attack group of + add a step, at the moment it says, for monsters and weapons alike. Its numbers are in the step's sentence, swing for 12 damage, 1.5 m long, from -60° to 60° over 0.2 s, and the rest (its pitch, lift, slow, knockback and so on) are under more of it. A newer swing replaces one still sweeping, and one during the cooldown does not happen; the preview shows both, as the engine does.

What a swing step leaves out comes from the default swing: Advanced → Its default swing, for a monster and for a weapon. A weapon swings it with the attack button while none of its states has a swing step. + swing gives a monster or weapon a default swing when it has none. Its preview plays it on a loop at the game's tick rate: from above for its reach and arc, from the side for its pitch. The player's fist is the same swing. The Enemies and Weapons tracks teach swings in depth.

In the game every melee swing is drawn as a slash: a streak near the tip of the swept arc that fades. Its look is under how it looks in the game, beside the default swing: show, colour, opacity, fade, width, blend and a texture (a picture under sprites/).

  • Player → slash sets the default every swing starts from (and other players' swings use as it is). back to the engine's look clears it.
  • A monster's or weapon's slash sets only what differs from the default. Each field shows the look the swing really has, and marks what is its own; back to the default look clears it.

The studio writes these into assets/sprites/swings.json, only when something is set.

  1. Open Monsters, tap the monster's name at the top left, type grunt and press Melee grunt.

  2. On Looks, choose its sprite, and under Each state plays check each state plays the right animation.

  3. On Behaviour, press Run in the preview. Press nearer until it attacks, and Flinch to see it flinch.

  4. Tap its attacking state. Under Timeline, pick the melee step and raise its damage to 12.

  5. Place a grunt on a map (Placing things) and press Play to meet it.

Weapons

A weapon is the same machine as a monster's brain: in the files its states are phases, in the editor they are states. Automatic fire, a charge, a combo and an alt-fire are shapes you draw rather than flags you tick. Its rules wait for the fire button (pressed, held, let go), for ammunition, for a cooldown. Its Arsenal tab is shared by every weapon: the ammunition pools (+ ammunition pool), health ceilings and pickups. Three buttons add a pickup, each starting as what it says: health pickup (heals 25), ammunition pickup (10 of the first pool, offered once there is a pool) and armour pickup (100 armour). Each asks for the pickup's name first. On Advanced, The weapon has its own fields (see Aim assist), heard at once by the one firing (+ predicted sound: played by the player's own client at once, rather than a round trip later) and what its own pickup grants.

The weapons run once for the whole game, not per level, so a player keeps what they hold from map to map.

Aim assist

Among The weapon's fields on Advanced:

  • Aim assist: (the default), on or off. (the default) is the game's rule: on for every body but a first-person one. on turns it on even in first person: the shot turns to the nearest body in its cone and climbs or drops to that body's middle, and with the cone empty it goes where the view looks. off turns it off for this weapon: a sniper's rifle, say.
  • Aim assist reach (°, 0 = the game's): the weapon's own cone, in degrees. A reach over 180 is flagged.

The shooter's own device works it out at once, as the server does, so an assisted shot never jumps. The Weapons track covers it in depth.

Missiles

Missiles, in the sidebar's Gameplay group, is the module's missiles, shared by every monster and weapon. Each one is drawn from the side, flying as the engine flies it to a stand-in target: Fire throws it, target at moves the target, and fired by a player (level, no throw) fires it as a player would. Under the picture it says what would go wrong at that distance, such as a shot that runs out first or a blast that reaches back to the one who fired it, or nothing to worry about at this distance.

  • Flight: Speed, Falls (empty: it flies straight; with a fall, a monster throws it on the arc that lands on its target), Homing, Lasts, Launch height (empty: it leaves the shooter's middle, as the engine launches it), Hit size, Bounces, and At most missiles from one shot.
  • When it hits, When it runs out, When it bounces and On its way: what it does there, as steps, such as a blast, a split into more missiles, or bomblets dropped as it flies. The effects chips burst the engine's particles where it lands.
  • Looks: what it is Drawn as, the states it plays flying and bursting, Its own light (not lit) (drawn at full light, not lit by the room and lamps; every missile drawn with the same prop shares it), and its Trail: one of the module's particle kinds, which follows it from launch until it bursts, at that kind's rate (see Particles). The picture draws the trail along the flight. The Enemies track covers it in depth.
  • Fired by: who fires it, each a link to that monster or weapon, and delete this missile.

To make one, type a name in new missile, named and press New. It gets a prop of its own name in Props, a walk-through glowing dot, so it is seen in the game at once; a missile with nothing to be drawn as is named as a problem before you press Play. The dot is 0.6 m tall, and the missile leaves a glowing trail in its colour: a particle kind of its own, on the Particles page. A missile drawn smaller than half a metre with no trail is flagged under its picture: "It is drawn 0.35 m tall with no trail: from across a room, on a phone, that is a few pixels, and it hits from nowhere. Give it a trail under Looks, or its prop a bigger picture." A step that fires a missile in a monster or a weapon has an edit missile link that comes here.

Player

The player keeps the window of forms the monsters and weapons had before their editors. It has no states to draw, because nothing about a player is a state machine. Its bar has back, weapons (across to the weapon editor), one player for the module; a map may override parts, and loads clean or a count of problems. Below are a preview and seven tabs: skin, size, moves, movement, kit, sounds and slash (the default look of every swing; see The swing and the slash). The movement tab takes the whole width, with no preview. On a narrow screen, preview and details switch between the two halves.

Long lists cope. A list of more than 12 chips (the props that can draw the player, the sheets for a body) gets a find field, such as "find among 32…" on Gem Hollow's drawn by, and scrolls in a box of its own. The picked chip and the + … ones always stay in view.

The skin: the player's own states

skin sets how the player is drawn, with no monster needed:

  • drawn by: any prop of the module, or + a body of its own, a new prop for the player alone (called player), drawn with the module's first sheet. The sheets end in + import sheet…, which takes a single .png as a one-picture sheet. Only the picture changes: the player's size, moves, kit and sounds stay.
  • drawn at (scale): how big the picture is drawn. It changes nothing the game simulates.
  • its states: which animation each plays: the sprite sheet, then one line per state, state 0 · idle to state 4 · death, and any state the conditions below use (Gem Hollow's hero has state 5 · when jump, 6 for fall, 7 for run). + another state adds a number.
  • cross-fade (s) between animations, and which state draws which condition: the engine's list of conditions (jump, fall, run …), each with the state it picks. It has hang, for a ledge, and hop, and each movement state of the module with an animation (see How the player moves).

If the prop that draws the player also draws a monster, the tab says so (a state changed here changes the monster's too), with give the player a body of its own (a copy).

Every map, or only this map

The player is set up once, for every map. A map that needs it different (a giant's level, a low-gravity one) overrides only the parts it names. The size, moves and kit tabs open with a switch:

  • every map edits the module's player.
  • only map edits the open map's override. Each part follows the module until you switch it to its own, and says what it follows.

An override is part of the map, so it undoes with the map.

New on moves, and the movement tab

moves has new settings. Under jumping: coyote_time, how long the ground jump stays after walking off an edge, and variable_jump and jump_release_keep, for a jump as high as JUMP is held. Under climbing: ledge_grab and ledge_reach, to catch the edge of a higher floor a jump just missed. The movement tab adds movement of the module's own: swimming, sliding, gliding, meters and dashes. Both are taught in How the player moves.

  1. Open Player. On skin, choose a prop under drawn by, or press + a body of its own.

  2. Under its states: which animation each plays, choose the sprite sheet and check each state's animation. Tap the states in the preview to play them.

  3. On moves, with every map, switch on a jumping parameter and raise it a little.

  4. Switch to only map. Turn on the same parameter — own here and give this map a different value. The other maps keep the module's.

  5. Press Play and jump.

Controls

Controls is the control mapper. At the top, how it is played chooses platformer — third person or first person (fps). Below it are the module's own keys: actions such as reload, map or inventory, which graphs answer by name.

  • + key adds a key called action and waits for you to press one: the button says press a key… (Esc).
  • Each key is one line: its number, its name, a button showing its key (tap it and press a key), a list to choose the key from where there is no keyboard, and, after a hand icon, a touch label of up to 12 characters, with no spaces. An empty touch label means no touch button.
  • ↑ moves a key up, and ✕ deletes it. The order matters: key i is bit i of what scripts see.
  • At most 8 keys. The section head counts them: ⌨ the module's own keys (3 of 8).
  • When a key has a touch label, place the touch buttons… opens Screens, where you place it beside the engine's buttons.

Below the keys, the engine's buttons lists the buttons every module has and their keys, for reference: jump on Space, dash on Shift, use on E, attack on F or the left mouse button, and so on.

Warnings and errors

Controls warns, under the key's line, about:

  • a key on one the engine already uses (E is use; Q and R turn the camera; W A S D move);
  • two keys on the same key;
  • a name one of the engine's buttons already has (a graph naming it would mean the engine's).

What the server would refuse is an error, here and on Home.

Renaming a key renames it in every graph that answers it. Graphs answer keys with when_button_pressed, when_button_released and button_held: see Graphs.

Players cannot rebind keys yet. The module's list is the key map.

In Cinderfall: a first-person player and two keys

Cinderfall is under examples on Home. If yours doesn't show it, update the studio.

Gem Hollow is a third-person platformer with no keys of its own. Cinderfall shows the other side:

  • Controls. how it is played is first person (fps). Two keys of its own: arms on Tab, with the touch button ARMS, and swap on X, with SWAP. They are written as key arms Tab touch ARMS and key swap KeyX touch SWAP in module.txt.
  • Who answers them. The game graph arms: when_button_pressed on arms opens the weapon wheel (the arms panel), or closes it; on swap it selects the weapon held before.
  • Player. Drawn by the warden prop. On moves: move_speed 10, jump_impulse 7, max_jumps 1 and max_dashes 0, so one jump and no dash. On kit it starts with the wrench only.
  • Weapons. Five: wrench, revolver, scatter, riveter and flare. In first person, each is drawn by its own sprite (VM_WRENCH, VM_REVOLVER…) with an animation per state, such as the revolver's cock and reload, set on the weapon's Looks → Held in first person.
  • Monsters. Six: husk, gunner, hound, spitter (it flies), brute, and the boss smelter (2400 health, with a brood state below half health).

You want your boss to turn faster and angrier once its health drops below half, only the first time. What is the quickest way?

  • Add a rule to each of its states that checks its health
  • Press + phase, then change the new phase's states
  • Make a second monster and swap it in with a graph

+ phase copies the last phase, makes it faster and adds a rule for any state, ticked only once, that starts it when the health drops.

You want a low-gravity moon level, with the player's jump higher only there. Where do you set it?

  • In the monster editor's Advanced tab, on a monster called player
  • In moves with every map
  • In moves with only moon, switched to its own

A map overrides only the parts it switches to its own, and every other map keeps the module's values.

Why is there no starting kit per map?

  • The weapons run once for the whole game, so a player arrives holding what they held in the last map
  • Kits are set in each map's Map settings instead
  • Only the first map of a module may have weapons

The arsenal is a game script, run once for the game. A map has nothing to hand out on arrival.

You add a ninth key in Controls. What happens?

  • The first key is dropped to make room
  • It is an error: a game has 8 keys of its own at most, and the server ignores the rest
  • It works on keyboard only, with no touch button

Each key is one bit of the input frame, and there are 8. Controls says so in red, and so does Home.
