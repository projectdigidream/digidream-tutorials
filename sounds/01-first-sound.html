Your first sound

DigidreamSound is the sound-effect editor. You pick a kind of sound, press until one is close, nudge it, and give it the name the game will ask for. A bank stores recipes, not recordings: every sound can still be changed, and a hundred of them are a couple of hundred kilobytes of JSON.

You'll learnhow to open the editor on its own or from the studio, how its screen is laid out, the difference between Randomize and Vary, and how to name a sound Time15 minutes Needsa checkout of the repository with Node.js (and emsdk, once), or the studio with a module open

Five ideas

Everything in the editor is one of these five things. The rest of the track keeps coming back to them.

WordWhat it is
SoundOne thing the game asks for by name, such as weapon.pistol.fire or monster.imp.death. The list on the left is the sounds.
RecipeHow a sound is made: a kind of sound (gunshot, explosion…) and the positions of its knobs.
TakeA version of a sound. The game picks one take at random each time the sound plays.
LayerA part of a take, such as the crack, the body or the tail. Layers start together and are added together.
GroupA bus: sounds that share a volume, a limit on how many play at once, and effect sends. Every sound plays on one group.

On its own

  1. From the sound/ directory, build the WebAssembly engine once. Do it again only after changing the engine. It needs emsdk.

    ./scripts/build_wasm.sh

  2. Start the editor's server:

    node tool/serve.js

    Then open http://localhost:8940/tool/sfx.html.

  3. Press Start. A browser only lets a page make sound after a click, so the editor waits for one. It then opens the small example bank, so there is always something to play.

The editor needs a secure context. localhost is one. A plain http:// address on your network is not: the page loads but makes no sound. To use another device, serve over HTTPS or use the Android app.

serve.js also takes a port, --host 0.0.0.0 to reach it from another device, and --root DIR to serve a packaged build instead of the source tree. The desktop and Android apps skip Start.

From the studio

DigidreamSound ships with the studio as a plugin. Open the 🔊 Sounds section, then either press + new sound pack or press ✎ edit in DigidreamSound on a pack. The editor fills the screen under a slim bar: ‹ studio goes back and saves, ✕ goes back without saving. Packs are covered in Mix, test and ship.

The screen

The top bar, left to right:

  • The bank's name. Click it to rename the bank. A dot beside it means there are unsaved changes.
  • The two views, Sounds and Mix & test.
  • The play button ▶, with the name of the sound it will play. Space does the same.
  • ↶ and ↷ (undo and redo), Open and Save.
  • ⋯, the menu for everything else: New bank, Open…, Open the example bank, Save, Save as…, Export for the game (.sfxb), Export this sound as WAV and How this works.

The library (left) lists every sound, grouped by the first part of its name. Each row shows a small picture of the sound, its length, a dot if something is wrong, and too loud if it would clip. The search box matches names and tags. ＋ New makes a sound. The library's own ⋯, at its foot, holds Even out the levels of all sounds… and Sort A → Z.

The sound (middle) is the selected sound, top to bottom in the order you work on it:

  • its name and tags;
  • the player: the waveform, one lane per layer with S and M, and the Repeat and Play on change boxes;
  • problems, each with a button for its fix;
  • Takes, with A B ＋;
  • the cards Start from, Shape, Layers and Timing & notes.

In the game (right) is how the game plays the selected sound: its group, how many may play at once, variation, distance, music and playback. See Takes and in-game rules.

The status line at the bottom says what just happened. Click it for the full log.

On a window narrower than 1250 px, In the game becomes a drawer: press In the game › at the top of the sound. On a phone (under 900 px), the library and the sound are two screens, and ‹ Sounds goes back.

Start from a recipe

The Start from card has thirteen kinds of sound. Pressing one replaces the selected take with a new sound of that kind. Every press makes a different one, so keep pressing until one is close.

KindGood for
🔫 Gunshotfirearms: a crack, a body, a tail, a mechanical action
💥 Explosionblasts, rockets, barrels
⚡ Laser / zapenergy weapons, magic, electricity
👊 Hit / punchimpacts, footsteps, thuds
🔔 Metal / bellanything struck that rings
✨ Pickup / blipitems, UI
🎵 Jinglea few notes in a row: power-ups, alerts
🚪 Door / mechanismdoors, switches, lifts
⚙️ Machineworking, buzzing things
🚗 Engine humheld drones, motors, chainsaws
👹 Monsterroars and growls
🗣️ Voice / gruntvowel-like shouts, pain
💨 Whooshswings, air, passing things

Under the kinds are three buttons:

  • 🎲 Randomize (R) makes another sound of the same kind, staying with the recipe you have.
  • ✨ Vary (V) moves every knob a little: the same sound, slightly different. It is greyed out for a take that was not made from a recipe.
  • 🎙 Match a recording… finds a recipe that sounds like a WAV you have. See Match a recording.

Every generated sound is levelled to −6 dB before you hear it, so pressing through kinds never blasts you. The card's subtitle says where the take came from: made from the Explosion recipe, matched to shotgun.wav, or hand-made.

Randomize jumps; Vary nudges. Use Randomize while you are still looking for the right sound. Once one is close, use Vary to search around it without losing it. If you overshoot, ⌘Z brings back the last one: every press is one undo step.

Make one, step by step

  1. In the library, press ＋ New. A sound appears, made from a random kind, and plays. It gets a placeholder name such as new.gunshot.

  2. In Start from, press ⚡ Laser / zap. Press it a few more times and listen to how different each one is.

  3. Press R until one is close to a plasma shot. Then press V three or four times to try its neighbours. Undo (⌘Z) back to the one you liked best.

  4. Click the name at the top of the sound and type weapon.plasma.fire. Press Enter. The sound moves into a WEAPON group in the library.

  5. Press Space to play it, and tick Repeat (L) to hear it again and again. A short sound is easier to judge by repetition. Untick it when you are done; it is only a listening aid.

  6. Press ⌘S to save the bank as a .sfxproj. In the studio, press ‹ studio instead.

Nothing is saved automatically. The .sfxproj is your document. The editor remembers only where you were (the view, the selected sound, the search, which cards were folded). If you reload with unsaved changes, it asks first.

Naming sounds

A sound's name is its whole identity. The game asks for weapon.shotgun.fire; there is no table of numbers anywhere. So the name you type is the name a script, a monster or a weapon has to use, letter for letter.

  • Use dots to go from general to specific: what.which.event. The library groups sounds by the part before the first dot.
  • Name the event, not the sound. world.door.open stays right when you replace its creak with a hiss.
  • Keep names stable. Renaming a sound silences everything that still uses the old name. The studio lists those on Home as a problem.

The example bank (⋯ → Open the example bank) shows the pattern. It has three groups, and each sound picks one:

SoundGroupWhy that group
weapon.pistol.fire, weapon.shotgun.fireplayeryour own gun, at the same level wherever you are
impact.flesh, player.step.stone, world.door.openworldthings that happen somewhere, quieter with distance
pickup.health, ui.selectuifeedback, not part of the world

The world group is placed in the world (its sounds fade with distance, and it sends a little to the room reverb); player and ui are not. A new sound starts on the first group that is placed in the world. Groups are set up in Mix & test.

Tags help you find sounds, not the game. Type one into + tag under the name and press Enter; the library search matches tags as well as names.

In Gem Hollow

Gem Hollow's sounds are one pack, meadow (sources/sounds/meadow.sfxproj): 30 sounds, synthesized for the game, no samples. The names follow the pattern above, and the library groups them by their first part:

PICKUP 4pickup.coin, .gem, .heart, .keyplayer

The pickups play on player, not world: a coin you grab is your own feedback and should be just as loud wherever the coin was. Every sound has a single take (A), and every take is hand-made: the Start from card says so and ✨ Vary is greyed out. To get a recipe back on one, press a kind; that replaces the take.

Several rows say too loud, player.jump among them: its peak is just over the ceiling (the badge reads too loud: +0.6 dB). In the game the group volumes (−5 dB for player and world, −8 dB for ui) bring them back down. Fix level is one click if you want the editor's view and the game's to agree.

  1. Open Gem Hollow, then 🔊 Sounds, and press ✎ edit in DigidreamSound on Meadow (generated) (meadow).

  2. Click coin under PICKUP and press Space. Its Timing & notes card says 2 steps: two notes 75 ms apart, a fourth up, the classic coin chime.

  3. Click jump, press Fix level, and play it again. The badge turns into a plain peak reading.

  4. Press ✕ at the top right to leave without saving. With ‹ studio, the pack is saved and merged into the module's bank again.

Keys

Practise the editor's keys here. ⌘ means Ctrl on Windows and Linux.

You have a laser that is almost right, but the end is slightly too low. Which button keeps the sound and tries close neighbours?

  • 🎲 Randomize
  • ✨ Vary
  • Press ⚡ Laser / zap again

Vary moves every knob a little. Randomize and pressing the kind both make a new sound of that kind, so the one you had is gone (until you undo).

A monster's script asks for monster.imp.sight, but your bank calls the sound imp.sight. What happens in the game?

  • The engine finds the closest name and plays it
  • It plays the first sound in the bank instead
  • Nothing is heard: a name the bank does not have is silence

The name is the whole identity of a sound. A missing name is silence, said once in the client's console, and the studio lists it on Home.

You opened the editor at http://192.168.1.20:8940/tool/sfx.html from a tablet. It loads, but nothing plays. Why?

  • A plain http:// network address is not a secure context, so the page cannot make sound
  • The example bank only plays on desktops
  • You need to export a .sfxb first

localhost and HTTPS are secure contexts; a LAN address over plain HTTP is not. Serve over HTTPS or use the Android app.
