Shape and layers

A recipe gets you close. The Shape card gets you the rest of the way with a few named knobs. When the knobs are not enough, Layers has a control for every setting a take can hold, including rings, which make a sound ring like a struck object.

The player: listen before you change

Under the sound's name is the player. It is worth reading before you touch a knob.

  • The waveform is drawn at the level the sound really plays at. Turn the volume up and it rises toward the orange ceiling lines. Past them it turns red, and a too loud badge appears with a Fix level button. That button sets the volume so the loudest moment is at −6 dB. A very quiet sound offers Make louder instead.
  • The timeline has one coloured lane per layer, on the same time axis. Each lane is rendered on its own, so you can see which part is which and when it comes in: the crack at 0, the body carrying on, a late layer entering at 200 ms. Click a lane's name to jump to that layer.
  • S on a lane plays that layer alone. M plays the sound without it. Hear everything goes back. Listening this way never changes the sound.
  • Length and peak badges, then Repeat (L) and Play on change, which plays the sound after every edit.

Leave Play on change on while you shape. A slider then plays the sound each time you let go of it, so your ears stay in the loop.

Shape: four knobs that work on any sound

KnobRangeWhat it does
Volume−40 to +12 dBthe sound's level
Pitch±24 semitonesup or down; tones and noise filters move together
Length¼× to 4×stretches or squeezes every time in the sound
Brightness−100% to +100%moves every filter darker or brighter

Pitch, Length and Brightness are applied to the take's original, so they never pile up. Set Pitch back to 0 and the sound is exactly what it was, however many times you moved it in between. Volume belongs to the whole sound; the other three belong to the take you are on.

Every slider in the editor works the same way:

  • it plays when you let go (with Play on change on);
  • double-click its name to reset it;
  • click its value to type one. 2.5k works for hertz, and so does a comma, as in 0,5.

The recipe's own knobs

A take made from a recipe has more knobs under the four, with a heading such as Gunshot recipe. They are grouped by the part of the sound they change, and each is labelled with a quality and a real unit (ms, Hz, notes, semitones). The subtitle of the card says how many there are, for example 14 recipe knobs.

Parts you will meetQualities you will meet
Crack, Body, Tail, Breath, Rumble, Thump, Thunk, Click, Slide, Mechanism, Motor, Buzz, Grit, Air, Spin, Tone, Noise, Strike, Voice, Growl, Sweep, Wobble, Ring 1–4, Filter, Timbre, and Main for the whole sound level, length (ms), pitch (Hz or a note), resonance, pitch drop (st), fade in (ms), starts at (ms), amount, grit, hold level, fade out (ms), speed (Hz), bite, pitch sweep (ms), sweep length (ms), brightness (Hz)

So Tail · length is how long the tail lasts, Crack · pitch is where the crack's noise sits, and Sweep · length is how long a laser takes to fall. Hover a knob's name to see the parameter it drives.

Vary moves these recipe knobs, not the four general ones. That is why a varied sound still sounds like the same sound: the shape you set stays put.

Try it on the toy

The toy below has the same four Shape knobs, and nine recipe knobs in two parts, Noise and Tone. Double-click a knob's name to reset it.

  1. In the toy, press Gunshot. Press S on 1 · Noise: that is the crack. Then press S on 2 · Tone: that is the low thump under it. Press Hear everything.

  2. Drag Volume to +12 dB. The waveform passes the dashed ceiling and turns red, and the peak badge says too loud. Press Fix level.

  3. Drag Pitch to −7 st and Length to ×2. It is a bigger gun now. Double-click Pitch to put it back.

  4. Press Vary a few times. The Noise and Tone knobs move a little; the Shape knobs do not.

  5. Now do the same in DigidreamSound on a real gunshot. Use S to find its crack and its tail, then move Tail · length and listen to what changes.

Layers: full control

The Layers card is folded by default. Its subtitle summarises the take, such as 3 layers · 2 rings — full control. Inside, every setting a take can hold has a control.

Editing layers detaches the recipe. A take made from a recipe is rebuilt from its knobs, so the card first says so and offers Edit the layers anyway. Once you edit a layer by hand, the recipe is off for that take: Shape keeps only its four general knobs, and Vary is greyed out. Undo brings the recipe back.

At the top is a small picture of how the parts connect: each layer is its own chain (source, tone, level). They all start together and are added; the sum goes through the rings, then the volume, and out to the game. Then come three settings for the whole take:

  • Ends by itself — on for almost every effect. Turn it off only for a sound the game holds and then releases, like an engine.
  • Choke group — a new sound in the same choke group cuts the others short.
  • Patch name — a label for the recipe itself. The game asks for the sound's name, not this.

Then each layer, up to four. Each has Duplicate and Remove, and ＋ Add a layer is under the last one. A layer is split into sections. The first four are open; the rest are folded one click away, with a summary in their header.

SectionWhat you set there
SourceNoise (White (hiss), Pink (wash), Brown (rumble)), Metal (a cymbal-like cluster), or Tone with its generator and every parameter of it
Level and pitchlevel, pan, starts after (a delay), fixed pitch or follow the notes, detune
Loudness over timea picture of the envelope, then wait, rise, hold at top, fall, stay at, fade out. Folded inside: the shape of each stage, loop, and faster when higher
Tonethe filter: low-pass, band, high-pass, notch, comb (a tuned echo that rings), vowel (with a start and an end vowel); frequency, resonance, steepness, grit before. A second filter, after or beside the first. A filter sweep. Distortion after
Pitch sweephow far off the pitch starts, and the envelope that brings it back
Timbre sweephow far the tone travels from its start shape to its end shape, and when
Stack and ringa stack of detuned copies (voices, spread in pitch, spread in space, start apart); ring or FM against a hidden second sine
Strength, turns and sendsthe strength window the layer answers to, how strength becomes level, take turns (layers in the same group alternate), and the layer's own room, chorus and echo sends
LFOsup to four: shape, speed, delay, restart each play, tempo sync. An LFO does nothing on its own; route it in Modulation
Modulationup to twelve routings: a source (an LFO, an envelope, strength…) to a destination (pitch, filter, level, pan, drive, timbre…) by an amount

A Tone source has a generator: Plain sine, Harmonics (morph), Drawn harmonics, Drawn wave, Drawbars (organ), Pulse, FM (metallic), Driven, Chebyshev or Sync. The drawn ones you edit with the pointer: drag across the bars to set each harmonic, or draw one cycle of the wave. Every drawn shape can morph to a second shape, and the Timbre sweep moves between them.

Think in parts. A good impact is usually a short bright layer (the crack) plus a longer dark layer (the body), and sometimes a late quiet layer (the tail) with starts after set. Use the lanes and S to hear each one while you build it.

Rings: a struck body

Below the layers is Rings (a struck body). Rings make a sound ring like a struck object: metal, glass, a bell. The idea is a split of roles:

  • the layers become the strike — the hammer, the stick, the bullet;
  • the rings are the object that rings after it is hit.

Press ＋ Add a ring to add one, up to eight. Each ring is a row of three sliders:

  • pitch — the frequency the object rings at;
  • ring time — how long it keeps ringing;
  • level — how loud this ring is.

With at least one ring, a dry strike slider appears: how much of the strike itself you hear, unringing. Remove ring deletes a row. Rings belong to the sound, so every take of it strikes the same object.

Real objects ring at pitches that are not neat multiples of each other. That is what makes a bell sound like a bell and not like an organ. Each new ring starts about 1.6 times higher than the last for that reason; leave them uneven.

  1. Make a new sound and press Hit / punch until you get a short, dry knock.

  2. Open Layers and press Edit the layers anyway, then scroll to Rings (a struck body).

  3. Press ＋ Add a ring three times. Play it: the knock now hits something that rings.

  4. Set the first ring's ring time to about 1500 ms and the others shorter. Long low rings and short high ones sound like a big metal plate.

  5. Lower dry strike until the knock is just the start of the ring. Name the sound world.pipe.hit.

In Cinderfall

Cinderfall is a second example, a first-person shooter, and it comes with the studio: on Home, under examples, press Cinderfall. The Cinderfall tour walks round it.

Cinderfall's pack, cinderfall, is 68 sounds for a gritty shooter, every one synthesized, no samples. It shows three recipes worth copying.

A gun is four layers and a body

wpn.scatter.fire, the scattergun, is built the way a real shot is heard:

  1. the crack: white noise through a 2 kHz band, gone in 35 ms;
  2. the blast: pink noise below 1.4 kHz, 600 ms, with a sample-and-hold LFO at 30 Hz chopping its volume, so the tail crackles;
  3. the kick: a pure tone at A1 whose pitch envelope starts 14 semitones up and falls in 60 ms, the thump in your chest;
  4. the boom: brown noise below 300 Hz for 500 ms, chopped by the same kind of LFO.

Two rings, 240 Hz for 180 ms and 760 Hz for 100 ms with dry strike 1.00, add the ring of the barrel. The other guns are the same recipe tuned: wpn.scatter.double rings lower and longer (200 and 700 Hz); wpn.revolver.fire rings higher (380 and 1150 Hz); wpn.riveter.fire has one short ring at 900 Hz, since it fires many times a second.

Monster voices: one recipe, many sizes

Every monster cry, from husk.sight to smelter.death, has the same two lanes: a Tone · Harmonics (morph) through a vowel filter that slides from one vowel to another, ring-modulated at half its pitch for a growl, with a sample-and-hold LFO on the pitch and a sine LFO on the volume; and a band of pink noise for breath. People: one blip each

The six people and the radio do not speak. Each has a voice: one 52 ms blip of a pulse wave through a vowel filter, played with every step of their typed subtitles. What tells them apart is pitch, pulse width and vowel: Pip is high (A4, i → e), Old Mags in the middle (A3, o → a), Brother Carrow lowest (G2, u → o, the narrowest pulse). voice.radio adds a white-noise layer: the voice comes over a radio. See Mix and ship for how the subtitles play them.

  1. Open Cinderfall from Home → examples, open Sounds and press edit in DigidreamSound on Cinderfall (generated) (cinderfall). Click scatter.fire under WPN.

  2. Press S on each lane in turn: crack, blast, kick, boom. Then M on lane 3: without the kick, the shot loses its weight.

  3. Click roar under SMELTER and play it. Then click sight under HUSK: the same recipe, more than an octave higher and under half the length.

  4. Click pip under VOICE and press Space a few times: a little high blip, a different pitch each time. Leave with ✕.

Timing & notes, and the JSON

Timing & notes holds the take's notes. With none, it plays once at its own pitch. Each step is a time, a note, a strength and, for a sound that does not repeat, how long it is held. Use it for parts at different pitches, such as a jingle, or a door's thunk then its creak. For a many-part hit (click, boom, tail), use layers instead: they are one note and cheaper.

Everything, as JSON, at the bottom of Layers, is the take exactly as the file stores it. Copy it to paste a take between sounds or banks, and press Apply JSON to take an edit in.

When something is wrong

Anything the engine or the editor objects to shows above the takes, in words, with a button for each fix. The one to know is a sound that never ends: a layer that holds and is never released. The fixes offered are ends by itself, hold level 0, give it a hold time, or mark it as repeating.

You set Pitch to +5, then to −3, then back to 0. What do you hear?

  • The sound 2 semitones lower than at the start
  • The sound slightly duller, because each change loses a little
  • Exactly the original sound

Pitch, Length and Brightness are applied to the take's original, so they never pile up.

You edited a layer of a recipe take by hand, and now the recipe knobs are gone from Shape. How do you get them back?

  • Press Vary
  • Undo the edit, or pick a kind again in Start from
  • Turn on Ends by itself

A hand edit detaches the take from its recipe. Undo brings the recipe back; a new kind gives a new recipe take.

You want a short tap on a glass. What do the layers and the rings each do?

  • The layers are the tap; the rings are the glass that rings after it
  • The rings are the tap; the layers are the glass
  • Rings replace the layers, so you remove them

The layers become the strike, and the rings are the object. The dry strike slider sets how much of the tap you hear on its own.
