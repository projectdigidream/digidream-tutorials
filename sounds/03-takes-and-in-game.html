Takes and in-game rules

A sound the player hears fifty times a minute must not sound the same fifty times, and must not stack up into a roar. Takes give a sound variety. The In the game panel decides how the game plays it: where, how often, how differently each time, how it fades with distance, and whether the music steps aside for it.

Takes

A take is a version of a sound. The game picks one at random each time the sound plays, so footsteps and gunshots stop sounding repeated. The takes are the letters A B C… beside Takes, under the waveform.

  • ＋ adds a take. For a take made from a recipe, the new one starts as a variation of the current one (as if you pressed ✨ Vary). For a hand-made take, it is a copy.
  • Click a letter to work on that take. Start from, Shape and Layers then edit that take only.
  • With more than one take, the row shows how often the selected take is picked, as picked 50% of the time · weight, followed by its weight.
  • Remove take deletes the selected take.

The weight is a whole number from 1 to 1000. A take with weight 2 is picked twice as often as one with weight 1. Use it to make a rare variant rare: three ordinary footsteps at weight 4 and a scuff at weight 1 means the scuff is one step in thirteen.

Two or three takes are enough for most sounds. Make them differ in the way a real repeat differs — a little in pitch, length, brightness — not in kind. A shotgun whose second take is a laser is two sounds, not two takes.

  1. Select a gunshot (the example bank's weapon.pistol.fire will do). Press ＋ next to Takes. Take B appears and plays: a close neighbour of A.

  2. Press ＋ again for C. Click A, B and C in turn and listen. If one stands out too much, select it and press V until it fits.

  3. Select C and set its weight to 1, and A and B to 3. Check the row: C says picked 14% of the time.

Distances are in world units. One unit is 48 Doom map units, about 1.46 m. The defaults, 3.33 and 25, are Doom's own close and clipping distances, so a converted map sounds the way its author placed it.

The Distance section only applies to a group placed in the world. For any other group it says so: The “player” group is not placed in the world, so distance does not apply.

Four situations

1. The chaingun that roars

A chaingun fires ten times a second, and in a big fight several shooters use the same sound. Without limits, the shots pile on top of each other into a wall of noise, and they use up voices that a rocket's explosion will need a moment later.

  • Set At most, at once to 3 on weapon.chaingun.fire. Three overlapping shots already sound like rapid fire.
  • Choose Cut the oldest. For rapid fire, the newest shot should be heard: the oldest is already fading. Cut the oldest only cuts this sound's own copies, never another sound.
  • Set Minimum gap to about 60 ms. When several shooters fire on the same tick, that is one shot heard and the rest skipped. You cannot hear the difference, and the engine does almost no work for the skipped ones.

Use Skip the new one (the default) for the opposite case: a long sound, such as a monster's death cry, that should finish rather than be cut off by the next one.

2. Footsteps that do not tick

A footstep plays every 300 ms for the whole game. Even with three takes, the ear notices.

  • Under Different every time, set Pitch to about ± 40¢ and Volume to ± 1.5 dB. Each step is now slightly different.
  • Keep pitch jitter small. Up to a few tens of cents sounds natural; whole semitones sound like a different object.

A pitch variation above 600¢ (± 6.0 st) makes the sound live: it is rendered on every play rather than once. Render live then says It will be live anyway and why. Live sounds cost more, so keep big jitter for the few sounds that need it.

3. A door across the map

A door opening next to you should be loud and wide; one in the next room should be quiet and dull; one far away should not be heard at all.

  • Put world.door.open on a group placed in the world (the example bank's world).
  • Keep Full volume to at about 3.3 and raise Silent from to 40 if the door matters to the player.
  • Try Natural under Fade instead of the default Even (a straight-line fade), and compare them in Test in the engine by dragging the listener away from the triggers.
  • Set Muffled far away to about 3 kHz, so a distant door loses its highs.
  • Set Quieter behind to −4 dB. Stereo alone cannot tell front from back; a few dB is enough for the ear to.

4. The boss's roar over the music

The boss roars when it sees you. That moment should cut through the soundtrack.

  • Set Lower music to −6 dB. For appears, set to 600 ms; set it to the length of the roar, about 1.2 s.
  • Raise Importance to high (above 170), so a full group refuses something less important instead.

Duck sparingly. The engine honours at most four lowering requests at once and takes the deepest. A bank where everything lowers the music is a bank where nothing stands out.

Try it

  1. Make a sound named weapon.chaingun.fire from 🔫 Gunshot, short and bright. Put it in the player group.

  2. Set At most, at once to 3, Then to Cut the oldest, and Minimum gap to 60 ms.

  3. Set Different every time → Pitch to ± 30¢ and Volume to ± 1 dB.

  4. Add a second take with ＋. You will hear the result under load in Test in the engine.

Sounds that repeat

An engine hum, an alarm or a humming generator plays until the game stops it. Turn on Repeats until stopped. A repeating sound is always live, because a loop has no finite render.

The game has to stop a repeating sound. Use one only where a script, a monster or a sector will stop it. For one-shot effects, keep it off, and keep Ends by itself on in Layers.

Eight zombies fire the same pistol on the same tick. Which setting turns that into one shot heard and seven skipped, without touching the voice pool?

  • Importance
  • Minimum gap
  • Lower music

Triggers closer together than the minimum gap are skipped before they reach the pool.

Take A has weight 3 and take B has weight 1. How often is B picked?

  • Half the time
  • One time in three
  • One time in four

The chance is a take's weight over the total: 1 / (3 + 1) = 25%.

You set Muffled far away on a sound in the ui group, and nothing changes. Why?

  • The ui group is not placed in the world, so distance does not apply
  • Muffled far away needs Render live
  • It only works on takes made from a recipe

Distance settings only act on a group placed in the world. Move the sound to such a group, or place the group in the world in Mix & test.
