Mix, test and ship

A sound on its own is half the job. This page covers the other half: the settings the whole bank shares, a stress test in the real engine, matching a recording, and getting the sounds into a module where scripts, monsters and weapons can call them by name.

You'll learnwhat Mix & test holds, how to flood the engine and read its refusals, how Match a recording works, how sound packs merge in the studio, and how to play a sound from Lua Time25 minutes Needsa bank with a few sounds, from the earlier pages, and a module open in the studio for the last part

Mix & test

Press Mix & test in the top bar. The library, the sound and In the game give way to four cards for the whole bank: Groups, Effects, Master and Test in the engine. Press Sounds to go back.

Groups

One card per group (a bus). Each card has the group's name and how many sounds use it, then:

  • Volume — the group's level, −40 to +12 dB;
  • At most, at once — how many sounds of this group may play together, 1 to 48;
  • Placed in the world — on, its sounds get quieter with distance and use the Distance settings; off, they play at the same level wherever the listener is;
  • Room, Chorus, Echo — how much the group sends to each effect.

Rename a group by editing its name, and its sounds follow. Remove group is greyed out while sounds still use it: move them first. ＋ Add a group adds one, up to eight.

A group's limit and a sound's limit both apply. The example bank's ui group allows 4 at once, so a menu that plays six clicks in a burst will lose two however the clicks are set up.

Effects

  • Room (reverb): size, darkness, width, level, gap before, cut lows.
  • Chorus: speed, depth, level. It thickens and blurs; good on engines and magic.
  • Echo (delay): time, repeats, level, ping-pong, and Echo time as a beat if you want it in note values.

An effect is heard only when its level is above zero and a group sends into it. If you hear no reverb, check both.

Master

A mastering chain on everything: EQ (three bands), Compressor, Colour and width (saturation, stereo width, output gain), and a look-ahead Limiter. It is off by default, and off means the output is exactly the unmastered engine. Turn it on with Mastering on.

Test in the engine

Everything else in the editor shows what a sound is. This card shows what a bank does: limits, gaps, group budgets, cutting and importance only exist once sounds compete in real time. It runs the real engine with the whole bank, as the game runs it.

  • Engine: Start the engine, then Reload bank after edits and Stop everything to silence it.
  • Trigger: pick a sound, then Play once, or Hold to flood to fire it again and again at the rate you set (per second, 20 by default). random sound each trigger picks a different sound each time; from random positions scatters the triggers around the listener.
  • World: a view from above. Drag in it to move the listener. Each dot is a trigger, where it fired, fading as it ages; the wedge is the way the listener faces.
  • Load: instances, synth voices and peaks, with how many were played and cut.
  • Refusals: every sound that was not played, counted by reason, each with what to change.
RefusalMeansChange
cooldownthe trigger came within the Minimum gaplower Minimum gap
instance capthe sound's own At most, at once was fullraise it, or choose Cut the oldest

Refusing politely is the design. The alternative is stealing a sound the player was listening to, or running out of voices mid-explosion. Refusals are not faults. The number that would be a fault is a dropped command, shown in red under Load.

  1. Press Mix & test, open Test in the engine and press Start the engine. Wait for it to say Engine running.

  2. Pick the chaingun from the previous page, set the rate to 40, and hold Hold to flood for a few seconds.

  3. Read Refusals. Most will be cooldown: the 60 ms gap is doing its job. Go back to Sounds, set the gap to 0, return, press Reload bank and flood again. Now they are instance cap, and the sound is denser.

  4. Tick from random positions, choose a sound in the world group, flood it, and drag the listener away in the World view. The sound fades and dulls with distance.

Match a recording

Give the editor a recording and it finds a recipe that sounds like it. Press 🎙 Match a recording… in Start from. A sheet opens with three steps:

  1. The recording. Drop a WAV, or click to choose one. Press ▶ Play it. Drag across the waveform to match one part — a real sound is often a crack and then a tail, and each matches better on its own — or press Use all of it. Then Next ›.
  2. What it is. Pick the kind, or Not sure (it tries every recipe briefly, then refines the best). Choose an Effort: Quick, Good or Best, with an estimate of the time. Then Find a match ›.
  3. The match. Progress, then ▶ Recording and ▶ Match side by side, and how close it got: very close, close, rough or loose. Use it for take A writes it into the selected take; ‹ Try another kind goes back.

The result is a recipe, so its knobs are in Shape straight away, and Vary works on it. The sound remembers which file it was matched to; the card says matched to shotgun.wav.

The recording itself is never saved. Only numbers reach the bank. Use your own recordings, or ones you are allowed to use.

A match in the browser takes about a minute. For many references, the command-line fitter is several times faster:

./build/dsound_fit --list
./build/dsound_fit reference.wav --arch gunshot --out patch.json --wav compare.wav

Advanced in step 2 sets how much the match cares about the spectrum, the loudness over time and the balance between octaves. Leave it unless you know why; a zero switches one off.

Anything that loads a .sfxb also loads a .sfxproj. The project is slower to load, because every wavetable is rebuilt from its recipe, but it means nothing has to be baked to be heard. This game uses that: a module's sounds are the .sfxproj itself, and the studio hides the .sfxb export. You need the .sfxb only for another game that embeds the engine.

The same jobs are available from the command line, built in sound/build/:

./build/dsound_bake bank.sfxproj bank.sfxb            # bake
./build/dsound_render bank.sfxproj script.sfxscript out.wav
./build/dsound_score reference.wav before.sfxproj after.sfxproj weapon.shotgun.fire

Sound packs in the studio

In the studio, a module's sounds come in packs. A pack is a DigidreamSound project (.sfxproj). A module can hold several — DOOM's monsters, a UI set, your own — and the studio merges them into the one bank the game plays.

Open 🔊 Sounds from the rail or Home. The page has:

  • + new sound pack — asks for a name, then opens DigidreamSound on the new pack;
  • + import sound packs (.sfxproj) — takes one or more project files. Each is checked by the sound engine's own loader first, so a file that parses but would not play is refused;
  • the packs, highest first, each with ✎ edit in DigidreamSound, ↑ and ↓ to change its priority, ✕ to remove it, an in the game switch to leave it out without removing it, and how many sounds it has (and how many of them a pack above already gives);
  • every sound, grouped by pack, with who uses it and a ▶ to hear it.

Each pack is kept as the file it was imported as, in sources/sounds/<id>.sfxproj. When the module is written, the packs are merged in order into assets/sounds/bank.sfxproj, the bank the game loads:

  • sounds are put together; a name a higher pack already gave is dropped, never renamed. The page reports it: In more than one pack -- the higher one's is kept: …;
  • groups are joined by name; the same name with different settings keeps the first and says so;
  • the room reverb is the first pack's that has one.

Priority is how you override. To replace DOOM's pistol without touching DOOM's pack, make your own pack with a sound named weapon.pistol.fire and move it above with ↑.

A sound that a monster or weapon names but no pack has is silent in the game. The Sounds page says Named but in no pack (silent in the game) with who uses it, and Home lists it under what is wrong.

Calling sounds from the game

There are no module.txt lines for sounds. A sound is addressed by its name, from three places.

From a script

dnp.sound("world.door.open", sector:center())        -- at a place
dnp.sound("monster.imp.sight", e)                     -- from a thing, following it
dnp.sound("player.death", e, { follow = false })      -- from a thing, staying put
dnp.sound("weapon.pistol.fire", e, { predicted = true })  -- e's client already played it

Everyone within earshot hears it (64 world units).

From a monster

In a monster's sequence, the action is sound. from says where it is heard from: "self" (the default, following the monster), "target", or "impact" for a missile's burst. It is from, not at, because in a sequence at is when.

sequence = {
  { at = 0, action = "sound", name = "monster.imp.sight", from = "self" },
}
-- in a missile's on_hit:
{ action = "sound", name = "missile.fireball.burst", from = "impact" }

From a weapon

In a weapon phase's sequence, the sound comes from the player and moves with them. Mark the shot predicted: the player's own client already played it the moment they pressed fire (from the weapon's predict entry), so it drops the server's copy instead of hearing the shot twice. Everyone else hears it.

sequence = {
  { at = 0, action = "sound", name = "weapon.shotgun.fire", predicted = true },
}

A sound may be dropped — by distance, by a limit, by the network. Never make it the only sign of anything. A door that opens should also move; a hit should also flash.

  1. In the studio, open 🔊 Sounds and press + new sound pack. Call it mine.

  2. In DigidreamSound, make world.door.open from 🚪 Door / mechanism, on the world group. Press ← studio.

  3. If the module has another pack with the same name, move mine to the top with ↑ and check the report says yours is kept.

  4. Press ▶ beside world.door.open to hear it from the merged bank. Then play the module and open a door.

File formats

FileWhat it is
.sfxprojthe authoring document: JSON, readable and diff-friendly. A bank's buses (groups), fx, master and sounds; each sound's in-game settings and its variants (takes). Defaults are not written. Full reference: sound/docs/SFX_FORMAT.md
.sfxbthe baked bank: sealed binary with a SHA-256, made by dsound_bake. Tags are stripped
sources/sounds/<id>.sfxproja pack in a module, as imported
assets/sounds/bank.sfxprojthe module's merged bank, which the game loads

A bank never holds audio samples, only recipes. A matched sound keeps only the name of the file it was matched to.

You flood a sound and Refusals says 38 cooldown, 2 instance cap. What is mostly stopping it?

  • The group's limit
  • The sound's Minimum gap
  • A more important sound

Cooldown refusals come from the Minimum gap. The group's limit shows as bus budget, and importance as outranked.

Two packs both have ui.select. Which one plays?

  • The one in the higher pack; the other is dropped and reported
  • Both, mixed together
  • The lower pack's, renamed to ui.select-2

The merge keeps a name from the highest pack that gives it, and never renames: a sound is its name.

A weapon's phase plays weapon.shotgun.fire, and the shooter hears every shot twice, a moment apart. What is missing?

  • from = "self" on the sound action
  • Repeats until stopped
  • predicted = true on the sound action

The shooter's client already played the shot from the weapon's predict entry. predicted = true makes it drop the server's copy.
