Markers: health bars, signs, pointers

A marker is a small piece of UI hung on something in the world: a health bar over a monster, a sign over a lever, a pointer over the next goal. It follows what it hangs on, and the game hides it behind walls. You design their looks in the studio's Screens, place signs on a map, give a monster a bar over it, or hang them from a graph or from Lua.

You'll learnwhat a marker hangs on and when it is hidden, the four built-in templates and the values they show, designing your own on Screens → markers, signs placed on a map, hanging, changing and removing markers from a graph (a pointer over Gem Hollow's red gate, a bar that counts the vault's keys), the same from Lua, a monster's health bar, the debug overlay's health counters, what is not saved, and the limits Time35 minutes NeedsTalking to the world and Variables, numbers and saving; the app for Play (Play, export, snapshots)

What a marker is

A marker is drawn from the HUD's own elements (text, numbers, bars), but it sits over a point in the level instead of a corner of the screen. Each frame the game puts it back over that point, so it moves when the point moves. Nobody can click it. For something a player answers, use a panel (Talking to the world).

A marker hangs on one of two things:

  • A thing: a monster, a prop or a player. It sits on top of the body as the game draws it, and follows it. It goes when the thing goes.
  • A place: a point in the level, x, y and z in the map's units.

height raises either one, in units: above the top of the body, or above the point. The marker's look comes from a template, a small box whose bottom-centre sits on the point.

When it is not drawn

A marker is hidden when …Unless
a wall of the map stands between it and the camera (a body in the way does not hide it)through_walls is on
it is further from the viewer than its rangeit has no range: then it shows at any distance
it was hung for one player (to), and you are someone elseit was hung for everyone, the default
it is a debug marker and the debug overlay is not showingthe overlay is on (see below)
what it hangs on is gone, or not drawn on this player's screen

The level keeps its markers and sends them to every player who arrives, the way it keeps a room's light. A player who joins late sees the same signs.

The four built-in templates

Every module has four templates built in. A template reads its values by name, so each marker carries the values its template shows. You can change the built-in ones for your module, or make your own, on Screens → markers.

Move the slider to see how the game draws them. The white dot is the point each one hangs from.

A marker that names a template the module does not have is not drawn. The studio catches it first: Home lists it under to fix (N), as in slime: its bar over it is the marker "boss", which the module's screens do not have, so it is never drawn. Make it on Screens → markers, or pick another.

Your own templates: Screens → markers

In ▣ Screens, the markers tab designs what markers look like. Its frame is a close-up: a monster standing on a floor with the marker over its head, drawn by the game's own marker code, at the size the device chosen in the bar gives it.

  • The list shows every marker the module has. Each is built in, replaces the built-in or the module's, and says how often it is used (used 2×).
  • A built-in one can't be changed where it is. ✎ change it for this module makes the module's own copy under the same name, so everything that hangs it gets the new look. ⧉ make a new one from it copies it under a new name. back to the built-in undoes a change.
  • + new marker asks for a name (a-z, 0-9 and _) and starts with a line of text, {text}.
  • Its box: name, width and height, in UI units like the HUD's. The box's bottom-centre sits on the point it hangs from. Renaming it renames it everywhere it is hung: in monsters, graphs and signs. Your own Lua scripts are yours to change.
  • Its elements are the HUD's: + element… adds a text, a number, a bar, an image or a group. Tap one in the close-up or the list to edit it, and drag it inside the box to move it. A text shows a value with {text} or {value}; a bar is bound to value with max as its max.
  • sample values fill value, max, text and any other value the template reads, so you can see a nearly empty bar without playing.
  • used by lists the monsters, graphs, signs and scripts that hang it.
  • delete marker takes one of your own away. If something still hangs it, the studio asks first.

The tabs 🌐 every map and 🗺 only map work as they do for the HUD: a map with screens of its own has its own markers, and a sign or graph on that map is checked against them.

Signs on a map

A sign is a marker hung at a place, there from the moment the level loads. In the map editor, choose ✦ Place. The Place palette has a sign for every marker but health and debug_health, which show numbers rather than text: sign: label, sign: marker and your own. Tap one, then tap the floor. On the map, the sign shows its text.

FieldWhat it does

The level's script hangs every sign as the level loads, and again when a saved game loads. A sign needs no graph, and everyone sees it. Use a graph when the marker should appear, change or go during play.

From a graph

Three nodes handle markers. In + node… they are in the other group, as mark, set marker and remove marker. Type mark to find all three. For a sign that is always there, you don't need a graph: place it on the map.

NodeWhat it does
markHangs a marker on a thing or at a place. Hanging one with the same name on the same thing again replaces it.
set_markerChanges what a marker shows: its text, value or max. Values you leave empty keep what they showed.
remove_markerTakes it down.

The mark node's fields

FieldWhat it says
markerIts name in this graph. set_marker and remove_marker find it by this name, and their own marker field offers the names the graphs' marks gave.
templateIts look. The field offers the module's markers: the built-in four and your own.
at?entity (the default) or place. On an entity, the entity pin says which: unlinked, it is the event's.
📍 where, x?, y?, z?The place, for at: place. pick a point on the map fills all three from one tap on the floor. Each can be linked instead.
text?What a label or a pointer's caption says. Typed: it has no pin.
value?, max?What a bar or debug_health shows. Type a whole number, or link one. A linked number with a fraction is rounded down.
height?Units above the top of the body, or above the place.
range?Shown only within this many units of the viewer. Empty: at any distance.
to?everyone (the default), or player: only the event's player sees it (or the player linked into entity). A quest marker of their own.
through_walls?Drawn even with a wall in the way.
debug?Drawn only while the debug overlay shows.
  • A marker is known by its name and by what it hangs on. One mark node after when_sector_entered (who: monsters) hangs a bar over every monster that walks in, each its own. A set_marker with that name changes the one on the entity it is given (the event's, or the linked one).
  • A place has one marker per name. When set_marker or remove_marker finds no marker of that name on its entity, it takes the place one. So a remove_marker run by a player walking in still finds a sign hung at a place.
  • The name belongs to the graph. A set_marker in another graph does not find it. Keep the nodes that hang, change and remove a marker in one graph.
  • A label's text can't be linked. A graph can't write a number into a sentence. To show a count, use a bar's value, or debug_health's value/max.

Worked example: a pointer over the red gate

In Gem Hollow, pulling the red lever opens the red gate (tag 11) and flies the camera over it. Its levers graph, on the world map, can also leave a pointer over the gate, so the player knows where to go, and take it down once they get there:

when_prop_used (lever_red) → once → … → camera_shake
    → mark   marker: isles   template: marker   at: place   📍 where: just past the gate
             text: The Isles   height: 2   through_walls ✓

when_sector_entered (tag 11, players) → remove_marker   marker: isles

The once means the pointer is hung once. through_walls makes it a beacon, seen from across the map. A pointer without it shows only while nothing blocks the view. The second chain runs when a player steps into the gate's room (tag 11). The player has no marker called isles, so remove_marker takes down the place one.

Worked example: a bar that counts the vault's keys

The vault graph counts keys in its variable keys. Give the vault door a bar that fills as keys are found:

when_level_starts → mark   marker: vault   template: health   at: place   📍 where: in front of the vault door
                           value ← get_var keys   max: 3   height: 2   range: 30

when_prop_touched (key) → remove → add_var keys → set_hud keys
    → set_marker   marker: vault   value ← get_var keys
    → play_sound → branch …   (true) → … → open_door (tag 14) → play_sound
    → remove_marker   marker: vault
  • when_level_starts hangs the bar as the level starts. Its value comes from get_var keys rather than a typed 0. A game loaded from a save has its keys back by then, so the bar starts where the player left it.
  • The set_marker goes between set_hud and play_sound. Link the same get_var into its value: it reads the count after add_var. The key run's entity is the player, who has no vault marker, so it changes the place one.
  • The bar is empty at 0 of 3, amber at 1 and green at 2. When the door opens, remove_marker takes it away.
  • range: 30 keeps it from showing across the whole map.

From Lua

A script has the same markers as four functions: dnp.mark, dnp.mark_set, dnp.unmark and dnp.debug_overlay. dnp.mark gives back the marker's id, which you keep to change it later:

-- a sign over the exit, for everyone
dnp.mark(vec3.new(fx.int(30), fx.int(12), fx.int(2)), 'label', { text = 'EXIT' })
-- a bar over the boss that follows its health
local bar = dnp.mark(boss, 'health', { value = 500, max = 500 }, { height = fx.frac(1, 2) })
-- ... when it is hurt:
dnp.mark_set(bar, { value = mobs.health(boss) })
  • Its options are the graph's: to (a player entity), height, range, through_walls and debug.
  • Numbers must be whole. Lua refuses an fx, so send v.int.
  • dnp.mark_set answers false once the marker is gone, and false as a value removes that value.

The Advanced track sets markers beside panels and HUD values.

A monster's health bar

A monster can carry a bar over its head all its life, with no graph at all. Open ☠ Monsters, choose the monster, and on Overview, under Toughness, set A bar over it. It offers none, health (the built-in bar), the other markers, and the module's own.

The game hangs it over the monster when it spawns, keeps its value (the monster's health) and max (its full health) up to date with every hit, and takes it down when the monster dies. A corpse has no bar. A revived monster gets its bar back.

The editor's preview (on Behaviour) draws the bar too, with the game's own code. Press Hit, or drag its health, and watch it go amber, then red.

Advanced has the same setting under its file name, health_bar?, in Its brain: blood, pain, drops and the rest.

Markers need engine 0.2.1 and studio 0.4.0. If your studio has no markers tab in Screens, get the latest one (Get the tools).

For one boss with a big bar at the top of the screen, a HUD bar is still the better fit: see Cinderfall's boss bar. A marker suits many monsters at once, each with its own small bar.

The debug overlay's health counters

With the debug overlay on, every monster carries a health counter (debug_health, such as 34/60) just above it. You don't have to set anything up. It's the quickest way to see whether a hit landed and how hard.

The overlay is a module setting. In Settings, show the debug overlay in the game decides what the module ships. when I press Play overrides it for your own play-tests on this device: always show it, never show it, or as the module says.

Your own markers can be the same kind: tick debug? on a mark (or pass debug = true in Lua). Such a marker is drawn only while the overlay is showing. When the module ships with the overlay off, it is not even sent: a released game pays nothing for it, and its players never see it. You can leave debug markers in a graph.

What lasts, and what doesn't

  • Markers are not saved. A saved game comes back without them. Hang yours from when_level_starts, which runs again as a saved game loads, so they come back too. Signs placed on a map are hung again by the level, and monsters get their health bars back as they spawn.
  • A marker on something that has gone is swept away. When the monster is removed or the prop is taken, its markers go with it. A set_marker on it afterwards does nothing.
  • A level's markers belong to that level. A player arriving later is sent all of them, except those hung for someone else.

A marker that is off screen or hidden costs almost nothing to draw. The game checks a few markers each frame to see whether a wall hides them. With many on screen, one that slips behind a wall can take a moment to hide.

Exercise: a sign over the lever, a pointer over the gate

  1. Open Gem Hollow (Home → ◆ examples → 📂 Gem Hollow; a fresh copy if you have changed it before), then ◇ Graphs, and tap levers under world. The red note is the red lever.

  2. Press + node…, type mark and choose mark. Set marker to lever, template to label (the field offers the module's markers), text? to pulled and height? to 0.5.

  3. Drag a link from whenpropus1's prop pin to the new node's entity pin. Without it, the sign would hang on the event's entity: the player who pulled.

  4. Drag a link from camerashak9's then, the end of the red note's top row, to the new node's in.

  5. Add a second mark. Set marker to isles, template to marker, at? to place. Press pick a point on the map and tap the floor just past the red gate. Set text? to The Isles, height? to 2, and turn on through_walls?. Link the first mark's then to its in.

  6. Close the graph window, open the world map and press ▶ Play. Walk to the red lever and pull it (use, E). When the camera comes back, pulled hangs over the lever and a ▼ The Isles over the gate.

  7. Walk away round a corner. The sign over the lever hides behind the wall. The pointer stays, because it draws through walls.

  8. Back in the graph, add when sector entered with tag? 11, and link it to a remove marker with marker isles. Play again: the pointer goes as you walk through the gate.

  9. Now a sign with no graph. On the world map, choose ✦ Place, tap sign: label, and tap the floor beside the red lever. Set its text to Pull me. Play: the sign is there from the start.

A graph hangs a label over a crate with no options set. The player walks behind a wall that hides the crate. What do they see?

  • The label, through the wall
  • Nothing: a marker behind a wall is hidden unless it has through_walls
  • The label, faded

The game hides a marker when a wall of the map stands between it and the camera. Bodies in the way don't hide it. through_walls turns this off.

A player saves, quits, and loads the game later. What happens to the pointer a graph hung at a place, and to the monsters' health bars?

  • Both come back from the save
  • Both are gone for good
  • Markers aren't saved: the pointer is back only if a graph hangs it again as the level starts; the monsters get their bars back as they spawn

A save keeps the world's state, not its markers. Hang yours from when_level_starts. A monster with a health_bar hangs its own.

You tick debug? on a mark, then ship the module with show the debug overlay in the game off. What do players get?

  • Nothing: a debug marker is not even sent when the module ships without the overlay
  • The marker, hidden until they press a key
  • A warning in their game

Debug markers cost a released game nothing and show its players nothing. You can leave them in your graphs.
