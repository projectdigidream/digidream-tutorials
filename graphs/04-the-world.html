Talking to the world

Variables are a graph's memory. This page is about its hands: the nodes that move, hurt, light, play, film and show things. Each section names the nodes, says what they act on, and points out what they cannot do.

Entities: doing things to them

An entity is anything with a body: a player, a monster, a prop. The menu's do to a thing group acts on one. Each node has an entity pin: unlinked, it is the event's entity; linked, it is that one; linked to nothing, the node does nothing.

NodeWhat it doesDefaults
damageHurts it as a weapon does. A monster feels pain, dies, and drops what it carries.amount 10
healGives health back, up to max.amount 25, max 100
killKills it outright, as if shot.
removeTakes it out of the world, with no death.
teleportPuts it at a spawn point (by tag), onto the entity linked to to, or at x, y, z. An axis you leave out stays as it is.
pushA shove it slides from: forward, backward, away or toward the entity linked to from, up, or at an angle (0 is east, 90 north).strength 10 units a second
stopTakes all its velocity away.
speed_upFaster or slower for a while: 2 is twice as fast, 0.5 half.factor 2, 5 seconds
spawnBrings a monster or a catalogue prop into the world, at the entity linked to at, or at x, y, z. Its entity output is what appeared, or nil.
play_soundA sound, from the entity.

A teleport lands at the end of the tick, like every change to the world. A position_of later in the same run still reads the old place.

Reading entities

The read group has pure nodes that give values:

  • position_of: x, y, z, in map units.
  • health_of: its health.
  • distance: how far apart the entities linked to a and b are.
  • nearest_player: the player nearest the entity linked to to (the event's, when nothing is), or nil.
  • sector_of: the sector it stands in. Link it into a door's sector to open "the door the player is at".

And through get_var, set_var and add_var, every property: hp, alive, grounded, facing, is_player, name, and the 45 movement rules. See Variables, numbers and saving.

To give a player a temporary power, set a movement rule and set it back later: set_var jump_impulse to 20, a delay of 600 ticks, then set_var it back to the module's value. On a player this is an effect, and a later set replaces it.

Sectors: doors, floors, lifts

The move the map group moves sectors. Each node acts on a sector in one of three ways: sector picked on the map, a sector pin linked (from sector_of), or a tag, which means every sector carrying that tag. Speeds are slow, normal or fast.

NodeMoves
open_door, close_doorA DOOM door: the ceiling rises to just under the lowest ceiling beside it, or comes down to the floor. close_after shuts it again after some seconds.
move_floor, move_ceilingUp or down by an amount, to the next floor up, the lowest or highest one beside it, or back where it started (original).
liftA DOOM lift: down to the lowest floor beside it, a wait (3 seconds), and back up.
slide_sectorThe whole sector sideways by dx, dy; back_and_forth keeps it going.
spin_sectorTurns it about its middle, 360° by default; forever keeps it turning.
stop_sectorStops it where it is.

These are the trigger runtime's own sector moves underneath. The server sends a move once, and every player's machine computes the rest, so a graph's door costs no more than a trigger's and moves just as smoothly.

A door you can press again while it moves will restart halfway. A trigger door guards against that with the condition sector_at_rest. In a graph, use the same condition from trigger words (if sector at rest) before the move.

Looks: pictures, light, weather, the sky

NodeChanges
set_floor_texture, set_ceiling_textureA sector's floor or ceiling picture. F_SKY1 on a ceiling opens it to the sky.
set_sector_lightIts light, 0 (dark) to 255 (full), at once or fading over seconds.
set_sector_effectrain, snow, dust, embers or fog; on or off, with a density and a color (#rrggbb).
set_wall_textureWhat one wall shows: its upper, middle (the default) or lower part. A switch flipped, a screen lit.
set_skyThe level's sky: strip, panorama, cube, or default to go back to the module's.

The server keeps these looks like a thrown switch. A player who arrives later sees them as they are, and they are saved with the level. They are looks only: nothing in the simulation reads them, so fog does not hide you from a monster and a dark room does not slow you down.

Sounds and music

  • play_sound plays one of the module's sounds from an entity, so it is heard from where that entity is. Its sound is chosen from the module's Sounds (see Takes and in-game rules). In a run with no entity (every, after, when_level_starts), link one in, or nothing plays.
  • The music is the trigger action music: in the node menu, do music in the trigger words group. It switches the map's soundtrack to another cue.
FieldMeaning
cueA cue of this map's soundtrack, by name. default is the one the map starts with.
fadeMilliseconds of crossfade; 800 when empty.
atWhen it lands: next_bar (the default), loop_end, or now.
everyoneEvery player in the level, not just the event's.

The change is not remembered: a player who joins later hears default. To keep a boss theme for latecomers, also play it from when_player_arrives while a boss_awake variable is true. Cues are named in the ♪ Music section; see Loops, cuts and cues.

Cutscenes and the camera

A cutscene is a chain of plain nodes, from the menu's cutscene and camera groups. Each acts on the event's player, or on every player in the level with everyone ticked. In a run with no player (every, after, when_level_starts) and nothing linked to entity, they act on every player too.

NodeDoesExits
start_cinematicTakes the controls (no move, jump, dash, use or attack) and brings in the letterbox bars: 0.12 of the screen, over 0.5 seconds.then
end_cinematicGives it all back: the controls, the camera following the player, the subtitle off.then
subtitleA line at the bottom, with an optional speaker. It stays seconds, or long enough to read (1.5 s plus 0.05 s a letter, at most 8).then, done
clear_subtitleTakes the line off now.then
fadeTo black or back to clear, over 1 second by default.then, done
camera_flythroughFlies the camera along a camera path, picked by its tag, looking where the path looks. 6 units a second, easing on over 1 second.then, done
camera_trackRides a path but keeps looking at the player: a dolly down a corridor.then
camera_followBack to following the player, from a distance, height and angle; lock holds them.then
camera_shakeA shake: 0.3 strength for 0.5 seconds by default.then

Chain the done exits, not the then ones, when the next step should wait:

when_level_starts → start_cinematic → camera_flythrough ─done→ subtitle "…" ─done→ end_cinematic

start_cinematic blocks every engine button, but the module's own keys still reach graphs. Make a skip key in Controls and answer it with when_button_pressed → end_cinematic.

Previewing with ▶ scene

Once a graph has nodes, the window's bar has ▶ scene. It plays the run from the picked node, or else from the first start node that reaches a cutscene or camera node, over the map, with the game's own timings: the bars, the fades, each subtitle as the HUD draws it, the camera on its curve, the shakes. Things only the server does (a door, a log line, a call) appear as a line in the corner when they happen. Esc or ■ comes back, and ↻ plays it again.

The preview has no game state to decide by, so every branch takes true, and it says so. It stops after 256 steps or two minutes.

Panels and the HUD

Panels are the module's library of small windows (a shop, a terminal, a main menu), designed in the Panels section (see Sky, screens and weather). A graph opens, fills and answers them by name. The menu's panels group has entries per panel and per widget, already filled in: open shop, close shop, when shop is closed, is shop open?, when shop › buy is pressed, set shop › volume, show shop › sell.

NodeWhat it does
open_panelShows the panel to the player. Opened again, it replaces itself.
close_panelTakes it away, if this player has it open.
when_panel_usedA button pressed, a toggle flipped, a slider moved, an option chosen. Gives widget (its name) and value. Leave widget empty to hear them all.
when_panel_closedThe player closed it with its ×. Not fired by close_panel.
panel_is_openWhether the player has it open.
set_widgetWhat a toggle (0 or 1), slider (position) or select (index) shows.
show_element, hide_elementShows or hides one widget, or one decor element of the panel's template. Hidden is not gone: a slider keeps its value.
set_hudWrites a HUD value. A panel's changing text (gold, a timer) is a template text with {hud.gold} in it, and a decor bar can follow a HUD value too.
  • A panel is answered where it was opened. A panel the game's graph opened is answered by the game's graphs, and stays open from level to level. A level's panel is answered by that level's graphs, and closes when the player leaves.
  • set_hud goes to the event's player by default. In a run with no player, set to to everyone, or it reaches nobody.
  • The older way still works: the trigger words open a panel (ui_open), and when a panel is used with if it was this widget … (on_ui_event and widget_is). That event's data outputs are numbers: ui_id and widget_id. Prefer the panel nodes, which name things.

A graph that names a panel it does not carry, or a widget the panel lacks, is refused when it loads. The studio says so first, in red.

The player's keys

  • when_button_pressed fires once, on the tick the button goes down.
  • when_button_released fires on the tick it comes up.
  • button_held reads whether it is down now (link the player into its entity).

A button is one of the engine's (jump, dash, grab, hover, fly, grapple, use, attack, alt_attack, crouch) or one of the module's own keys, made in Controls (Controls). A module key can have a touch button too. A button nobody has is a problem: no button or key called …

The weapon nodes follow the same pattern: act (give_weapon, take_weapon, select_weapon, give_ammo, set_ammo), read (held_weapon, has_weapon, ammo_of) and start (when_weapon_changed, when_fired, when_out_of_ammo). See the Weapons track.

Exercise: a power cut

A switch kills the lights, fog rolls in, the music turns tense, and a ten-second cutscene shows the danger.

  1. Give the rooms you want darkened a tag, say 5, in their room settings. Draw a camera path over them with tag 1 (Camera and cinematics).

  2. Make a graph power. Add when_wall_used and pick the switch wall. Link it to a once.

  3. Then a set_sector_light: tag 5, light 40, seconds 2. Then a set_sector_effect: tag 5, effect fog.

  4. Then do music with cue set to one of your map's cues, at now and everyone on.

  5. Then start_cinematic → camera_flythrough (path 1). From its done, a subtitle Something is moving in the dark., and from the subtitle's done, end_cinematic.

  6. Pick the when_wall_used node and press ▶ scene. Watch the light and music lines go by in the corner, then the flight and the line. Press Esc.

A graph sets a room's light to 40. A second player joins a minute later. What do they see?

  • The room at its original light
  • The room at 40: the server keeps sector looks and sends them to newcomers
  • A fade from the original light to 40

Pictures, light and effects are kept by the server like a thrown switch, and saved with the level. The music is the one that is not remembered.

During a cutscene started with start_cinematic, the player presses your module's skip key. Does a when_button_pressed skip node fire?

  • Yes: the module's own keys still reach graphs
  • No: every button is blocked until end_cinematic
  • Only if the cutscene was started with everyone

The cinematic takes the engine's buttons away, but leaves the module's keys to graphs, so a skip is possible.

▶ scene plays a graph with a branch in it. Which way does the preview go?

  • Both ways, one after the other
  • It stops at the branch
  • true, and it says so

The preview has no game state to decide by, so it takes true and notes it.
