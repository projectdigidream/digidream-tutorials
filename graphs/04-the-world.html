Talking to the world

Variables are a graph's memory. This page is about its hands: the nodes that move, hurt, light, play, film and show things. Each section names the nodes, says what they act on, and points out what they cannot do.

You'll learnhow to act on entities and read them (Gem Hollow's springs and spikes), move and restyle sectors (its rafts and turning platforms), play sounds and change the music, run a cutscene (its intro and ending), type out subtitles, preview it all with ▶ scene, open and answer panels, write HUD values, and answer the player's keys Time45 minutes NeedsThinking in graphs and Variables, numbers and saving; camera paths (Camera and cinematics) and panels (Sky, screens and weather) for those sections

Entities: doing things to them

An entity is anything with a body: a player, a monster, a prop. The menu's do to a thing group acts on one. Each node has an entity pin: unlinked, it is the event's entity; linked, it is that one; linked to nothing, the node does nothing.

NodeWhat it doesDefaults
damageHurts it as a weapon does. A monster feels pain, dies, and drops what it carries.amount 10
healGives health back, up to max.amount 25, max 100
killKills it outright, as if shot.
removeTakes it out of the world, with no death.
teleportPuts it at a spawn point (by tag), onto the entity linked to to, or at x, y, z. An axis you leave out stays as it is.
pushA shove it slides from: forward, backward, away or toward the entity linked to from, straight up (the strength is all lift: a spring), or at an angle (0 is east, 90 north). up adds lift to any of them.strength 10 units a second
stopTakes all its velocity away.
speed_upFaster or slower for a while: 2 is twice as fast, 0.5 half.factor 2, 5 seconds
spawnBrings a monster or a catalogue prop into the world, at the entity linked to at, or at x, y, z. Its entity output is what appeared, or nil.
play_soundA sound, from the entity.

A teleport lands at the end of the tick, like every change to the world. A position_of later in the same run still reads the old place.

Gem Hollow's springs and spikes

The second note of Gem Hollow's moving graph (below) is two tiny chains:

when_prop_touched (spring) → push (direction: up, strength: 15) → play_sound world.spring
when_prop_touched (spikes) → damage 15 → push (direction: away, strength: 9, up: 5)

The spring throws the player 15 units a second straight up, more than a jump. The spikes are meant to knock the player back, away from them. But away means away from the entity linked to from, and nothing is linked there. With no from, the push has no direction, so only the up 5 is felt: a hop on the spot. Link the start node's prop into from and the spikes throw you clear. That is the exercise at the end of this page.

Reading entities

The read group has pure nodes that give values:

  • position_of: x, y, z, in map units.
  • health_of: its health.
  • distance: how far apart the entities linked to a and b are.
  • nearest_player: the player nearest the entity linked to to (the event's, when nothing is), or nil.
  • sector_of: the sector it stands in. Link it into a door's sector to open "the door the player is at".

And through get_var, set_var and add_var, every property: hp, alive, grounded, facing, is_player, name, and the 45 movement rules. See Variables, numbers and saving.

To give a player a temporary power, set a movement rule and set it back later: set_var jump_impulse to 20, a delay of 600 ticks, then set_var it back to the module's value. On a player this is an effect, and a later set replaces it.

Sectors: doors, floors, lifts

The move the map group moves sectors. Each node acts on a sector in one of three ways: sector picked on the map, a sector pin linked (from sector_of), or a tag, which means every sector carrying that tag. Speeds are slow, normal or fast.

NodeMoves
open_door, close_doorA DOOM door: the ceiling rises to just under the lowest ceiling beside it, or comes down to the floor. close_after shuts it again after some seconds.
move_floor, move_ceilingUp or down by an amount, to the next floor up, the lowest or highest one beside it, or back where it started (original).
liftA DOOM lift: down to the lowest floor beside it, a wait (3 seconds), and back up.
slide_sectorThe whole sector sideways by dx, dy; back_and_forth keeps it going.
spin_sectorTurns it about its middle, 360° by default; forever keeps it turning.
stop_sectorStops it where it is.

Gem Hollow's moving platforms

Gem Hollow's rafts slide over the water and the lava, and a cross, a disc and a bridge turn over the lava. All of it starts in one chain in the moving graph:

  • One start, a row of moves. Each move starts and hands on at once, so all six platforms start on the level's first tick. Nothing waits: a sector move runs on by itself.
  • By tag. Each platform's room carries a tag (31, 32, 33 for the rafts; 41, 42, 43 for the turning ones), set in its room settings. A tag needs nothing picked on the map, so the chain survives redrawing the rooms.
  • Rafts are slide_sector with back_and_forth on: dx 18 for the first, a diagonal (dx -9, dy 12) for the second. A player can land on a raft from its open sides while it moves.
  • Turning platforms (tags 41–43) turn a full 360° about their middle, over and over. Gem Hollow drives them with the trigger word do sector move in an action node, because it takes a finer speed than spin_sector's slow, normal and fast: spin 360, spin_speed in degrees a tick (a third of a degree for two of them, one turn in 18 seconds; a quarter for the third, 24 seconds), loop loop, surface both. For a quick spinner, spin_sector with forever on does the same in one node.

These are the trigger runtime's own sector moves underneath. The server sends a move once, and every player's machine computes the rest, so a graph's door costs no more than a trigger's and moves just as smoothly.

A door you can press again while it moves will restart halfway. A trigger door guards against that with the condition sector_at_rest. In a graph, use the same condition from trigger words (if sector at rest) before the move.

Looks: pictures, light, weather, the sky

NodeChanges
set_floor_texture, set_ceiling_textureA sector's floor or ceiling picture. F_SKY1 on a ceiling opens it to the sky.
set_sector_lightIts light, 0 (dark) to 255 (full), at once or fading over seconds.
set_sector_effectrain, snow, dust, embers or fog; on or off, with a density and a color (#rrggbb).
set_wall_textureWhat one wall shows: its upper, middle (the default) or lower part. A switch flipped, a screen lit.
set_skyThe level's sky: strip, panorama, cube, or default to go back to the module's.

The server keeps these looks like a thrown switch. A player who arrives later sees them as they are, and they are saved with the level. They are looks only: nothing in the simulation reads them, so fog does not hide you from a monster and a dark room does not slow you down.

Sounds and music

  • play_sound plays one of the module's sounds from an entity, so it is heard from where that entity is. Its sound is chosen from the module's Sounds (see Takes and in-game rules). In a run with no entity (every, after, when_level_starts), link one in, or nothing plays.
  • The music is the trigger action music: in the node menu, do music in the trigger words group. It switches the map's soundtrack to another cue.
FieldMeaning
cueA cue of this map's soundtrack, by name. default is the one the map starts with.
fadeMilliseconds of crossfade; 800 when empty.
atWhen it lands: next_bar (the default), loop_end, or now.
everyoneEvery player in the level, not just the event's.

Gem Hollow's soundtrack has four cues: default, cliffs, keep and victory. The second and third checkpoint flags end with do music (cliffs, then keep; fade 1500, at next_bar), so each zone has its tune and the change falls on the beat. The ending switches to victory with at now, because a cutscene can't wait for a bar.

The change is not remembered: a player who joins later hears default. To keep a boss theme for latecomers, also play it from when_player_arrives while a boss_awake variable is true. Cues are named in the ♪ Music section; see Loops, cuts and cues.

Cutscenes and the camera

A cutscene is a chain of plain nodes, from the menu's cutscene and camera groups. Each acts on the event's player, or on every player in the level with everyone ticked. In a run with no player (every, after, when_level_starts) and nothing linked to entity, they act on every player too.

NodeDoesExits
start_cinematicTakes the controls (no move, jump, dash, use or attack) and brings in the letterbox bars: 0.12 of the screen, over 0.5 seconds.then
end_cinematicGives it all back: the controls, the camera following the player, the subtitle off.then
subtitleA line at the bottom, with an optional speaker. It stays seconds, or long enough to read (1.5 s plus 0.05 s a letter, at most 8). With mode typed, it is typed out (see below).then, done
clear_subtitleTakes the line off now.then
fadeTo black or back to clear, over 1 second by default.then, done
camera_flythroughFlies the camera along a camera path, picked by its tag, looking where the path looks. 6 units a second, easing on over 1 second.then, done
camera_trackRides a path but keeps looking at the player: a dolly down a corridor.then
camera_followBack to following the player, from a distance, height and angle; lock holds them. lead aims that many units ahead of where the player faces, and lead_velocity further ahead the faster they run forward.then
camera_shakeA shake: 0.3 strength for 0.5 seconds by default.then

How the follow camera moves between cutscenes (its smoothing, its weight, how far it looks ahead, its dead zones) is the map's, set in the camera sheet under how it moves; see Camera and cinematics. From a graph, camera_follow's lead and lead_velocity change the look-ahead.

Chain the done exits, not the then ones, when the next step should wait:

when_level_starts → start_cinematic → camera_flythrough ─done→ subtitle "…" ─done→ end_cinematic

start_cinematic blocks every engine button, but the module's own keys still reach graphs. Make a skip key in Controls and answer it with when_button_pressed → end_cinematic.

Typed subtitles

A subtitle with mode typed types its line out, as in an old adventure game:

  • speed: letters a second (30 when empty).
  • pack: how many letters appear at each step (2 when empty).
  • sound: a sound played with each step that shows a letter. A step of only spaces is silent, so the gaps between words are heard. With no sound, it types in silence.

Unless seconds is set, a typed line stays long enough to be typed (letters ÷ speed) and then read. The speaker's name shows at once. The sound is centred, wherever the camera is.

Gem Hollow's intro, node by node

The first time a player arrives in the world level, Gem Hollow flies the camera over the whole hollow and tells the story. It is the intro graph:

when_player_arrives → once → start_cinematic (0.12, 0.6 s) → fade black (0.01 s)
  → fade clear (2 s) → play_sound cine.whoosh → camera_flythrough (path 1, speed 12, blend 1)

camera_flythrough.then → subtitle "Long ago…" (5 s) ─done→ subtitle (6 s) ─done→ subtitle (6 s) ─done→ subtitle (4 s)
camera_flythrough.done → fade black (0.8 s) ─done→ camera_follow → end_cinematic (0.6 s) → fade clear (1 s)
  → subtitle "Find the RED lever…" (6 s)
  • Two clocks. After the flight starts, two paths run side by side: the story, timed by the subtitles (21 seconds), and the flight, timed by the path (about 43 seconds at 12 units a second). The bottom row waits for the flight, not for the words.
  • Black, then in. The first fade's then, not its done, starts the second, so the screen cuts to black and fades in over 2 seconds while the bars come in.
  • Out under cover. The camera goes back to the player behind a fade to black, so the jump is never seen. end_cinematic also takes off any line still showing.
  • once. The intro plays the first time only. Gem Hollow does not keep level state, so it plays again whenever the level starts over.

Gem Hollow's ending

Touching the Star at the end runs the ending graph: a victory cutscene, then a panel.

  • camera_track has no done: it rides its path until something else takes the camera. So the timing comes from the two subtitles (4 + 4 seconds) and the fade (1.5 seconds).
  • The panel theend shows Coins {hud.coins} Gems {hud.gems} / 5 in its template, written by the pickups graph. Its buttons are answered in the same graph, because a panel is answered where it was opened. The title screen's side of it is in The game graph, calls and maps.

Previewing with ▶ scene

Once a graph has nodes, the window's bar has ▶ scene. It plays the run from the picked node, or else from the first start node that reaches a cutscene or camera node, over the map, with the game's own timings: the bars, the fades, each subtitle as the HUD draws it (a typed one typed out, with its sound, and timed as the engine times it), the camera on its curve, the shakes. Things only the server does (a door, a log line, a call) appear as a line in the corner when they happen. Esc or ■ comes back, and ↻ plays it again.

The preview has no game state to decide by, so every branch takes true, and it says so. It stops after 256 steps or two minutes.

Panels and the HUD

Panels are the module's library of small windows (a shop, a terminal, a main menu), designed in the Panels section (see Sky, screens and weather). A graph opens, fills and answers them by name. The menu's panels group has entries per panel and per widget, already filled in: open shop, close shop, when shop is closed, is shop open?, when shop › buy is pressed, set shop › volume, show shop › sell.

NodeWhat it does
open_panelShows the panel to the player. Opened again, it replaces itself.
close_panelTakes it away, if this player has it open.
when_panel_usedA button pressed, a toggle flipped, a slider moved, an option chosen. Gives widget (its name) and value. Leave widget empty to hear them all.
when_panel_closedThe player closed it with its ×. Not fired by close_panel.
panel_is_openWhether the player has it open.
set_widgetWhat a toggle (0 or 1), slider (position) or select (index) shows.
show_element, hide_elementShows or hides one widget, or one decor element of the panel's template. Hidden is not gone: a slider keeps its value.
set_hudWrites a HUD value. A panel's changing text (gold, a timer) is a template text with {hud.gold} in it, and a decor bar can follow a HUD value too.
  • A panel is answered where it was opened. A panel the game's graph opened is answered by the game's graphs, and stays open from level to level. A level's panel is answered by that level's graphs, and closes when the player leaves.
  • Gem Hollow uses two panels, both opened from map graphs: title on the menu map and theend in the ending graph. Their buttons are answered with when title › play is pressed and friends, in the same graph.
  • set_hud goes to the event's player by default. In a run with no player, set to to everyone, or it reaches nobody.
  • The older way still works: the trigger words open a panel (ui_open), and when a panel is used with if it was this widget … (on_ui_event and widget_is). That event's data outputs are numbers: ui_id and widget_id. Prefer the panel nodes, which name things.

A graph that names a panel it does not carry, or a widget the panel lacks, is refused when it loads. The studio says so first, in red.

The player's keys

  • when_button_pressed fires once, on the tick the button goes down.
  • when_button_released fires on the tick it comes up.
  • button_held reads whether it is down now (link the player into its entity).

A button is one of the engine's (jump, dash, grab, hover, fly, grapple, use, attack, alt_attack, crouch) or one of the module's own keys, made in Controls (Controls). A module key can have a touch button too. A button nobody has is a problem: no button or key called …

The weapon nodes follow the same pattern: act (give_weapon, take_weapon, select_weapon, give_ammo, set_ammo), read (held_weapon, has_weapon, ammo_of) and start (when_weapon_changed, when_fired, when_out_of_ammo). See the Weapons track.

In Cinderfall: people to talk to, burning floors and a boss bar

Cinderfall comes with the studio: on Home, under ◆ examples, press 📂 Cinderfall. Cinderfall, the tour walks round it.

Cinderfall, an example first-person shooter, uses the same nodes as Gem Hollow for things Gem Hollow never does. Each district map has a story graph for them; open one with ◇ Graphs, under the map's name.

A conversation

Cinderfall's people (Doc Imre and Pip in the hub, Old Mags at the railhead, Engineer Vos in the foundry, Rusk in the mine, Carrow in the old town) are props. You press use (E) on them, and the map's story graph answers with typed subtitles. Every conversation has this shape:

when_prop_used (mags) → do set_animation (state 1, who: target)       she turns to talk
  → branch (compare get_var talked_mags == 0)
      true  → subtitle "So the Company sent a warden…" ─done→ subtitle ─done→ subtitle ─done→ subtitle
              → do set_animation (state 0, target) → add_var talked_mags +1 → set_hud objective "Find a fuse…"
      false → branch (compare talked_mags == 1) → …the next thing she has to say
  • The person, not the player. do set_animation with who target plays a state on the prop that was used: state 1 while talking, state 0 after.
  • Typed, with a voice. Each line is a subtitle with a speaker (Old Mags), mode typed, speed 38, pack 2 and a sound of its own per person (voice.mags, voice.imre …). Lines are chained by done, so each waits for the last to be read.
  • A counter decides what is said. talked_mags goes up after each conversation, and a ladder of compare and branch nodes picks the next one. Pip, in the hub, has five: each new talk gives away one secret, and the last says That's all I know. Honest.
  • The objective is a HUD value. set_hud objective writes the line Cinderfall's HUD shows at the top right ({hud.objective}). Mags, the fuse, the winch and the seal each write the next goal.
  • Two start nodes on one prop. The hub's story has two when_prop_used for imre. One talks; the other compares the hub's copy of cores with healed, and for every Core she has not seen yet runs heal 100 and sets healed to the count. Both run on the same press.

Cinderfall talks in subtitles, not panels. Its three panels are the title screen (title, on the menu map), the end panel (theend, on n_epilogue, whose decor shows {hud.cores} / 3, {hud.secrets} / {hud.secrets_total} and {hud.kills}), and the weapon wheel arms, which the game's graph opens and answers (Your first weapon).

A floor that burns until it doesn't

The foundry's slag channel (tag 901) and pour pit (tag 904) are floors with a variable in front of them:

when_floor_touched (tag 901, players) → branch (get_var drained)
    false → damage 15 → push (up, strength 9) → play_sound player.pain
when_floor_touched (tag 901, monsters) → kill

on_call valves → branch (compare value >= 3) → once → set_var drained 1
  → set_floor_texture (tag 901, ASHFLOOR) → set_sector_light (tag 901, 120, over 3 s)
  → set_floor_texture (tag 904, FLOORPLATE) → play_sound rumble → subtitle "The slag hisses and sets…"

The burning is a graph, not a sector special, so turning it off is one variable. The new pictures and light are kept by the server like any look. How the three valves are counted across two maps is Recipe 7.

A boss bar

The Smelter, Cinderfall's boss, waits in w_heart_w. Its story graph drives a bar on the HUD with three values:

when_sector_entered (the arena island, players) → once → set_hud boss_on 1 → set_hud boss_hp_max 2400 → set_hud boss_hp 2400
  → do music (cue boss, fade 800, at now) → subtitle "THE SMELTER" (2.5 s)
when_hurt (monsters, kind smelter) → set_hud boss_hp (value ← health_of, entity ← when_hurt.entity)
when_dies (monsters, kind smelter) → set_hud boss_on 0 → start_cinematic → do music victory → play_sound rumble
  → camera_shake (0.5, 4 s) → subtitle ─done→ fade black (2 s) ─done→ change_map n_epilogue

In ▣ Screens, the HUD's bossname text and bossbar bar are shown only while hud.boss_on is above 0, and the bar is bound to hud.boss_hp, with hud.boss_hp_max as its max. when_hurt fires on every hit, so the bar follows each one. The Smelter's own brain is in Monster recipes.

Exercise: spikes that bite, and a story that types

  1. Open Gem Hollow (Home, examples, Gem Hollow), then ◇ Graphs, and tap moving under world.

  2. In the green note, drag a link from whenpropto11's prop pin to push13's from pin. The spikes now push the player away from themselves.

  3. Switch to the intro graph with the picker in the window's bar. Tap subtitle8, set mode? to typed, speed? to 25, and choose ui.select for sound?. Do the same for subtitle9, subtitle10 and subtitle11.

  4. Each of those lines has its own seconds, which now has to cover the typing too. Clear seconds? on all four, so each line stays long enough to be typed and read.

  5. Tap whenplayer1 and press ▶ scene. Watch the lines type out over the flight, with a tick at each step. Press Esc.

  6. Play. The intro types its story. Then find a row of spikes and step on them: they now throw you back.

A graph sets a room's light to 40. A second player joins a minute later. What do they see?

  • The room at its original light
  • The room at 40: the server keeps sector looks and sends them to newcomers
  • A fade from the original light to 40

Pictures, light and effects are kept by the server like a thrown switch, and saved with the level. The music is the one that is not remembered.

During a cutscene started with start_cinematic, the player presses your module's skip key. Does a when_button_pressed skip node fire?

  • Yes: the module's own keys still reach graphs
  • No: every button is blocked until end_cinematic
  • Only if the cutscene was started with everyone

The cinematic takes the engine's buttons away, but leaves the module's keys to graphs, so a skip is possible.

▶ scene plays a graph with a branch in it. Which way does the preview go?

  • Both ways, one after the other
  • It stops at the branch
  • true, and it says so

The preview has no game state to decide by, so it takes true and notes it.
