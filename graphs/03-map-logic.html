Map logic recipes

Six graphs that most maps need, wired node by node. The first three are Gem Hollow's own: open them in the studio and read along. The next three are recipes to build on your own map, and the seventh is read from Cinderfall, a new example.

You'll learnhow Gem Hollow's levers open its gates on camera, how its keys open the vault, how its checkpoints bring you back, and how to build an ambush, a wave arena and a timed switch puzzle, with the traps each one hides Time45 minutes NeedsGem Hollow open; for the last three, a map with a few rooms and a door (Your first map), a monster (Your first monster), and Variables, numbers and saving

How to read a recipe

Each recipe has a table of nodes and a list of links. The id is a short name for the node in this page; the studio gives its own ids, and you can ignore them. Fields are what you set in the side panel. A field that says picked is tapped on the map: the board steps aside while you tap a room, a wall, or a spot on the floor.

Links are written from.pin → to.pin. White (exec) links come out of then, true, false, 0, 1 … and go into in. Everything else is a data link.

Put each recipe in its own graph: + new graph on map in ◇ Graphs, named keydoor, secrets and so on. Each graph uses one of the map's script slots, so on a busy map you can also put two recipes in one graph. They don't interfere: each start node has its own runs.

Warm-up: the shape of an ambush

The lab is loaded with an ambush: entering sector 3 shuts the door behind you, sounds an alarm, and opens the door three seconds later. Press 🚶 player enters sector 3 twice. The second time, once stops the run at once.

Recipe 1: a lever opens a gate, on camera (Gem Hollow)

Gem Hollow has three gates, each opened by a lever in the zone before it. Its levers graph (on world) has three copies of one chain, one per lever, each in a note of its colour. Here is the red one.

NodeFieldsWhat it is for
when_prop_usedprop: lever_redThe player presses use on the lever. The prop is marked usable in Props.
onceOne pull only. Which once nodes have passed is part of a level's saved state.
do set animationstate: 1 · who: targetThe lever's picture flips to its pull animation. who: target is the thing used; the default, entity, would be the player.
play_soundsound: world.leverThe clunk.
start_cinematicletterbox: 0.1 · seconds: 0.4Controls off, bars in.
camera_flythroughpath: 2 · speed: 2.5 · blend: 0.8Flies a short camera path that looks at the gate.
open_doortag: 11 · speed: normalEvery sector tagged 11, the red gate, opens like a DOOM door.
play_sound, camera_shakeworld.door.open; strength 0.25, 1.2 sThe grinding gate.
subtitleThe red gate grinds open to the east. · 4 sSaid when the flight ends.
camera_follow, end_cinematicseconds: 0.4Camera back on the player, controls back.
subtitleBeyond it: the Isles. … · 5 sA hint, while the player can move again.
 
use.then → once → set_animation → play_sound → start_cinematic → camera_flythrough
camera_flythrough.then → open_door → play_sound → camera_shake      (at once: the gate opens during the flight)
camera_flythrough.done → subtitle ─done→ camera_follow → end_cinematic → subtitle
  • Two exits, two jobs. The flythrough's then fires at once, so the gate starts opening as the camera sets off and is moving when the camera gets there. Its done waits for the end of the path. Swap them and the camera arrives at a closed gate.
  • Doors by tag. Each gate is a door room with a tag (11, 12, 13) in its room settings. A tag survives editing the map better than a picked sector, and one tag can open a two-sector gate.
  • Copy, don't rebuild. The blue and gold chains differ only in the prop, the tag, the camera path and the lines.
  • end_cinematic already puts the camera back on the player. The camera_follow before it is there to say so on the board, and to set a distance if you want one.

Recipe 2: keys that open a vault (Gem Hollow)

The vault graph is taken apart node by node in Thinking in graphs. In short: each key prop touched is removed, counted in keys, written to the HUD, and the run asks keys >= 3. Not yet: a subtitle. Yes: the same cutscene shape as the levers, on camera path 6, opening the door tagged 14.

  • The data link into remove's entity matters. It comes from the start node's prop. Unlinked, remove would act on the event's entity: the player.
  • A single key is simpler: a variable has_key (starts false), a set_var to true on touch, and a branch on it in front of the door. Since 0 is true for a branch, use true/false for a flag and a compare for a count.
  • The keys are the level's, not the player's: in co-op, anyone's key counts for everybody.

The DOOM modules carry keys of their own (key_red and friends), picked up and checked by their Lua. A graph can't see that inventory. This recipe is for your own keys in your own module.

Recipe 3: checkpoints and respawning (Gem Hollow)

Gem Hollow has three checkpoint flags, water, lava, and monsters that can knock you out. The checkpoints graph handles all of it, with three variables, cpx, cpy and cpz, that hold the last checkpoint. They start at the level's start: 22, 70, 0.

flag1 touched → once → set_animation (target, state 1) → set_var cpx 142 → set_var cpy 70 → set_var cpz 0
              → play_sound world.checkpoint → subtitle "Checkpoint!"
flag2, flag3: the same, then do music (cliffs; keep)

floor tag 900 (water) → play_sound player.pain → damage 15 → teleport (x ← cpx, y ← cpy, z ← cpz)
floor tag 901 (lava)  → play_sound player.pain → damage 25 → teleport (the same)
when_dies (players)   → teleport (the same)
floor tag 900 or 901 (monsters) → kill
  • A floor by tag. Every water sector carries tag 900 and the lava tag 901, so one when_floor_touched covers them all. It fires when somebody stands on the floor, not when they jump over it.
  • Hurt first, then move. The damage runs before the teleport, and the teleport lands at the end of the tick. If the damage knocks the player out, the when_dies chain sends them to the same place.
  • A knock-out. When a player's health runs out, the monster runtime heals them and sends them home. The when_dies chain then moves them on, to the checkpoint.
  • Music per zone. do music with at next_bar and fade 1500 changes the soundtrack's cue on the beat. everyone is off, so only the player who reached the flag hears it change.
  • Per level, not per player. cpx, cpy and cpz are the graph's, so in co-op the last flag anyone raised is where everybody comes back.

Recipe 4: an ambush

The player walks into a room, the door slams behind them, and imps appear.

There is no node that wakes a sleeping monster placed on the map. A dormant monster wakes by its own brain (see The brain). A graph's ambush spawns its monsters instead; a spawned monster starts awake.

idNodeFields
enterwhen_sector_enteredsector: picked, the trap room · who: players
firstonce
shutclose_doorsector: picked, the door you came in by · speed: fast
boomcamera_shakestrength: 0.4 · seconds: 0.5
threesequencecount: 3
aspawnwhat: imp · x, y, z: picked, a dark corner
bspawnwhat: imp · x, y, z: picked, another corner
yellplay_soundsound: a roar · (entity linked)
 
enter.then → first.in
first.then → shut.in
shut.then  → boom.in
boom.then  → three.in
three.0    → a.in
three.1    → b.in
three.2    → yell.in
b.entity   → yell.entity        (the roar comes from the second imp)
  • sequence runs 0, then 1, then 2, in the same tick. By the time 2 runs, b has spawned, so its entity output is the new imp.
  • Give spawn its z: picking a spot on the floor fills x, y and z. Without z it spawns at height 0, which may be inside the floor or the void.
  • If a spawn fails (no room), its entity is nil, and the linked play_sound does nothing rather than roaring from the player.

Recipe 5: an arena that opens when all are dead

Two waves of three. When the last monster of the last wave dies, the exit opens. This joins recipe 4 with a counter.

Variables: alive (0), wave (0), fighting (false).

  • Why fighting? when_dies fires for every monster that dies in the level. The flag makes the counter listen only while the arena is on. If the arena has its own monster, set kind on dies instead, or as well.
  • Why the delay loop is safe: rest.then → next.in goes back up the chain, but each pass after a delay is a new run, so it never meets the 256-step limit. The again branch ends it.
  • Why left reads the new value: the cleared branch pulls left after down has run, and nothing in this run read alive before.
  • Who sees the win line? In a when_dies run, the event's entity is the monster that died, not a player. An unlinked subtitle would reach nobody, and the server log would say acted on no player. Tick everyone on win, or link dies.by (the killer) into its entity.

Recipe 6: a timed two-switch puzzle

Two switches, far apart. Each one lights up for five seconds when pressed. If both are lit at once, the vault opens.

Before you start: pick two textures, one for an unlit switch and one for a lit switch. Mark both walls usable in their wall flags.

Variables: a_on (false), b_on (false).

idNodeFields

darkAset_wall_textureside: picked, switch A · texture: the unlit one

chkBget_varvar: b_on (a second node, not isLitB)
bothlogicop: and

vaultopen_doorsector: picked, the vault door · speed: slow
 
pressA.then  → guardA.in
isLitA.value  → guardA.cond
guardA.false → setA.in          (guardA.true unlinked: already lit, ignore)
setA.then    → lightA.in
lightA.then  → seqA.in
seqA.0       → check.in         (B's seqB.0 links here too)
seqA.1       → waitA.in
waitA.then   → clearA.in
clearA.then  → darkA.in

chkA.value   → both.a
chkB.value   → both.b
both.value   → check.cond
check.true   → vault1.in
vault1.then  → vault.in
  • The guard ignores a press on a switch that is already lit. Without it, a second press would start a second timer, and the first timer would put the light out early.
  • The sequence does the check first (exit 0), then starts the timer (exit 1), both in the same tick.
  • Why chkA is a separate node: in a run from switch A, isLitA was read at the guard, while a_on was still false. A pure node is worked out once per run, so reading isLitA again in the check would still say false. A fresh get_var reads the value setA just wrote.
  • Five seconds is 300 ticks. To make the window shorter for a harder puzzle, lower both delays.

Recipe 7: one objective across two maps (Cinderfall)

Cinderfall, a new example first-person shooter that is still being tested (the tour says how to import it), splits its foundry into two maps, w_foundry_w and w_foundry_e, joined without a load. Three coolant valves are spread over both: valve_a and valve_c in the west, valve_b in the east. Turning all three drains the slag, in both halves, and brings up a Furnace Core in the east. Neither map can count the other's valves, so the count is the game's.

  • The answer goes where the player is. The game's call to level takes the player who turned the valve, so only the map they stand in hears valves at once.
  • The other half hears on arrival. Every map's world graph calls sync when the player enters a sector, and the game answers it with valves too (The game graph, calls and maps). Walk from west to east after the third valve and the east drains as you step in. The once in front of the drain makes the many sync answers harmless.
  • The valve's own once keeps a second turn from counting twice. With persist levels on, Cinderfall keeps it passed when the map unloads and loads again.
  • The floors read drained. How the slag burns until then is in Talking to the world.

Exercise: kinder lava

Gem Hollow's lava takes 25 health and sends you back. Make it take less, and say why you were moved.

  1. Open Gem Hollow (Home, examples, Gem Hollow), then ◇ Graphs, and tap checkpoints under world.

  2. In the red note, find the lava chain: whenfloort34 (tag 901) → playsound39 → damage40 → teleport35. Tap damage40 and set its amount to 10.

  3. Add a subtitle with the text Too hot! Back to the checkpoint. and seconds 2. Link teleport35's then into it.

  4. Press + node…, choose a note, and write Kinder lava: 10 damage and a line. Drag it by its grip so it sits beside the chain.

  5. Play. Raise the first flag in the meadow, walk on to the Keep, and step into the lava. You lose 10, hear the line, and stand at the flag.

In the vault graph, you delete the link from the start node's prop to remove's entity. What does remove do when a key is touched?

  • Removes the key: it is what was touched
  • Acts on the event's entity, the player who touched the key
  • Nothing

Unlinked, an entity input means the event's entity, and for a touch that is whoever walked in. The prop is a separate output.

In the red lever's chain, open_door hangs off the flythrough's then, not its done. Why?

  • A door can't be opened during a cinematic
  • done never fires for a flythrough
  • So the gate is already opening while the camera flies to it

then fires at once and done when the path ends. From done, the camera would arrive at a gate that has not moved yet.

Why does the arena check fighting before counting a death?

  • Because when_dies fires for every monster that dies anywhere in the level
  • Because a dead monster's entity is nil
  • Because add_var cannot go below zero

Without the flag, a monster killed elsewhere before or after the fight would change the count.

In the switch puzzle, the check reads isLitA (the guard's own get_var) instead of a new chkA. Pressing B then A within five seconds…

  • opens the vault
  • is refused by the studio
  • does not open the vault: in A's run, isLitA was already read as false at the guard

A pure node is worked out once per run. The read at the guard, before setA, is the value the whole run sees.
