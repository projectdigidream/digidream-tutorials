Cinderfall, a tour

Cinderfall is an example first-person shooter. A smelting colony has gone wrong. You walk through five districts and a hub without a load, in any order, talk to the people who are left, and fight a boss at the bottom. It was made in the studio alone: no Lua or JavaScript was written by hand, and all its logic is graphs with notes.

You'll learnhow to get Cinderfall into the studio, how its one world is laid out, how its graphs are split, how people talk and the boss fights, its keys, saving, fonts and panel skins, and what it shows that Gem Hollow does not Time30 minutes Needsa checkout of the repository with Node.js, and the studio open (see Getting started)

New, and still being tested. Cinderfall is not bundled with the studio yet, so it is not under ◆ examples on Home. You bring it in as a pack (below). This page describes the files as they are now; details may still change.

Get it

  1. From the repository root, pack it:

    node tutorials/tools/pack-example.mjs cinderfall

    It writes cinderfall.zip (about 5 MB) and says how many files went in.

  2. In the studio, on Home, press ⇩ import pack and choose cinderfall.zip. The studio opens it as a module of its own, cinderfall, beside your others.

  3. Check Home: 12 maps, and "✔ nothing wrong that the studio can see". Under other modules there is now a cinderfall chip, to come back to it later.

Importing a pack again over it replaces the module, after a snapshot of the old one (see Play and export).

One world: five districts and a hub

Cinderfall has 12 maps. Nine of them, the ones named w_…, are the world. On 🗺 Maps, the ▦ metamap has put the maps on a grid and one shared world (walk from map to map) both on, with cells 112 × 112:

RowCells, west to east
north (y 0)w_mine_w, w_mine_e, w_foundry_w, w_foundry_e
middle (y 1)w_oldtown, w_hub, w_railhead
south (y 2)w_heart_w, w_heart_e

So the five districts are the Railhead, Old Town, the Mine, the Foundry and the Heart. The Mine, the Foundry and the Heart are two maps each, an east and a west half. The hub, w_hub, is the town square in the middle. Its doors lead into every district, and the seal to the Heart is south of it. Each district has its own music track, and the hub has two cues: default (hub) and seal (heart). When the seal opens, the hub's story graph switches the square to seal (for the player who opened it: everyone is off), and Mags says over the radio: "Warden, I felt that up here. Whatever was holding its breath down there just let it out."

Where two maps share an open side along the same line, they join, and the player walks across with no load. In the map editor, the neighbours are drawn around the map you are editing, with their names on them.

The other three maps are not in the world:

  • menu, the title screen. Its camera tracks along a platform (path 1) while the title panel offers NEW GAME and QUIT.
  • n_arrival, a short scene: the last train, a slow flight along the platform, and the warden's thoughts typed out. Then on to w_railhead.
  • n_epilogue, the morning after the boss: Mags on the platform, then the theend panel with what you found.

Story graphs and world graphs

Cinderfall has no triggers: every map says 0 triggers. Each world map has two graphs, split by job:

  • world: the plumbing every map needs. Doors that grind open when used, coloured doors that ask the game for a keycard, secrets, kills, dying near a seam, and asking the game how far the player has got (sync).
  • story: what makes that map itself. The people, the Cores, the Foundry's valves and slag, the winch at the Railhead, the seal in the hub, the boss.

Every group of nodes sits in a note that says what it does, in plain words. Open any graph and read the notes first.

Taught in Graphs and the Graph scripting track.

The game's graphs: arms and progress

Two graphs belong to the whole game, not to a map: arms and progress. They run once for the game, and their variables follow the player from map to map.

  • progress (70 nodes) keeps what the player has found: the three keycards (key_red, key_blue, key_yellow), the Furnace cores, the Foundry valves, secrets and kills. Maps talk to it with call and on_call. A map reports got_red; a red door asks try_red, and the game answers that map open_red or need_red. The seal asks try_seal, and opens only with three Cores. It also handles dying near a seam: a death is told to the map of whatever killed you, so the game passes it back to the map you died in (respawn) and fades the screen.
  • arms (81 nodes) is the weapon wheel and the quick swap, answering the module's own keys (below).

Talking to people

Six people are placed as props: mags at the Railhead, imre (Doc Imre) and pip in the hub, rusk in the Mine, vos in the Foundry and carrow in Old Town. A conversation is a chain in the map's story graph, with no panel:

  1. when_prop_used on the person: the player pressed use (E) on them.
  2. An action with set_animation state 1, so they talk.
  3. A branch on a variable such as talked_rusk: the first time, or again?
  4. subtitle nodes, one per line, with speaker set (Rusk), mode typed, and a sound for the voice (voice.rusk).
  5. set_animation state 0 again, and add_var to remember the talk.

Some people do more. Doc Imre heals you to full each time you bring a Core she has not seen. Pip gives one more hint each time you ask: four secrets, one per district, then "That's all I know. Honest." The subtitle line's look (font, colours, speaker colour) is set in 🎨 Style under 💬 subtitles.

The game's three panels are for the screens around the play: title, theend, and the arms wheel.

Taught in Talking to the world (typed subtitles).

The boss

The Smelter waits in w_heart_w, on an island in the lava. It is a monster like any other, in ☠ Monsters, with 2400 health and nine states: look, roar, chase, aim, volley, slam, brood, pain and death.

  • volley fires cinder_shell missiles, one every 10 ticks, each aimed a little differently. slam hits everything within 5.5 and throws it back, when the target is close.
  • Below half health (hp_below 50), brood spawns three husks, at most nine, every 600 ticks.

The fight's staging is the map's story graph. Stepping onto the island switches the music to the boss cue and turns on the HUD's boss bar (set_hud on boss_on, boss_hp, boss_hp_max). when_hurt sends its health to the bar each time it is hit. When it dies, the bar goes, the cavern shakes, the music turns to victory, and change_map goes to n_epilogue.

Taught in Recipes: five enemies (a boss with two phases).

First person, and keys of its own

  • 🎮 Controls: how it is played is first person (fps), so the camera is at the eyes and aims where the player looks.
  • Two keys of its own: arms on Tab (touch button ARMS) opens the weapon wheel, and swap on X (touch button SWAP) goes back to the weapon held before. The arms game graph answers both.
  • 🧍 Player: drawn by the warden prop. Its moves: move_speed 10, jump_impulse 7, one jump and no dash. It starts with the wrench.
  • 🔫 Weapons: Wrench, Revolver, Scattergun, Riveter and Flare Launcher, with four ammunition pools (bullets, shells, rivets, flares). Each has a first-person sprite (VM_WRENCH, VM_REVOLVER…) with an animation for each phase: the revolver cocks and reloads, the scattergun pumps, the riveter spins up and down. Each starts in a short draw phase (8 ticks, the wpn.draw sound) before it is ready, and the flare's blast throws you without hurting you.

Taught in Workshops: Controls and Phases in first person.

Saving

In ⚙ Settings, levels keep their state is on (persist levels in module.txt). A district that unloads keeps what happened in it, such as doors opened and things taken, and gets it back when it loads again. save and load games and survive a server restart are off. What the player has found across the whole game lives in the progress graph's variables.

Taught in Play and export and Variables, numbers and saving.

Fonts and panel skins

  • Two fonts in assets/ui/fonts/: Special Elite (typewriter, for the whole UI, subtitles and the objective) and Bebas Neue (for the HUD numbers, panels and buttons).
  • Two panel styles in 🎨 Style. rust uses four pictures from assets/ui/skins/, cut from Freedoom textures: panel.png, title.png, button.png and button_down.png (for a pressed button). wheel is a round, see-through box for the weapon wheel.
  • The HUD: a crosshair, health, the three keycards once found, the weapon and its ammunition, the Cores ({hud.cores}/3), the objective, and the boss bar, shown only during the fight.
  • The skies: a strip, SKYDUSK, four times round the horizon, for every map but the title screen, which has its own, SKYASH (sky menu strip SKYASH in module.txt).

Exercise: follow a keycard

  1. Open 🗺 Maps and scroll to ▦ metamap. Find w_oldtown, west of the hub.

  2. Open ◇ Graphs and tap world under w_oldtown. Find the note The red keycard: picking it up calls got_red.

  3. Go back and tap progress under the game's graphs. Find the on_call for got_red: it sets key_red and shows it on the HUD.

  4. Back in the list, open world under w_foundry_w. Find The red door: using it calls try_red, and on_call nodes for open_red and need_red answer.

  5. Open story under w_mine_e. Tap the first subtitle and read its speaker, mode and sound in the side panel.

  6. Open 🎮 Controls and read the two keys. Then open ▢ Panels and pick arms to see the wheel the arms key opens.

You pick up the red keycard in Old Town, then use the red door in the Foundry. How does the Foundry know you have the card?

  • Both maps read the same variable in the Foundry's graph
  • The door asks the game's progress graph (try_red), which kept the card and answers open_red
  • The keycard prop opens every red door by itself

A map's variables stay in that map. The game graph runs once for the whole game, so it is where the cards are kept, and maps ask it with calls.

Where is Rusk's conversation written?

  • In a panel with a button per line
  • In a Lua script
  • In w_mine_e's story graph, as typed subtitle nodes

People talk with subtitle nodes, after when_prop_used on them. Cinderfall has no hand-written scripts.

Why does the player walk from the hub into the Railhead without a load?

  • Both are cells of a metamap with one shared world on, and they share an open side
  • The hub's graph runs change_map at the edge
  • They are one map

With one shared world, maps in neighbouring cells join where both have an open side on the same line. change_map is only used for the title, the two scenes and the epilogue.
