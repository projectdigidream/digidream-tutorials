Palettes, dither, layers

Retro textures look right because they use a few colours and use them well. qPainter keeps you inside a palette, shades along ramps, blends with dither instead of new colours, and lets you build a texture in layers.

You'll learnthe palette panel, Lock and its two snaps, ramps and the Shade brush, the Dither brush and Dithered gradient, Replace, palette files, layers, and how to recolour a texture to a fixed palette Time25 minutes NeedsqPainter open on a texture (see Your first texture)

The palette panel

qPainter starts with Sweetie 16, a 16-colour palette. The palette is the first thing in the side panel, and stays there whatever tool you pick. At its top are the primary and secondary colour boxes, the 50% dither preview, and ⇄. Below the swatches is a row of buttons:

ButtonWhat it does
+Adds the primary colour to the palette.
−Removes the primary colour from the palette.
SortSorts the swatches from dark to light.
Import…Replace palette… or Add to palette… from a file. Adding skips colours already there.
Export…Saves the Whole palette, or only the Colours used in the texture.
ExtractReplaces the palette with this texture's own colours, reduced to the number you ask for.
Remap…Converts the active layer to the palette: nearest, ordered dither or Floyd–Steinberg.
  • Drag a swatch onto another to reorder the palette.
  • The line under the palette counts the colours the image uses, the colours in the palette, and the pixels that are off-palette. Before you ship a texture, off-palette should be 0.

Lock

Lock in the top bar restricts all painting to the palette. Any colour a tool makes is snapped to a palette colour, and a half-transparent result becomes fully opaque or fully transparent. While Lock is on, a menu appears beside it. It decides what happens to soft blends, such as the edge of a soft brush:

  • snap nearest: each blended pixel becomes the closest palette colour. You get hard bands.
  • snap dither: each blended pixel alternates between its two closest palette colours in a Bayer pattern. You get a dithered gradient, as if you had dithered it by hand.

Colours change as you paint? Lock is on, and your colour isn't in the palette. Add it with +, or turn Lock off.

Ramps and the Shade brush

A ramp is a row of colours ordered from dark to light. Ramps drive three things: the Shade brush, Noise with Use active ramp, and the Dithered gradient with Use active ramp.

You build a ramp from the row that starts with Ramp, under the palette buttons:

  • Hue-shift builds a ramp around the primary colour. The first number is how many steps on each side, so 2 makes five colours. The second is the hue shift per step, in degrees. Darker steps drift toward blue and lighter ones toward yellow, which keeps shading from looking flat.
  • Lerp builds a straight blend from primary to secondary, with 2 × steps + 1 colours.
  • New ramp colours are added to the palette for you.
  • Click a ramp to make it the active ramp; it is highlighted. × deletes it.

The Ramp shade brush (H) moves each pixel it touches one step lighter along its ramp. Right-click or Shift moves it one step darker. Each pixel changes at most once per stroke, so you build shading up with repeated strokes. A pixel whose colour isn't in any ramp is left alone, which is why the brush seems to do nothing on a texture with no ramps.

Dither

Dithering blends two colours by arranging pixels of both, instead of inventing a colour in between. qPainter has two tools for it.

The Dither brush

The Dither brush (D) paints a pattern of primary and secondary. Its options:

  • Pattern: Checker 50%, Bayer 2×2, Bayer 4×4, Bayer 8×8, Lines —, Lines |, Diagonal, or Custom (click to edit). For Custom, click the cells of the 8×8 preview to toggle them.
  • Density: how much of the pattern is primary. The Bayer patterns go smoothly from 0 to 100%.
  • Paint 2nd color too: off, only the primary pixels are painted and the art underneath shows through the gaps. On, the gaps get the secondary colour.
  • Offset X and Offset Y (0–7) shift the pattern. A checker at Offset X 1 is the exact inverse of the checker at 0.

Patterns are anchored to the canvas, not to the stroke. Overlapping strokes, symmetry copies and wrapped edges all line up. Fill with pattern on the Fill tool uses the same pattern settings.

The Dithered gradient

The Dithered gradient (J) fills from where you start dragging to where you stop. It goes from primary to secondary, or through the whole active ramp with Use active ramp. Radial makes it round, and a right-drag reverses it. It is always dithered with the current pattern, so it only uses the palette colours it blends between.

Replace and Smooth

  • The Replace color brush (R) recolours only the colour under the start of the stroke, and leaves every other colour alone. Tolerance widens what counts as the same colour. It is the quickest way to recolour one material, such as the moss on a stone wall.
  • The Smooth / AA helper (A) replaces each pixel with the palette colour nearest to the average of it and its four neighbours. Run it lightly along a jagged edge.

Exercise: a two-tone transition

  1. Pick two neighbouring colours of a ramp as primary and secondary. Press D and choose Bayer 4×4, with Paint 2nd color too off.

  2. Fill a band with the secondary colour. Set Density to 25% and paint across the band.

  3. Set Offset X to 1 and paint the same band again at 25%. The second pass lands on a different set of dots, and together they make a 50% band.

  4. Undo both passes, press J and drag across the band. The gradient does in one drag what the two passes did by hand.

Palette files

Import… and Export… read and write these formats:

FormatExportImportNotes
.gpl✓✓GIMP palette; also read by Aseprite, Krita and Inkscape.
.hex✓✓One rrggbb per line: Lospec's download format.
.pal✓✓JASC-PAL: Aseprite, Paint Shop Pro and many pixel-art tools.
.png✓ (swatches)✓A strip with one pixel per colour. On import, any image works: its colours in order, exact up to 256.
.texproj.json—✓Takes the palette of another qPainter project, such as one you took out of the studio with export project on a texture's card.
.aseprite / .ase—✓Takes the Aseprite file's palette.

Exporting Colours used in the texture looks at every layer and every frame, and skips hidden layers and transparent pixels. Palettes hold opaque colours only.

Import reads another file. Extract takes the colours of this texture's current frame. Any palette on Lospec downloads as .hex and imports as it is.

One palette for a set of textures

  1. Finish your first texture. Choose palette Export… → Colours used in the texture → .gpl (GIMP, Aseprite).

  2. In each new texture, choose Import… → Replace palette… with that file, and turn on Lock.

  3. For a texture that needs a few extra shades, use Import… → Add to palette…. It merges the shared colours in without duplicates.

  4. Before shipping, check the line under the palette: no off-palette pixels.

A palette shared by the whole module, kept in the module and picked from the studio, is planned but not started. For now, carry a palette between textures with palette files, as above.

Layers

The Layers section of the side panel works like layers anywhere:

  • + new layer, ⧉ duplicate, − delete, ↑ ↓ reorder, ⤓ merge down.
  • The checkbox shows or hides a layer. The slider on the active layer sets its opacity. Double-click a layer to rename it.
  • Tools paint on the active (highlighted) layer. Fill, Replace and Remap only look at the active layer. The eyedropper and the 3D preview see all visible layers together.

A useful split for a wall is a base layer (stone and noise), a detail layer (cracks, moss) and a light layer (highlights from the Shade brush). You can then hide the moss, or redo the lighting, without touching the stone.

When the studio bakes a texture, it composites the visible layers only. A layer you hid while working is left out of the game's picture. Show it again before you go back to the studio.

Recolour an existing texture

Turn a photo or someone else's texture into one that matches your palette.

  1. Open the texture: edit in qPainter on its card in the studio, or Open standalone.

  2. Palette Import… → Replace palette…, and pick the target palette: a .hex from Lospec, a .gpl, a .pal or a swatch PNG.

  3. Remap…: Floyd–Steinberg for photos and gradients, ordered dither for a retro look, nearest for flat art. With Wrap on, Floyd–Steinberg carries its error across the edges, so the result still tiles.

  4. Turn on Lock, so any touch-ups stay in the palette.

In Gem Hollow

Gem Hollow's textures each carry their own palette, the colours of that picture and no more: 3 for PLANKS, 5 for GRASS, 16 for CRATE and the HUD icons, 33 for LAVA and WATER, 60 for BRICKGREY and BRICKBROWN. Every one has a single layer, Kenney tile (Kenney icon on the HUD icons, Sky on SKYHILLS), so a new detail layer on top is yours to add or hide.

Open BRICKGREY and the line under the palette reads 60 colors used · 60 in palette: nothing off-palette. SKYHILLS is the exception. Its palette holds 16 colours but its soft clouds use 1617, so it reads 1615 off-palette. A sky is a smooth picture, not pixel art, so that is expected; don't turn on Lock there.

In Cinderfall

Cinderfall, the first-person shooter example, comes with the studio: on Home, under examples, press Cinderfall. The Cinderfall tour walks round it.

Cinderfall's 119 textures also carry their own palettes, made from their own pixels: from 3 colours (HUDCROSS, HUDSECRET) through 16 for many, such as TECHTAN, MACHINE2 and PIPEWALL, 26 for BRICKRED and 38 for ROCKBROWN, up to 59 for PIPES and RUSTPANEL. Each line under the palette reads N colors used · N in palette, the sky SKYDUSK included (21 colors used · 21 in palette): unlike Gem Hollow's soft sky, it is pixel art like everything else.

The Freedoom pictures were drawn for DOOM's one 256-colour palette, but after compositing and renaming they do not share one here: together the 105 Freedoom textures use 424 different colours. That leaves you free to pull any of them towards a look of your own, which is what the exercise below does.

Every texture has one layer: Freedoom NAME for the Freedoom ones (the original's name), Sky for the two skies, Icon for the HUD and weapon icons. Nothing is Locked.

Exercise: a hot rock wall in lava's colours

  1. Open Cinderfall from Home → examples and open Textures. Click LAVA and press export project: you get LAVA.texproj.json, 19 colours.

  2. Click STONEDARK, press duplicate and keep STONEDARK_2. Press edit in qPainter.

  3. Palette Import… → Replace palette…, and pick LAVA.texproj.json. Then Remap… → ordered dither. The stone is now drawn in lava's reds and yellows, and still tiles.

  4. Turn on Lock and add a few bright cracks with P: every stroke stays in the lava palette. Press studio and use it round the boss arena's lava.

Exercise: snowy grass from two Gem Hollow textures

  1. On Home, under examples, press Gem Hollow and open Textures.

  2. Click SNOWROCK and press export project. You now have SNOWROCK.texproj.json.

  3. Click GRASS, press duplicate and keep GRASS_2. Press edit in qPainter.

  4. Palette Import… → Replace palette…, and pick SNOWROCK.texproj.json. The swatches turn to SNOWROCK's greys and whites; the picture doesn't change yet.

  5. Remap… → nearest. The grass is now drawn in snow colours, and still tiles.

  6. Press + under Layers for a detail layer, and scatter a few white specks with N. Hide and show the layer to compare, leave it visible, and press studio.

Check yourself

You drag the Shade brush over your texture and nothing changes. Why?

  • Lock is off
  • The pixels' colours aren't in any ramp
  • The brush only works with Wrap on

The Shade brush steps a colour along its ramp. A colour that is in no ramp has nowhere to step to. Build a ramp with Hue-shift or Lerp first.

With Lock on, you want a soft brush to leave a dithered gradient rather than hard bands. What do you pick?

  • snap nearest
  • Pixel-perfect
  • snap dither

snap dither turns each blended pixel into a Bayer mix of its two nearest palette colours. snap nearest picks one colour, which gives bands.

You look for snap dither in qPainter's top bar, and it isn't there. Why?

  • It moved into the palette panel
  • Lock is off: the snap menu shows only while Lock is on
  • Only standalone qPainter has it

The top bar shows a control only while it applies. Tick Lock, and the snap menu appears beside it.

You want to hand a friend exactly the colours a finished texture uses. Which export?

  • Export… → Colours used in the texture
  • Export… → Whole palette
  • Extract

Colours used in the texture looks at every layer and frame, and writes only what is there. Extract changes your own palette; it doesn't write a file.
