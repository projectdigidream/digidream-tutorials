Your first texture

qPainter is the studio's pixel texture painter. You paint walls, floors, animated liquids and skies pixel by pixel, with a palette, dithering and a live 3D preview. This page gets a texture open and walks you through the screen and the tools.

Two ways to run qPainter

qPainter is the same app in both places. What changes is who owns the file.

From the studio

Open 🧱 Textures from the rail or from Home. The page has two lists:

  • 🧱 the module's own (N): the textures that belong to this module and travel in its pack.
  • the app's (N): the textures that come with the engine build. A map may use any of them, but they are not yours to edit. The box under the heading filters them by name.

Above them are two buttons:

  • + new texture asks for a name and opens qPainter on an empty texture. A name is capital letters, digits and _, up to 24 characters. Anything else is dropped, so my wall-2 becomes MYWALL2.
  • + import texture (PNG) brings in a PNG you already have.

Click one of the module's own textures to open its card. The card shows the size and where the texture is used, the cut out (see-through where transparent) switch, and whether it is animated (animate… or cut & speed…, see Animation and skies). Its buttons are edit in qPainter, open with… (when a plugin can open it too), duplicate, the export buttons, rename and delete. A rename follows the texture onto every map surface that uses it.

Duplicate and export

  • duplicate asks for the copy's name and offers the first free NAME_2, NAME_3 … A taken name is refused. The copy takes every file the texture is made of: the PNG, its settings (cut out, animation) and its qPainter project, so it is a variant you can change without touching the original. Its card opens next.
  • export PNG sends NAME.png out of the studio, and export project sends NAME.texproj.json, which standalone qPainter opens. The export goes through the app's share sheet, or downloads in a browser. A texture imported as a PNG and never edited has no project, so it shows export PNG alone.

On a new texture qPainter shows a New texture dialog. You can't close it without choosing, because the studio is waiting for a texture.

  • Kind: Texture, Skybox: strip (DOOM sky), Skybox: panorama (2:1) or Skybox: cube (six faces). Skies are covered in Animation and skies.
  • Size: a square from 16 × 16 to 1024 × 1024 (64 is chosen first), or W × H… for any width and height.
  • Create makes it.

Two messages can stop Create. "Sizes go from 1 to 4096." means a side is out of range. "Use an even size." appears for a sky whose size is odd.

On its own

From a checkout of qPainter:

./start.sh          # dev server on port 5173, opens your browser
./start.sh prod     # production build with offline support

The script also prints a Network URL, such as http://192.168.1.20:5173/. Open it on a phone on the same Wi-Fi to paint with touch or a stylus. ./build-apk.sh builds an Android app, qPainter.apk, which you can copy to the phone and install. qPainter also installs as a web app from any HTTPS host.

On its own, qPainter starts with the texture you had open last. The New… menu in the top bar offers the square sizes 16 to 1024, W×H… for any size up to 4096, and three skyboxes. A new texture keeps your palette, ramps and settings.

Standalone qPainter saves every change to its Recent list about a second after you make it. Recent lives in the browser's storage, though, so clearing site data loses it. Save anything you want to keep to a file.

The screen

qPainter has three parts around the canvas.

The top bar

From left to right:

  • File actions (standalone only): New…, Open, Recent, the texture's name (click it to rename; a • after it means unsaved changes), Save and Save as….
  • Export…, then ↶ and ↷ for undo and redo (200 steps).
  • Seams: Wrap, Tiles, Offset ½, and the symmetry menu (No sym to start with). See Seamless tiling.
  • Palette lock: Lock and snap nearest / snap dither. See Palettes, dither, layers.
  • View: Grid, the tile grid (no tile grid, 4 to 64), Fit and ☰.

On a narrow screen the top bar scrolls sideways.

The tool bar

The tool bar holds the sixteen tools, one button each. Hover a button to see its name and key.

The side panel

The side panel shows, from the top:

  • Tool options for the current tool. Only the controls that tool uses are shown.
  • Palette, then Frames, then Layers.
  • 3D preview: your texture on a cube, a wall or your own model, updated as you paint.

Tab or ☰ hides and shows the panel. On a phone, the tool bar moves to the bottom. A thin bar under the top bar keeps ↶ ↷, the frame controls and ☰ in reach. ☰ opens the panel over the canvas, and ✕ Close, a swipe right, the back gesture or a tap outside closes it.

Grid lines appear by themselves once you zoom in to 8× or more. Grid turns them off. The tile grid draws a guide every 4 to 64 pixels, which helps when you lay out an atlas.

Colours

Every tool paints the primary colour with the left button and the secondary colour with the right.

  • Click a swatch in the palette to make it primary. Right-click it, or long-press it on touch, to make it secondary.
  • The two colour boxes at the top of the palette open the system colour picker.
  • ⇄ or X swaps primary and secondary.
  • The small checkered strip beside them shows the two colours mixed in a 50% dither, so you can judge the blend before you paint it.
  • The eyedropper is I. You can also hold Alt with any tool except Move. It samples what you see, all visible layers together. Right-click picks into the secondary colour.

The sixteen tools

Every tool honours Wrap, symmetry, the selection and Lock. You don't switch these on per tool.

ToolKeyWhat it does
✏️ PencilPHard-edged pixels. Pixel-perfect (at size 1) removes the extra "elbow" pixel on diagonals.
🖌️ Soft brushBA soft round brush with Hardness and Opacity.
🧽 EraserEErases to transparent, soft or hard.
▦ Dither brushDPaints a pattern of primary and secondary: checker, Bayer, lines, diagonal or your own.
◐ Ramp shade brushHMoves each pixel one step lighter along its ramp. Right-click or Shift makes it darker.
⁘ Noise / scatterNSprinkles random pixels at a Density, for grain, dirt and stone.
⇄ Replace color brushRRecolours only the colour under the start of the stroke.
≈ Smooth / AA helperAHand anti-aliasing: each pixel becomes the palette colour nearest to the average of it and its neighbours.
╱ LineLA straight line. Shift snaps it to 0°, 45° or 90°.
▭ RectangleUAn outline, or Filled. Shift makes a square.
◯ EllipseCAn outline, or Filled. Shift makes a circle.
🪣 FillGFlood fill, with Tolerance, Contiguous and Fill with pattern.
▤ Dithered gradientJDrag from start to end for a gradient that only uses the colours it blends between.
💧 EyedropperI or hold AltPicks a colour.
⬚ SelectMA rectangular selection. While one exists, painting only changes what is inside it.
✥ MoveV or OMoves the selection. With nothing selected, it drags the whole layer, wrapping at the edges.

Line, Rectangle and Ellipse show a live preview while you drag, and commit when you let go. The ramp, dither and gradient tools get a page of their own: Palettes, dither, layers.

Brush size

[ and ] change the size, from 1 to 64. The Size slider in the tool options does the same. Each tool remembers its own size: the soft brush starts at 8, the eraser at 4, and the rest at 1. A pen's pressure can drive size or opacity (Pressure → size, Pressure → opacity). A mouse or finger always paints at full pressure.

Selections

  • ⌘A selects everything. Esc deselects.
  • With the Move tool, drag inside a selection to lift its pixels as a floating selection. Hold Alt as you start the drag to copy them instead of cutting.
  • Enter puts the floating pixels down. Esc puts everything back where it was.
  • The Select and Move tools show Copy, Cut, Paste, Delete, Flip ↔, Flip ↕, Rotate ⟳, All and None.

Painting does nothing? A selection is probably active, and you are painting outside it. Press Esc. Also check that the active layer is visible.

Exercise: a first brick

  1. In the studio, open 🧱 Textures, press + new texture and type BRICK1. In the New texture dialog, keep Texture, choose 32 × 32 and press Create. Standalone, choose 32 from New… instead.

  2. Press 0 to fit the canvas. Pick a dark red as primary and a darker one as secondary, then press G and click the canvas to fill it.

  3. Press X to swap the colours. Press L and draw the mortar lines, holding Shift to keep them straight: two horizontal rows, with the vertical joints staggered between them.

  4. Press N for Noise. Set Density low and scatter a little grain over the bricks.

  5. Press Tab twice to hide and bring back the side panel. Then look at the cube in the 3D preview.

  6. In the studio, press ‹ studio. The texture is saved as a project and baked to assets/textures/BRICK1.png, and its card appears under 🧱 the module's own (N). Standalone, press ⌘S and pick where to save it.

The studio always keeps what qPainter returns as a project, sources/textures/NAME.texproj.json, with its layers and frames, and bakes the PNG from it. The PNG is a composite of the visible layers. A texture that started as an imported PNG becomes a project the first time you edit it in qPainter.

Exercise: a variant of a Gem Hollow wall

  1. On Home, under Examples, press 📂 Gem Hollow, then open 🧱 Textures. You work on the example's own copy, so the bundled one stays as it was.

  2. Click BRICKGREY, press duplicate and accept the offered name, BRICKGREY_2. Its card opens.

  3. Press edit in qPainter. Tick Tiles to see the bricks repeat, then use R (Replace color brush) to recolour the light stones a warmer grey.

  4. Press ‹ studio. BRICKGREY is unchanged; BRICKGREY_2 is the new tile.

  5. On BRICKGREY_2's card, press export PNG and then export project. You now have BRICKGREY_2.png and BRICKGREY_2.texproj.json outside the studio.

In Cinderfall

New, and still being tested. Cinderfall is a second example, a first-person shooter. It is not bundled with the studio yet: make its pack with node tutorials/tools/pack-example.mjs cinderfall and bring it in with Home → ⇩ import pack. The Cinderfall tour walks round it.

Cinderfall has 122 textures, every one a qPainter project in sources/textures/. Gem Hollow's were painted from Kenney tiles; Cinderfall's are almost all Freedoom's, the free replacement for DOOM's art, so they have DOOM's sizes and DOOM's look:

Exercise: a sooty variant of a Cinderfall wall

  1. Import Cinderfall and open 🧱 Textures. Click BRICKRED, press duplicate and keep BRICKRED_2.

  2. Press edit in qPainter. The layer reads Freedoom BRICK11; Wrap is already on. Tick Tiles.

  3. Pick the darkest grey of the mortar as the primary colour. With R (Replace color brush), paint over the lower rows of bricks: they turn to soot, and the strokes run on across the edges into the tiled copies.

  4. Press ‹ studio. Put BRICKRED_2 on the lower wall of a room, BRICKRED above it.

Practise the keys

The trainer names a tool, and you press its key.

Check yourself

You opened a texture from the studio and want to keep your changes. Where is Save?

  • In the top bar, as always
  • There is none in the studio: ‹ studio saves and goes back
  • Under Export…

Inside the studio, qPainter is a guest. The texture belongs to the module, so the file buttons are hidden, and going back to the studio is what saves it.

Which key gives you the Dithered gradient?

  • G
  • D
  • J

G is Fill and D is the Dither brush. The gradient is J.

You type moss wall as the name of a new texture in the studio. What is it called?

  • MOSSWALL
  • moss_wall
  • Nothing: the studio refuses the name

The name is turned to capitals, and anything other than A–Z, 0–9 and _ is dropped, up to 24 characters.
