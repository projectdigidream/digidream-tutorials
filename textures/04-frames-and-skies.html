Animation and skies

A texture doesn't have to stand still. Lava can churn, water can ripple and a light can flicker, all from frames you paint in qPainter. qPainter also paints the sky in the engine's own formats, and its 3D preview shows the texture on a wall, a model or all round you.

You'll learnframes and playback, the Export… menu, how the studio cuts a sprite sheet and writes its sheet line, the three kinds of sky, and the 3D preview Time30 minutes NeedsqPainter open on a texture, and for the studio parts a module open in the studio

Frames

Every texture can have several frames. A texture with one frame works as before. Frames share the layers: each layer has one image per frame, and a layer's visibility, opacity and order apply to all of its frames. Every tool paints on the current frame, and undo jumps back to the frame an edit was made in.

The Export… menu

Export… writes a copy of the texture without changing which file it is linked to. Inside the studio it downloads the file.

Animated textures in the game

The game plays an animated wall or floor from one picture holding every frame, a sprite sheet. A line in the module's assets/animations.txt says how to cut it:

sheet LAVA1 vertical 4 fps 10
  • LAVA1 is the texture the map uses, assets/textures/LAVA1.png.
  • vertical or horizontal is how the frames are laid out.
  • 4 is the number of frames, 2 to 256. The frames are equal: the picture's height (or width) divided by that number.
  • fps 10 is the speed. It is optional; without it the speed is 8 frames a second. The animation always loops.

Each frame tiles across a wall exactly as an ordinary texture of the frame's size would. A 64×256 sheet of 4 frames tiles like a 64×64 texture.

You don't write these lines yourself. The studio writes animations.txt from the module's textures, in one of two ways.

A texture painted in qPainter

When you go back to the studio with an animated texture, the studio bakes it as a vertical sheet, sets it to play at the texture's FPS, and writes its line. You only paint the frames and set the speed.

A sheet you import or set by hand

On a texture's card, a still picture shows animate…, and an animated one shows cut & speed…. + import texture (PNG) opens the same dialog, with the name editable and import in place of save. The dialog has:

  • cut: a still picture, frames stacked ↓ (vertical) or frames side by side → (horizontal). When the picture's shape suggests a sheet, for example one exactly four squares tall, the dialog proposes it.
  • frames, or each frame, px: set either one, and the other follows.
  • frames a second.
  • Two previews: the picture, cut, with yellow cut lines and the frame being shown outlined, and a frame, tiled as on a wall, which plays.
  • A warning in red when the picture doesn't divide into that many equal frames. The pixels left over are shaded red and are not shown in the game.
  • cancel, and save (or import).

Try it on a small lava sheet drawn right here. It is 16 pixels wide and four frames tall. The line under the previews is the one the studio would write. It leaves fps out when the speed is the default, 8.

  1. Leave the cut on frames stacked ↓ (vertical) with 4 frames. The tile on the right churns, and the line reads sheet LAVA1 vertical 4.

  2. Move frames a second to 12. The line gains fps 12.

  3. Set frames to 3. The 64 pixels don't divide by 3, so the last pixel row is shaded red and the warning says so. Each "frame" now cuts across two of the real ones, and the tile stutters. Set it back to 4.

  4. Try frames side by side → (horizontal). The frames become 4-pixel strips: the wrong cut for this sheet. Then choose a still picture: there is no line, and the game would show the whole sheet, standing still.

The card says what a texture is: a still picture, or for example 🎞 animated: 4 frames stacked, 8 a second. Only the map's own textures animate. A picture put up later by a switch or a graph is cut too, but does not move.

Skyboxes

qPainter paints skies in exactly the formats and orientation the engine uses. What its 3D preview shows is what the game draws.

KindThe pictureSettings
Strip (DOOM's sky)One picture of any size, wrapped round the horizon. DOOM's are 256×128. The left edge is at north, and it runs clockwise. The horizon is 100/128 of the way down.Repeat (copies round the horizon, default 4), Height (default 0.625, about 32° up), Yaw
PanoramaOne 2:1 equirectangular picture. The centre column faces north, east is at 3/4, west at 1/4, and south is at both edges.Yaw
CubeSix square faces, edited as a cross: east, south, west and north across the middle, up above south and down below it. Each face is upright as seen from inside, facing it.Yaw

Yaw turns the sky, in turns: 0.25 is a quarter turn anticlockwise, seen from above.

Starting a sky

  • In the studio: + new texture, then in the New texture dialog pick Skybox: strip (DOOM sky), Skybox: panorama (2:1) or Skybox: cube (six faces). You give the width for a strip or panorama (the height is half), or the face size for a cube (the cross is 4×3 faces). The size must be even.
  • Standalone: New… → Strip — DOOM sky…, Panorama (2:1)… or Cube (six faces)….
  • From a picture you have: open it, then choose the kind in 3D preview → Skybox. A cube must already be a 4×3 cross of square faces.
  • From six face pictures: Import faces… (shown for cube skies) builds the cross from six PNGs named …_east, _west, _north, _south, _up, _down, or three.js's px nx py ny pz nz.

Painting a sky

  • Guides mark the way: cube faces are outlined and labelled EAST … DOWN, a panorama shows its horizon and compass lines, and a strip shows its horizon and where north, east, south and west fall.
  • Cube edges are seamless. A stroke that crosses a face's edge continues on the face next to it on the cube, turned the right way, even if that face sits elsewhere in the cross. Paint in the unused corners of the cross is dropped.
  • Strips and panoramas wrap left to right. On a panorama, painting over the top or bottom continues over the pole.
  • Symmetry works on strips and panoramas, and is off for cubes.
  • A strip fades, above and below the picture, into the average colour of its top and bottom 8 rows. Keep those rows calm.

For a sky, the 3D preview puts you at its centre. Drag to look round, and use the wheel to zoom. Tick Paint to paint straight onto the sky where you are looking. This is the easiest way to work across cube edges or near the poles.

Getting the sky into the game

Standalone, Export to engine… asks for a texture name and the level the sky is for: a level name such as map12, or default for every level without a sky of its own. Then it asks for a folder:

  • Pick the module's folder, the one with module.txt. The pictures go into assets/textures/, a cube's as NAME_east.png … NAME_down.png. module.txt gets the matching line, for example sky map12 strip NAME repeat 2. A sky line for that level is replaced; nothing else changes.
  • Pick any other folder, and only the pictures are written. qPainter shows the line for you to add.

Only the current frame is exported, since skies don't animate. Settings are written only where they differ from the engine's defaults.

In the studio, a strip or panorama you paint is an ordinary module texture. Open 🌌 Sky, pick the kind, and type the texture's name. See Sky, screens and weather.

The studio's Sky page takes a cube as six separate textures, one per face. qPainter keeps a cube as one cross. For a cube, use standalone qPainter's Export to engine…, which writes six face pictures, then import them into the module or name them in 🌌 Sky.

The 3D preview

The preview at the bottom of the side panel updates as you paint. Drag to orbit; scroll or pinch to zoom.

ControlWhat it does
Shapecube, pyramid, sphere, cylinder, torus, plane, wall, or custom once you have loaded a model.
RepeatTiles the texture 1× to 4× across the surface.
NearestCrisp pixels. Off, the texture is smoothed.
LitDirectional light. Off, flat colours.
SpinTurns the shape slowly.
PaintPaint on the shape with the current tool. Left-drag (one finger) paints; right-drag (two fingers) turns the view.
Model…Loads your own .glb, single-file .gltf or .obj. It must have UV coordinates.
Colour boxThe background colour.
  • The cube and the pyramid show the whole texture on every face. The sphere repeats it twice round the equator, so the pixels stay square.
  • The wall is a large plane tiled 3×3: the best check for seams and for animated textures.
  • When painting on a model, a stroke that crosses a seam in the model's UVs is split, instead of drawing a line across the texture. Brushes and dither work best; shapes and selections are meant for the flat canvas.

Exercise: an animated lava floor

  1. Make a 32 × 32 texture called LAVA1, and turn on Wrap.

  2. Build a hot ramp with Hue-shift around an orange. Fill with the middle colour, then add blobs of the lighter colours with N and the pencil.

  3. Press ⧉ three times for four frames. On each frame, shift the blobs a little, using the Move tool (V) with nothing selected to drag the whole layer a pixel or two.

  4. Set FPS to 6, set the 3D shape to wall, and press F. Fix any frame that jumps, using Onion skin.

  5. Press ‹ studio. On LAVA1's card, check that it says 🎞 animated: 4 frames stacked, 6 a second. Put it on a floor and press ▶ Play.

Check yourself

A 32 × 128 picture holds four 32 × 32 frames, one above the other, to play at 8 frames a second. Which line does the studio write?

  • sheet NAME horizontal 4
  • sheet NAME vertical 4
  • sheet NAME vertical 128 fps 8

The frames are stacked, so the layout is vertical, and the count is the number of frames, not pixels. 8 is the default speed, so fps is left out.

In a strip sky, where is north?

  • At the picture's left edge, with the picture running clockwise
  • In the centre column
  • At both edges

A strip starts at north on its left edge. The centre column is north on a panorama, whose edges are south.

Which 3D preview shape is the best check that an animated texture tiles?

  • sphere
  • cube
  • wall

The wall is tiled 3×3 in perspective, so you see seams in space and, while it plays, in time.
