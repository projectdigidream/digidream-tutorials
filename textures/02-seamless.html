Seamless tiling

A wall in the game repeats its texture over and over. If the right edge doesn't match the left, every repeat shows a line. qPainter's seam tools let you paint across the edges as if they weren't there, and check the result while you work.

You'll learnWrap, Tiles and Offset ½; the six symmetry modes; a full seamless stone tile; and what cut out does in the studio Time20 minutes NeedsqPainter open on a texture (see Your first texture)

Why seams show

A texture tiles by placing copies side by side. The last column of one copy touches the first column of the next, and the bottom row touches the top row. A crack that runs off the right edge has to come back in on the left edge, or the wall gets a cut in it every 32 or 64 pixels. Seams are hard to see on the single picture you are painting, and easy to see on a wall in the game.

The three seam controls

They sit together in the top bar.

  • Wrap (W) makes every tool wrap round the edges. A stroke that leaves the right edge continues from the left, and one that leaves the bottom continues from the top. This applies to brushes, lines, shapes, fills, dither patterns, moved selections and symmetry copies.
  • Tiles shows the texture repeated 3×3 around the real one while Wrap is on. The copies are dimmed, so you can tell which is the real one. You see seams as you paint.
  • Offset ½ shifts the current layer by half its width and half its height. The four edges then meet in the middle of the canvas, where you can paint over them. Press it again to shift back. It can be undone like any edit.

Two more things help:

  • The Move tool with nothing selected drags the whole layer, wrapping at the edges. It is a free-form Offset ½.
  • In the 3D preview, the wall shape is a large plane tiled 3×3 in perspective. It is the closest thing to a wall in the game.

Try it here

This is a 16×16 tile with the 3×3 repeat beside it. The stones in it stop short of the edges, so the repeat looks fine at the start.

  1. Turn Wrap off. Pick the lightest grey, size 2, and draw a crack from the middle out past the right edge. In the repeat, the crack stops dead at the tile's edge.

  2. Press ↶ Undo, turn Wrap on and draw the same crack. It comes back in on the left, and in the repeat it runs on unbroken.

  3. Press Offset ½. The tile's corners are now in the middle. Paint a stone right across the centre, then press Offset ½ again. The stone is split across all four edges, and the repeat joins it back up.

The dither brush in the demo paints a checker that is anchored to the canvas, not to the stroke. qPainter's patterns are anchored the same way, so they line up across the seam. A pattern can't tile cleanly if its size doesn't divide the texture's, and qPainter shows a ⚠ beside the pattern when that happens. On power-of-two textures (16, 32, 64…), use the 2, 4 or 8 px patterns.

Symmetry

The menu after Offset ½ picks a symmetry mode. S steps through them in this order:

ModeWhat you get
No symOne stroke, where you paint it.
Mirror XA mirror copy, left ↔ right.
Mirror YA mirror copy, top ↔ bottom.
Mirror XYFour-way: both mirrors at once.
DiagonalA copy mirrored across the diagonal.
Radial2 to 12 rotated copies. Set the count in the number box beside the menu.
  • The axes are drawn as dashed pink lines.
  • ⌘+click (Ctrl+click on Windows and Linux) moves the symmetry centre. It snaps to pixel edges and pixel centres. ⊕ puts it back in the middle.
  • Symmetry works with every painting tool, fills and shapes included, and together with Wrap. The Dithered gradient ignores it.

Symmetry and Wrap work together. With Mirror X and Wrap on, a mirrored stroke that runs off one edge wraps too. That makes it quick to paint ornaments, grates and floor tiles that are symmetric and seamless.

Exercise: a seamless stone tile

This is qPainter's own worked example, step by step.

  1. Make a 32 × 32 texture. Turn on Wrap (W) and check Tiles is on. In the 3D preview, set the shape to wall.

  2. Pick a mid-grey as primary. In the palette panel, press Hue-shift to build a five-colour ramp around it (2 steps each side), then click the ramp to make it active.

  3. G: fill the canvas with the middle colour of the ramp.

  4. N: Noise with Use active ramp on, at about 30% density. Scatter texture over everything.

  5. P: pencil the cracks and stone outlines. Paint across the edges freely. The 3×3 tiles and the wall show any seam at once.

  6. H: the Shade brush. Drag to light the stone tops, and Shift-drag to darken the edges below them.

  7. Press Offset ½. Fix anything that looks wrong in the middle, where the edges now meet. Press Offset ½ again.

  8. Standalone, choose Export… → PNG 1×. In the studio, press ‹ studio.

Cut out: holes in a texture

A texture can have transparent pixels: the gaps in a grate, a fence or a window frame. By default the game ignores transparency and draws those pixels solid, not as holes. On the texture's card in the studio's 🧱 Textures page, turn on cut out (see-through where transparent), and the game lets you see through every pixel that is less than half opaque.

  • Paint the holes with the Eraser (E) at full hardness. A pixel is either a hole or solid, so a feathered edge only turns ragged.
  • Leave cut out off for ordinary solid walls and floors.

A grate is usually symmetric and must tile. Use Mirror XY with Wrap on, and erase one quarter of the holes. The other three quarters appear by themselves.

In Gem Hollow

Gem Hollow's projects keep their seam setting. The 20 floor, wall, door and liquid textures, STONE, DIRT, GRASSEDGE, BRICKGREY, PLANKS, LAVA and the rest, are saved with Wrap on, so they open ready to paint across the edges. The four HUD icons and the sky SKYHILLS have it off: they are never repeated side by side.

The same split shows on the studio's cards. HUDCOIN, HUDGEM, HUDHEART and HUDKEY have cut out (see-through where transparent) on, so their transparent corners stay holes. Every floor and wall leaves it off.

Exercise: a crack across STONE's edge

  1. On Home, under Examples, press 📂 Gem Hollow. In 🧱 Textures, click STONE and press duplicate, keeping STONE_2, so the stone on the map stays as it is.

  2. Press edit in qPainter. Wrap is already ticked; tick Tiles and set the 3D preview to wall.

  3. Press Offset ½. The tile's edges now cross in the middle of the canvas.

  4. With P and a dark grey from the palette, draw a crack straight through the middle cross. Watch it continue on the dimmed copies.

  5. Press Offset ½ again. The crack now runs off one edge and back in on the other, with no seam on the wall.

  6. Press ‹ studio, then export PNG on STONE_2's card if you want the tile outside the studio.

Check yourself

You want to repaint the seam of a finished tile where the edges meet. What is the quickest way?

  • Turn on Tiles and paint on the dimmed copies
  • Offset ½, paint over the middle, then Offset ½ again
  • Export at PNG 2× and crop the middle

Offset ½ brings the four edges into the centre of the canvas. Pressing it a second time puts everything back, and the repair is now on the edges.

You press S while the symmetry menu says Mirror XY. What do you get?

  • No sym
  • Radial
  • Diagonal

The order is No sym, Mirror X, Mirror Y, Mirror XY, Diagonal, Radial, and then back to No sym.

You painted a fence with transparent gaps, but in the game the gaps are drawn solid. What do you change?

  • Turn on cut out (see-through where transparent) on the texture's card in the studio
  • Turn on Lock in qPainter
  • Erase the gaps again with a softer eraser

Transparency only becomes holes in the game when the texture is marked cut out. That setting belongs to the module, so it is on the studio's card, not in qPainter.
