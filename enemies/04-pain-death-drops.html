Pain, death and drops

A fight is mostly the player hitting things. How a monster answers a hit tells the player whether it worked: a flinch, a scream, a body on the floor, a medikit where it fell. This lesson covers everything that happens between the first hit and the last, plus the monster's own numbers and its voice.

You'll learnthe order of events when a hit lands; how the flinch chance and the flinch's length work, and how a flinch goes back to what it interrupted; how death, corpses and revival work; drops and weighted choices; the brain's own numbers under Its numbers; and how to give a monster sounds Time35 minutes NeedsGem Hollow open in the studio; for the last exercise, the grunt from the earlier lessons, a pickup prop in the module (a medikit, say), and a sound pack with a few monster sounds (Mix, test and ship)

When a hit lands

Hits do not wait for the monster's next decision. The moment one lands, the engine does this, in order:

  1. Take the health. Health on Overview (hp) is what it starts with. The engine itself has no idea of health; this number belongs to the brain.
  2. Dead? At 0 or below, it dies at once. See Death.
  3. Infighting. If another monster did it and Fights others is on, that monster becomes its target.
  4. The flinch roll. It may flinch. See below.

The hit is also remembered for the brain's own conditions: it was hurt in the last … s (was_hurt), another monster hurt it (hurt_by_mob) and its health is below …% (hp_below, of its starting health).

Give it was hurt a window. A monster only decides every 8 ticks (0.13 s), so a window shorter than that almost never sees the hit. Keep it at 0.13 s or more; the editor starts it at 0.3 s.

The brain's own numbers are on Advanced, under Its brain: blood, pain, drops and the rest, in plain words: initial state, Health, Notices a target within (m), Flinch chance (of 256), Turns a second, Hops up (m), Flies, Hovers above the target (m), Fights back at monsters, Blood (particle kind), Leaves a corpse and Weight; then the flinch and drops. Several of them are also on Overview, the same numbers.

Fights others, when it was never set. Overview shows Fights others ticked for a monster that never set it, such as Gem Hollow's slime, but the engine only lets a monster fight back when it is switched on. Every recipe switches it on. For a monster of your own making, check that Fights back at monsters is on here.

Pain: the flinch

A flinch tells the player a hit landed, and gives them a moment. It happens only when all of these are true:

  • the brain has a state called exactly pain (the first row of Whatever it is doing says so);
  • its flinch chance is set (Flinches on Overview; empty or 0 means it never flinches);
  • the state it is in now has Pain can cut it short on;
  • a roll from 0 to 255 comes out below its Flinch chance (of 256).

So the chance is out of 256; Overview shows it as a percentage of hits. Doom's own numbers are good guides: an imp 200 (78%: it flinches most of the time), a baron 50 (20%), a cyberdemon 20 (8%). Half the time is 128. Keep it well under 100% for a monster that matters: one that nearly always flinches can be held in pain forever by a fast weapon.

The pain state itself is a normal state: it plays the pain animation, stands still, faces where it was, and lasts a moment. Then it goes where its Then goes to says. Every recipe's pain goes back to what it was doing.

Back to what it was doing

A pain that then goes back to what it was doing (in the files, next = "@previous") returns to the state the hit interrupted and resumes it where it was: its time in that state and its place among its steps. So:

  • a hit during a barrage does not fire the barrage again from the start, and a hit during a summon does not summon twice;
  • a boss in its third phase stays in its third phase: the flinch does not send it back to a chase of phase 1;
  • a monster hit while it was standing guard goes back to standing guard, rather than charging.

Any state entered as a reaction can end this way, not only pain: the editor groups such states under Reactions. A pain may instead go to a state of its own: to chase, as Gem Hollow's do, or to a flee state for a coward. The preview's Flinch button shows which.

Attacks that cannot be interrupted

Switch Pain can cut it short off on an attack state, and a hit during it never causes a flinch: "a hit lands but it never flinches here. Dying always interrupts." The monster finishes its blow. Use it for a big wind-up the player must dodge rather than stagger. Its card says (never flinches here).

A bigger hit, a longer flinch

By default every flinch lasts as long as the pain state does, whether a pistol or a rocket caused it. On Advanced, under the flinch, the switch a bigger hit, a longer flinch adds a share of each hit's damage. Switching it on writes a pain_time with two fields, in ticks:

FieldStarts atWhat it does
per_damage0.5Extra ticks per point of damage. 0.5 is one tick for every two points.
max40, or three times the pain state's ticks if that is moreThe longest a flinch may last. Always keep one: without it, one big hit is a stun-lock.

The flinch is the pain state's ticks, plus the damage times per_damage (rounded down), capped at max. The section works it out for you: "A flinch lasts 10 ticks (60 a second); a 10-point hit 15, a 50-point 35, a 100-point 40." Without a pain state, it says the monster never flinches, and offers nothing to switch.

A graph can change a monster's base flinch while the game runs: set_var on its pain_ticks property. A boss that grows tougher as it is hurt can shorten its flinches this way. It applies from its next flinch.

Death

When its health reaches 0, these happen at once, not on the next decision:

  1. It stops blocking, so you can walk over it, and forgets its target.
  2. It enters the state called death.
  3. The steps of death at 0 s run, once: a scream, a last burst, a final brood.
  4. Its drops are rolled and placed.
  5. With Leaves a body on (corpse), the body stays. With it off, the body is removed straight away.

The dead do not think. After that moment the monster never decides again. Steps of death at any later time never run, and its rules are never tried. Every recipe's death reads "plays death, stands still and faces where it was, for 1.67 s and stays dead", with Blocks off: a state that ends and stays. Keep that shape.

Without a body, there is no death animation to see. The body is removed in the same moment it dies. Leave Leaves a body on for anything with a death animation. The world keeps at most 32 bodies and recycles the oldest, so a long fight does not fill the map.

A body can come back. The step raise a corpse within … m (revive), run by another monster, puts bodies within that radius back on their feet. A monster that leaves no body leaves nothing to raise.

Drops

A drop is what a monster leaves where it dies. It is rolled on the server the moment it dies, so every player sees the same result. On Advanced, under drops:

  • + drop adds an entry with an item: a catalogue name, dropped every time. count drops several, spread round the body.
  • + weighted choice turns the entry into one of several choices, each with an item and a weight. Exactly one choice drops. A choice with no item drops nothing, which is how you make a drop a chance. ✕ choice removes a choice; ✕ drop the whole entry.
  • Under the choices, the section says how often something drops: "Something drops 25% of the time."

A medikit one time in four is two choices: medikit with weight 1, and nothing with weight 3. In the files:

drops = {
  { item = "ammo_clip" },                                    -- always
  { one_of = { { item = "medikit", weight = 1 }, { weight = 3 } } },  -- one in four
}

An item is a prop, by name. To make it give the player something when they walk over it, it must also be a pickup: a pickup is named by a prop, and walking over that prop grants it. Pickups are set up on the weapons' Arsenal (Ammo, pickups and the arsenal). A dropped item works exactly like a placed one: a dropped clip gives a full clip.

If the item's name is not in the catalogue, nothing drops, and the server prints once that the monster could not drop it. The item box suggests names as you type; use them, and check the spelling.

Its numbers: the brain's own memory

A number (the files call it a var) is a whole number each monster keeps for itself. Every monster of the kind gets its own copy when it spawns. On Advanced, Its numbers lists them: "Numbers it remembers, like which phase it is in or a cooldown. One exists as soon as a step sets it or a rule checks it: add a "set the number" step to make one."

  • Each number says where it is used, such as checked in chase; a cooldown started in attack.
  • starts at sets the value it starts with, 0 unless you say.

You never declare a number by hand: name it in a step or a condition, and it exists. Numbers hold three kinds of thing:

KindWritten byRead by
a deadlinestart cooldown … for … s (set_cooldown)cooldown … is over (cooldown_ready)
a count or a flagset the number … to … (set_var), add … to the number … (add_var), make … a random number from … to … (var_random)the number … is …, is not … (var_is), is at least … (var_at_least)
a headingpick a heading … into the number … (set_angle)a state that wanders (the number called wander)

Examples: a shots count that sends a gunner to reload after six; a phase number for a boss; a roll from make roll a random number from 1 to 100 that three rules read, so one roll picks one of three attacks.

A saved game keeps them. A monster comes back from a save in the state it was in, where it was in it, with its numbers, and its cooldowns as the time they had left. So a boss's "only once" change of phase is not undone by a reload, and it carries on rather than starting its state again.

Sounds

A monster speaks through the step play the sound … (sound, in the Sound and look group), placed in a state like any other step. Its box is a picker of the module's sounds, grouped by sound pack, with ▶ to hear each one. The sound's from (where it is heard from: the monster, its target, or where a missile landed) has no word in the editor yet; it is heard from the monster unless the files say otherwise.

Looks → Sounds lists every sound the monster's steps play, so you can change them in one place, and Bring in sounds of your own… goes to the Sounds page. A sound that no pack has is counted as a problem of the rail's Sounds entry, and Home lists it.

Where each sound goes, the way the DOOM II module does it:

SoundWhere
sight (a shout when it notices you)A short alert state between look and chase: it lasts 0.02 s, then goes on to chase, with the sound at 0 s. look goes to alert instead of chase. The shout then plays once per noticing, not every time it returns to chase. Cinderfall's wake states do the same, with a longer pause.
painAt 0 s of pain.
deathAt 0 s of death. It runs as the monster dies, the one time a death step runs.
an attackAt the same time as the blow or the shot, once per moment: three pellets are one bang.
a missile's launch and burstThe launch beside the step that fires it; the burst in the missile's When it hits.

A sound may be dropped on a busy or distant client. So a sound is never the only sign of anything: a wind-up should also show in the animation, or a warning step. Sound packs are made in the Sounds track; see Mix, test and ship for how a pack's names reach the game.

In Gem Hollow: soft monsters that vanish

Open each monster's Overview and Advanced, and its pain card:

MonsterHealthFlinchespainLeaves a bodyDrops
slime2078% (200)0.2 s, sound monster.slime.hurtoffcoin, always
bee10100% (255)0.17 s, no soundoffnone
snail4047% (120)0.23 s, sound monster.snail.hurtoffstar_ammo, always

The hero's fists hit for 10 (Hitscan, missiles and swings), so a bee dies to one punch, a slime to two and a snail to four. What to notice:

  • The bee always flinches (255 of 256), which would be a stun-lock on a tough monster. On a 10-health bee it never matters: the first punch kills it.
  • Their pain goes to chase, not back to what it was doing: a monster punched while it wandered comes for you after the flinch.
  • No bodies. With Leaves a body off, the body is removed the moment it dies. Each death state still matters for one thing: its step at 0 s, the death sound (monster.slime.death and so on), which runs as it dies. Its length and its then goes on to gone never run, because the dead do not think. The gone state is reached only from the slime's squash.
  • A stomped slime leaves no coin. Drops are rolled only when hits take its health to 0. A slime you land on goes through squash and gone, whose vanish removes it without dying, so nothing drops. Punch it to death and it drops its coin.
  • The snail feeds the Star Toss. Its star_ammo is a pickup that gives 5 stars (Ammo, pickups and the arsenal), so the hardest monster pays for the ranged weapon.
  • One number each, ready, the attack cooldown, as the recipes' grunt has.

Exercise: snails that sometimes heal you

  1. Open Gem Hollow, choose ☠ Monsters, pick snail and open Advanced. Scroll to drops: one entry, star_ammo.

  2. Press + drop for a second entry. Press + weighted choice, type heart in the choice's item, weight 1. Press + weighted choice again, leave its item empty and give it weight 2. The section says "Something drops 33% of the time."

  3. Under the flinch, switch on a bigger hit, a longer flinch and read what it says a 10-point hit (one punch) does to the snail's 14-tick flinch.

  4. Check the rail shows no problem count, press ▶ Play, and punch the four snails of the third zone to death. Each leaves its stars, and about one in three also leaves a heart.

Exercise: make the grunt feel hits

  1. Pick grunt and open Overview. Set Health to 60 and Flinches to 63% (a flinch chance of 161 of 256).

  2. On Advanced, under the flinch, switch on a bigger hit, a longer flinch. Read what it says a 10-, 50- and 100-point hit do. Set max to 30 and read it again.

  3. On Behaviour, open the attack state and switch Pain can cut it short off. Its card now says (never flinches here), and your claw from the last lesson always lands, if you stay in its way. Try it in the preview: Run, wait for attack, and press Flinch.

  4. On Advanced, under drops, press + drop. It starts with the first prop's name; type your medikit's name in item instead. Press + weighted choice, type your medikit's name in the choice's item, weight 1. Press + weighted choice again for a second choice, leave its item empty and set its weight to 2. The section should say "Something drops 33% of the time."

  5. Open the pain state. Press + add a step at 0 s, pick play the sound (Sound and look) and pick a pain sound from the list. Do the same on death with a death sound.

  6. Add a state alert: plays walk, stands still, faces the target, Lasts 0.02, then goes to chase, with play the sound at 0 s. In the rule that went into chase from your idle state (wander or look), set go to to alert instead.

  7. Check the rail shows no problem count, then ▶ Play. Kill three or four grunts. Listen for the shout, count the flinches, and see how often a medikit drops.

A monster flinches 78% of hits but has no state called pain. What happens when you shoot it?

  • It flinches with the default length
  • It never flinches: the flinch roll needs a pain state
  • The server refuses the monster

The roll is only made when there is a pain state to enter, and the current state may be cut short. Whatever it is doing says so: no state is named pain, so this never happens.

Your death state plays a scream at 0 s and shows a puff of smoke at 0.5 s. Only the scream happens. Why?

  • Two steps in one death state is not allowed
  • The puff needs Leaves a body on
  • The dead do not think: only the steps at 0 s run, once, as it dies

A dead monster never decides again, so later steps of its death state never run. Put everything a death does at 0 s.

One drop has three weighted choices: shells weight 2, medikit weight 1, nothing weight 1. How often does a medikit drop?

  • One time in four
  • One time in three
  • One time in two

The weights add up to 4, and exactly one choice drops. The medikit's share is 1 of 4. Something drops three times in four.
