Recipes: five enemies

Five complete monsters, each with a different way to fight it. Each one starts from one of the editor's own recipes and is changed in the editor's words, state by state, so you can build it and play it straight away. Then two to open rather than build: Gem Hollow's slime and Cinderfall's Smelter.

You'll learnhow the pieces of this track combine into real designs: an imp that claws close and throws far, a charger you sidestep, a turret you outflank, a sniper you chase down, and a boss in three phases Time60 minutes, or 10 for one recipe Needsthe whole Enemies track; a sprite sheet for each monster; small props to draw the missiles; a few sounds in a sound pack

Reading a recipe

Each recipe says which of the editor's recipes to start from (Your first monster), what to set on Overview, and then every state as its card reads on Behaviour:

  • the first line is what it does: Plays, Moves, Faces, how long it Lasts and where it Then goes to;
  • → state when … is a rule, in the order they are checked; every condition must hold;
  • 0.27 s · … is a step at its time, in the Timeline.

To turn a recipe into a monster: make the monster from the starting recipe, rename the states that are already there (a state's name box; every rule that goes to it follows), add the missing ones with + state, and edit each card until it reads as written. Sound names are from the DOOM II module's bank: pick your own pack's sounds in their place. A warning's particle kind (warn the player with effect 12) is one of your module's ✨ Particles; pick one that reads as a warning.

Remember the two timing rules from The brain: a monster decides every 8 ticks (0.13 s), and a state's steps are first looked at one decision after it was entered. The recipes put their steps on multiples of 0.13 s (0, 0.13, 0.27, 0.4, 0.53 …), so what you read is what happens. Try each state in the preview as you go: Run, and move the target with nearer and further.

1. The imp: claws close, fireballs far

Doom's imp, from the engine's own DOOM monsters, with Doom's map units turned into metres (48 to one). It stands until it sees you, shouts, then walks at you. Close up it claws; at range, once it is lined up, it throws a fireball you can sidestep. The order of chase's rules is the design: claw first, because when both hold the claw is the right answer.

Start from Melee grunt, named imp. Overview: Health 60, Flinches 78%, Runs at 5.83, Sees 42.67, Turn speed 12. Looks: Width (radius) 0.42; on Advanced, height 1.17.

The missile, on the Missiles page: New, named fireball. Speed 7.29, no fall, Lasts 5, Launch height 0.67. When it hits: hurt what it hit for 12; play the sound missile.fireball.burst. Looks: Drawn as a prop with state 0 flying and state 1 bursting (radius 0.125), While flying, plays state 0, When it bursts, plays state 1, Burst lasts 0.33.

2. The charger: a wind-up, then a rush

It stalks you, stops, glows, then commits: a leap straight at where you were. If it reaches you it hits hard. If you step aside after the glow, it rushes past. This is the editor's Charger recipe as it comes; make one named charger and read it.

Overview, as the recipe sets it: Health 60, Runs at 7.

  • The line is the leap. leap target at 14 m/s throws the body once, at where you are on the decision it runs (0.13 s in). It does not steer after you, so stepping sideways after the warning makes it miss. (Charges straight ahead does not move a monster on the engine's fast loop yet; the leap does the moving.)
  • The warning's length (0.5 s) is the time from the glow to the rush.
  • The hit waits until 0.3 s, so it lands where the leap took it, not where it started.
  • Make it yours: give it an opening. Add a state tired: plays idle, stands still, faces where it was, lasts 1 s, then goes to chase; set rush's Then goes to to tired. Switch Pain can cut it short off on wind_up, so the player must dodge rather than shoot it off course; leave it on in tired, so hits during the opening make it stagger.

3. The turret: slow to turn, quick to shoot

It never moves. It tracks you slowly, and fires a three-round burst when you are in front of it. You beat it by moving faster than it turns, or by staying out of its line. It has no pain state, so it never flinches.

Start from Blank, named turret. Overview: Health 150, Sees 24, Fights others off. Advanced: Turns a second 0.5, Weight 1000; under Its body states, radius 0.5, height 1, speed 0.

4. The sniper: keeps its distance

It wants you at middle range. Too far, it closes in. Too near, it backs away while still facing you. In range and in sight, it aims for most of a second, with a warning, then fires one heavy shot and sidesteps. You beat it by rushing it, or by hitting it while it aims.

Start from Shooter, named sniper; it already keeps its distance, warns and fires. Overview: Health 40, Flinches 70%, Sees 40, Runs at 4.5: a little slower than the player, so rushing works. Advanced: under drops, + drop ammo_clip (or one of yours).

  • The aim is the counterplay. Rush within 4 m and it breaks off. Hit it, and its pain goes to back_off, not back to the aim: a hit both flinches it and makes it retreat. (The recipe's own pain goes back to what it was doing, which would resume the aim.)
  • Make it yours: a sniper that blinks. Give reposition a step at 0 s, teleport near the target, 12 m away, with effect …: it vanishes and reappears somewhere around you that it could walk to you from, facing you, and only where its body fits.

5. The boss: three phases by health

A big, slow warden that changes as it is hurt. In phase one it slams if you come close and lobs at range. At two thirds of its health it roars, calls three minions and gets faster, slams harder and fires barrages. At one third it summons again, leaps at you and calls more help on a timer. This is the editor's Boss (3 phases) recipe as it comes; make one named warden.

Overview, as the recipe sets it: Health 900, Flinches 10%, Runs at 4. Its numbers (Advanced): phase starts at 1; slam_cd, shot_cd, leap_cd and adds_cd are cooldowns. It fires the module's first missile (or a new bolt), and its minions are the module's first other monster (or a new warden_minion, a melee grunt of 40 health).

Phases

On List, each phase is a group with a header: its name (type to rename it), how many states it has and when it starts ("4 states · starts when its health is below 66%, once", or "where it begins"), runs at … m/s (empty: the body's own speed), duplicate and remove. Then come Reactions: pain and death. On Graph, each phase is a lane.

A phase is a group of states, nothing more, with one thing of its own: its speed, which its states set as they start. What moves the boss from one phase to the next is two rules under Whatever it is doing:

1. go to roar     when its health is below 66%    only once
2. go to enrage   when its health is below 33%    only once

They are checked first, in every state, so crossing two thirds of its health interrupts whatever it is doing. only once makes each fire a single time; the boss remembers it, saved games too. And its pain goes back to what it was doing, so a flinch in phase three resumes phase three's state: nothing ever sends it back to phase one.

PhaseStates, and what they do
Phase 1: slam and shellsidle ★ → chase when it can see the target. chase → slam within 3 m when slam_cd is over; → lob within 16 m when shot_cd is over. slam (1.1 s): a warning at 0 s, a blast of 35 damage within 3 m at 0.55 s. lob (0.7 s): a warning, then the missile at 0.3 s.
Phase 2: the roar, runs at 5 m/sroar (1.5 s, never flinches): set the number phase to 2; at 0.6 s summon 3 (minion) beside it and alert the monsters within 20 m; then chase2, which slams harder (slam2, 45 within 3.5 m) and fires a barrage of three shots at 0.4, 0.6 and 0.8 s.
Phase 3: frenzy, runs at 6.5 m/senrage (1.2 s, never flinches): set the number phase to 3, summon 2, start adds_cd for 8 s; then frenzy: → leap within 9 m (a leap at 14 m/s at 0.3 s, a hit of 40 within 2.5 m at 0.6 s); → call_adds when adds_cd is over (summon 2 more); → barrage within 18 m.
  • Two phases share a state. frenzy borrows phase two's barrage. As the barrage ends it branches: "then frenzy if the number phase is at least 3; otherwise then goes on to chase2". That is a branch under When its time is up, and the reason the boss keeps the number phase.
  • The roar and the enrage cannot be flinched out of (Pain can cut it short off), so the change of phase always plays out.
  • A phase of your own: + phase copies the last phase, its states renamed with the phase's number (frenzy_4 …), 30% faster, and adds a rule under Whatever it is doing that starts it, once, at half the health of the last one: "Phase 4: a copy of Phase 3: frenzy that runs at 8.5 m/s. It starts when its health drops below 17%…". Then make it angrier: change its states, its speed, or what it hits for. On a monster with no phases yet, + phase first puts its states in Phase 1.
  • In the preview, drag its health below 66% and watch it roar; its numbers line shows phase = 2, and the rule's card says fired in this run.

Make the slam readable. A blast draws nothing but its warning. To show the danger zone, make the slam a swing instead (Advanced → Its own swing: a wide thrust from above, sweep_from = sweep_to 0, half_arc 0.25, pitch_from 0.125, pitch_to −0.125), and give it a red color, width 1 and a longer fade such as 0.5 (Attacks).

6. Gem Hollow's slime: a monster you jump on

Not a recipe to build but one to open: the slime in the example project. It is the platformer's oldest enemy: walk into it and it bumps you; land on it and it bounces you up and is gone. Its cards are listed in The brain; these are the ones that make it a platformer enemy:

  • The stomp comes first in both look and chase, so landing on it wins over anything else.

Another one to open rather than build: the boss of Cinderfall, the first-person shooter example that comes with the studio (Home → examples → 📂 Cinderfall; see the tour). A Lua condition or step moves a monster to the slower runtime. None of these recipes needs one. The server prints at load how many monster kinds run on the fast C++ loop and how many fall back, with the reason. Keep the Lua function … says yes and run the Lua function … for what the vocabulary truly cannot say.

Exercise: build the turret

The turret is the smallest recipe. Building it once teaches you how a recipe maps onto the editor.

  1. Make a sheet for it, or reuse any sheet with an idle, an attack and a death animation. Open ☠ Monsters, tap the name at the top left, type turret under New monster and press Blank. On Looks, pick its sprite.

  2. On Overview: Health 150, Sees 24, Fights others off. On Advanced: Turns a second 0.5, Weight 1000; under Its body states, radius 0.5, height 1, speed 0.

  3. On Behaviour, open pain and press delete. Open idle and rename it scan in its name box; set Faces to where it was.

  4. Add the states track and fire with + state, and make each read as in the recipe: track faces the target and has its two rules, fire lasts 0.53 s, goes to track, plays attack, and has its five steps. Use the preview's playhead: with the preview running in fire, + add a step at … adds the step at the moment it is at.

  5. Give scan its rule: → track when it has a target and it can see the target. On death, add play the sound at 0 s.

  6. Run the preview, and press further until the target is past 24 m: after half a second in track, it goes back to scan. Check the rail shows no problem count.

  7. Place two turrets covering one corridor, press ▶ Play, and cross it. Strafe across one's front and watch it fail to keep up. Then compare with Turns a second 4.

The charger's rush starts with leap target at 14 m/s. Why can you sidestep it?

  • A leap cannot go faster than the body's own speed
  • The leap throws the body once, at where you were when it ran; it does not steer after you
  • A leap ignores walls, so it flies past you

A charge you can dodge must commit to a line. The leap sets the line once, and the warning before it tells you when. Walls still stop it.

In the three-phase boss, why does pain go back to what it was doing rather than to chase?

  • So a flinch in phase two or three does not send it back to phase one's chase
  • Because pain may not name a chase state
  • So it cannot be hurt during the flinch

Going back resumes the state the hit interrupted, in whatever phase it is. A pain that went to chase would drop it into phase one's chase, and its phase-one rules.

The turret has no pain state, but you set Flinches to 50%. What happens when you shoot it?

  • It flinches half the time, into its idle animation
  • The editor refuses it
  • It never flinches: there is no pain state to enter, so nothing is rolled

The flinch roll needs a state called pain. Whatever it is doing says so plainly: no state is named pain, so this never happens.
