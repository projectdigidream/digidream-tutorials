The brain: states and transitions

A monster's brain is a small graph you draw. Each box is a state: what the body does right now. Each arrow is a transition: when to stop doing it and do something else. This lesson explains how the engine walks that graph, then you build a brain that wanders, notices you, hunts you and gives up when you hide.

You'll learnthe fields of a state; how transitions and conditions are checked; the order of one decision; how a monster finds a target and why it keeps it; and the patterns for idle, wander, chase, attack and give up Time40 minutes NeedsGem Hollow open in the studio (Your first monster); for the last exercise, the grunt from that lesson and a map with two rooms joined by a door

Try it first

The demo runs the brain a new monster starts with: look, chase, attack, pain and death. Drag the green dot (you) into the dotted circle, then behind the wall. Press hit it a few times. Watch the diagram light the state it is in and the arrow it just took, and read why in the log.

Three things to notice:

  • It notices you as soon as you cross the dotted sight circle, even behind the wall. But it only leaves look when it can also see you.
  • Once it has you as a target, it never lets go, however far you run. Only target_none makes it forget.
  • Changes happen in steps, not smoothly. A monster decides 7.5 times a second.

Two levels: the graph and the sequence

A brain has two levels, and the editor shows both:

  • The graph says which state the monster is in, and what moves it to another. These are the boxes and arrows in the graph pane. Six to ten states is a lot. Most monsters need five.
  • The sequence says what one state does while the monster is in it: "at tick 10 after entering, claw". It is the list under ▶ at tick … do … in the state's tab.

The difference matters. A wind-up, a strike and a recovery are not three states. They are one attack state with a duration and two steps in its sequence.

A state's fields

Tap a state in the graph. Its tab shows a name box, then the fields from the vocabulary:

FieldWhat it does
animThe animation state the body plays, 0–4 (see Your first monster). Required.
moveHow the body is driven: stop, chase (follows a route to the target), flee (walks directly away from where it is aiming), strafe (walks a quarter turn to the left of its aim), wander (walks the heading stored in the var wander), charge, or free (the runtime keeps its hands off, for a leap you drive with actions).
faceWhere it looks: target turns towards its target, hold keeps its heading. Left empty, it means target.
ticksHow long the state lasts. Empty means "until a transition fires", which is what chase wants.
ticks_jitterUp to this many extra ticks, rolled each time it enters. Monsters that entered together then leave at different times and stop moving as one animal. Needs ticks.
nextWhere it goes when ticks runs out. A state with ticks must have one. A state that ends and stays, like death, names itself.
interruptibleWhether a hit can send it to pain. Left empty, it can, even though the empty switch is drawn off. Death interrupts everything.
solidCollision while in this state. Only death normally sets it, to off. Leave it empty elsewhere.

Two values do less than their names say. face = "movement" behaves like hold in both of the engine's mob runtimes: nothing turns the body to where it walks. And move = "charge" walks straight at the aim on the Lua path, but the faster C++ loop, which runs most monsters, treats it as stop. Until they are finished, rush in a straight line with wander and a heading stored by set_angle (as the charger recipe does), and turn with an explicit face action.

Press + state to add a state. The studio asks for a "state name" and suggests wander. A new state starts as anim 0, move stop, face hold, ticks 30, with next set to the initial state. Clear ticks? and next? if the state should last until a transition fires.

Transitions and conditions

Under the fields is ❓ go to … when all of …. Each card is one transition: a target state at the top, then its conditions, then add when to add another. Press + transition for a new card, or pick the state and press → link, then tap the state to go to. In the graph, the arrow is labelled with its conditions, such as has_target & target_visible; a negated one shows a !.

Four rules decide what happens:

  1. All of them. A transition fires only when every condition on its card holds. It is an AND.
  2. An OR is two cards. To go to flee when hurt or outnumbered, make two transitions to flee, one per reason.
  3. First match wins. Cards are tried top to bottom, and the first one that holds is taken. Order is priority. There is no scoring.
  4. The not switch beside a condition turns it round. has_target with not on means "has no target". There is no separate no_target.

A card with no conditions always fires. → link and + transition both make an empty card, and the graph labels it always. Add a condition straight away, or the monster leaves the state on its very next decision.

Conditions stop at the first false one, so put the cheap ones first. target_within is a little arithmetic. target_visible casts a ray. can_reach searches for a route.

chance is rolled every decision. A transition with chance 10 is asked 7.5 times a second while the monster is in that state, so it fires after about 1.3 seconds on average, not "one time in ten". To roll once and branch several ways, roll into a var with var_random in the sequence and test it with var_at_least.

How one decision runs

A monster does not think every tick. The game runs at 60 ticks a second, and each monster decides every 8th tick, about 7.5 times a second. Monsters are spread over the 8 ticks, so a room of them does not all think at once. Between decisions the body keeps moving on the last input it was given, so walking stays smooth.

One decision, in order:

  1. Find a target, if it has none: the nearest player inside its sight. This is distance only, with no ray.
  2. Run the sequence entries that are due, in order. A goto leaves the state at once, and the rest does not run.
  3. Try the transitions, top to bottom. The first that holds is taken, and the decision ends.
  4. Check ticks. If the state's time is up, go to next. An explicit transition always beats running out of time.
  5. Drive the body by the state it ended up in: its move and its face.

Hits do not wait for a decision. Damage, death and the pain roll happen the moment a hit lands (see Pain, death and drops).

Times land on decisions. A sequence entry at = 10 is a deadline, checked when the monster decides. A state is entered during a decision, and the monster next looks at its sequence 8 ticks later, then 16, and so on. So at = 0 runs 8 ticks after entering, at = 10 runs at 16, and a state with ticks 20 ends at 24. A state's ticks works the same way. Think in steps of 8, and do not expect a 3-tick difference to show.

A step must come before the state ends. A sequence step whose at is equal to or later than the state's ticks would never run, and the server refuses the monster for it: "fires at tick 24 of a state that lasts 24, so it never runs". The workshop's problem count does not catch this one yet, so check it yourself.

Targets: finding, keeping, forgetting

A monster hunts one thing at a time, its target. How it gets one, and loses it:

  • Noticing. With no target, it takes the nearest player inside sight (on the 🧠 brain tab). sight is a cheap distance gate. It does not check walls, so a brain should ask target_visible before it acts.
  • Keeping. Once it has a target, it keeps it until the target is gone from the map or dead. Running out of sight does not lose it. That is why chase's first card, "go to look when not has_target", rarely fires.
  • Forgetting. The action target_none drops the target. On a later decision it notices again, if anyone is inside sight.
  • Choosing. target_nearest points it at the nearest player, mob or prop that matches. target_attacker turns it on whatever hurt it last.
  • Infighting. With infighting on, a monster hurt by another monster makes that one its target. Doom's monsters do this.
  • Telling others. alert_nearby hands its target to every monster within a radius, and wakes the dormant ones. This is how a room reacts as a room, and the only way, with wake, to wake a dormant monster.

Patterns

Most brains are made of a few shapes.

Idle: wait to see

move stop, face hold, no ticks. One transition: to chase when has_target and target_visible. This is the new monster's look.

Wander

move = "wander" walks the heading in the var wander, and nothing writes that var for you. So a wander state:

  • declares wander on the # vars tab;
  • has ticks 90, ticks_jitter 60, and next itself, so it picks a new heading every 1.5–2.5 seconds;
  • runs, at 0, set_angle with var wander and towards random, then face with towards var and var wander, so it looks where it walks.

Add a transition with stuck back to the same state, and it turns away from walls instead of pressing into them.

Chase

move chase, face target, no ticks. Its feet follow a route around walls while it keeps looking at you. Its transitions are its choices, in priority order: attack if close and ready, otherwise keep running.

Attack with a cooldown

The transition into attack asks cooldown_ready on a var (the new monster uses ready). The attack's sequence runs set_cooldown on that var. The state has ticks and next chase. See Attacks.

Give up

Because a target is kept, giving up is something you build: a lost state the monster enters when it cannot see you, which goes back to chase if it sees you again, and otherwise runs out into a state that runs target_none. The exercise builds exactly this.

In Gem Hollow: the slime's brain

Open ☠ Monsters and pick slime. It has the five states a new monster has, plus two of its own: squash and gone. Read it state by state.

What to notice:

  • The stomp is a transition, not a hit. squash is the first card in both look and chase, so landing on a slime beats everything else it might do. target_above with by 0.6 means "you are at least 0.6 above me", and target_within 1.3 means "and nearly on top of me". The melee in squash does 1 damage but lifts the hero by 14: that is the bounce off the slime's head. Then it switches solid off, waits 40 ticks for the death animation, and gone removes it.
  • Noticing has a shorter range than sight. sight is 14 on 🧠 brain, so it takes you as its target from 14 away, but look only leaves for chase inside 9. The bee does the same with 16 and 10, the snail with 8 and 6.
  • Its wander is a straight walk. move wander walks the heading in the var wander. The slime has no such var, only ready, so it walks along the heading it already has until something stops it. And face movement behaves like hold (see the callout above). The bee and the snail are built the same way. The exercise below gives the snail a real wander.
  • The bee and the snail have the same look, chase, attack, pain, gone and death, and no squash: you cannot stomp them. The snail's look uses anim 1, so it crawls with its walk animation even before it has seen you.

In Cinderfall: brains for a shooter

Cinderfall, the first-person shooter example that comes with the studio (Home → ◆ examples; see the tour), has five monsters and a boss. Their brains fight at range, which Gem Hollow's never do. Open ☠ Monsters and pick each chip.

Monster🧠 brainStates, and what is new in them
huskhp 40, sight 22, turn_speed 6look (wander) → wake → chase → attack within 1.7: a plain shambler with one claw (melee 9 at 12).
gunnerhp 30, sight 30, turn_speed 10look (stop) → wake → move, which strafes: move strafe, face target. From move: to close when not target_within 20; to aim when visible and ready. close chases until target_within 14, then back to move. aim (18 ticks) → shoot.
houndhp 28, sight 26, turn_speed 14chase has two attacks: leap between 2.5 and 6 away, on a cooldown and a chance of 40; bite within 1.5. leap has move free, so the body flies where its impulse threw it.

brutehp 300, mass 4, pain_chance 40chase chooses slam within 3.2 on its own cooldown var slam, else aim → fire on ready. Two cooldowns, so a slam does not delay a shot.
smelterhp 2400, sight 60, mass 12The boss: roar instead of wake, three attacks and a brood below half health. See Recipes.

What to notice:

  • A wake state. Every Cinderfall monster but the Smelter goes from look to wake, not straight to the chase: move stop, face target, a sight sound at 0, 6 to 20 ticks, next the chase. You hear it before it comes, and you get a beat to shoot first. Gem Hollow's monsters have no such pause.
  • Strafing, and a range band. The gunner and the spitter use move strafe: they side-step while facing you. The gunner's two numbers, 20 to start closing in and 14 to stop, are a band on purpose: with one number it would flick between the two states at the edge every decision.
  • A chance on a transition. The hound's leap card ends with chance 40, so in the band it does not always leap.
  • A real wander. The husk, the hound and the spitter declare a wander var, and their look is the pattern above: anim 1, move wander, ticks 70, ticks_jitter 50, next look, with set_angle (var wander, towards random) at 0. So every few seconds they set off a new way. They have no face step, and face movement behaves like hold, so the body does not turn to where it walks.
  • Waiting in place. The gunner and the brute look with move stop: they stand guard where they were put until they see you.

Exercise: a snail that really wanders

  1. Open Gem Hollow, choose ☠ Monsters and pick snail. On # vars, press + var and name it wander.

  2. Tap look. Set ticks 120, ticks_jitter 60 and next look, so it picks a new heading every two to three seconds. Set face to hold.

  3. Under ▶ at tick … do …, use add step to add set_angle: at 0, var wander, towards random. Add face: at 0, towards var, var wander.

  4. Check the bar says "loads clean", then press ▶ Play. Go to the third zone and watch the snails from a distance: they now turn every few seconds instead of crawling into a wall and staying there.

Exercise: a watcher that gives up

You will turn the grunt's brain into this:

  1. Open ☠ Monsters, pick grunt, and open # vars. Press + var and name it wander. It shows as the chip wander = 0.

  2. In the graph bar press + state and name it wander. In its tab set anim 1, move wander, face hold, ticks 90, ticks_jitter 60, and next wander.

  3. Under ▶ at tick … do …, use add step to add set_angle: at 0, var wander, towards random. Add a second step, face: at 0, towards var, var wander.

  4. Press + transition. Set its target to chase, then add when has_target and add when target_visible. Add a second transition to wander with stuck (leave its ticks empty for the default 16).

  5. With wander picked, press ★ initial. The ★ moves to it, and 🧠 brain's initial state now says wander. Leave look in place for now.

  6. Add a state lost: anim 0, move stop, face target, ticks 120, next forget (you make forget next; pick it once it exists). Give it one transition to chase when target_visible.

  7. Add a state forget: anim 0, move stop, ticks 1, next wander. Add one step: target_none at 0. Now set lost's next to forget.

  8. Pick chase. Change its first transition (to look, when not has_target) so it goes to wander. Press + transition: to lost when target_visible with not on. Leave it below the transition to attack. Nothing goes to look any more, so you can pick it and press ✕ state.

  9. Check pain's next is chase, and that the top bar says "loads clean". Press auto layout to tidy the graph.

  10. Place the grunt in the far room, press ▶ Play, and let it see you. Then step through the door and out of its line. It stops and stares at where you are for two seconds, then turns away and wanders. Step back into view while it is in lost, and it comes straight for you.

Why must lost go below attack in chase's list? It would not matter here, since attack needs target_visible and lost needs its opposite. But when two cards can both hold, the upper one always wins, so put the more urgent one first.

A monster's sight is 20. You step inside 20 behind a pillar, then run 100 units away. What does its default brain do?

  • Nothing: it never had a target, because it could not see you
  • It took you as its target when you came within 20, and keeps you; it leaves look the first time it also sees you
  • It forgets you as soon as you leave its sight range

Noticing is distance only, and a target is kept until it is gone. look waits for has_target and target_visible, so the chase starts when you come into its line.

chase has two transitions: first to attack when target_within 1.5, then to lunge when target_within 4. You stand 1 away. Where does it go?

  • To lunge, because 4 is the bigger range
  • It picks one at random
  • To attack: both hold, and the first match wins

Transitions are tried in order and the first that holds is taken. Put narrower, more urgent cases above wider ones.

An attack state's sequence claws at at = 10. When does the claw actually happen?

  • At the monster's first decision on or after tick 10, which is 16 ticks after it entered
  • Exactly 10 ticks after it entered
  • On the next tick after the state's ticks run out

at is a deadline checked when the monster decides, every 8 ticks. Entered on a decision, it decides again at 8 and 16, and 16 is the first that is not before 10. Even at = 0 waits for the decision at 8.
