The brain: states and transitions

A monster's brain is a set of states. A state is what the body does right now; its rules (the engine calls them transitions) say when to stop doing it and do something else. This lesson explains how the engine runs them and how the editor writes them as sentences, then you build a brain that wanders, notices you, hunts you and gives up when you get away.

You'll learnhow a state reads and is edited on Behaviour; how rules and conditions are checked; the rules under Whatever it is doing; what the preview runs; the order of one decision; how a monster finds a target and why it keeps it; and the patterns for idle, wander, chase, attack and give up Time40 minutes NeedsGem Hollow open in the studio (Your first monster); for the last exercise, the grunt from that lesson and a map with two rooms joined by a door

Try it first

The demo runs a brain like the Melee grunt recipe's: look, chase, attack, pain and death. Drag the green dot (you) into the dotted circle, then behind the wall. Press hit it a few times. Watch the diagram light the state it is in and the arrow it just took, and read why in the log.

Four things to notice:

  • It notices you as soon as you cross the dotted circle (how far it Sees), even behind the wall. But it only leaves look when it can also see you.
  • Once it has you as a target, it never lets go, however far you run. Only a step that makes it forget its target does.
  • A flinch goes back to what it was doing. Hit it during its attack and, after the flinch, the attack carries on from where it was: a hit does not start it again.
  • Changes happen in steps, not smoothly. A monster decides 7.5 times a second.

Two levels: states and steps

A brain has two levels, and Behaviour shows both:

  • The states and their rules say which state the monster is in, and what moves it to another. With List, each state is a card that reads as a sentence; with Graph, a box with arrows. Six to ten states is a lot. Most monsters need five.
  • The steps say what one state does while the monster is in it: "at 0.17 s, melee hit for 10 damage". They are the chips on its card, and its Timeline when you open it.

The difference matters. A wind-up, a strike and a recovery are not three states. They are one attack state with a length and a few steps.

Reading a state

Open ☠ Monsters, pick slime and open Behaviour with List. Each card reads as a sentence:

  • The first line is what the state does: what it plays, how it moves and where it faces, and how long it lasts: "…until a rule below sends it on", "…for 0.4 s then goes on to chase", "…then goes back to what it was doing", or "…and stays here". The ★ marks where it begins.
  • Its rules follow, one a line, in the order they are checked: "→ attack when the target is within 1.4 m and cooldown ready is over".
  • Its steps are chips with their time: "0.17 s · melee hit for 10 damage within 1.2 m knocking back 6 m".

The cards are in two groups. Flow is "where it begins (the star) and what it does". Reactions are the states "entered from any state when something happens to it": pain, death, and any state that goes back to what it was doing. A state nothing goes to says "nothing goes here yet". Tap a card, or its edit, to open the state beside the list; tap a word on the card to jump to that part of it.

Editing a state

The state you pick opens in the side panel, under the preview. At its top are its name (type a new one to rename it; every rule that went to it follows), make it where it begins (or where it begins, for the state with the ★), duplicate and delete, which says how many rules point at the state before it removes them with it. The buttons does, goes to, at the end and steps jump to its four parts. ✕ closes it.

What the editor does not do yet. Faces where it walks behaves like where it was in both of the engine's mob runtimes: nothing turns the body to where it walks. Press + state to add a state. A field, New state, named, asks for its name (e.g. spit), and says where it will go: placed after the state picked, or at the end. Press Create or Enter. A new state plays the idle animation, stands still, faces the target and lasts until a rule fires. Nothing goes to it yet: give another state a rule that goes to it.

Rules and conditions

Under Goes to are the state's rules, each a card: its number, go to and the state it goes to, then when: and its conditions, each one a sentence with its numbers in boxes. + add a condition adds one; ✕ removes one; the arrows check the rule earlier or later; remove takes the rule away. + another rule adds a rule. The go to list offers the states of the same phase first.

Four rules decide what happens:

  1. All of them. A rule fires only when every condition on its card holds ("when all of these hold:"). It is an AND.
  2. An OR is two rules. To go to flee when hurt or outnumbered, make two rules to flee, one per reason.
  3. First match wins. Rules are tried top to bottom, and the first one that holds is taken. Order is priority. There is no scoring.
  4. The opposite is its own sentence. "it has no target" beside "it has a target", "the target is further than" beside "the target is within", "it is in the air", "no more than … monsters", "the number … is not". The files keep them as the same condition with negate.

An unfinished rule never fires. A rule with no state to go to, or with no condition yet, says so in red ("incomplete: add a condition (it never fires until then)") and is left out of the files. Add its condition before you play.

Conditions stop at the first false one, so put the cheap ones first. The target is within is a little arithmetic. It can see the target casts a ray. It can walk to the target searches for a route.

+ add a condition opens a chooser, Add a condition (every condition must hold). Its conditions are in groups, one group at a time, picked from a row of chips; it opens on the group you used last. On a wide screen a find among … box searches all of them. Tap one, and it is added with its default numbers, ready to change.

A chance is rolled every decision. A rule with a 10% chance is asked 7.5 times a second while the monster is in that state, so it fires after about 1.3 seconds on average, not "one time in ten". To roll once and branch several ways, roll a number with the step make … a random number from … to … and test it with the number … is at least ….

When its time is up

A state with a length can also branch as it ends. Under When its time is up (optional branches), + a branch adds a rule that is checked only as its time runs out ("as it ends, if:"); with none holding, it goes where Then goes to says ("otherwise → chase"). The card reads "…for 1.2 s then frenzy if the number phase is at least 3; otherwise then goes on to chase2". A state with no length has no end to branch at.

Whatever it is doing

Above the states, the panel Whatever it is doing holds the rules that apply in any state. Its first two rows are built in: "when it is hurt (78% of hits) → pain" and "when its health reaches 0 → death", the engine's own, for states named exactly pain and death (with no such state, the row says "this never happens"). Under them go rules of your own: + a rule for any state, for example: flee when its health is below 30%.

  • They are checked first, before a state's own rules, and the first that holds wins.
  • only once makes a rule fire a single time: a boss's change of phase. The monster remembers it fired, saved games too.
  • A state with Follows "whatever it is doing" off skips them, and so does death. A rule never sends a monster into the state it is already in.

The files have no such list: when the module is written, the studio puts these rules at the head of every state's own rules.

The preview

The preview beside the states runs the monster's brain by the engine's own rules: it decides every 8 ticks, tries the rules in order, flinches by the flinch chance, and goes back to what a flinch interrupted. A stand-in for its target stands 8 m away.

  • Run and Pause (also ▶ at the top), Reset to start again where it begins.
  • Hit hurts it for 25, with the flinch roll; Flinch makes it flinch for sure, with no damage; Kill kills it.
  • the target: in sight / hidden, nearer and further, a metre at a time; its health as a slider, for a rule on its health.
  • Under them, what it does now and why: "In look: goes to chase (rule 2).", "Now in attack: no rule holds; after 0.4 s, on to chase."; and its numbers and cooldowns: "cooldown ready: 0.8 s left".

The card it is in is marked, and the rule that would fire now is outlined. What the preview does not have: walls, sight lines, routes, and other monsters. A rule that leans on them says so ("The preview has no other monsters or props…"); check those in a level. The monsters it summons are the one crowd it has.

How one decision runs

A monster does not think every tick. The game runs at 60 ticks a second, and each monster decides every 8th tick, about 7.5 times a second. Monsters are spread over the 8 ticks, so a room of them does not all think at once. Between decisions the body keeps moving on the last input it was given, so walking stays smooth.

One decision, in order:

  1. Find a target, if it has none: the nearest player inside how far it Sees. This is distance only, with no ray.
  2. Run the steps that are due, in their order. A leave now for step leaves the state at once, and the rest does not run.
  3. Try the rules, top to bottom (the ones for any state first). The first that holds is taken, and the decision ends.
  4. Check its length. If the state's time is up, take a branch that holds, or go where Then goes to says. A rule always beats running out of time.
  5. Drive the body by the state it ended up in: how it moves and where it faces.

Hits do not wait for a decision. Damage, death and the flinch roll happen the moment a hit lands (see Pain, death and drops).

Times land on decisions. A step's time is a deadline, checked when the monster decides. A state is entered during a decision, and the monster next looks at its steps 8 ticks later, then 16, and so on. So a step at 0 s runs 8 ticks (0.13 s) after entering, a step at 0.17 s (10 ticks) runs at 16 ticks (0.27 s), and a state that lasts 0.33 s (20 ticks) ends at 24 ticks (0.4 s). A state's length works the same way. Think in steps of 0.13 s, and do not expect a difference of 0.05 s to show. The steps are listed in the order the engine runs them, each with the time it really runs: a step listed after a later one waits for it, and says so.

A step must come before the state ends. A step whose time is equal to or later than the state's length would never run, and the server refuses the monster for it: "fires at tick 24 of a state that lasts 24, so it never runs". The studio's problem count does not catch this one yet, so check it yourself.

Targets: finding, keeping, forgetting

A monster hunts one thing at a time, its target. How it gets one, and loses it:

  • Noticing. With no target, it takes the nearest player inside how far it Sees (sight, on Overview). That is a cheap distance gate. It does not check walls, so a brain should ask it can see the target before it acts.
  • Keeping. Once it has a target, it keeps it until the target is gone from the map or dead. Running out of sight range does not lose it. That is why a rule such as "→ look when it has no target" rarely fires.
  • Forgetting. The step forget its target (target_none) drops it. On a later decision it notices again, if anyone is inside its sight range.
  • Choosing. target_nearest (under More in the step chooser) points it at the nearest player, mob or prop that matches. turn on whoever hurt it (target_attacker) turns it on whatever hurt it last.
  • Infighting. With Fights others on (infighting), a monster hurt by another monster makes that one its target. Doom's monsters do this.
  • Telling others. alert the monsters within … m (alert_nearby) hands its target to every monster within that radius, and wakes the dormant ones. This is how a room reacts as a room, and the only way, with wake (under More), to wake a dormant monster.

Patterns

Most brains are made of a few shapes.

Idle: wait to see

Stands still, faces where it was, no length. One rule: → chase when it has a target and it can see the target. This is the Melee grunt recipe's look.

Wander

Wanders walks the heading kept in the number wander, and nothing writes that number for you. So a wander state:

  • lasts 1.5 to 2.5 seconds and then goes to itself (then starts it again), so it picks a new heading each time;
  • has the step pick a heading random into the number wander at 0 s (set_angle, in the chooser's Moving group). The number appears on Advanced under Its numbers by itself.

Chase

Runs at the target, faces the target, no length. Its feet follow a route around walls while it keeps looking at you. Its rules are its choices, in priority order: attack if close and ready, otherwise keep running.

Attack with a cooldown

The rule into attack asks cooldown ready is over. The attack's steps start cooldown ready for … s. The state has a length and goes back to chase. See Attacks.

Flinch and carry on

pain stands still for a moment and then goes back to what it was doing: it resumes the state the hit interrupted, where it was, so a hit during a summon or a barrage does not start it again, and a boss in its third phase stays in it. Every recipe's pain does this.

Give up

Because a target is kept, giving up is something you build: a lost state the monster enters when you get away, which goes back to chase if you come back, and otherwise runs out into a state that forgets its target. The exercise builds exactly this.

In Gem Hollow: the slime's brain

Open ☠ Monsters and pick slime. It has the five states a melee monster has, plus two of its own: squash and gone. Read it card by card on List:

What to notice:

  • The stomp is a rule, not a hit. squash is the first rule in both look and chase, so landing on a slime beats everything else it might do. The target is 0.6 m or more above it and within 1.3 m mean "nearly on top of me". The hit in squash does 1 damage but lifts the hero by 14: that is the bounce off the slime's head. Then it stops blocking, waits for the death animation, and gone removes it. The lift has no word in the editor's sentence; it is kept as written.
  • Its pain goes to chase, not back to what it was doing: the slime was made before pain could resume. A slime hit in look comes for you after its flinch. Set its Then goes to to back to what it was doing (resumed), and it would wander on.
  • Noticing has a shorter range than sight. It Sees 14 m, so it takes you as its target from 14 m away, but look only leaves for chase within 9 m. The bee does the same with 16 and 10, the snail with 8 and 6.
  • Its wander is a straight walk. It wanders along the heading in its number wander, but no step sets that number (it has only ready), so it walks the way it already faces until something stops it. The bee and the snail are built the same way. The exercise below gives the snail a real wander.
  • The bee and the snail have the same look, chase, attack, pain, gone and death, and no squash: you cannot stomp them. The snail's look plays walk, so it crawls even before it has seen you.

What to notice:

  • A wake state. Every Cinderfall monster but the Smelter goes from look to wake, not straight to the chase: stands still, faces the target, a sight sound at 0 s, 0.1 to 0.33 s, then the chase. You hear it before it comes, and you get a beat to shoot first. Gem Hollow's monsters have no such pause.
  • Circling, and a range band. The gunner and the spitter circle the target: they side-step while facing you. The gunner's two numbers, further than 20 m to start closing in and within 14 m to stop, are a band on purpose: with one number it would flick between the two states at the edge every decision.
  • A chance on a rule. The hound's leap rule ends with a 40% chance, so in the band it does not always leap.
  • A real wander. The husk, the hound and the spitter wander: their look wanders, lasts 1.17 s (plus up to 0.83 s more: a ticks_jitter the editor keeps but does not show), then starts again, with pick a heading random into the number wander at 0 s. So every few seconds they set off a new way. Their body does not turn to where it walks.
  • Waiting in place. The gunner and the brute look standing still: they guard where they were put until they see you.

Exercise: a snail that really wanders

  1. Open Gem Hollow, choose ☠ Monsters and pick snail. On Behaviour, tap its look card.

  2. Set Lasts to 2. Then goes to becomes look, the state itself, and the card says "for 2 s then starts it again": it picks a new heading every two seconds. Set Faces to where it was.

  3. Press steps to jump to its Timeline, then + add a step at 0 s. In the chooser's Moving group, pick pick a heading random into the number wander. Leave its heading on random and its number on wander.

  4. Open Advanced: under Its numbers, wander is there, set in look.

  5. Check the rail's ☠ Monsters shows no problem count, then press ▶ Play. Go to the third zone and watch the snails from a distance: they now set off a new way every two seconds instead of crawling into a wall and staying there.

Exercise: a watcher that gives up

You will turn the grunt's brain into this:

  1. Open ☠ Monsters and pick grunt. On Overview, set Sees to 12: once it has forgotten you, it should only notice you again close up.

  2. On Behaviour, press + state, type wander and press Create. Set Plays to walk, Moves to wanders, Faces to where it was, Lasts to 1.5, and Then goes to to wander.

  3. Under Timeline, press + add a step at 0 s and pick pick a heading random into the number wander (Moving).

  4. Under Goes to, press + another rule. Set go to to chase, then + add a condition it has a target and + add a condition it can see the target.

  5. Press make it where it begins. The ★ moves to wander, and the preview starts there.

  6. Add a state forget: plays idle, stands still, Lasts 0.02, Then goes to wander. Give it one step at 0 s: forget its target (Others).

  7. Add a state lost: plays idle, stands still, faces the target, Lasts 2, Then goes to forget. Give it one rule: → chase when it can see the target and the target is within 9 m.

  8. Open chase. In the rule that goes to look when it has no target, set go to to wander. Press + another rule: → lost when the target is further than 12 m. Leave it below the rule to attack. Nothing goes to look any more (its card says nothing goes here yet), so open it and press delete.

  9. Check that pain goes back to what it was doing, and that the rail shows no problem count. Try it in the preview: Run, then press further until the target is past 12 m, and read the line under the buttons as it goes to lost, then forget.

  10. Place the grunt in the far room, press ▶ Play, and let it see you. Then run back through the door and on. Past 12 m it stops and stares at you for two seconds, then turns away and wanders. Come back within 9 m while it is in lost, and it comes straight for you.

Why must lost go below attack in chase's list? It would not matter here, since attack needs the target within 1.5 m and lost needs it further than 12. But when two rules can both hold, the upper one always wins, so put the more urgent one first. And why 12 out and 9 back? With one number, a target standing at the edge would flick it between the two states every decision.

A monster Sees 20 m. You step within 20 m behind a pillar, then run 100 m away. What does a Melee grunt brain do?

  • Nothing: it never had a target, because it could not see you
  • It took you as its target when you came within 20 m, and keeps you; it leaves look the first time it also sees you
  • It forgets you as soon as you leave its sight range

Noticing is distance only, and a target is kept until it is gone. look waits for it has a target and it can see the target, so the chase starts when you come into its line.

chase has two rules: first → attack when the target is within 1.5 m, then → lunge when the target is within 4 m. You stand 1 m away. Where does it go?

  • To lunge, because 4 m is the bigger range
  • It picks one at random
  • To attack: both hold, and the first match wins

Rules are tried in order and the first that holds is taken. Put narrower, more urgent cases above wider ones.

An attack state has a melee hit at 0.17 s (10 ticks). When does the hit actually happen?

  • At the monster's first decision on or after tick 10, which is 16 ticks (0.27 s) after it entered
  • Exactly 0.17 s after it entered
  • On the next tick after the state's length runs out

A step's time is a deadline checked when the monster decides, every 8 ticks. Entered on a decision, it decides again at 8 and 16, and 16 is the first that is not before 10. Even a step at 0 s waits for the decision at 8.

A boss is in the middle of a summon (a state 1.5 s long, its summon step at 0.6 s) when your shot makes it flinch. Its pain lasts 0.3 s and goes back to what it was doing. What happens after the flinch?

  • It starts the summon again, and summons a second time
  • It carries on the summon from where it was, so the summon step runs once
  • It goes to the state where it begins

Going back resumes the interrupted state: its elapsed time and its place among its steps. Steps that already ran do not run again.
