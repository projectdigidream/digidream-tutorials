Attacks: swings and missiles

An attack is a state with a few steps: turn to the target, wait, strike, start a cooldown. What the strike is decides how the player fights back: step out of reach, step aside, take cover, or dodge something flying. This lesson covers every kind of attack, the Missiles page, and the timing around them.

You'll learnhow an attack state is built from steps; the difference between a melee hit and a swing; a swing step and its numbers, the default swing, and what happens when two swings meet; how a swing looks in the game; instant shots; missiles on the Missiles page: falling, thrown, bouncing, homing, splitting and trailing particles; cooldowns, wind-ups and warnings; aim, turning and teleports Time50 minutes NeedsGem Hollow and Cinderfall open in the studio (Home → examples); for the last exercise, the grunt from the earlier lessons; a prop of your own to draw a fireball with (a small sheet with a flying animation and a burst) is optional

The shape of an attack

The Melee grunt recipe's attack state is the pattern almost every attack follows. Its card reads:

attack   plays attack, stands still and faces the target, for 0.45 s then goes on to chase
         0 s    · turn to face the target
         0.2 s  · melee hit for 9 damage within 1.6 m knocking back 4 m
         0.2 s  · start cooldown ready for 0.5 s

And chase enters it only when the target is close and the cooldown is over:

→ attack   when the target is within 1.5 m and cooldown ready is over

Read it as four beats:

  1. Stop and turn. It stands still, faces the target, and a turn to face the target step at 0 s.
  2. Wind up. The gap between entering and the strike. This is the player's time to react.
  3. Strike. The step that hurts.
  4. Recover and cool down. The rest of its length, then back to chase; start cooldown keeps it from attacking again at once.

Open the state and press steps: its Timeline marks each step at its time, and lists them in the order the engine runs them. + add a step at 0 s opens the chooser Add a step (what happens, at that moment of the state), in groups: Attack, Sound and look, Numbers and timers, Moving, Others, Flow and Lua, then More for the engine's words the editor has no sentence for. While the preview runs in that state, the button adds the step at the moment the preview is at. Tap a step to edit it: its time (at … s), its numbers in the sentence, and remove step.

Remember the steps of 8. A monster decides every 8 ticks, and step times are checked on those decisions (see The brain). In the state above, the turn at 0 s happens 8 ticks (0.13 s) after entering, the hit at 0.2 s (12 ticks) lands at 16 ticks (0.27 s), and the state ends at 32 ticks (0.53 s), not 27. The timeline below shows this.

The Attacks tab gathers every attack step of every state in one place, each editable where it is ("In attack, at 0.2 s"), with Open attack to go to its state; and the module's missiles, with Open Missiles.

Melee: an instant hit

melee hit for … damage within … m knocking back … m (melee) is the simplest attack. On the decision it runs, it hurts the target if the target is close enough, and nothing else happens.

ArgumentWhat it does
damageHealth taken.
within (range)How far it reaches, centre to centre across the floor.
knocking back (knockback)A shove away from the attacker, in m/s.
lift, slow, slow_ticks, hit_vfx, hit_vfx_radiusA shove up; multiply the target's speed by slow for slow_ticks; a particle burst where it lands (a monster's own blood overrides it). These are not in the sentence: tap the step and open its more of it, which holds every number a step takes that its sentence does not say, by the engine's own names.

Melee measures height too. The gap between the two bodies, above or below, must also be within its reach. A claw does not reach a player on a ledge overhead, and a monster on a balcony does not bite someone walking underneath. The test uses each body's height: the target's feet must be no higher than the attacker's top plus the reach, and the attacker's feet no higher than the target's top plus the reach.

Melee needs a clear reach. A wall or a shut door between the two stops it, as it stops a swing: shut a door in a monster's face and its claw no longer finds you through it. A ledge is not a wall here; the height test above judges that.

The editor checks the rule into an attack against the hit: when the rule fires from further away than the hit reaches, the rule says so, as in "attack hits only 1.6 m away: from 2 m it can swing and miss" (a leap before the hit is allowed for).

Melee can still be dodged, a little. It checks range when it runs, not when the attack began. A player who steps back during the wind-up is out of range when the hit comes. What they cannot do is step aside: a melee hit does not care which way the monster faces. For that, use a swing.

Swings: an attack you can dodge

A swing is a swept collider: a wedge in front of the monster that moves through an arc over several ticks. A target the wedge passes through is hit, once. A target that steps out of its path is missed. A monster's claw and a player's fist are the same engine feature, edited the same way.

A swing is an attack like the others: a step of the attack state, with its own numbers in it. Press + add a step at 0 s and pick swing for … damage, … m long, from … to … over … s (swing) in the Attack group. It happens at the moment of the state its at says, on the monster's next decision, like a melee hit; then the wedge sweeps on for its own time, whatever the state does next.

A new swing step reads swing for 12 damage, 1.5 m long, from -60° to 60° over 0.2 s. Tap a number to change it:

In the sentenceThe engine's wordWhat it does
swing for … damagedamageWhat a hit costs.
… m longreachHow far it reaches, to the target's surface.
from …° to …°sweep_from, sweep_toWhere the wedge starts and ends, in degrees from where the monster faces. Positive is to its left. Equal values are a thrust that does not turn: a horn, a spear.
over … sticksHow long it is live. This is what makes it readable.

Under the sentence, more of it folds out the rest, listed in its title by the engine's own names: more of it (half_arc, min_reach, cooldown, pitch_from, pitch_to, half_pitch, knockback, hit_vfx, hit_vfx_radius, lift, slow, slow_ticks). These boxes keep the engine's units, turns and ticks, and each has its ⓘ. A new step starts with a half_arc of 0.0833 (30° each way) and a cooldown of 10 ticks.

Under "more of it"What it does
half_arcHow wide the wedge is, either side of where it points, in turns. 0.125 is 45° each way.
min_reachAn inner edge. Anything pressed against the monster is missed, so closing in beats it.
cooldownTicks after this swing ends before another may start, at most 255. The brain's own cooldown usually covers this.
pitch_from, pitch_to, half_pitchThe vertical part, in turns (0.25 is straight up). With half_pitch empty or 0 there is no height check beyond the body's height. Set it, and an overhead chop misses something at its feet.
knockback, lift, slow, slow_ticks, hit_vfx, hit_vfx_radiusAs for a melee hit. A second slow from the same attacker replaces the first rather than stacking.

Fractions such as 1/8 are fine in these boxes, and a decimal comma is read as a point.

The default swing

Whatever a step leaves out comes from the monster's default swing, on Advanced under Its default swing: "What a swing step leaves out. A swing is a step like any other attack (Behaviour: a state, + add a step, Attack): its numbers in it." A monster with none says "This monster has no swing: …" and offers + swing, which starts a short jab: damage 10, reach 1.5, half_arc 1/8, sweep_from −1/8, sweep_to 1/8, ticks 12, cooldown 20. Above its fields, a preview plays it on a loop: from above for reach and arc, and from the side for height. remove the swing takes it away. What neither the step nor the default swing gives, the engine's own default fills.

  • Share, then differ. Put what every swing of the monster shares in the default swing (its reach, its knockback) and give each step only what makes it different. A jab and a slam are two steps with their own numbers.
  • A step from before. A monster written when a swing was a field of the monster has a step with no numbers at all. It reads swing its default swing (on Advanced), and says "It swings the default swing, set on Advanced. Give it numbers of its own to make this swing different from the others." give it numbers of its own copies the default swing's numbers into the step, to change there.
  • It must hurt. A swing step that ends up with no damage, none of its own and no default swing to take one from, is refused when the monster loads.

Two swings, and the cooldown

  • A newer swing replaces one still sweeping. The first stops where it is and hits nothing more. The second starts from its own sweep_from with a fresh list of who it has hit, so the two are two blows, and the same player can be hit by both.
  • The cooldown starts when a swing ends. A swing step that comes during it does not happen, and is not kept for later. A replaced swing never ends, so it starts none.
  • The preview knows. On Behaviour, the preview runs a swing by these rules, as it runs the other attacks, and says what it did: "swings for 12", "swings and misses (2.3 m, it reaches 1.5)" or "swing skipped: still cooling down".

Timing a swing

The demo puts the attack state on a timeline: the wind-up before the swing step, the ticks the collider is live, and the recovery. Grey lines are the monster's decisions. Drag the green dot round the monster during the wind-up to see when a dodge works.

Good swings follow three rules:

  • A visible wind-up. 0.27 to 0.53 s (16 to 32 ticks) between entering and the swing step. The attack animation should show the arm going back during it.
  • A sweep the eye can follow. 0.13 to 0.2 s (8 to 12 live ticks) for a claw. A big monster can take longer and sweep wider.
  • A recovery that leaves an opening. The state's length should end after the swing, so the monster stands still for a moment before chasing again.

How a swing looks in the game

Every swing is drawn in the game as a slash streak: a band near the tip of the swept wedge, laid down tick by tick between where the blade was and where it is, fading behind it. It is built from the same numbers as the collider, so what the player sees is what can hit them. It is drawn whether or not the debug overlay is on; the overlay's faint wedge and red collider are for tuning, and go with the overlay.

On Advanced, under Its default swing, how it looks in the game sets this monster's look, for every swing step it has ("How this monster's swing is drawn in the game, over the default look (Player → slash)."). It is there whether or not the monster has a default swing:

Each field shows the look the swing really has. A field you set is marked (own), and the rest comes from the default, which is set once for the module in Player → slash (it is also how other players' swings look). back to the default look clears everything this monster set. A monster that is deleted takes its look with it.

  • Only swings are drawn. A melee hit, an instant shot or a missile has no streak: the streak follows the swept collider, and only a swing has one.
  • The name is the catalogue name. A monster's look is stored under its body's name in assets/sprites/swings.json, which the studio writes only when something is set. A module that sets nothing gets the engine's look.

Instant shots: bullets

fire an instant shot for … damage, range … m, spread …° (hitscan) is a shot along a ray. Whatever the ray reaches first, a wall or a body, is where it ends. The player dodges it by not being in the line: cover, corners, distance.

ArgumentWhat it does
damage, rangePer bullet, and how far the ray goes.
spreadDegrees of random cone, per bullet.
countBullets in one shot. Default 1. Doom's shotgun guy fires 3.
tracer, knockback, hit_vfx, hit_vfx_radius, lift, slow, slow_ticksA tracer drawn along the ray, by particle kind; a shove per ray; the rest as for a melee hit.

The sentence has damage, range and spread; the rest are under the step's more of it, where Cinderfall's gunner has its count of 3.

Aiming up and down is automatic: a monster's shot goes for the middle of its target's body, even on a step or a balcony. Aiming sideways is not. A shot goes the way the monster's body points, and the body turns at a rate. That is why Doom-style shooters add the target is within 12° of where it faces (target_in_front) to the rule into their attack: they wait until they are lined up.

A burst is several steps, not a special field. Doom's chaingunner is two instant shots at 0.12 and 0.23 s, then a short cooldown.

Missiles: the Missiles page

A missile flies. The player can see it coming and step out of its way. Missiles belong to the module: every monster and every weapon can fire every missile. They have a page of their own: choose Missiles in the rail, or press Open Missiles on a monster's Attacks, or edit name on a step that fires one.

On the left are the module's missiles, each with its speed, whether it falls, homes or bounces, and who fires it (used by spitter · spit, or not used yet). At the top, type a name in new missile, named and press New (a missile of 15 m/s that lasts 3 s and hurts what it hits for 10, drawn as a glowing dot; see Looks), or Copy of slag_glob to start from the one picked, drawn as it is.

The side view

The picture is the missile's flight seen from the side, as the engine flies it: the shooter on the left, a target in front of it, its path, and the circle of its blast. Above it, one line says how it flies: "thrown to land on the target's middle · reaches 8 m in 0.73 s (44 ticks) · peaks 0.5 m up". Fire flies it and bursts its effects where it lands. target at moves the target from 2 to 30 m away, and fired by a player (level, no throw) fires it as a player does, along a level aim. Under the picture the page says what would go wrong at this distance, or "nothing to worry about at this distance":

  • No lifetime: a shot that misses would stay in the world for ever.
  • It does not reach the target, climbs out of reach, passes over it, or runs out first.
  • Its blast reaches back to the one that fired it.
  • Nothing fires it yet.

What it does

Four panels hold what it does, each a list of sentences with a button to add one:

  • When it hits (on_hit): when it reaches a body or a wall. "What it reached is the target; a blast hurts everything in range." A row of effects chips bursts a particle kind where it lands at a tap. + when it hits adds a step.
  • When it runs out (on_expire): when its life ends. "A split in the air is a short life and missiles here."
  • When it bounces (on_bounce), shown once it bounces: run where it bounced, each time, such as a clink or a puff.
  • On its way (sequence): steps at moments of its flight, each with its time in seconds: "bomblets dropped as it flies, a split part way."
Its stepsWhat they do
a blast of … damage within … m, weaker at the edge, knocking back … m lifting … m, hurts the one who fired itA blast (damage_area): everything in range is hurt, a near miss too. Leave hurts the one who fired it off, and a rocket never kills the monster that fired it.
hurt what it hit for …A plain hit on what it reached (damage).
split into … (missile) spread over …° heading on / back / targetMore missiles, from where it is (missile). The angle is the one between two neighbours: 3 spread over 10° fly at −10°, 0° and 10°. They head the way it was going (on), the way it came (back), or at the target.
leave … (monster) where it isA monster where it lands (spawn): an egg that hatches.
play the sound …, show the effect … at impact / selfA burst you hear and see, even when nobody was there.

Its harm is only what these steps do: a missile has no damage of its own. Use hurt what it hit for, not a melee hit (under More): a melee hit measures from the monster that fired, which by then is somewhere else.

Looks and who fires it

Looks has Drawn as, the prop that draws it (the module's own props first, then those served with the engine); Trail (below); While flying, plays state and When it bursts, plays state, the prop's animation states; Burst lasts; and Drawn at, how big that prop is drawn. Fired by lists every monster state, weapon state and missile that fires it, each a link, and delete this missile.

A missile needs a body to exist. With nothing picked in Drawn as, it is drawn as the prop with the missile's own name; if there is no such prop, the game launches nothing at all. So the studio gives every new missile one:

  • A missile made with New, and one a recipe makes for you (bolt), gets a prop of its own name in Props: a small glowing dot the studio paints, which nothing bumps into (walk-through). It shows in the game at once. Drawn as still reads (nothing: an unseen shot), because the missile is drawn by its own name.
  • A copy made with Copy of missile is drawn as the one it was copied from.
  • When a missile has nothing to be drawn as (you deleted its prop, say), the studio names it as a problem before you press Play, with a count on the rail and a line on Home under What is wrong: "missile "spit": is drawn as the prop "spit", which the module does not have, so the game cannot fire it. Make that prop, or pick one under "Drawn as" on the Missiles page."

For a real look, make a small prop in Props with a sheet whose state 0 flies and state 1 bursts, and pick it in Drawn as. Or draw over the dot: its picture is one of the module's own, named after the missile in capitals (SPIT). Renaming that prop later keeps the missile drawn: what Drawn as names follows the new name, as a monster's body does.

A trail

Trail, under Looks, picks one of the module's particle kinds to follow the missile for its whole flight, from launch until it bursts or runs out, through every bounce (trail). It lists the particle kinds only, by their look (13 · smoke), and none. The specks come out at that kind's own rate: "particles that follow it on its way; the kind's rate (Particles page) is how thick". Press Fire and the side view draws the trail along the flight. In the list of missiles, one with a trail says trails.

  • Smoke or flame. On the Particles page, the kind's on a body says what its specks do once out: left behind where they came out (smoke, a trail), or carried with it (a flame, an aura). A smoke trail stays in the air behind a rocket; a flame stays wrapped round a fireball.
  • The starter's kinds. A module made from the starter has three kinds of its own: 20, a muzzle flash; 21, a tracer; 22, sparks. A tracer is a line, not specks, so it is not offered as a trail.
  • A kind that is not there. A trail that names a tracer, or a kind the module does not have, draws nothing, and the side view's checks say so: "Its trail is particle kind 21, which is a tracer, not particles: nothing follows it. Pick one under Looks."

Firing it

In a monster's attack state, add the step fire the missile … at the target (missile, in the Attack group); a weapon's is launch the missile. Tick aiming ahead of it (lead) and it aims where a moving target will be, from its speed and the missile's. Like bullets, missiles go for the target's middle; a falling one is thrown on the arc that lands there.

A fan of shots is the step's count and arc, and where it appears is its offset, how far in front of the body. They are under the step's more of it: count 3 and arc 10 fire three shots 10° apart, as Cinderfall's Smelter fires its volleys. A missile that splits at once does the same from the missile's side: a short Lasts, and split into under When it runs out, so every monster and weapon that fires it fans. The first exercise below builds one.

Missiles that make missiles

A split into step can go in any of the four panels. In When it hits, it is a cluster that bursts into more; in When it runs out, a split in the air; in On its way, bomblets dropped as it flies. The new missiles launch from where the old one is, and may split again. At most keeps the total from one shot within a budget, 16 unless you say, because each one is a prop in the world.

Cooldowns

A cooldown is a deadline in a number, not a countdown:

  • start cooldown … for … s (set_cooldown) writes "now + that long" into a number. Its jitter, under more of it, adds up to that many extra ticks, so two monsters that started together stop firing in step.
  • cooldown … is over (cooldown_ready) holds once that moment has passed. A number that was never set is ready at once.

The number appears on Advanced under Its numbers as soon as a step or a rule names it: the recipes' ready is one. A monster with two attacks can have two, claw_ready and spit_ready, so a claw does not delay a spit. The preview shows each one: "cooldown ready: 0.4 s left". A saved game keeps a monster's cooldowns, as the time left.

A warning before the blow

warn the player with … for … s (telegraph) tells clients an attack is coming, so they can draw a warning: the particle kind to draw, and how long until the blow lands. Put it at 0 s of the attack state. The gap between the warning and the strike is the player's reaction time, and you can tune it as one number. The Shooter, Flyer, Charger and boss recipes all warn before they strike.

Facing, aim and teleports

  • Turn speed on Overview is how fast the body turns to face its target. The recipes use 12, quick enough to keep up with a strafing player.
  • A state that faces the target turns towards it on every decision.
  • The step turn to face the target (face) turns its aim once, at that instant. Put one at 0 s of an attack, and another just before each shot of a volley, so each shot goes where you are rather than where you were.
  • Because shots follow the body, use the target is within …° of where it faces on the rule into a ranged attack.
  • teleport behind / near the target, … m away, with effect … (teleport, in the Moving group) takes it from here to beside its target, facing it, with an effect at both ends. behind is at the target's back (or a little to one side); near is a random spot around the target that it could walk to the target from. It only lands where its body fits (a floor, headroom, no wall or body in the way); with no such spot it stays put. Players see a jump, never a slide. The preview shows it on the other side of the target.

A missile's When it hits has a melee hit (from More) with a range of 1 m. It hits the player square on, and does nothing. Why?

  • Missiles cannot do damage
  • The range is too small for a missile
  • A melee hit measures from the monster that fired, far away by now; a missile's hit should be hurt what it hit for

A missile's steps run as its owner, with what it reached as the target. Contact is already proven by the flight, so hurt what it hit for (damage) takes the amount with no range test.

Your monster stands on the floor; you stand on a ledge 3 m up, right beside it. Its melee hit reaches 1.5 m and its body is 1 m tall. Does the hit land?

  • No: your feet are higher than its top plus the reach (1 + 1.5)
  • Yes: across the floor you are within 1.5 m
  • Only if it is facing you

Melee measures height too. Your feet at 3 m are above 2.5 m, so it misses.

You want a horn charge: a stab straight ahead that does not sweep. Which swing step does that?

  • From -90° to 90°, with a half_arc of 0.25
  • From 0° to 0°, with a narrow half_arc under more of it
  • One over 0 s

Equal angles make a thrust that does not rotate. A narrow arc makes it a line you can step out of. A swing over no time is an instant hit, not a thrust.

A monster's attack has two swing steps, at 0.13 s and 0.4 s, each over 0.2 s. The first is still sweeping when the second comes. What happens?

  • The second is dropped: a monster swings one swing at a time
  • The first stops where it is, and the second starts fresh, from its own angle, and can hit you again
  • Both sweep at once, and a hit from either counts once

A newer swing replaces one still sweeping, with a fresh list of who it has hit: two blows. Only a swing during the last one's cooldown does not happen.

You set Gem Hollow's slime slash colour to red under how it looks in the game and play. No red streak appears when it bumps you. Why?

  • The streak is only drawn with the debug overlay on
  • The slime attacks with a melee hit; only a swing, the swept collider, draws a streak
  • The colour only applies to weapons

The streak follows the swing's sweep, debug or not. A melee hit has no sweep to draw. Give the slime a swing step in place of its melee hit, and the red shows.

A spitter throws a glob that Falls 9 m/s² at you 8 m away. You step back to 12 m before it fires. Where does the glob land?

  • At 8 m: the arc was worked out when it entered its attack
  • Short of you: a falling missile always drops before the target
  • On you: a monster throws a falling missile on the arc that lands on its target, worked out as it fires

A monster's throw is solved when the missile is fired, from where the target is then. Its Speed is along the ground, so a further throw is a higher lob. Move after it fires, and it misses.
