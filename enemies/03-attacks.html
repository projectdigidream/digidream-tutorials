Attacks: swings and missiles

An attack is a state with a sequence: turn to the target, wait, strike, start a cooldown. What the strike is decides how the player fights back: step out of reach, step aside, take cover, or dodge a ball of fire. This lesson covers all four kinds of attack and the timing around them.

You'll learnhow an attack state is built; the difference between melee and a swing; how to tune a swing on the 🌀 swing tab, and how it looks in the game on the 💨 slash tab; hitscan bullets; missiles on the ☄ missiles tab; cooldowns, wind-ups and warnings; and how aim and turning work Time45 minutes NeedsGem Hollow open in the studio; for the last exercise, the grunt from the earlier lessons, and a prop in the module to draw a fireball with (a small sheet with a flying animation and a burst)

The shape of an attack

The new monster's attack state is the pattern almost every attack follows:

attack = {
  anim = 2, move = "stop", face = "target", ticks = 20, next = "chase", interruptible = true,
  sequence = {
    { at = 0,  action = "face" },
    { at = 10, action = "melee", damage = 8, range = "1.5" },
    { at = 10, action = "set_cooldown", var = "ready", ticks = 60 },
  },
}

And chase enters it only when the target is close, visible and the cooldown is over:

{ to = "attack", when = { { cond = "target_within", distance = "1.5" },
                          { cond = "target_visible" },
                          { cond = "cooldown_ready", var = "ready" } } }

Read it as four beats:

  1. Stop and turn. move stop, face target, and a face action at 0.
  2. Wind up. The gap between entering and the strike. This is the player's time to react.
  3. Strike. The action that hurts.
  4. Recover and cool down. The rest of ticks, then back to chase; set_cooldown keeps it from attacking again at once.

Remember the steps of 8. A monster decides every 8 ticks, and sequence times are checked on those decisions (see The brain). In the state above, the face at 0 happens 8 ticks after entering (the first decision in the state), the claw at 10 lands at 16, and the state ends at 24, not 20. The timeline below shows this.

Melee: an instant hit

melee is the simplest attack. On the decision it runs, it hurts the target if the target is close enough, and nothing else happens. Its arguments:

ArgumentWhat it does
damageHit points taken. Required.
rangeHow far it reaches, centre to centre across the floor. Required.
knockback, liftA shove away from the attacker and up, in world units per second.
slow, slow_ticksMultiply the target's speed by slow for slow_ticks. Both are needed.
hit_vfx, hit_vfx_radiusA particle burst where it lands. A monster's own blood overrides it.

Melee measures height too. Since a recent engine change, the gap between the two bodies, above or below, must also be within range. A claw no longer reaches a player on a ledge overhead, and a monster on a balcony no longer bites someone walking underneath. The test uses each body's height: the target's feet must be no higher than the attacker's top plus range, and the attacker's feet no higher than the target's top plus range.

So a monster that cannot reach you should not try. Add a condition to the transition into attack: target_above with by 1 and not on means "you are not more than 1 above me".

Melee can still be dodged, a little. It checks range when it runs, not when the attack began. A player who steps back during the wind-up is out of range when the claw comes. What they cannot do is step aside: melee does not care which way the monster faces. For that, use a swing.

Swings: an attack you can dodge

A swing is a swept collider: a wedge in front of the monster that moves through an arc over several ticks. A target the wedge passes through is hit, once. A target that steps out of its path is missed. A monster's claw and a player's fist are the same engine feature, edited the same way.

A swing has two parts:

  • the shape, on the monster: the 🌀 swing tab writes the swing field;
  • the moment, in the attack state: a swing action in its sequence. It has no arguments. It starts the swing, which then runs for its own ticks.

Open 🌀 swing. With no swing, it says so and offers + swing. The new swing is a short jab: damage 10, reach 1.5, half_arc 1/8, sweep_from −1/8, sweep_to 1/8, ticks 12, cooldown 20. Above the fields, a preview plays it on a loop: from above for reach and arc, and from the side for height. remove the swing takes it away.

FieldWhat it does
damageWhat a hit costs. Required.
reachHow far, to the target's surface.
half_arcHow wide the wedge is, either side of where it points, in turns. 0.125 is 45° each way.
sweep_from, sweep_toWhere the wedge starts and ends, relative to the aim. Equal values are a thrust that does not turn: a horn, a spear.
ticksHow long it is live. This is what makes it readable. 0 counts as 1, an instant hit.
min_reachAn inner edge. Anything pressed against the monster is missed, so closing in beats it.
pitch_from, pitch_to, half_pitchThe vertical part, in turns (0.25 is straight up). With half_pitch empty or 0 there is no height check beyond the body's height. Set it, and an overhead chop misses something at its feet.
cooldownTicks before another swing may start, at most 255. The brain's own cooldown usually covers this.
knockback, lift, slow, slow_ticks, hit_vfx, hit_vfx_radiusAs for melee. A second slow from the same attacker replaces the first rather than stacking.

The studio says it under the fields: "Angles are turns: 0.125 is 45°. `sweep_from` equal to `sweep_to` is a thrust that does not rotate. `half_pitch` of 0 means no height check at all." Fractions such as 1/8 are fine in any of these boxes.

Timing a swing

The demo puts the attack state on a timeline: the wind-up before the swing step, the ticks the collider is live, and the recovery. Grey lines are the monster's decisions. Drag the green dot round the monster during the wind-up to see when a dodge works.

Good swings follow three rules:

  • A visible wind-up. 16 to 32 ticks between entering and the swing step. The attack animation should show the arm going back during it.
  • A sweep the eye can follow. 8 to 12 live ticks for a claw. A big monster can take longer and sweep wider.
  • A recovery that leaves an opening. The state's ticks should end after the swing, so the monster stands still for a moment before chasing again.

A monster with a swing step and no swing on the 🌀 swing tab is refused when it loads. It would otherwise swing with the engine's defaults, which nobody chose.

How a swing looks in the game: the slash tab

Every swing is drawn in the game as a slash streak: a band near the tip of the swept wedge, laid down tick by tick between where the blade was and where it is, fading behind it. It is built from the same numbers as the collider, so what the player sees is what can hit them. It is drawn whether or not the debug overlay is on; the overlay's faint wedge and red collider are for tuning, and go with the overlay.

The 💨 slash tab, beside 🌀 swing, sets this monster's look under how it looks in the game:

FieldWhat it doesEngine's look
showOff: this swing is not drawn at all. The collider still hits.on
colorThe streak's colour. With a texture, it tints the texture.#fff2c8, a warm white
opacityHow solid a new piece of the streak is, 0 to 1.0.6
fadeSeconds a piece takes to fade away.0.22
widthHow much of the swept wedge the band covers, from the tip inward: 1 is all of it, 0.4 the outer 40%.0.4

textureA picture under sprites/ (.png or .webp), stretched along the sweep and across the band. (none: a plain band) draws a flat band.none

Each field shows the look the swing really has. A field you set is marked (own), and the rest comes from the default, which is set once for the module in 🧍 Player → 💨 slash (it is also how other players' swings look). back to the default look clears everything this monster set. A monster that is deleted takes its look with it.

  • Only swings are drawn. A melee step, a hitscan or a missile has no streak: the streak follows the swept collider, and only a swing has one.
  • The name is the catalogue name. A monster's look is stored under its body's name in assets/sprites/swings.json, which the studio writes only when something is set. A module that sets nothing gets the engine's look.

Hitscan: bullets

hitscan is an instant shot along a ray. Whatever the ray reaches first, a wall or a body, is where it ends. The player dodges it by not being in the line: cover, corners, distance.

ArgumentWhat it does
damage, rangePer bullet, and how far the ray goes. Both required.
countBullets in one shot. Default 1. Doom's shotgun guy fires 3.
spreadDegrees of random cone, per bullet.
tracerA tracer style to draw, by particle kind. Empty for none.
knockbackPer ray, away from the muzzle.
hit_vfx, hit_vfx_radius, lift, slow, slow_ticksAs for melee.

Aiming up and down is automatic: a monster's shot goes for the middle of its target's body, even on a step or a balcony. Aiming sideways is not. A shot goes the way the monster's body points, and the body turns at a rate. That is why Doom-style shooters add target_in_front with arc 12 to the transition into their attack: they wait until they are lined up.

A burst is several steps, not a special field. Doom's chaingunner is two hitscan steps at 7 and 14 ticks, then a short cooldown.

Missiles

A missile flies. The player can see it coming and step out of its way. Missiles belong to the module: every monster can fire every missile. They live on the ☄ missiles tab.

Press + missile. The studio asks for a "missile name" and suggests fireball. The new missile has speed 8, gravity 0, life 300, z_offset 0.5, anim_fly 0, anim_burst 1, burst_ticks 20, and one on_hit action, damage 10. Each missile is a card titled Missile: name, with add on_hit, add on_expire and delete missile.

FieldWhat it does
propThe catalogue record that draws it, and whose radius it hits with. Empty means a record with the missile's own name.
speedWorld units per second. Required.
radiusWhat it collides as. Empty uses the prop's radius, which is usually right.
z_offsetHow far above the shooter's feet it starts. Mid-body is good; at the ankles it hits the first step.
gravity0 flies flat, like a fireball. More makes a grenade that drops.
lifeTicks before it expires on its own. Always set one.
homingDegrees per tick it may turn towards its target. Empty flies straight.
anim_fly, anim_burst, burst_ticksThe prop's animation states while flying and bursting, and how long the burst shows.
on_hitActions when it reaches a body or a wall. This is where the damage goes.
on_expireActions when its life runs out.

A missile needs a body to exist. If no prop in the catalogue has the missile's name (or the name in its prop field), nothing is launched and nothing complains. Make a small prop in 📦 Props with a sheet whose state 0 flies and state 1 bursts, and either give it the missile's name or pick it in prop.

What goes in on_hit

  • damage with an amount, for a plain hit. Use this, not melee: melee measures from the monster that fired, which by then is somewhere else.
  • damage_area for a blast: damage, radius, falloff, knockback, lift. hurts_owner off keeps a rocket from killing the monster that fired it.
  • vfx with at impact, to draw an explosion where it landed, even when nobody was there.
  • sound with from impact, for the burst.

Firing it

In the attack state's sequence, add a missile step:

  • kind: which missile. Required.
  • count and arc: a fan. Three with an arc of 12 fly at −12°, 0° and +12°, like Doom's mancubus.
  • offset: how far in front of the body it starts. Use at least the monster's radius, or the missile is touching its owner when it appears.

Like bullets, missiles are aimed up or down at the target's middle for you.

Cooldowns

A cooldown is a deadline in a var, not a countdown:

  • set_cooldown writes "now + ticks" into a var. Its jitter adds up to that many extra ticks, so two monsters that started together stop firing in step.
  • cooldown_ready holds once that tick has passed. A var that was never set is ready at once.

The var must exist on # vars: the new monster's ready is this. A monster with two attacks can have two vars, claw_ready and spit_ready, so a claw does not delay a spit.

A warning before the blow

telegraph tells clients an attack is coming, so they can draw a warning. Put it at 0 of the attack state, with the particle kind to draw in effect, a radius, and ticks until the blow lands. The gap between the telegraph and the strike is the player's reaction time, and you can tune it as one number.

Facing and aim

  • turn_speed on 🧠 brain is turns per second. The engine's default is 4, a quarter of a second for 90°. New monsters get 12, quick enough to keep up with a strafing player.
  • The state field face = target turns it towards its target on every decision.
  • The face action turns its aim once, at that instant. Put one at 0 of an attack, and another just before each shot of a volley, so each shot goes where you are rather than where you were. Its offset and jitter make it aim beside you, on purpose.
  • Because shots follow the body, use target_in_front on the transition into a ranged attack.

In Gem Hollow: three bumps and a dash

Gem Hollow's monsters all attack with melee, each with its own timing. Open each one's attack state to read it:

MonsterInto attack from chase whenThe attack stateWhen it lands
slimetarget_within 1.4, cooldown_ready readyticks 24. At 10: melee damage 10, range 1.2, knockback 6; a sound; set_cooldown ready 60.16 ticks in; back to chase at 24
beetarget_within 2.5, cooldown_ready readyticks 30. At 0: a buzz. At 6: impulse speed 9 towards target. At 14: melee damage 8, range 1.3, knockback 5; set_cooldown ready 90.the buzz at 8, the dash at 8 too, the sting at 16
snailtarget_within 1.5, cooldown_ready readyticks 30. At 14: melee damage 12, range 1.4, knockback 7; a sound; set_cooldown ready 50.16 ticks in
  • The bee starts from further away. It enters attack at 2.5 but only stings within 1.3. The impulse closes the gap: a shove of 9 units a second towards you, which makes the dive. Step sideways during the buzz and the dive goes past you, so the sting finds nobody.
  • All three land on a decision. Steps at 10 and 14 both run at the decision 16 ticks in, so the slime and the snail hit at the same moment after they stop; what differs is how long they then stand still (8 and 16 ticks, since a state also ends on a decision) and their cooldowns (60 and 50).
  • None of them has a swing, so none draws a slash streak, and their 💨 slash tabs change nothing yet. The exercise below gives the snail one.
  • Gem Hollow's one missile, star_shot, on ☄ missiles, is the player's: the Star Toss weapon throws it. No monster fires it. Its on_hit has one step, damage 20, so a star kills a slime and takes two to a snail.

A missile hurts only through on_hit. A missile has no damage field of its own; its harm is a damage step in on_hit, as on star_shot. See Hitscan, missiles and swings.

In Cinderfall: bullets, globs and shells

Cinderfall, a new example first-person shooter that is still being tested (the tour says how to import it), has every kind of attack but the swing. Open each monster's attack states:

Monster, stateTicksSequenceCooldown
husk, attack2612: melee damage 9, range 1.6, knockback 4ready 30
hound, bite146: melee damage 6, range 1.4, knockback 3none
hound, leap202: impulse speed 13 towards target, vertical 4; 12: melee damage 8, range 1.5ready 90
gunner, shoot140: hitscan damage 3, range 36, count 3, spread 7ready 70, jitter 30
spitter, spit2412: missile slag_globready 55, jitter 30

  • The gunner aims first. Its aim state stands still facing you for 18 ticks before shoot. That pause is the player's warning, and the time a strafing player has to step out of line. The shot is three pellets of 3 with a spread of 7: at range, one or two land.
  • The hound throws itself. leap is the bee's dive from Gem Hollow made bigger: an impulse of 13 with 4 upward, then a melee that only lands if you are still within 1.5 when it comes down.
  • The slam is a blast with no warning. damage_area centred on the brute hurts everything within 3.6 and throws it back and up. It has no telegraph; the 16 ticks of wind-up and the animation are the only sign.

The two monster missiles, on ☄ missiles:

slag_glob     speed 11, radius 0.35, z_offset 1.0, gravity 9, life 140
              on_hit: damage_area 10, radius 1.8, falloff · sound glob.splat
cinder_shell  speed 16, radius 0.35, z_offset 1.9, life 120
              on_hit: damage_area 20, radius 2.4, falloff, knockback 6 · sound boom
  • A glob falls. gravity 9 makes the spitter's glob a lob, not a line, so it can splash you behind low cover.
  • Both are blasts. Neither has a damage step: a near miss still hurts. Neither turns off hurts_owner, so a shell that bursts against the brute's own wall can catch the brute.

offset is a distance, not an angle. On a missile step it is how far in front of the body the missile appears, in world units. The brute's second shell has offset 8, so it starts 8 units in front of the brute, and the Smelter's volley uses −12, −4, 4 and 12: 12 units behind it, then 4 behind, then 4 and 12 in front. For a fan of shots, use count and arc (degrees) instead, and keep offset a little over the body's radius.

Exercise: a brute that fans its shells

  1. Import Cinderfall (the tour says how), choose ☠ Monsters and pick brute. Tap the fire state.

  2. Delete the second missile step (at 14, offset 8) with its ✕, and the sound step at 14 beside it.

  3. On the first missile step, set count 2, arc 10 and offset 1.2, a little more than the brute's radius (0.9).

  4. Check the bar says loads clean, then press ▶ Play and find a brute (the old town has one). It now fires two shells at once, 10° apart, both from just in front of it, and the gap between them widens with distance.

Exercise: a snail that swipes

Exercise: claws you can dodge, and a fireball

  1. Pick grunt and open 🌀 swing. Press + swing. Set damage 12, reach 1.2, half_arc 1/6, sweep_from 1/6, sweep_to −1/6, and ticks 10. Watch the preview sweep from its left to its right.

  2. Tap the attack state. Delete the melee step with its ✕. Use add step to add swing, and set its at to 16. Set the state's ticks to 40. Move the set_cooldown step's at to 16 too.

  3. Pick chase and open its transition to attack. Change target_within to 1.8: the monster should start winding up a little outside its reach, as you walk in.

  4. Open ☄ missiles and press + missile. Name it spit. Set its prop to the small prop you made for it, speed 7, z_offset 0.6, and the on_hit damage amount to 8.

  5. Add a var spit_ready on # vars. Add a state spit: anim 2, move stop, face target, ticks 32, next chase. Its steps: face at 0; missile at 16 with kind spit and offset 0.6; set_cooldown at 16 with var spit_ready, ticks 150, jitter 40.

  6. On chase, add a transition to spit, below the one to attack: target_within 12, target_visible, target_in_front with arc 12, cooldown_ready with var spit_ready.

  7. Check "loads clean", then press ▶ Play. From a distance, sidestep the spit. Up close, let it wind up, then step sideways as the claw starts. Try stepping to its left, then to its right, and find which side is safe.

The safe side is the one the sweep starts on, once the wedge has left it. Positive angles are to the monster's left. With sweep_from 1/6 (its left) and sweep_to −1/6 (its right), step to its left just after the claw starts. Stepping right runs into the claw.

A missile's on_hit uses melee with range 1. It hits the player square on, and does nothing. Why?

  • Missiles cannot do damage
  • The range is too small for a missile
  • melee measures from the monster that fired, far away by now; a missile's hit should use damage

A missile's actions run as its owner, with what it reached as the target. Contact is already proven by the flight, so damage takes the amount with no range test.

Your monster stands on the floor; you stand on a ledge 3 units up, right beside it. Its melee has range 1.5 and its body is 1 tall. Does the claw hit?

  • No: your feet are higher than its top plus the range (1 + 1.5)
  • Yes: across the floor you are within 1.5
  • Only if it is facing you

Melee measures height too. Your feet at 3 are above 2.5, so it misses. Use target_above on the transition so it does not try.

You want a horn charge: a stab straight ahead that does not sweep. Which swing does that?

  • half_arc 0.25, sweep_from −0.25, sweep_to 0.25
  • sweep_from and sweep_to both 0, with a narrow half_arc
  • ticks 0

Equal sweep values make a thrust that does not rotate. A narrow arc makes it a line you can step out of. ticks 0 is an instant hit, not a thrust.

You set Gem Hollow's slime slash colour to red on its 💨 slash tab and play. No red streak appears when it bumps you. Why?

  • The streak is only drawn with the debug overlay on
  • The slime attacks with melee; only a swing, the swept collider, draws a streak
  • The colour only applies to weapons

The streak follows the swing's sweep, debug or not. A melee step has no sweep to draw. Give the slime a swing and use a swing step, and the red shows.
