Your first monster

A monster is two things with one name: a body the game can draw and bump into, and a brain that decides what the body does. In this lesson you make both in the Monsters editor, starting from a recipe, give it a sprite, put it on a map and play against it.

You'll learnhow the Monsters editor is laid out; what each recipe gives you; the numbers on Overview; how Looks picks the sprite and the animation each state plays; how the game lights a monster, and when it wants its own light; where the body's size is set; how to place a monster, and what dormant really does Time25 minutes Needsthe studio's example project, Gem Hollow (open it from Home → examples → Gem Hollow); for your own monster, a module with at least one sprite sheet that has idle, walk, attack, pain and death animations (see the Sprites track), and a map with a start spawn (Placing things)

Where monsters live

Monsters belong to the module, never to one map. Every map in the module sees the same monsters. A map only says where each one stands. The editor says so too: "Monsters belong to the module; every map can place them."

Open the studio and choose Monsters in the rail on the left, under Gameplay (on a phone held upright, Home is the list of sections). The Monsters editor opens on a monster. Its top bar holds:

  • the monster's picture and name with a ⌄, such as slime ⌄: tap it for the list of every monster, and to make a new one;
  • five tabs: Overview, Behaviour, Attacks, Looks and Advanced;
  • on Behaviour, List and Graph, two ways to show its states;
  • at the right, ⋯, with Rename, Duplicate and Delete.

The five tabs

Each tab shows one side of the monster. You will use all of them in this track:

TabWhat it holdsLesson
OverviewA sentence that sums the monster up, then its main numbers: health, how often it flinches, how fast it runs, how far it sees, whether it flies, and what happens when it dies.this page
BehaviourIts states, as sentences or as a graph, beside a preview that runs them and an editor of the state you pick.The brain
AttacksEvery attack step of every state, editable in place, each with Open state; and the module's missiles, with Open Missiles.Attacks
LooksIts sprite, played by animation and by each side it has; the animation each state plays; the sounds its steps play; and how wide its body is and how big it is drawn.this page
AdvancedIts numbers (the brain's own memory), Its brain: blood, pain, drops and the rest, Its default swing (what a swing step leaves out) and how its swings look in the game, and Its body states.Pain, death and drops, Attacks

The editor talks in metres, seconds and percentages. The game runs at 60 ticks a second, and the files keep times in ticks, so a state that lasts 0.4 s lasts 24 ticks. Wherever the editor shows a field by the engine's own name, its ⓘ button shows the runtime's description of it, the same text as the monster vocabulary reference.

Problems. The editor checks every monster as you work, by the same rules the server uses when it loads them. The rail's Monsters entry shows a count when something is wrong, and Home lists each problem under to fix, in the runtime's own words; tap one to come back here. With nothing wrong, Home says "nothing wrong that the studio can see".

Make a monster

Tap the name at the top left, slime ⌄ in Gem Hollow. The list of monsters opens: each one with its health and how many states it has, and above them New monster.

Type a name in the field (its name, e.g. spitter), then press a recipe. Capitals become lower case and spaces become _ as you type; a name starts with a letter and has at most 24 letters. With no name, the editor says "Type a name first."; with a name already taken, "There is already a monster called …". The new monster opens straight away, and a line says which recipe it came from.

RecipeWhat it doesIts states
BlankStands there; can be hurt and dies. 50 health.idle ★, pain, death
Melee gruntWalks up and hits: waits until it sees you, runs at you, hits for 9 within 1.6 m, then waits half a second before it can hit again. 40 health.look ★, chase, attack, pain, death
ShooterKeeps its distance, warns, fires a missile: backs off inside 4 m, aims with a warning, fires every 2 s. 30 health.idle ★, chase, back_off, aim, fire, pain, death
FlyerHovers 2 m up, keeps away, fires from range. 25 health.idle ★, keep_away, back_off, shoot, pain, death
ChargerWinds up where you can see it, then rushes you and hits hard. 60 health, runs at 7 m/s.idle ★, chase, wind_up, rush, pain, death
Boss (3 phases)Three phases, at 66% and 33% of its health; slams, lobs, summons and leaps. 900 health.fourteen, in three phases; see Recipes

Every recipe also gives the monster a body and a few numbers: a body with a radius of 0.35 m, as tall as its sprite and solid, running at 4 m/s (7 for the charger); drawn with the module's first sprite sheet, each state matched to one of the sheet's animations by name; and it sees 40 m, has a turn speed of 12, fights back at monsters that hurt it, and leaves a body when it dies.

So a new monster already plays. You can place it and fight it before you change anything. The next lessons take its brain apart; this one is about its numbers and its body.

  • Copy of slime in the list, and Duplicate in the ⋯ menu, copy the monster you were editing, brain and body.
  • Rename may have capitals and spaces, such as Fire Imp. The list shows that name; the files use it lower-cased (the editor tells you: the files call it fire_imp), and every brain and missile that names the monster follows.
  • Delete asks first, and takes the monster's body with it.

A recipe that fires a missile (the shooter, the flyer, the boss) uses the module's first missile. A module with none gets a new one called bolt, drawn as a glowing dot the studio paints for it (a prop called bolt), so it shows in the game at once. The dot is drawn 0.6 m tall and leaves a glowing trail in its colour; the module's first missile, if it had no trail, is given one too. For a look of your own, open Missiles (under Gameplay), pick bolt and choose a prop under Drawn as (Attacks).

Two halves, one name

The game reads a monster from two files, and the studio writes both:

  • The body is a record in the module's catalogue, assets/props.txt. The server and every client read it, because a client needs to know how big a thing is, how fast it moves and what it looks like.
  • The brain goes into maps/module.lua, as one mobs.load{…} table for the whole module. Only the server reads it. A client never learns a monster's health or pain chance.

The name is the join. A monster called grunt wears the catalogue record called grunt. Nothing says so twice. Here is what Gem Hollow's catalogue holds for its slime:

prop 2001 slime radius 0.55 height 1 speed 2.5 solid
atlas 2001 sheet SLIME anim idle
atlas 2001 state 1 sheet SLIME anim walk
atlas 2001 state 2 sheet SLIME anim attack
atlas 2001 state 3 sheet SLIME anim hurt
atlas 2001 state 4 sheet SLIME anim death

The first line is the body. The atlas lines say which animation of the sheet SLIME plays in each animation state. A monster with its own light (below) has bright on its first line too. You never type these lines: Overview, Looks and Advanced write them.

The vocabulary also has a prop field, for two monsters that share one body on purpose. The editor does not show it: each monster you make wears the record with its own name, which is almost always what you want. If you rename a prop on the Props page, what used it follows the new name: a monster's or a missile's body, what a monster drops, the nearest thing it looks for.

Overview: its numbers

Overview starts with a sentence that sums the monster up: "20 health, runs at 2.5 m/s, sees 14 m. In attack: melee hit for 10 damage within 1.2 m knocking back 6 m. …". Below it are its main numbers:

FieldWhat it meansIn the files
HealthWhat it starts with. A hit takes its damage from it, and at 0 it dies.hp
FlinchesHow often a hit makes it flinch (its pain state), as a % of hits. The file keeps it out of 256, so 78% is 200.pain_chance
A bar over itA marker over its head while it lives, showing its health: health (the built-in bar), another marker, or none. The preview draws it too. See Markers.health_bar

A number the editor would not keep is said at once: Runs at takes 0.5 to 40, Flinches 0 to 100. The rest of the brain's own fields (blood, weight, how long a flinch lasts, drops) are on Advanced; see Pain, death and drops.

Looks: its sprite and its animations

Open Looks. On the left the sprite plays, and the line under it says what you see: idle · frame 5 of 8 · 8 fps · loops · side 1 of 8 · 2.91 m tall. On the right:

  • sprite picks the sprite sheet that draws the monster, from the module's sheets. Import a sprite… brings in a new one and picks it: a ThreeSprite project (.tsprite), or an atlas (its .json, then its .png). It also takes a single .png, as a sheet of one picture, the same from every side; a file that is none of these is refused with a message.
  • Edit in ThreeSprite opens the sprite's own project in ThreeSprite, when the sheet was made there. A sheet that came in as pictures has Draw a sprite of its own… instead, which starts a new ThreeSprite sprite under a name you give, for this monster alone, so the art other things use is never drawn over. A sprite just started has no animations yet: until it has some, the monster is drawn as one still picture of the drawing, an idle of one frame, in the preview and in the game. Each state plays then names every state that shows that picture for want of its own animation ("chase: the sprite has no animation called walk, so the game plays idle instead."), and the names go as you draw the animations.
  • animation has a chip for every animation of the sheet, and side one for each of its eight views (front, 45° … back … 315°). A sprite drawn from one view has no sides to pick: the row says "side: one picture, the same from every side". Pause holds the frame; Play goes on.
  • Each state plays lists every state of the brain, each with the animation it plays, picked by name. A state whose animation the sheet does not have shows it as roar (missing) and says SLIME has no "roar": pick one. Above the list, each such state is named with what the game plays instead, which is the sheet's first animation: "death: the sprite has no animation called death, so the game plays idle instead. Pick one, or draw it."
  • Sounds lists every sound the monster's steps play, each with a picker of the module's sounds. Bring in sounds of your own… goes to the Sounds page.
  • Its body has Width (radius), in metres; Drawn at, how big the picture is drawn ("the picture only; its body stays its size"); and Its own light (not lit) (below).

When you pick another sprite, the editor matches the states to its animations by name, and makes the body as tall as the sprite. Check the matches on Each state plays.

Animation states

The server never sends frames. It sends a small number, the animation state, and the client plays the frames for that number at its own pace. A body has five standard ones, and the editor guesses which animation each plays from the animations' names:

NumberMeaningGuessed from an animation whose name contains
0idleidle or stand
1walkwalk, run or move
2attackattack, fire or melee
3painpain or hurt, and otherwise the idle one
4deathdeath or die

Upper or lower case does not matter. If nothing matches, it uses the sheet's first animation. Check the guesses: a sheet with an animation called dead_pose and another called die gets die for death, which may not be what you meant.

You never have to think in numbers: on Each state plays, and in a state's Plays on Behaviour, you pick an animation by its name, and the editor writes the number into the state's anim. An animation the five do not cover, such as a leap or a roar, gets a number of its own, 5 and up. Several states may play one animation: a wander state can play walk, just like chase. The recipes name animations such as aim, fire, slam and leap: a sheet that has one plays it; one that does not plays the nearest of the five (idle for aim, attack for the others).

Its own light

In the game, a monster is lit the way the map around it is: by its room's light, with the lamps near it added on top. One in a dark room is dark, and one beside a torch takes the torch's colour. That is right for most monsters: a grunt waiting in the shadows should be hard to see.

A monster that glows is different: a fire spirit, a ghost, a skull on fire. Tick Its own light (not lit) under Its body ("drawn at full light in the dark, like a fireball, not lit by the room and lamps"), and it is drawn at full brightness wherever it stands. The switch belongs to the body, so Its body states on Advanced has it too, as its own light (not lit). The picture on Looks is always drawn at full light: play to see the difference.

The body's size: Advanced

Its width and how big it is drawn are on Looks, and its speed on Overview. The whole body record is at the end of Advanced, under Its body states:

FieldWhat it means
radiusHow wide the body is, in metres, from its centre. Walls, players and other monsters stop at this circle. The same number as Width (radius) on Looks.
heightHow tall the body is when it collides. A shot over its head misses. A melee attack measures against it too.
sprite heightHow tall the sheet is drawn. This is the sheet's own height, so changing it here grows every monster and prop drawn with that sheet.
scaleMultiplies how big this body is drawn. It does not change what it collides as. The same number as Drawn at on Looks.
speedMetres per second when it walks: Runs at on Overview. Here it can also be 0, for a monster that never moves.
solidWhether things bump into it. A state can switch this off (its Blocks); dying always does, so you can walk over a body.
its own light (not lit)Drawn at full light, not lit by its room and the lamps near it. The same switch as Its own light (not lit) on Looks (above).
sprite sheetOne chip per sheet in the module, then + import sheet…, which takes what Import a sprite… takes, a single .png included. With more than 12 sheets (Gem Hollow has 24), a find among … field appears above the chips.
state 0 · idle … state 4 · deathWhich animation each animation state plays, by number. A number of the monster's own (5 and up) says which states use it. + another state adds a number.

The studio puts it in one line under the fields: "height is what the body collides as. sprite height is how tall the sheet is drawn, for everything that uses it; scale multiplies that for this body alone."

Keep the drawing and the body close. A monster drawn twice the size of its body gets shot "through" its shoulders, and one drawn too small gets hit by bullets that seem to miss. When you pick a new sprite, the studio copies the sheet's height into height for you. After that they are separate numbers.

In Gem Hollow: three bodies

Gem Hollow, the example project bundled with the studio, has three monsters. Each was drawn in ThreeSprite (its source is sources/sprites/SLIME.tsprite, BEE.tsprite and SNAIL.tsprite, which is why Looks offers Edit in ThreeSprite) and baked into a sheet with the same five animations: idle, walk, attack, hurt and death. Because the pain animation is called hurt, the guess put it on state 3, and Each state plays shows pain playing hurt.

Put it on a map

Open a map and choose Place (key 3). The Place palette has a chip for every monster in the module. Tap your monster's chip, then tap the floor. Tap the chip again to stop placing.

A placed monster has a small form, titled Monster and its name:

  • name: which monster this is. Rename it to another monster to swap it.
  • kind: monster or prop.
  • dormant: it starts asleep.
  • height: where its feet start.
  • facing (°): which way it should face, drawn as an arrow at its feet.

Monsters do not take their facing yet. The studio writes the facing into the map, but the engine's mobs.spawn ignores it, so every placed monster starts facing 0 (east). A change is proposed. When it lands, maps you already made take their monsters' facings without a new export. Until then, a monster turns to you as soon as it has a target, so this mostly matters for ambushes.

What dormant means

A dormant monster makes no decisions and does not move. It costs almost nothing. It wakes only when another monster wakes it:

  • a monster that runs alert_nearby (the step alert the monsters within … m) wakes every monster within its radius, and hands them its target;
  • a monster that runs wake with a radius (under More in the step chooser) wakes the dormant ones near it.

Shooting a dormant monster does not wake it. The engine takes its health, and can kill it, but it never starts thinking. A room of dormant monsters with nothing to wake them is a room of statues. Use dormant for an ambush that another monster sets off, as The brain shows. For "asleep until it sees you", leave dormant off: a look state that waits for it can see the target already does it.

Play against it

Press Play. The studio writes the module and the map, and the server loads them. The server checks the same rules the editor does, and refuses a monster that breaks one, so fix what the rail's count and Home's to fix list say before you play.

While you play, watch for these:

  • It does not move. Is it dormant? Can it see you? A look state waits until you are inside its Sees distance and in a clear line.
  • It slides. Its feet and its Runs at disagree. Change the walk animation's stride or fps, or the speed.
  • It sticks in doorways. Its Width (radius) is too wide for your doors.
  • Its picture floats or sinks. The sheet's feet are not on its bottom edge. Fix it in the sprite editor, not with height.

Exercise: a heavier slime

Start with a monster that already works. You change the example's own copy, so nothing you do here can break the bundled Gem Hollow; a fresh copy beside Gem Hollow on Home gives you a new one at any time.

  1. On Home, under examples, press Gem Hollow. The studio opens it as a module of your own.

  2. Choose Monsters. If the editor is not on the slime, tap the name at the top left and pick slime (20 health · 7 states). Open Overview and check the numbers against the table above: Health 20, Runs at 2.5.

  3. Open Looks. Tap the animation chips from idle to death and watch each one, then tap the side chips to turn the slime round.

  4. Under Its body, set Drawn at to 1.6 and Width (radius) to 0.85, so it is drawn bigger and collides bigger. On Overview, set Runs at to 1.5: a big slime is a slow slime.

  5. Check that the rail's Monsters shows no problem count, then press Play. Go into the first zone: its slimes are now big, slow and harder to jump over.

Exercise: a grunt of your own

  1. Open Monsters in your own module and tap the name at the top left (a module with no monsters opens on the list straight away). Under New monster, type grunt, then press Melee grunt. The grunt opens on Behaviour, with its five states.

  2. Open Looks. Under sprite, pick the sheet you want. Then read Each state plays, and fix any guess that is wrong.

  3. Tap each animation chip and watch it. Tap the side chips to check the side and back views; a sprite drawn from one view has none to tap. If Each state plays names a state the game plays something else for, pick an animation for it.

  4. Set Width (radius) so the body fits the picture's shoulders. On Overview, set Runs at to 3 for a slow, heavy grunt.

  5. Check the rail shows no problem count for Monsters.

  6. Open a map, choose Place and put one grunt in a room you can see from the start spawn. Leave dormant off.

  7. Press Play. Walk into its view and let it come to you. Step back when it stops to hit, and see that a hit only lands when you stand within 1.6 m.

Keep this grunt. Every lesson in this track changes it a little, until it has a brain of its own, a swing, a fireball, a flinch and a drop.

On Advanced, you raise the sprite height of a sheet from 1 to 2. What changes?

  • Only this monster is drawn bigger
  • Every monster and prop drawn with that sheet is drawn bigger; their collision does not change
  • The monster is drawn and collides twice as tall

Sprite height is the sheet's own height, shared by everything that uses the sheet. Collision is height and radius. To draw one body bigger alone, use Drawn at on Looks (its scale).

You place three monsters in a room, all dormant, and fire at them from the door. What happens?

  • They wake up when the first one is hit
  • They wake up when they see you
  • They lose health and can die, but they never move: only another monster's alert_nearby or wake wakes them

A dormant monster makes no decisions at all, so neither seeing you nor being hurt reaches its brain. Something awake has to wake it.

Why can a client draw a monster but never know its pain chance?

  • The body is in the catalogue, which clients read; the brain is in maps/module.lua, which only the server runs
  • The pain chance is encrypted in the map
  • Clients ask the server for it on every hit

The two halves are split on purpose. What a client needs (size, speed, pictures) is in assets/props.txt. What decides behaviour stays on the server.
