Your first monster

A monster is two things with one name: a body the game can draw and bump into, and a brain that decides what the body does. In this lesson you make both in the Monsters workshop, check its animations, put it on a map and play against it.

You'll learnhow the Monsters workshop is laid out; what + new monster gives you; what each field of the body means; how the five animation states are chosen; how to place a monster, and what dormant really does Time25 minutes Needsa module with at least one sprite sheet that has idle, walk, attack, pain and death animations (see the Sprites track), and a map with a start spawn (Placing things)

Where monsters live

Monsters belong to the module, never to one map. Every map in the module sees the same monsters. A map only says where each one stands.

Open the studio and choose ☠ Monsters in the rail on the left (on a phone, open the rail with ‹). The studio opens the monster workshop, which fills the screen. Its top bar holds:

  • ‹ back, to go back to where you were;
  • 🧍 player, which jumps across to the player's window;
  • one chip for each monster, then + new monster;
  • the reminder "monsters belong to the module, not to one map";
  • on the right, either "loads clean" or a red count such as "2 problem(s)". Hover over the count to read the problems, in the runtime's own words.

Below the bar are three panes. On a wide screen you see them side by side. On a phone, the tabs 🎞 preview, ⬡ graph and ☰ details switch between them.

The six details tabs

The details pane has six tabs. You will use all of them in this track:

Every field has an ⓘ button. It shows the runtime's own description of the field, the same text as the monster vocabulary reference. A field whose label ends in ?, such as sight?, is optional: leave it empty and the engine uses its default.

Make a monster

Press + new monster. The studio asks for a "monster name (letters, digits, underscore)" and suggests grunt. Capitals become lower case, and other characters are dropped. If the name is empty or another monster already has it, the status line says "that name is empty or taken".

The new monster is not empty. It gets a working brain and a body:

PartWhat you get
Brainhp 30, turn_speed 12, sight 40, pain_chance 128, infighting on, corpse on, initial state look, and one var, ready = 0.
Stateslook ★, chase, attack, pain and death. It stands still until it sees you, runs at you, claws for 8 when you are within 1.5, then waits 60 ticks before it can claw again.
Bodyradius 0.35, height as tall as the sheet, speed 4, solid on, drawn with the module's first sprite sheet.

So a new monster already plays. You can place it and fight it before you change anything. The next lessons take the brain apart. This one is about the body.

Two halves, one name

The game reads a monster from two files, and the studio writes both:

  • The body is a record in the module's catalogue, assets/props.txt. The server and every client read it, because a client needs to know how big a thing is, how fast it moves and what it looks like.
  • The brain goes into maps/module.lua, as one mobs.load{…} table for the whole module. Only the server reads it. A client never learns a monster's hit points or pain chance.

The name is the join. A monster called grunt wears the catalogue record called grunt. Nothing says so twice. Here is what the catalogue holds for the starter module's monster, spirit:

prop 1000 spirit radius 0.35 height 1 speed 5.2 solid
atlas 1000 sheet SPIR anim idle
atlas 1000 state 1 sheet SPIR anim walk
atlas 1000 state 2 sheet SPIR anim Attack
atlas 1000 state 3 sheet SPIR anim idle
atlas 1000 state 4 sheet SPIR anim death

The first line is the body. The atlas lines say which animation of the sheet SPIR plays in each animation state. You never type these lines: the 👤 body tab writes them.

The vocabulary also has a prop field, for two monsters that share one body on purpose. The workshop does not show it: each monster you make wears the record with its own name, which is almost always what you want.

The body

Open 👤 body. Its fields, top to bottom:

FieldWhat it means
radiusHow wide the body is, in world units, from its centre. Walls, players and other monsters stop at this circle.
heightHow tall the body is when it collides. A shot over its head misses. A melee attack measures against it too.
sprite heightHow tall the sheet is drawn. This is the sheet's own worldHeight, so changing it here grows every monster and prop drawn with that sheet. The preview's ruler shows it.
scaleMultiplies how big this body is drawn. It does not change what it collides as.
speedWorld units per second when it walks. It becomes the body's move_speed, which every client knows, so a slow monster is slow everywhere.
solidWhether things bump into it. A brain state can switch this off; the death state does, so you can walk over a corpse.
sprite sheetOne chip per sheet in the module, then + import sheet….
state 0 · idle … state 4 · deathWhich animation of the sheet each of the five animation states plays.

The studio puts it in one line under the fields: "height is what the body collides as. sprite height is how tall the sheet is drawn, for everything that uses it; scale multiplies that for this body alone."

Keep the drawing and the body close. A monster drawn twice the size of its body gets shot "through" its shoulders, and one drawn too small gets hit by bullets that seem to miss. When you pick a new sheet, the studio copies the sheet's height into height for you. After that they are separate numbers.

Animation states

The server never sends frames. It sends a small number, the animation state, and the client plays the frames for that number at its own pace. A body has five:

NumberMeaningGuessed from an animation whose name contains
0idleidle or stand
1walkwalk, run or move
2attackattack, fire or melee
3painpain or hurt, and otherwise the idle one
4deathdeath or die

When you pick a sheet, the studio fills the five lists by these guesses. Upper or lower case does not matter. If nothing matches, it uses the sheet's first animation. Check the guesses: a sheet with an animation called dead_pose and another called die gets die for death, which may not be what you meant.

Each brain state names one of these numbers in its anim field. The new monster's look uses 0, chase 1, attack 2, pain 3 and death 4. Several states may share one number: a wander state can use 1, the walk, just like chase.

The preview

The preview pane draws the monster with the client's own code, so what plays here is what the game draws:

  • The chips 0 idle · idle … 4 death · death play each animation state, and say which animation it uses.
  • animation plays any animation of the sheet by name, whether a state uses it or not. the state's goes back to the chips.
  • pause or play, and turn, which turns it an eighth. Dragging across the picture, or the facing (turns) slider, turns it too, so you can check every view.
  • The ruler on the left is in world units. Use it to judge the sprite height against your doors and corridors.

Put it on a map

Open a map and choose ✦ Place (key 3). The Place palette has a chip for every monster in the module. Tap your monster's chip, then tap the floor. Tap the chip again to stop placing.

A placed monster has a small form, titled Monster and its name:

  • name: which monster this is. Rename it to another monster to swap it.
  • kind: monster or prop.
  • dormant: it starts asleep.
  • height: where its feet start.
  • facing (°): which way it should face, drawn as an arrow at its feet.

Monsters do not take their facing yet. The studio writes the facing into the map, but the engine's mobs.spawn ignores it, so every placed monster starts facing 0 (east). The change is proposed in docs/MOB_FACING.md. When it lands, maps you already made take their monsters' facings without a new export. Until then, a monster turns to you as soon as it has a target, so this mostly matters for ambushes.

What dormant means

A dormant monster makes no decisions and does not move. It costs almost nothing. It wakes only when another monster wakes it:

  • a monster that runs alert_nearby wakes every monster within its radius, and hands them its target;
  • a monster that runs wake with a radius wakes the dormant ones near it.

Shooting a dormant monster does not wake it. The engine takes its health, and can kill it, but it never starts thinking. A room of dormant monsters with nothing to wake them is a room of statues. Use dormant for an ambush that another monster sets off, as The brain shows. For "asleep until it sees you", leave dormant off: the default look state already waits.

Play against it

Press ▶ Play. The studio writes the module and the map, and the server loads them. If a monster's brain has a problem the server would refuse, the workshop has already said so in its top bar, and the Home page lists it too. The server checks the same rules when it loads the map and prints what it refuses, so fix the red count before you play.

While you play, watch for these:

  • It does not move. Is it dormant? Can it see you? The default look needs you inside its sight and in a clear line.
  • It slides. Its feet and its speed disagree. Change the walk animation's stride or fps, or the speed.
  • It sticks in doorways. Its radius is too wide for your doors.
  • Its picture floats or sinks. The sheet's feet are not on its bottom edge. Fix it in the sprite editor, not with height.

Exercise: a grunt of your own

  1. Open ☠ Monsters and press + new monster. Type grunt and confirm. A grunt chip appears and is picked, and the graph shows its five states.

  2. Open 👤 body. Under sprite sheet, tap the sheet you want. Then read the five lists state 0 · idle to state 4 · death, and fix any guess that is wrong.

  3. In the preview, tap each chip from 0 idle to 4 death and watch it. Drag across the picture to turn it and check the side and back views.

  4. Set radius so the body fits the picture's shoulders: watch the ruler. Set speed to 3 for a slow, heavy grunt.

  5. Check the top bar says "loads clean".

  6. Press ‹ back, open a map, choose ✦ Place and put one grunt in a room you can see from the start spawn. Leave dormant off.

  7. Press ▶ Play. Walk into its view and let it come to you. Step back when it stops to claw, and see that a claw only lands when you stand within 1.5.

Keep this grunt. Every lesson in this track changes it a little, until it has a brain of its own, a swing, a fireball, a flinch and a drop.

You raise the sprite height of a sheet from 1 to 2. What changes?

  • Only this monster is drawn bigger
  • Every monster and prop drawn with that sheet is drawn bigger; their collision does not change
  • The monster is drawn and collides twice as tall

Sprite height is the sheet's own worldHeight, shared by everything that uses the sheet. Collision is height and radius. To draw one body bigger alone, use scale.

You place three monsters in a room, all dormant, and fire at them from the door. What happens?

  • They wake up when the first one is hit
  • They wake up when they see you
  • They lose health and can die, but they never move: only another monster's alert_nearby or wake wakes them

A dormant monster makes no decisions at all, so neither seeing you nor being hurt reaches its brain. Something awake has to wake it.

Why can a client draw a monster but never know its pain chance?

  • The body is in the catalogue, which clients read; the brain is in maps/module.lua, which only the server runs
  • The pain chance is encrypted in the map
  • Clients ask the server for it on every hit

The two halves are split on purpose. What a client needs (size, speed, pictures) is in assets/props.txt. What decides behaviour stays on the server.
