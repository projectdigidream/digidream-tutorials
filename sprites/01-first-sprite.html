Your first sprite

ThreeSprite draws characters that live in a 3D world but are pictures: one picture per view, the angle the camera sees them from. You draw each view by hand, a 3D rig moves them all at once, and the studio bakes the result into the sheet a monster or a prop is drawn with.

You'll learnwhat views, mirroring and the canvas are; how the New sprite form asks how a sprite is seen; how to make a sprite from the studio and come back with it baked; where everything sits in ThreeSprite's window Time15 minutes Needsthe studio, which comes with ThreeSprite (see Get the tools), and a module open

Views: the one idea to get right

A sprite has a number of views, from 1 to 64. Each view is the picture a camera sees when it stands at one angle around the character. With 8 views there is one every 45°. A sprite with one view is one picture, the same from every side: right for a coin or a bush, and where ThreeSprite starts you.

  • View 1 faces the viewer. It is the character seen from the front.
  • The views go anticlockwise seen from above. View k is what a camera sees from (k−1)/N of a turn around the character.
  • So with 8 views, view 3 is the camera at the character's left, and the character appears facing screen-left. View 5 is its back, and view 7 its right side.

This is the same convention Doom uses for its rotations, so Doom-style exports need no remapping.

The game picks a view from where the camera stands relative to where the character heads. The demo below does the same sum as ThreeSprite. Drag the camera round the circle, or turn the character with Facing.

Each view owns a wedge of the circle centred on its own angle. With 8 views, view 1 covers 22.5° either side of straight ahead, and the view swaps halfway between two angles.

Mirror symmetry

Most characters look the same from the left as from the right, flipped. With mirror symmetry on, you draw only ⌊N/2⌋+1 of the N views, and the rest are made by flipping:

ViewsYou drawFlipped copies
11—
41, 2, 34 (from 2)
81 – 56 (from 4), 7 (from 3), 8 (from 2)
161 – 910 – 16 (from 8 down to 2)

View 1 (front) and, with an even count, the back view are their own mirrors. Turn Mirror symmetry on in the demo above and watch the dashed views: those are the ones you never draw. In the New sprite form, All the way round is exactly the 8-view row of this table: 8 views, 5 drawn and 3 mirrored.

Mirroring flips the whole finished frame, so anything lopsided flips too. A sword in the right hand becomes a sword in the left hand in every flipped view. The little knight in the demo shows it. If that matters, turn mirroring off and draw every view.

One square canvas, feet on the bottom row

Every frame of every view is drawn on one square canvas. You pick its size under Size in pixels when you make the sprite: 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128 or 256, or a custom size from 8 to 256. The size is fixed after that. The character's feet go on the bottom row, and an orange ground line under the canvas marks it. The game hangs the sprite from its feet, so a character drawn floating will float in the game too.

Make a sprite from the studio

Open Sprites from the rail or from Home. The page shows every sheet in the module as a tile:

  • A tile with a small icon in front of its name is a sheet with no ThreeSprite project behind it: it was imported as a finished atlas. Its pictures can't be edited, but its animations can, on its card (see An imported sheet's animations).
  • Click a tile to see its card: its animations, which props draw it, and whether it was baked from a project. A baked sheet's card also says which of its animations loop (see Which animations loop).
  • A sheet with a project has edit in ThreeSprite. When more than one editor can open it, open with… lets you choose.
  • When a module has more than 12 sheets, a box over the tiles reads find among N sprites…. Type part of a name to narrow the tiles down.

  1. Press + new sprite. The studio asks for the new sheet's name (A-Z, 0-9, _). Type BLOB and confirm. Lower-case letters are turned into capitals, and anything else is dropped.

  2. ThreeSprite opens on an empty project and shows the New sprite form. Name and Sprite id already read BLOB. Leave them as they are.

  3. Under Size in pixels, pick 32. Under How is it seen?, pick All the way round: 8 views, 5 drawn and 3 mirrored.

  4. Press Create. The project starts with one bone called body, one part called body covering the whole canvas, and one animation called idle (4 frames at 8 fps, looping), so you can draw straight away.

  5. With the Pencil, draw a blob standing on the bottom row in view 1. Then press . to go to view 2 and draw it again, a little turned.

  6. Press studio, at the left of the bar above ThreeSprite. The studio saves the project into the module and bakes it. BLOB now has a tile on the Sprites page, with no icon in front of its name.

What the studio keeps, and what the game reads

  • The source is the ThreeSprite project, kept at sources/sprites/NAME.tsprite.
  • The bake makes what the game draws: assets/sprites/NAME.json (the atlas manifest) and NAME.png (the packed sheet). It runs when you press studio, and again before Play and Export for any source that changed.
  • Props and monsters pick a sheet by name. Re-bake a sheet under the same name and everything drawn with it changes.

You can also bring in work made elsewhere with + import a sprite (.tsprite, an atlas, or one PNG picture). A .tsprite is kept as the source and baked. An atlas (its .json, then its .png) is taken as a finished sheet, and its tile gets the small icon in front of its name. A single .png becomes a sheet of one picture, the same from every side; make it in ThreeSprite when it should turn and move.

The new sheet takes the sprite's own name. A ThreeSprite project nobody named is still called SPRT, ThreeSprite's default, so the studio names it after its file instead: blob.tsprite becomes BLOB. If the module already has a sheet by that name, the studio never takes it over unasked. It says There is a sheet called NAME already and offers a free name, such as NAME_2. Keep the offer to import it beside the old one. Type the old name instead to replace that sheet, and the studio asks once more, because everything drawn with it changes.

Duplicate and export

A sheet's card has two more kinds of button:

  • duplicate asks for the copy's name, and offers the first free one, such as HERO_2. The copy takes everything the sheet is made of: the baked sheet (its manifest renamed), the ThreeSprite project and its bake record. The copy is its own sheet: change it, and the original stays as it was. Use it to make a variant, such as a blue slime from the green one.
  • export .tsprite saves the ThreeSprite project out of the studio. export sheet saves the baked sheet as a .zip of its .json and .png, which + import a sprite can bring back in. In the desktop app these use the system's share or save. In a browser they download.

A sheet imported as a finished atlas has no project, so it offers export sheet only.

An imported sheet's animations

The card of a sheet imported as a finished atlas has an animations section. Its chips name each animation with its frame count, such as walk · 8. Pick one: it plays beside its name, fps and loops, and its frames are laid out in a strip under them.

  • Under each frame, the arrows move it earlier or later, the copy button repeats it, and the cross takes it out.
  • + a frame… opens every frame the sheet has, from all its animations. Tap one to add it at the end.
  • + new animation asks for a name and makes a new animation from the one shown, to change from there.
  • delete it deletes the animation shown. It asks first, and names any prop or weapon that uses it.
  • Renaming an animation in name renames it in the props and first-person weapon views that name it too.

Only the sheet's .json changes. The pictures stay exactly as they were imported, and each change is one undo step. A sheet baked from a ThreeSprite project has no such section: its animations are the project's, so edit them in ThreeSprite.

Inside the studio, ThreeSprite is slightly different

  • Its project menu (☰) has no New project…, Open… or Save as…: the studio decides which file you are editing.
  • ⌘S saves only a draft, which survives a reload. ThreeSprite also keeps a draft by itself every few seconds while you edit.
  • studio, top left, is what saves into the module and bakes. The ✕ at the top right discards your changes and goes back.
  • Its Export tab opens with The game needs nothing from here: the studio builds this sprite into it by itself. Export only to use the sprite somewhere else. See The Export tab.
  • ThreeSprite wears the studio's skin. Its own colours are mapped onto the skin's, so it looks like the rest of the studio and changes when you change the skin. Run on its own, it has its own light and dark look.

Leaving with ✕ throws the session's changes away. If you are not sure, use studio: an unwanted change can be undone on the studio's page afterwards.

In Cinderfall

Cinderfall is a second example, a first-person shooter, and it comes with the studio: on Home, under examples, press Cinderfall. The Cinderfall tour walks round it.

Cinderfall has 57 sprites, and makes one choice Gem Hollow does not: everything has 8 views, pickups and props included. In a first-person game you walk right round a crate or a medkit, so a one-view picture would turn with you like a cardboard cut-out. None of the 57 is mirrored, and not one pixel in them is drawn by hand: every part is a solids part, so each view is rendered from the same shapes.

The studio cuts the front view of each frame from the baked sheet. See the Weapons track.

Running ThreeSprite on its own

ThreeSprite is also a standalone app, for drawing sprites outside the studio. Download it from the ThreeSprite releases and install it the same way as the studio: see Get the tools. It is the same editor, with these differences:

  • On its own, ThreeSprite has its own New project…, Open… and Save in the ☰ menu, and keeps projects in a library of its own. It saves .tsprite files, which a studio module can import.
  • It saves as you go. A project in its library is saved a second after you stop, and at least every ten seconds while you keep drawing, so a new sprite is not lost if the app closes. The ☰ menu says Stored in the app library, saved automatically. On a desktop, a project you saved to a file of its own changes on disk when you Save, unless Save to the file automatically is ticked in the same menu.
  • On its own, the Export tab writes the sprite sheets. Inside the studio you never need Export: studio saves into the module, and the studio bakes.

On a phone held sideways, ThreeSprite gives the canvas the whole height: the mode tabs move up into the top bar, the views run down the canvas's left edge, and the tools, palette and Parts (or Solids) sit in a rail on the right.

Finding your way around

The top bar

From left to right:

  • ☰ — the project menu: Save, Import PNG as part…, and (outside the studio) new, open and recent projects.
  • The project's name, with a • while it has changes still to be saved, then its canvas size, view count and mirrored, for example 32px · 8 views · mirrored, or 96px · 8 views for HERO, which isn't mirrored. Click it to open Project settings.
  • Undo and Redo (⌘Z, ⇧⌘Z or ⌘Y).
  • The mode tabs, in the middle.
  • ? — every shortcut and gesture — and the settings gear, which opens Project settings too.

The canvas's corner

In the Draw, Rig and Animate tabs, the top-right corner of the drawing canvas holds a small swatch, Canvas background. Click it for five backgrounds to draw over: Dark checks (the one you start with), Light checks, Grey, White and Black. Dark art disappears into the dark checks, so switch to a light one while you draw a black outline or a shadow.

The choice is kept on your device, not in the sprite: it changes nothing in the game, and every sprite you open shows the same background. In Animate the corner also has a bones button; see Animating.

A sprite has 8 views. The camera stands exactly at the character's left side. Which view does it see?

  • View 7
  • View 3
  • View 5

Views go anticlockwise seen from above, starting with view 1 in front. A quarter turn round is (3−1)/8 = ¼, and that puts the camera at the character's left, with the character facing screen-left.

You make a 16-view sprite with mirror symmetry on. How many views do you draw?

  • 8
  • 16
  • 9

Mirroring draws ⌊16/2⌋+1 = 9 views: the front, the back and the seven on one side. Views 10 to 16 are flipped copies of views 8 down to 2.

In Gem Hollow, why does COIN have 1 view and HERO 8?

  • One-view sprites bake faster
  • A coin looks the same from every side; a character must turn to face where it heads
  • Kenney's art only comes in one view

A pickup never faces anywhere, so one picture serves every camera angle. A character turns, and the game picks the view from where the camera stands relative to its heading.

You made a sprite with One picture, and now it has to turn to face where it walks. What do you do?

  • Start again with a new sprite: the choice is fixed
  • Open Project settings and give it more views
  • Add a variant for each side

The form says it: You can change this later, in Project settings. Only the canvas size is fixed once the sprite is made.

You edit a sprite in ThreeSprite inside the studio and press ⌘S. What happens?

  • A draft is kept; the module changes only when you press studio
  • The module is saved and the sheet is baked
  • A Save as dialog asks where to put the .tsprite

Inside the studio, ⌘S only keeps a draft. The studio button, top left, saves the project into sources/sprites/ and bakes assets/sprites/NAME.json and NAME.png.
