Animating

You animate the rig, not the pictures. Keys on bones turn, move and scale them; keys on parts show, hide and swap them. Because the bones are 3D, one set of keys animates every view at once.

Animations

A sprite holds any number of animations, each with its own frame count, speed and loop. A new sprite has one called idle: 4 frames at 8 fps, looping. The left end of the timeline's transport bar manages them:

  • A drop-down of the sprite's animations.
  • + Add animation asks for a name (it suggests anim 2, anim 3 …).
  • Pencil Rename animation and trash Delete animation. Deleting removes its keys, and is undoable.

Then come the controls for the one you chose:

The timeline

The timeline has one row per bone (as a tree, the same as the Bones panel) and then one row per part. Each column is a frame, and the header numbers them from 1.

  • Click or drag along the header to scrub. It pauses playback.
  • Click a cell to go to that frame.
  • A ◆ in a cell is a key.

Setting keys

There are two ways to set a key on the current frame:

  1. On the canvas: drag a bone, as in the Rig tab. Dragging the tip sets a rotation key, and dragging the root sets a move key. The drag works in the plane of the view you are looking at.
  2. In the Inspector, now titled Keys at frame: type values for the selected bone. It says Frame f of n at the top.

For a bone the Inspector shows three groups, each with a ◆ badge that is lit when there is a key at this frame. Click the badge to set or remove that key.

  • Rot yaw, Rot pitch, Rot roll (degrees)
  • Move right, Move up, Move forward (pixels)
  • Scale right, Scale up, Scale forward

Under them:

  • Ease chooses how this frame's keys blend to the next ones. It is greyed out when the bone has no key at this frame.
  • Key all keys rotation, move and scale at this frame, with their current values.
  • Delete keys removes all three at this frame.

Ease: linear, easeInOut, step

The key at the start of a stretch decides how it gets to the next key:

EaseBetween this key and the nextGood for
linearmoves at an even speedwalk cycles, steady swings
easeInOutstarts slowly, speeds up, slows into the next keybreathing, a weight being lifted
stepholds this key's value, then jumpssnappy, Doom-like poses; anything that must not blend

Sprites often have only 4 to 8 frames, and blending between so few can look mushy. Try step on every key: each frame is then exactly the pose you set.

Parts in the timeline

Select a part (in its timeline row or in Parts) and the Inspector shows two more keys for it. Both are always step, since a part can't be half visible or half one variant.

  • Visible: a tick box. Untick it to hide the part from this frame on. Use it for a muzzle flash, or for a part that only shows in one pose.
  • Variant: which of the part's variants is shown. Use it to open a hand, or to open a mouth to shout.

The onion skin

With the onion skin on, the previous frame is drawn faded under the current one. On frame 1 the "previous" frame is the last one, so the loop point can be checked as well. Try it below: a four-frame bounce, with frame 1 squashed on the ground, then a stretch going up, round at the top, and a stretch coming down.

Copying keys

  1. Click a key, and Shift-click or ⌘-click more. On a touch screen, turn on the multi-select button first and then tap each key.
  2. Press Copy, or ⌘C.
  3. Go to another frame and press Paste, or ⌘V. The keys land at the current frame, with the same spacing between them and the same ease.

Keys don't wrap round the loop. Before the first key a bone holds the first key's value, and after the last key it holds the last one. When a looping animation reaches its last frame it jumps back to frame 1. So key the last frame as the step before frame 1's pose, not as frame 1's pose itself, or the loop shows the same pose twice and stutters.

Exercise: a bob

  1. Open Animate. Choose idle in the drop-down, set frames to 4 and fps to 6, and make sure loop is ticked.

  2. On frame 1, select the body bone in the timeline and press Key all.

  3. Press ] twice to reach frame 3. Type -2 in Move up. A key appears on frame 3.

  4. Press ] to reach frame 4 and type -1 in Move up. That is the step on the way back to frame 1.

  5. Press Space to play. The body bobs in every view: press . while it plays to see.

  6. Click the key on frame 1 and set its Ease to step. Play again: frame 2 now holds frame 1's pose.

  7. Click the key on frame 3, Shift-click the one on frame 4, and press ⌘C. Set frames to 6, go to frame 5 and press ⌘V. Then press ⌘Z twice to put things back.

  8. Turn on the onion skin, pause, and step through with [ and ].

What the studio does with animations

Each animation becomes one animation in the baked sheet, with its frame count, fps and loop. A monster's body card picks one animation for each of its five states. When a sheet is chosen, the studio guesses from the names:

StateAn animation whose name contains
idleidle or stand
walkwalk, run or move
attackattack, fire or melee
painpain or hurt (else the idle one)
deathdeath or die

If nothing matches, the first animation is used. You can change any of them on the card afterwards.

Name your animations idle, walk, attack, pain and death, and a new monster is wired up the moment you give it the sheet.

A bone has a key on frame 1 set to step, and another on frame 5. What does it do on frames 2 to 4?

  • Moves evenly towards frame 5's pose
  • Eases out of frame 1 and into frame 5
  • Holds frame 1's pose, then jumps on frame 5

The key at the start of a stretch decides how it gets to the next key, and step holds.

You want a muzzle flash only on frame 2 of the attack. What do you key?

  • The flash part's Visible: on at frame 2, off at frames 1 and 3
  • The flash bone's scale to 0 on the other frames
  • A second animation with the flash in it

Visibility keys show and hide a part, and are always step. Keying it off at frame 1 matters, because before the first key a part shows its first key's value.

You copy keys on frames 1 and 3, then paste with the playhead on frame 5. Where do they land?

  • On frames 1 and 3 again
  • On frames 5 and 7
  • Both on frame 5

Paste puts the first copied frame at the playhead and keeps the spacing between them.
