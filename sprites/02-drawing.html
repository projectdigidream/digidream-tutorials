Drawing

The Draw tab is a pixel editor that paints one part's picture in one view at a time. This page covers the eight tools, dithering, mirror drawing and colours, and how to step round the views without leaving the keyboard.

You'll learnevery drawing tool and its key, brush sizes, the dither density and offset, the three toggles under the tools, colours, and the view strip Time20 minutes Needsa sprite open in ThreeSprite (see Your first sprite)

What you are drawing on

A sprite is made of parts, such as a body, a head or an arm. Each part has its own picture in every view. The Draw tab paints the part selected in the Parts panel, in the view selected in the view strip. You see the whole sprite composed, and by default the other parts are dimmed so you can tell what you are painting.

When the selected part has no picture yet in the current view, a banner says "This part has no image for view k. Drawing creates one, or:" with two buttons. Blank starts an empty picture. Copy view N starts from the nearest view that already has one, which is usually the quickest way to start a new angle.

Brush sizes

Under the tools are four dots, sizes 1 to 4. The brush is a square of that many pixels a side. The size applies to the Pencil, the Eraser and Dither.

Dithering

Dither paints through an ordered 4×4 Bayer pattern. When the Dither tool is chosen, two controls appear under the brush sizes:

  • Density: a slider, shown as a percentage, from nothing to solid in 16 steps. Half, 50%, is a checkerboard. Lower values give a thinning stipple, and higher ones a thickening one.
  • Offset: a button reading →0. Each press slides the pattern one pixel sideways (→0, →1, →2, →3, then back to →0).

The pattern is fixed to the picture, not to your stroke. Going back and forth over the same area never doubles up, and two strokes at the same density land on the same pixels. That is what the offset is for: change it, and a second pass meshes in between the first instead of landing on top of it.

For a two-tone gradient, paint the light colour solid, then dither the dark colour at 25% over one band and 50% over the next. To mix three colours, dither the second at 50% with →0, then the third at 25% with →1 or →2 so the dots don't collide.

The three toggles

Below the brush sizes are three buttons that stay on or off:

ButtonWhat it does
Mirror drawing (symmetry)Everything you paint is painted again mirrored about the vertical centre of the part's picture. A dashed blue line shows that centre. Fill mirrors too. Off by default.
Dim other partsShows every part except the selected one faded, so you can see what you are painting. On by default.
Pixel gridDraws a line between pixels. It only appears when you are zoomed to 8× or closer.

Mirror drawing is not mirror symmetry. Mirror drawing makes the left and right halves of one picture match, which is what you want in the front and back views. Mirror symmetry, set when the sprite is made, flips whole views into the ones you don't draw. In a side view, turn mirror drawing off: a character seen from the side isn't symmetrical.

Colours

The palette panel, top right, holds:

  • The active palette, in a drop-down. A project can hold several palettes.
  • The primary colour (the big swatch; click it for the system colour picker) and the secondary colour (the small swatch). Clicking the small swatch, the swap button, or pressing X swaps them.
  • The swatches. Click one for the primary colour, and Shift-click one for the secondary.
  • A + that appears when the primary colour isn't in the palette, to add it.
  • Replace secondary → primary everywhere, which repaints every pixel of the secondary colour with the primary colour in every picture.

The palette button opens the full palette editor. See Palettes.

Moving round the views

The view strip over the canvas has one tab per view. Each tab shows the view's number and a short label: F for front, L and R for the sides, B for the back, and the angle in degrees for the others. Hover a tab for its full name, for example Front-left 45°.

  • A flipped view (with mirror symmetry) is dimmed and labelled ⇄ and its source, such as ⇄2. You can look at it but not paint on it. Its banner says it is mirrored, and its Edit view N button takes you to the view it comes from.
  • A tab is marked as empty when the selected part has no picture in that view. Hover it and the tooltip ends with no image for this part.
  • , and . step to the previous and next view, wrapping round.

Exercise: a turned head

  1. In view 1, select the body part and turn on Mirror drawing. With the Pencil (B) at size 1, draw a round head with two eyes. The second eye appears by itself.

  2. Press D for Dither, set density to 50%, and shade the bottom half of the head in a darker colour. Then press the offset button once and shade the same band in a third colour. The two patterns mesh.

  3. Press . twice to reach view 3, the character's left side. If the part has no picture there yet, the banner offers Blank and a Copy view N button. Press Blank.

  4. Turn Mirror drawing off, and draw the head in profile, looking to the left of the screen with one eye.

  5. Press . until you reach view 7. It is labelled ⇄3 and shows your profile flipped, looking right. Press Edit view 3 to jump back.

  6. Press M, drag a box round the eye, then press V and drag it one pixel down. Press ⌘Z to put it back.

You dithered a band at 50%, and want a second colour to fill the gaps instead of covering the same pixels. What do you change?

  • The brush size
  • The dither offset
  • Mirror drawing

The pattern is anchored to the picture, so the same density always hits the same pixels. The offset slides the pattern sideways, and the next pass lands in between.

In an 8-view sprite with mirror symmetry, you click view 6 and try to paint. What happens?

  • It paints, and view 4 updates to match
  • It paints, but only in view 6
  • Nothing: view 6 is a flipped copy of view 4, and the banner offers Edit view 4

With mirroring, views 6, 7 and 8 are flipped from 4, 3 and 2. They can be looked at, not painted. You change them by painting their source.

The pixel grid button is on, but you see no grid. Why?

  • You are zoomed out below 8×
  • Dim other parts is on
  • The grid only shows in the Rig tab

The grid only appears from 8× zoom, where a pixel is big enough for the lines to help.
