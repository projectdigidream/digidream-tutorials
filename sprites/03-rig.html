Rig, bones and parts

The art is 2D, but the skeleton is 3D. Bones live in the character's own space and are projected into every view, so one rig and one set of keys move all the views at once. This page builds a rig, hangs parts on it, and covers skinning, layer order and solids.

Bones and parts

  • A bone has a position, a rotation and a length in 3D. Its axes are the character's: right, up and forward, in canvas pixels, with the origin at the feet. A bone with no rotation points straight up. A child bone's position is measured in its parent's frame.
  • A part is a picture, one per view, that hangs off one bone. When the bone turns, the part turns with it in every view.

Because the bones are 3D, an arm that sticks out sideways in the front view points at the camera in the side view. With Foreshorten with the bone on, the part's picture is squashed along the bone when the bone points towards the camera.

A new sprite starts with one bone, body, standing at the feet, and one part, also body, hanging from it.

The Bones panel

The Bones panel lists the skeleton as a tree, with children indented under their parents and a count of the parts each bone carries. Its header has three buttons:

  • The pencil (Rig tab only): draw a new bone on the canvas. Press at the bone's root and drag to its tip. The new bone is a child of the selected one. Esc cancels.
  • + Add child bone: adds a child at the tip of the selected bone, 70% of its length.
  • Trash Delete bone: its children and parts move to its parent, and the parts stay where they are on screen. A rig needs at least one bone.

The Parts panel

The Parts panel lists the parts in painting order: the one at the top is drawn in front. A part made of solids has a ⬡ after its name.

  • + Add part on the selected bone. The new part has a canvas-sized picture in view 1, stands upright in the front view, has its pivot at the bottom centre, and foreshortens. Double-click a part to rename it.
  • Under the list: the name, Bring forward, Send backward and Delete part.

Variants

A variant is a second set of pictures for the same part: an open and a closed hand, a mouth shut and shouting. Every part starts with one called default. Under the part's name, the Variant drop-down chooses which one you are drawing. + asks for a name (for example open hand), and × deletes the selected variant (a part keeps at least one). An animation switches between variants with variant keys. See Animating.

Layer order, per view

Normally every view stacks its parts the same way. But an arm in front of the body from one side is behind it from the other. The chip in the Parts header sets the layer order scope:

  • All views: the shared order. Bringing a part forward changes every view.
  • Click it, and it reads View N: this view now keeps its own order, and bringing a part forward changes it here only. Click again to follow the shared order.

The view strip marks views with their own order (the tooltip ends with own layer order). A flipped view uses the order of the view it is flipped from.

Skinning: bending one picture between bones

A part normally follows one bone rigidly. With Skin (bend between bones), it follows several. Tick the other bones that should pull on it. The picture is then cut into a grid, and each point of the grid follows the bones near it, so one arm picture bends at the elbow, stretches when a bone moves away, and twists between bones. At rest it still draws pixel for pixel as you painted it.

  • Influence radius (px): a point farther than this from a bone ignores it. Larger gives softer bends.
  • Grid cell (px, 1–64): the grid's size. Smaller is smoother.

In Gem Hollow: how HERO is rigged

HERO has eleven bones. hips is the root, 19 px above the feet, and everything else hangs from it:

hips          root, Pos up 19, length 4
├─ torso      length 15
│  ├─ head    Pos up 15, length 20
│  ├─ armR    Pos right 9.5, Pos up 13, length 8
│  │  └─ foreR   Pos up −8, length 7
│  └─ armL    Pos right −9.5, Pos up 13, length 8
│     └─ foreL   Pos up −8, length 7
├─ thighR     Pos right 4, length 9
│  └─ shinR   Pos up −9, length 9
└─ thighL     Pos right −4, length 9
   └─ shinL   Pos up −9, length 9
  • The limbs come in mirrored pairs: armR at +9.5 right, armL at −9.5. A forearm starts 8 px below its upper arm's root: that is the elbow.
  • There is one part per bone except hips: ten parts, each named after its bone, each a solids part of about three shapes. The head is two spheres (face and hair) and small cubes for the eyes.
  • The parts are stacked with the right leg at the back and the left arm in front, the same in every view (the Parts header reads All views).

In Cinderfall: rigs for a shooter

Cinderfall, the first-person shooter example, comes with the studio: on Home, under ◆ examples, press 📂 Cinderfall. The Cinderfall tour walks round it.

Cinderfall's people and most of its monsters share one rig of 13 bones. It is HERO's rig with two more bones, handR and handL, 3 px long, at the end of each forearm. Here is GUNNER:

hips          root, Pos up 33, length 4

│  ├─ head    Pos up 22, length 12
│  ├─ armR    Pos right 8.5, Pos up 20, length 11
│  │  └─ foreR   Pos up −11, length 10
│  │     └─ handR   Pos up −10, length 3   ← the rifle hangs here
│  └─ armL    (the same, Pos right −8.5)
├─ thighR     Pos right 3.6, length 16
│  └─ shinR   Pos up −16, length 16
└─ thighL     Pos right −3.6, length 16
   └─ shinL   …
  • A hand bone holds what the hand carries. The rifle part (4 shapes) hangs on handR, so it follows the arm through aim, fire and death. HUSK's pick, WARDEN's gun, BRUTE's and SMELTER's cannon hang the same way; MAGS's lantern and CARROW's staff hang on handL.
  • Clothes hang on the hips. MAGS's skirt, WARDEN's duster and CARROW's robe are parts on hips, not bones of their own.
  • Big is the same rig, scaled. BRUTE (128 px) and SMELTER, the boss (176 px), have the same 13 bones as GUNNER at larger sizes: SMELTER's hips stand 44.55 up and its arms start 17.85 out.

Not everything is a person. HOUND's 13 bones are a dog's: body at Pos up 18 is the root, with neck → head → jaw at its front (Pos forward 11.5), tail at its back (Pos forward −11.5), and four two-bone legs, upFR/loFR … upBL/loBL, at Pos forward ±8.05 and Pos right ±4. SPITTER is a sac with a mouth and four two-bone tentacles. HUMANOID and QUADRUPED are kept in the module as the starting rigs these were built from.

A first-person weapon has a rig too. VM_REVOLVER hangs everything from a root called rig, at Pos right −22 and Pos up 18 and turned (Rot pitch −40, Rot yaw −20), so the gun sits low on the right and points into the screen. Its children are body, then hammer, cyl (the cylinder) and flash on the body, and hand. flash sits at Pos forward −17, out at the muzzle: the muzzle flash is a part of its own, so an animation can show it on one frame only.

  1. Open Cinderfall's HOUND in ThreeSprite and open Rig. Press . twice for view 3, the side: the legs are now spread front to back, where in view 1 they hid behind each other.

  2. Click jaw in the Bones panel and drag its tip down. The jaw opens in every view. Press ✕ to throw the change away.

  3. Open VM_REVOLVER, open Rig and click rig. Set Rot yaw to 0: the gun swings to point straight away from you. Leave with ✕.

Exercise: an arm that bends

  1. Open the Rig tab and select body in the Bones panel. Press + twice to add a chain of two child bones. Name them upper and lower in the Inspector.

  2. Drag the tip of upper so it points out to the side, at shoulder height. Hold Shift and drag its tip again to shorten it.

  3. Select upper, go back to Draw, and press + in Parts. Name the new part arm and draw a straight arm in view 1, as long as the two bones together.

  4. Back in Rig, with arm selected, drag the pink cross until the arm's top sits on the shoulder.

  5. In the Inspector's Skin list, tick lower. Now drag the tip of lower: the arm bends at the elbow.

  6. Press . to view 2, then 3. The bones follow in every view. Draw the arm in view 3, then use the chip in the Parts header to give view 3 its own order and send the arm backward there.

Solids: modelling with shapes

Instead of drawing a part in every view, you can build it from 3D shapes. A solids part is rendered into every view from the same description, with shared depth, stepped pixel-art shading and an outline. It animates with the rig in 3D. The Solids panel is in the Draw tab (and beside the 3D view).

  1. Select a bone, then in the Solids panel press Add a solids part on “…”. A new part appears in Parts with ⬡.

  2. Pick a colour, then press + Cube, + Sphere, + Cylinder, + Cone or + Pyramid. The shape takes the primary colour, and the Move tool is chosen.

  3. Drag the shape on the canvas: it moves in the plane of that view. Switch view and drag again to place it in depth.

For the selected shape, the panel has a name, Move up, Move down, Duplicate and Delete; Visible; Kind; Use primary colour; position (Pos right, Pos up, Pos forward, in the bone's frame), size (Width, Height, Depth) and rotation (Yaw, Pitch, Roll). Under Shading: Tones (2 to 6), Outline, and Light, either fixed to the viewer or turns with the character.

Bake in the panel header keeps the rendered pixels and drops the shapes. The views become ordinary pictures you can paint over. It is undoable.

Block out a body with solids to get the proportions right in every view, then bake and paint the details on top.

You want an arm to swing towards the camera. Which view do you drag its tip in?

  • View 1, the front
  • Any view: the drag rotates it in 3D the same way
  • A side view, such as view 3 of 8

A tip drag rotates the bone in the plane of the current view. Towards the camera is the forward axis, which only lies in the screen's plane when you look from the side.

The shield must be in front of the body from the left and behind it from the right. How?

  • Give the right-side views their own layer order with the Parts header chip, and send the shield backward there
  • Make a second shield part for those views
  • Use a variant with an empty picture

Per-view layer order lets a view stack its parts its own way, with the same parts and pictures.

HERO's foreR has Pos up −8. Measured from what?

  • From the feet
  • From the hips
  • From the root of its parent, armR, in armR's frame

A child bone's position is in its parent's frame. So −8 puts the elbow 8 px below the shoulder, the length of the upper arm.

You delete a bone that carries two parts. What happens to them?

  • They are deleted with it
  • They move to its parent bone and stay where they are on screen
  • They jump to the root bone's position

Deleting a bone re-hangs its children and parts on its parent without moving them on screen. It is undoable.
