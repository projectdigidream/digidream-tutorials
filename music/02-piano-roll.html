Piano roll and recording

The Track tab is where notes are made. You draw them with the mouse or a finger, shape them with a lane of velocity, controllers or pitch bend, or play them in from a MIDI keyboard.

You'll learnthe three tools, moving and resizing notes, the controller lanes and ramps, recording with count-in and quantize, and the touch gestures Time15 minutes Needsthe DAW running (see A tour of the DAW); a MIDI keyboard for the recording part

The toolbar

  • track: which track the roll shows. It follows the track you select in Arrange.
  • The three tools: ✎ draw (D), ▭ select (S) and ⌫ erase (E).
  • lane: what the strip under the roll edits.
  • vel: the velocity new notes get, 1 to 127. It is 100 at first.

The three tools

ToolOn empty gridOn a note
✎ drawDrag to make a note. It starts on the snap grid and its length follows the drag.Drag to move it. Drag its right edge to resize it.
▭ selectDrag a box to select notes. Hold ⇧ to add to the selection.Same as draw: click selects, drag moves, right edge resizes.
⌫ erase—Click to delete it.

Whatever the tool, right-click a note to delete it. Clicking a note plays it, so you hear what you picked.

  • ⇧-click adds a note to the selection; ⇧A selects every note of the track.
  • ⌫ or Delete deletes the selection.
  • ⌘D duplicates the selection and puts the copy right after it, rounded to the snap grid.
  • Moving and resizing follow snap in the header. Set it to off for free placement.

Getting around

  • The wheel scrolls up and down through pitch; a sideways swipe or ⇧ + wheel scrolls through time.
  • ⌘ + wheel zooms (Ctrl on Windows and Linux).
  • The bar under the grid scrolls the song: drag it, or click it to jump.
  • The keyboard on the left: click a key to hear it on the track's channel; drag along the keys to scroll pitch.
  • Click the ruler at the top of the roll to move the playhead.

  1. In Arrange, select a track playing a piano or a lead, and double-click its lane. Leave snap at 1/4.

  2. With ✎ draw, click four notes into the first bar, one on each beat. A click makes a note one grid step long.

  3. Press S, drag a box around the four notes, then press ⌘D. The figure now fills two bars. Press ⇧A to select all of it and ⌘D again: four bars.

  4. Drag the right edge of the last note to make it two beats long. Right-click the second note to delete it.

  5. Press Space. While it plays, move a note up a step: you hear the change on the next pass without the music stopping.

The lane

The strip under the roll edits one thing at a time. Pick it in lane:

LaneWhat it changes
velocityHow hard each note is played, one bar per note.
CC1 modThe mod wheel. A patch decides what it does (often vibrato; see the mod matrix in Designing patches).
CC7 volumeThe channel's volume.
CC10 panLeft to right.
CC11 exprExpression: a second volume, for swells inside a phrase.
CC64 sustainThe sustain pedal.
CC91 reverb · CC93 chorus · CC94 delayHow much of the channel goes to each send effect. The effects themselves are set on the Master tab.
pitch bendBends the channel's pitch up or down.
  • Drag to paint values.
  • ⇧-drag draws a straight ramp between where you pressed and where you let go, at grid resolution. This is the tool for a slow filter sweep or a long fade.
  • Right-click, or the ⌫ erase tool, deletes points.

The delay effect is silent until something is sent to it. Paint the CC94 delay lane on a track to give just that track echoes.

  1. Set lane to CC11 expr. ⇧-drag from low at the start of bar 1 to high at the end of bar 4.

  2. Play it. The track swells over four bars. Then right-click along the lane to clear the ramp.

Recording

The Record box at the bottom of the tab takes notes, controllers and pitch bend from a MIDI keyboard.

SettingWhat it does
deviceWhich MIDI input to listen to, or all inputs.
modeoverdub adds the take to what is there. replace first clears the loop region of the track (or the whole track, if loop is off).
quantizeSnaps the take to 1/4, 1/8, 1/16 or 1/32 when you stop, or leaves it as played (off).
latency msShifts the take earlier by this much, if your setup makes everything land late. Negative values shift it later.

The keyboard always plays through the armed track's channel, even when you are not recording, so you hear the track's own sound as you play.

  1. Connect a MIDI keyboard. The header should read the device's state instead of "MIDI: probing…".

  2. In Arrange, press R on the track to arm it. (With nothing armed, recording uses the selected track.)

  3. Set count-in in the header to 1, mode to overdub and quantize to 1/16.

  4. Press ● Rec or R. The click plays one bar, then the song plays from the start and you play along.

  5. Press ● Rec, R or ⏹ to stop. The take is snapped to the grid and added as one step, so a single ⌘Z takes the whole take back.

Web MIDI needs a secure page (localhost counts) and does not exist in the Android app at all. There, draw the notes instead.

On a touch screen

A drag on the roll draws or moves, so the page cannot scroll with one finger. Instead:

  • Two fingers pan and pinch-zoom. Spread sideways to zoom time, spread up and down to zoom pitch.
  • Hold a note to delete it. There is no right-click on a touch screen.
  • Slide along the keys to scroll up and down; drag the bar under the grid to scroll through the song.
  • The ⌫ erase tool deletes on a tap.
  • A second finger landing in the middle of a drag cancels that drag, so a pinch never leaves a stray note behind.

You want a filter to open slowly over 32 bars on one track. What is the quickest way?

  • Draw 32 bars of points by hand in the velocity lane
  • Record yourself turning a knob
  • Pick the controller's lane and ⇧-drag from the start to the end

⇧-drag in a lane writes a straight ramp between the two ends at grid resolution.

Loop is on, set to bars 5–8. You record a take in replace mode. What is cleared?

  • The whole track
  • The armed track's notes, controllers and bends inside bars 5–8
  • Nothing: replace only affects the next take

Replace clears the loop region of the track before the take goes in. With loop off, it clears the whole track.

You select a one-bar figure and press ⌘D. Where does the copy go?

  • Right after the selection
  • On top of the original, to be dragged away
  • At the playhead

⌘D places the copy straight after the selection's span, rounded to the snap grid.
