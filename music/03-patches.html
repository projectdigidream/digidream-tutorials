Designing patches

A patch is one instrument: up to four layers, each an oscillator shaped by envelopes, a filter and modulation. The Synth tab edits the patch the selected track plays, and you hear every change in the song as you make it.

You'll learnhow the patch editor picks what it edits, the oscillator, envelope, filter, LFO and mod-matrix controls, how to audition, and how banks and kits are stored Time25 minutes Needsthe DAW running with a track that has a few notes (see Piano roll and recording)

Where the patch editor is

The same editor appears in two places:

  • the DAW's Synth tab, editing the project's own bank and kit;
  • the stand-alone tool at localhost:8930/tool/, which plays test songs and edits a bank or kit file directly.

On the Synth tab, the left column lists every track with its program. Click one to edit the instrument it plays. A green dot lights on a track while the engine is sounding it. The line above the list names the patch you are editing and which tracks play it. When no track plays it, the line turns amber: you can still audition it from the keyboard, but you will not hear the edit in the song.

Two tracks on the same program share one patch. An edit to the patch changes both.

The top rows

  • The target row. The first list switches between melodic program and drum note; the second picks which program (or the fallback patch) or which drum note. Picking a program here makes the selected track play it.
  • ↶ Undo and ↷ Redo undo patch edits. While the Synth tab is open, ⌘Z works on the patch, not on the project.
  • Store B keeps a copy of the patch as it is now. A⇄B swaps the working patch with that copy, so you can flip between two versions and compare.
  • Init resets the patch to a plain starting patch.
  • name. For a drum note, also oneShot (the sound plays to its end and ignores note-off) and choke (a group number: a new hit in the group cuts the others off, like a closed hi-hat cutting an open one; -1 is none).

Under them, Layer mix draws how the layers combine and their amp envelopes, and a row of tabs picks Layer 1 to Layer 4. ＋ adds a layer (up to four) and × removes the current one. Everything below the tabs belongs to the selected layer.

Oscillator

The first list is the source:

  • wavetable: a waveform, built by a generator, that can morph across frames.
  • noise: noise, for snares, wind and breath. Shape it with the filter.
  • metal: six detuned square waves at a classic drum machine's ratios. Bandpass it high for hi-hats and cymbals.

For a wavetable, classic gives quick starting points (sine, triangle, saw, square, pulse 25%, pulse 12%). The generator list holds the rest:

GeneratorMakes
custom (draw)One cycle you draw with the mouse.
additiveA harmonic series with a falloff (saw, square, triangle, flat), from one harmonic count to another across the frames.
pulse (PWM)A pulse whose width sweeps across the frames.
drawbar organNine drawbars, 16′ to 1′.
waveshaperA sine, triangle or saw bent through soft, hard, fold, crush or stairs.
chebyshevA transfer curve you shape by dragging points.
harmonicsThe levels of harmonics 1 to 32, drawn as bars.
hard syncA synced saw or pulse, the ratio sweeping across the frames.
FM 2-opTwo-operator FM, the index sweeping across the frames.

frames sets how many steps the wavetable has (1 to 32). With more than one, morph preview scrubs through them; a WtPos envelope or the mod matrix moves through them while a note plays.

Mix & pitch

  • gain and pan: the layer's level and place. Layers add up, so a patch with several layers usually keeps each one between 0.3 and 0.6.
  • detune ¢: cents added to the pitch. ±5–10 thickens; 1200 adds an octave.
  • delay ms: the layer speaks this much later. Three layers at 0, 12 and 25 ms make a hand clap.
  • vel curve and vel low–high: how velocity maps to level, and a velocity window (a soft layer for 1–90, a bright one for 91–127).
  • fixed pitch (note#): the layer always sounds at this note. −1 follows the played note. Drum layers nearly always set it.
  • unison (1–7 voices), its detune ¢ and spread: a stack of detuned copies, the supersaw sound.
  • ring × and amt: ring modulation against a sine at that ratio of the pitch (0 is off).
  • rel-vel: how much the key-release speed changes the release time. RR: a round-robin group; layers in the same group take turns, one per note.
  • sends rev, cho, dly: per-layer effect sends. Blank means "use the channel's CC". Useful on drums, where every drum shares one channel.

Envelopes

Each layer has four envelopes, on sub-tabs: Amp (loudness), Filter (cutoff), Pitch and WtPos (wavetable position). A dot after a name means that envelope is not in use yet. Drag the handles on the drawing, or type the times:

FieldStage
dlyDelay: wait this many ms before the attack starts.
AAttack: ms to rise to full.
HHold: ms to stay at full.
DDecay: ms to fall to the sustain level.
SSustain: the level held while the key is down, 0 to 1. It is a level, not a time.
RRelease: ms to fall to silence after the key is let go.
  • loop cycles attack and decay while the key is held, a pulsing pad without an LFO.
  • curves sets the shape of attack, decay and release: linear; exp (fast start, natural tail); expAttack (slow start, a swell); sCurve (soft at both ends).
  • The Filter, Pitch and WtPos envelopes have a depth: depth ±1 of the cutoff range, semis of pitch, or depth 0-1 of the frames.

A kick drum is mostly a Pitch envelope: attack 0, decay about 45 ms, sustain 0, and a depth of about 24 semitones. The note starts high and drops into a thump.

Filter

  • Modes: bypass, lowpass, bandpass, highpass, notch, comb (a tuned resonator; noise through it sounds plucked) and formant (vowels: pick a vowel and an end vowel the filter envelope morphs to).
  • cutoff (up to 12 kHz; use bypass for a fully open sound), resonance (0.2–0.4 is character, 0.6 and up squelches) and keytrack (the cutoff follows the note, so high notes are not duller).
  • slope 12 or 24 dB; drive saturates before the filter, post after it.
  • filter 2: a second filter with its own mode, cutoff and res, in serial or parallel. Tick static to keep its corner fixed while filter 1 sweeps.

The curve under the controls draws the filter's response.

LFOs and the mod matrix

Each layer has four LFOs. Tick LFO1 to LFO4 to turn one on, then pick its shape (sine, triangle, saw, square, sampleHold), rate Hz, a tempo sync (free, or 1/1 to 1/16 with triplets) and a delay ms before it fades in.

An LFO does nothing on its own. The Mod matrix connects it: + routing adds a row with a source, a destination and an amount, and × removes one.

SourcesDestinations
LFO1–LFO4, AmpEnv, FilterEnv, PitchEnv, WtPosEnv, Velocity, KeyTrack, ModWheel, ChannelAftertouch, PolyAftertouch Pitch, FilterCutoff, FilterRes, Amplitude, WtPosition, Pan, FilterDrive, PostDrive

An amount of 1 on Pitch is a whole octave, so vibrato is tiny: 0.003 to 0.008. On FilterCutoff, 0.1 to 0.5 gives a wobble.

  1. On the Synth tab, pick a lead or pad track. Press Store B so you can get back.

  2. Tick LFO1, shape sine, rate Hz 5, delay ms 300.

  3. Press + routing. Leave it at LFO1 → Pitch and set the amount to 0.005.

  4. Hold A. The note starts straight and the vibrato fades in. Press A⇄B to hear the patch without it, and again to come back.

Audition

The Audition box at the bottom plays the edited patch:

  • A S D F G H J K are the white keys; W E T Y U the black ones; Z and X move an octave down and up (octaves 2 to 6).
  • velocity, sustain, and mod and bend sliders to try the mod wheel and pitch bend.
  • In drum note mode the keyboard becomes pads, one per drum, and the letter keys hit them.

On the Synth tab, S, D and E play notes; they only switch piano-roll tools on the Track tab. R does not record on the Synth tab either.

JSON, banks and kits

  • Export patch saves this one patch as JSON.
  • Export bank (or Export kit in drum mode) saves the whole document. import loads one. In the DAW, the File menu has the same for the project's bank and kit.
  • JSON ▾ opens the patch as text. Edit it and press Apply JSON; the fields and the text stay in step both ways.

A bank holds the melodic patches by General MIDI program number, plus a fallback for any program it does not have, so an unknown program always makes some sound:

{
  "version": 1,
  "fallback": { …a patch… },
  "programs": {
    "0":  { …a patch… },
    "48": { …a patch… }
  }
}

A kit holds drum patches by MIDI note number. The General MIDI drums are notes 35 to 81 (the format accepts 27 to 87). Unlike a bank, a note the kit does not have is silent.

{
  "version": 1,
  "notes": {
    "36": { …kick… },
    "42": { …closed hi-hat… }
  }
}

The shipped files are midisynth/content/patches/gm_bank.json and midisynth/content/drums/gm_kit.json. A new project starts from them. When a track is baked, only the programs and drum notes the song actually plays are kept in the .mstk.

In Gem Hollow

Gem Hollow's five projects carry the shipped bank unchanged: the same 71 programs as gm_bank.json. Its chiptune sound comes from a handful of them. Here is how two are built, as you see them on the Synth tab of airship:

PatchLayer 1Layer 2Modulation
38 Synth Bass 1 (synth_bass) additive, 20 harmonics, square falloff, gain 0.58. Amp: A 1, D 300, S 0.55, R 110. lowpass at 500 Hz, resonance 0.4, slope 24, drive 2.2, keytrack 0.7. Filter envelope: D 260, S 0.15, depth 0.3: each note starts bright and closes. A plain sine (1 harmonic) at detune ¢ −1200, one octave down, gain 0.20, held at full: the sub under the bass. Velocity → FilterCutoff 0.2; ModWheel → FilterCutoff 0.22
81 Lead 2 (sawtooth) (saw_lead) additive, 30 harmonics, saw falloff, detune −5 ¢, pan −0.15, lowpass 3800 Hz, drive 1.8 the same saw at +6 ¢ and pan +0.15: two slightly detuned saws, one each side, are what make it wide LFO1 (sine, 5.4 Hz, delay 300 ms) → Pitch 0.004: the vibrato that fades in on long notes; ModWheel adds more

Every track in airship plays at velocity 80, so the Velocity routing never opens the bass's filter further. That is one reason the imported song sounds flat until you shape it.

  1. Open airship from Gem Hollow's ♪ Music page. On the Synth tab, click Instrument_01 in the list. The line above it says the patch is played by Instrument_01 and Instrument_02.

  2. Press Store B. On Layer 1, raise resonance to 0.6 and lower cutoff to 350 Hz. Play the song: the bass squelches.

  3. Press A⇄B to compare with the original, and again to come back. Keep whichever you like.

  4. Leave with ✕ to throw it away. With ‹ studio, the patch is saved in airship's own bank only; the other four tracks keep theirs.

The program list marks the missing ones with ·fallback, and selecting one opens a blank patch called new: nothing is saved for that program until you edit it.

  1. Import Cinderfall, open boss from ♪ Music, and go to the Synth tab. Click ovr: the program list at the top reads 29 Overdriven Guitar ·fallback.

  2. Pick 30 Distortion Guitar in the program list. Picking a program makes the selected track play it, so ovr now plays 30: press Space and hear the difference. Open JSON ▾ and copy the text.

  3. Pick 29 Overdriven Guitar ·fallback in the list: ovr is back on 29, and the editor shows the blank new patch. Open JSON ▾, paste over it, change "name" to "overdriven_guitar" and press Apply JSON.

  4. Play: both ovr tracks now crunch. Leave with ‹ studio to keep the patch, in boss's bank only, or ✕ to throw it away.

Fit: a patch from a recording

The Fit tab searches for a patch that sounds like a short recorded sample:

  1. Under Target, load a sample: a single hit, or a single held note. A phrase of several notes cannot be matched by one note.

  2. Under Architecture, pick a preset: kick, snare, hat or bass for drums, or piano, strings, pad, organ, brass, pluck, bell, lead for melodic sounds. Keep window ms short so the room's echo in the recording is left out.

  3. Under Likelihood, pick one of the weight presets (balanced, spectral only, tonal balance, percussive). Then press Fit.

  4. Compare with audition fit and audition target. If you like it, choose add as bank program or kit note and press Add to instruments. It lands in the project's bank or kit as one undoable step.

More restarts help more than more iters: the search stops improving after about 20 steps, but a fresh start can find a better place.

You edit program 33 on the Synth tab and the hint line is amber. Why do you hear nothing change in the song?

  • Edits only apply after a bake
  • No track plays program 33, so the song never uses that patch
  • The Synth tab edits a copy until you press Apply JSON

Edits apply live, but only to the patch you are editing. Click a track in the list on the left to edit the patch it plays.

In the envelope row, S is set to 0.7. What does that mean?

  • The sustain lasts 0.7 seconds
  • The sustain lasts 0.7 ms
  • While the key is held, the envelope stays at 70% of full

Delay, attack, hold, decay and release are times in ms. Sustain is a level from 0 to 1.

A song plays drum note 52 and your kit has no patch for 52. What do you hear?

  • Nothing, for that note
  • The kit's fallback patch
  • The nearest drum note the kit has

A bank falls back so a melody is never silent, but a kit does not: a drum the kit lacks is silent by design.
