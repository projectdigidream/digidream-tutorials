Mastering

The Master tab is the last stage before the speakers: a chain that evens out the whole mix and stops it from clipping, an EQ per channel, and the song's reverb, chorus and delay. All of it is baked into the .mstk, so the game plays the track exactly as you mastered it.

The chain

Mastering runs in the engine, in this order:

On, off and compare

  • enabled: mastering is off in a new project. Off, the output is exactly what an unmastered engine makes, bit for bit.
  • A/B bypass: hold it down to hear the mix without mastering; let go to hear it with. It changes nothing in the project.
  • reset: every control back to its default. It leaves enabled as it is.

With mastering on, the line beside the buttons shows the limiter's latency, for example "+128 samples latency (2.7 ms)". The song's timing does not change; the sound simply arrives that much later.

EQ

Three bands: a low shelf, a mid bell and a high shelf. On the curve, drag a handle: left and right move its frequency, up and down its gain. ⇧-drag the mid handle up or down to make the bell narrower or wider (its Q).

The sliders under the curve do the same: low shelf and low dB, mid Hz, mid dB and mid Q, high shelf and high dB. Gains go from −24 to +24 dB.

Cut before you boost. A small dip in the low mids (around 300 Hz) often clears up a muddy mix better than a boost anywhere else.

Width & gain

  • width: 0 is mono, 1 leaves the stereo image as it is, 2 doubles the sides.
  • gain dB: output gain before the limiter.

Compressor

The compressor turns the loud parts down, so the mix sits at a steadier level.

ControlWhat it does
thresholdThe level (−60 to 0 dB) above which it starts turning down.
ratioHow hard: 1 is no compression, 4 means 4 dB over the threshold comes out as 1 dB over.
attack ms · release msHow fast it reacts, and how fast it lets go.
makeup dBGain after compression, to win back the level it took.
saturationSoft saturation of the whole mix, between the compressor and the limiter. 1 is off; 1.5 to 2.5 adds glue and loudness.
knee dBHow gently it starts around the threshold. 0 is a hard corner.
gain reductionA meter: how much it is turning down right now.

Limiter

The limiter is the safety net: the output never goes above its ceiling dB. It looks ahead, so it starts turning down before a peak arrives instead of clipping it.

  • ceiling dB: −12 to 0. Around −1 leaves room for the player's own system.
  • lookahead: 1 to 8 blocks of 64 samples. More is smoother on sharp peaks, and adds latency.
  • release ms: how fast the level comes back.
  • limiter gain: a meter of what it is doing.

Output

  • peak L, peak R, RMS L, RMS R: meters for each side.
  • loudness: the average level over the last 3 seconds, in dBFS RMS, and a count of clipped samples if there were any.
  • dither (TPDF): adds a tiny noise when the output is turned into 16-bit samples, so very quiet tails fade smoothly instead of crackling. It applies to what you hear and to Render .wav; it is a setting of your session, not part of the baked track.

The meters read the signal before the final soft clip, and they keep reading when mastering is bypassed, so you can compare levels both ways.

  1. Play a loop of your song. Tick enabled.

  2. Set threshold to −18, ratio to 3, and watch gain reduction. Aim for a few dB of movement on the loud parts.

  3. Raise makeup dB until the loudness reading is back where it was. Set the limiter's ceiling dB to −1.

  4. Hold A/B bypass and let go a few times. If the mastered version only sounds better because it is louder, lower makeup dB and compare again.

Per-channel EQ (mixer strips)

Below the chain, a small EQ for each MIDI channel. Pick a channel, then set low dB (a shelf at 120 Hz), mid dB and mid Hz (a bell), and high dB (a shelf at 6 kHz).

A flat channel costs nothing, so leave the ones you do not need alone. Use it to give each part its own room: take some low end out of a pad so the bass is clear, or some highs out of a busy drum channel.

Send effects (song-level)

Three effects are shared by the whole song. Each channel decides how much to send to them with its CC91 reverb, CC93 chorus and CC94 delay lanes in the piano roll (and a patch's layers can override that, see Designing patches).

EffectControls
reverbroom (size), damp (how quickly the highs die), width, wet
chorusrate, depth, wet
delaytime, fbk (feedback: how many repeats), wet, and ping-pong, which bounces the echoes between left and right

The delay's wet starts at 0, and no channel sends to it until you paint its CC94 delay lane. Turning up the wet alone does nothing.

  1. Set the delay's wet about halfway and tick ping-pong.

  2. Open a lead track in the Track tab, set lane to CC94 delay and paint a value near the top at the start.

  3. Play. Only the lead echoes, bouncing left and right.

Baking it in

File › Bake .mstk (with mastering) makes the game's file. It carries the effects, the per-channel EQ and, when enabled is ticked, the mastering chain. The game loads all of it with the track, so there is nothing to set up on the game's side, and every player hears the same master. Render .wav uses the same chain.

To reuse a master on another song, use Export master.json here and Import master.json… there.

You master a track in the DAW. What does the game need to play it the same way?

  • A master.json shipped beside the .mstk
  • Nothing more: the mastering is baked into the .mstk
  • The same settings entered again in the studio

Bake .mstk (with mastering) puts the chain in the track, and the game applies it when it loads the track.

What does holding A/B bypass do to the project?

  • Nothing. It only lets you hear the mix without mastering while you hold it
  • It turns mastering off until you press it again
  • It swaps between two saved masters

It is a momentary compare. Let go and mastering is back; the enabled box never changes.

You turn the delay's wet all the way up and hear no echo. Why?

  • Ping-pong is off
  • Mastering is not enabled
  • No track sends to the delay: its CC94 lane is empty

A send effect only hears what channels send to it. Paint the CC94 delay lane on the tracks that should echo.
