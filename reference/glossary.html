Glossary

The words used across the five tools, grouped by tool.

Module
A whole game: maps, monsters, weapons, textures, music, sounds and screens. It is one folder, and one pack when exported.
Pack
A module exported as a .zip. The standalone engine loads it unchanged, and import pack brings it into a studio.
Map
One level, made of rooms. The metamap places maps on a grid so they form one world.
Room
A sector: a floor polygon with a floor height, a ceiling, light, and edges. It is drawn with the Room tool.
Effect area
A room whose kind is effect area: a volume laid over other rooms, for its effects (fog, rain, a fill) and its events only. Nothing bumps into it or stands on it, and it draws no floor, ceiling or walls. A body can be in several at once, and when_sector_entered and when_sector_left hear each one. See Effect areas and water.
Liquid
An effect area that is water (liquid (water) on its panel). Its top is the surface, and the game measures how deep a body stands in it (to the feet, the waist or over the eyes) for the module's movement states: wading, swimming.
Fill
A sector effect (+ fill) that tints a room's whole volume: whatever is seen through it takes its colour, as much as its alpha hides, and a camera inside sees everything tinted. On an effect area or a liquid, its top can wear a picture, the surface: water you can see into.
Wall
One edge of a room. Its flags say whether it blocks, can be climbed, is a trigger, and so on.
Thing
A spawn, light, monster or prop placed on a map with the Place tool.
Facing
The direction a placed thing turns to, from 0° to 355°. 0 points along +x, and angles go counter-clockwise. Props honour it; monsters do not yet.
Marker
A small UI hung on something in the world: a bar over a monster, a sign over a door, a pointer over a goal. Its look is a template made on Screens → markers. See Markers.
Sign
A marker placed on a map with the Place tool (sign: label, sign: marker or one of your own), showing a text you type. The level hangs it as it loads, with no graph. See Signs on a map.
Trigger
A card that says WHEN something happens, IF a condition holds, THEN do something. The presets are door, lift, switch, teleport and others.
Graph
A Blueprint-style board of nodes and links that runs logic for the game or for one map.
Game graph
A graph of the module's, not of one map. It runs once for the whole game, keeps its variables from map to map, and hears what happens on every map: players arriving and leaving, and every death and hurt (when_dies, when_hurt). It cannot touch a map's rooms; it calls the map's graph for that. See The game graph.
Death screen
What a player sees on dying. A new module has one: the starter's death game graph darkens the screen, says "You died. Back to the start!" and clears, on every map. See the death screen.
Movement state
A way of moving that a module defines on Player → movement: swimming, sliding, gliding, a stun. A player is in exactly one; default is the walking, jumping and climbing every body has. The ways under each state take the player to another when their conditions hold. See Movement states, meters and moves.
Meter
A number every body carries, from 0 to its max, named by the module: breath, stamina. It fills or drains at a rate that can differ per state. A way can ask whether it is empty or full, or pay from it, and a HUD bar shows it bound to meter.name.
Layered move
A push that lasts a moment, on top of whatever movement state runs: a dash, a jump pad, a boost into a slide. It can cost a meter, and it keeps going through a change of state.
Coyote time
The moment after walking off an edge when a jump still counts as a jump from the ground: coyote_time (0.1 s by default), on the Player page's jumping settings. After it, a body that fell has only the air jumps left.
Ledge grab
Catching the edge of a higher floor that a jump just misses, on the way down, and hanging from it: ledge_grab (off by default) on the Player page's climbing settings, and ledge_reach, how far above the head the edge may be. JUMP with the stick towards the ledge pulls up onto it; with the stick still, a small hop; with the stick away, a leap off.
Monster editor · weapon editor
Where Monsters and Weapons are made: tabs Overview, Behaviour, Attacks, Looks and Advanced (and Arsenal for weapons). Behaviour shows the states as sentences or as a graph, beside a preview that runs the real rules.
Workshop
The older window of tabs (brain, swing, body…). The Player is still edited in one; monsters and weapons have their editors.
Missiles page
Missiles in the rail: the module's missiles, shared by every monster and weapon, each with a side view of its flight and what it does when it hits, runs out or bounces.
Phase (of a monster)
A group of a monster's states, such as a boss's second, angrier half. + phase copies the last phase, makes it faster, and starts it once the monster's health drops. Not to be confused with a weapon's phases, the states of its phase machine.
Recipe (editors)
A monster or weapon to start from: Blank, Melee grunt, Shooter, Flyer, Charger, Boss (3 phases); Blank, Pistol, Launcher, Charge shot.
Whatever it is doing
The monster or weapon editor's rules for any state, checked before a state's own rules. A monster's built-in pain and death are listed there too. "Only once" makes a rule fire a single time, as a boss's phase change does.
@previous
A state that ends by going back to what it was doing: next = "@previous", "back to what it was doing (resumed)" in the editor. A hit's pain returns to the interrupted state where it was, without running its steps again.
Cooldown
A number that counts down to "ready": a step starts it ("start cooldown ready for 1 s"), and a rule waits for it ("cooldown ready is over"), so an attack or a shot comes at most once in a while. Monsters and weapons both have them; a saved game keeps the time left.
Swing
A melee attack: a sweep with its own numbers (damage, how long, from which angle to which, and the rest), a step of a monster's or a weapon's state. A newer swing replaces one still sweeping. In Lua, e:swing() starts one.
Trail
A particle kind that follows a body: a missile's Trail (Missiles page, Looks), or e:set_trail(kind) and p:set_trail(kind) from Lua. The particle kind's on a body says whether its specks stay where they came out (smoke) or move with the body (a flame, an aura).
Slash
How a melee swing is drawn in the game: a fading streak near the tip of the swept arc. Its look (colour, width, fade, texture…) is set per weapon and per monster over a default.
Style
The look the studio itself wears on this device: one of the shipped uikit skins (Claude, Paper, 90s, PS1, Aero) or one imported. It is never part of a module. Not to be confused with the Style section, which styles the game's screens.
Example project
A module that ships with the studio, opened from Home → examples as a copy of your own. Gem Hollow is the one the tutorials use.
Stable · dev release
The studio comes out in two lines. A stable release (0.4.3: three numbers) is the one to make a game with. A development release (0.4.3.1: four numbers; Nightly on the website) gets new features first, and they reach the next stable release later. A stable studio offers only stable updates. On these pages a badge such as studio 0.4.3.1 marks a feature that only the development releases have so far, from that one on. See Get the tools.
Plugin
An editor that runs inside the studio and edits one kind of source, such as ThreeSprite or qPainter.
Source
A project file an editor can open again. It lives under sources/.
Bake
Turning a source into what the game loads, under assets/. Play and Export bake whatever is stale.
Draft
Unsaved work in a plugin, kept every couple of seconds so it survives a reload. When you reopen, you are asked "go on with it" or "start from the saved one".
Snapshot
A copy of the module kept on the device. You can restore the whole module from it, or one map.
View
ThreeSprite: one rotation angle of a sprite. View 1 faces the viewer, and views go anticlockwise seen from above.
Mirror
ThreeSprite: you draw only half the views plus one, and the rest are flipped copies.
Rig · bone · part
ThreeSprite: a 3D skeleton. Hand-drawn 2D parts hang on its bones, and each part can have variants.
Solid
ThreeSprite: a 3D shape (cube, sphere, cylinder, cone or pyramid) drawn in every view.
Onion skin
Showing the previous frame faintly behind the current one while you animate.
Atlas
One PNG holding every frame of every view, plus a JSON manifest that says where each one is.
Wrap
qPainter: strokes that run off one edge come back on the opposite edge, so the texture tiles without seams.
Offset ½
qPainter: shifts the picture by half its size, so the seams come to the middle where you can see them.
Palette lock
qPainter: every colour you paint snaps to the palette, either to the nearest colour or as a dither between two.
Ramp
An ordered run of colours from dark to light. The ramp shade brush steps along it.
Sheet
An animated texture: frames stacked vertically (or side by side) in one picture, played at a set speed.
Skybox
A sky picture. A strip is DOOM-style, a panorama is 2:1, and a cube has six faces.
Patch
DigidreamSynth: one instrument's sound, made of oscillator, envelopes, filter, LFOs and mod matrix.
Bank · kit
The melodic patches (programs 0–127) and the drum sounds, one per note number (General MIDI drums are 35–81).
Envelope (ADSR)
How a value moves over a note's life: attack, hold, decay, sustain, release, with an optional delay first.
Mastering
The final EQ, compressor and limiter on the whole mix. It is baked into the .mstk.
Loop region
The part of a track that repeats. It is written as loopStart / loopEnd markers.
Cue · piece
A piece of music the game can switch to. A switch can happen now, at the next bar or at loop end, with or without a fade.
Recipe
DigidreamSound: a kind of sound to start from (Gunshot, Explosion, Laser / zap…). Randomize makes a new one of that kind.
Randomize · Vary
🎲 makes a fresh sound from the recipe; ✨ nudges the current one slightly.
Take
One version of a sound. The game picks one of the takes at random each time, so repeats don't sound identical.
Layer
One voice inside a take (noise, tone or metal), each with its own envelope and tone. Layers start together and add up.
Rings
Resonators that make a sound ring like a struck body. There can be up to 8.
Group
DigidreamSound: a shared bus (such as world, player or ui) with its own volume, limit and effect sends.
Sound pack
One .sfxproj in the module. The studio merges all packs into one bank, and a higher pack wins on a shared name.
